Twelve suspects were arrested simultaneously in mainland France and Martinique
without particular incident. [@1] The main organizer was acting directly from
his prison cell and has since been placed in an organized-crime detention unit.
[@1] Criminal cells that imported cannabis into Martinique and cocaine to
mainland France were dismantled in an operation led by the anti-drugs office
Ofast, the Versailles gendarmerie Research Section and Junalco. [@1] The flows
ran in both directions, with cannabis sent from mainland France to Martinique
and cocaine carried back to the continent. [@2]

More than a tonne of cannabis was seized in Martinique, described as an absolute
record. [@1] The case began with a 2024 customs seizure of nearly 500 kilos of
cannabis at the port of Fort-de-France. [@1] Several military-grade weapons,
vehicles, clothing and tens of thousands of euros in cash were seized during the
arrests. [@1] Investigators also traced nearly 500,000 euros spread across
different bank accounts. [@1] In Martinique, where cannabis consumption is
rising sharply, a kilo sells for 5,000 to 6,000 euros, more than cocaine. [@1]

A year of investigation, tailing and surveillance led police to one of France’s
most-wanted trafficking networks. [@1] Investigators linked the network to a
trafficker on Ofast’s national Top list of the 50 most dangerous narco-criminals
in the country. [@1] After surveillance, telephone interceptions and tailing,
police spotted a new drug shipment leaving the port of Le Havre in Normandy.
[@1] The total cannabis intercepted was well over a tonne, a new record for an
operation involving Martinique. [@2] Ofast treats the roundup as a historic
case. [@1] The roundup is expected to slow cannabis trafficking in the overseas
territory for a time. [@1]
