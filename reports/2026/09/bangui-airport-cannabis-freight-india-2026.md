Cannabis was loaded as freight at Bangui M'Poko International Airport onto an
Ethiopian Airlines aircraft and found when the shipment arrived in India. [@1]
Indian authorities then contacted their Ethiopian counterparts to report that
the shipment had travelled on an aircraft of Ethiopia's national carrier. [@1]
Ethiopian Airlines' management in Addis Ababa ordered its own services to
explain how cannabis entered the freight system and was loaded onto the
aircraft. [@1]

The airline's station representative at Bangui, named in the report as Erlens,
and a second official, M. Solomon, demanded explanations of how the freight was
boarded. [@1] The report names the head of freight, Sandos Stevy, and Luango
Tedi among staff directly implicated in the handling and control chain for
cargo. [@1] A customs officer assigned to the tourism service was sanctioned and
has been suspended from duty at home. [@1] The commissioner handling the case in
Bangui is said to have helped block and bury the follow-up to the affair. [@1]

The cannabis is reported to have reached India because checks before boarding
were deliberately bypassed. [@1] Corbeau News Centrafrique says it based its
account on freight documents, customs checks, loading registers and exchanges
between services after the discovery in India. [@1]
