On 2 September 2026 Santiago's Fourth Guarantee Court granted pretrial detention
to eight accused formalized in Operación Imperio Amsterdam for drug trafficking,
cannabis cultivation, diversion of an authorized grow, criminal association and
money laundering. [@3] The hearing involved 14 other accused present, for whom
the court applied other precautionary measures. [@3] Those measures included
total or partial house arrest, national travel bans, no-contact orders and
monthly reporting during the investigation, with a 120-day investigation period.
[@3] Two weeks earlier the Aysén regional prosecutor had said it would seek
pretrial detention for 23 accused, including managers of Dispensario Nacional
and M.A. Botanics, before the same Santiago court. [@1] In that earlier round
the court had already placed Mathiuws Van Eijck Edwards and Raúl Bravo under
total house arrest with a travel ban and no-contact order, and Eduardo Gendelman
under a travel ban and no-contact order. [@1]

The investigation totals 72 accused detained in the national operation. [@3] The
simultaneous sweep was carried out on 13 August in nine regions from Antofagasta
to Aysén. [@3] Two of those detained were held in Coyhaique at the Dispensario
Nacional branch on Arturo Prat 408. [@3] The sweep involved 42 entry and search
orders with more than 500 police officers and about 20 prosecutors deployed.
[@2] Prosecutors allege the organization's gains exceeded 8 billion pesos. [@1]
A case report says DN Medcorp received transfers, deposits and other income of
8,089 million pesos during the period under review. [@2]

Prosecutors say leaders ran the scheme through DN Medcorp using cannabis
produced by MA Botanics and other clandestine growers, sold through franchises
or directly via Dispensario Nacional. [@3] MA Botanics kept a grow in Pelarco in
the Maule region under a farm-agency authorization to cultivate CBD cannabis.
[@2] That authorization was openly and deliberately breached to produce high-THC
cannabis that was then distributed and sold massively across regions. [@3] The
inquiry began in June 2024 with an investigation of a Coyhaique cannabis
dispensary. [@2] From the consumer side members were asked for a medical
prescription with a generic diagnosis and cannabis treatment plus a
criminal-record certificate free of drug convictions, requirements prosecutors
call merely formal. [@3]
