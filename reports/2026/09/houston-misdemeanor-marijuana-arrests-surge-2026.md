Houston police officers have arrested about 440 people per year for misdemeanor
marijuana possession under Mayor John Whitmire, up from an annual average of
about 35 from 2021 through 2023, according to Houston Police Department data
obtained by Houston Public Media. [@1] In Texas, possession of up to 4 ounces of
marijuana carries up to a year in jail and a fine of as much as $4,000. [@1]
Department data showed Black people comprised about 74% of marijuana-related
arrests over the past five years, while U.S. Census Bureau estimates put
Houston's Black population at less than 23% as of 2025. [@1]

The figures were published two months before voters could instruct the Houston
Police Department to make misdemeanor marijuana possession its lowest possible
enforcement priority. [@1] Ground Game Texas, the advocacy group behind the
ballot measure, qualified it for the November ballot with over 40,000
signatures. [@2] Its executive director Catina Voellinger said the spike makes
the case even stronger for the policy. [@2] Whitmire said in August that
low-level marijuana had not been a priority for a long time. [@1] The police
union president questioned the data as a possible reporting issue in how arrests
are reported. [@1] The mayor's office did not respond to requests for comment on
the data. [@1]

The Harris County District Attorney's Office, which prosecutes crimes in
Houston, operates a program in which officers can choose to divert people from
jail into 90-day community supervision and a 4-hour cognitive class. [@1] A
spokesperson for that office called it a robust marijuana diversion program
focused on drug trafficking and organized crime. [@1]
