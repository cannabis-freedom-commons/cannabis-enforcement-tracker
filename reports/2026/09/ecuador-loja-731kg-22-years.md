A court in Loja sentenced Pablo S. to 22 years in prison on 28 September 2026 for illicit drug
trafficking, after police found 731 kg of cannabis hidden inside metal scaffolding near Ecuador's
border with Peru. [@1,2]

The seizure took place in Amaluza, in the Espíndola canton of Loja province. [@1,3] Anti-narcotics
police acting on a tip searched a property where five people were loading metal scaffolding onto a
truck; the structures had been altered and contained a green substance that tested positive for
cannabis. [@1,2,4] The scaffolding and the truck were moved to the Loja police command, where officers
counted 150 pieces of contaminated scaffolding and weighed 731,000 grams of cannabis. [@1,2,4]

The tribunal convicted Pablo S. and acquitted the other four defendants, finding that they did not
know what the metal structures contained. [@1,2] The prosecutor's office said the conviction rested
on testimony from the officers involved, a technical inspection report and the evidence log. [@1]

Article 220 of Ecuador's penal code punishes illicit trafficking in controlled substances with up to
26 years in prison. [@1,2]

The operation was called Estrategia Operacional 3D and followed intelligence identifying a warehouse used as a collection centre. [@3] The scaffolding tubes had been altered to hide the drug inside, a concealment method police called encaletamiento. [@3] Two of those detained were Ecuadorian and three were Peruvian nationals allegedly part of a cross-border trafficking structure on the Peruvian border. [@3] The two Ecuadorians were listed as Pablo S. M., 44, and Segundo A. J., 53. [@3] The three foreigners were listed as Marcos G. J., 25, Elit G. J., 28, and Héctor G. J., 32. [@3] Police said 1,462,000 doses of marijuana were removed and estimated the load at about 5.1 million dollars on the European market. [@3] Police also seized five mobile phones for tracing the network. [@3] A judge ordered preventive detention for Pablo S., Segundo A., Marcos G., Elit G. J. and Héctor G. J. [@4]
