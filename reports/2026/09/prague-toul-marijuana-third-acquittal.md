Prague's municipal court, the first-instance court, on 14 September 2026
acquitted Jiri Toul of marijuana trafficking for the third time, ruling the
evidence was insufficient for conviction. [@1] Prosecutor Simona Krupova
appealed the acquittal and asked Prague's High Court, the appellate court, to
quash the judgment and order a different municipal-court panel to retry the
case. [@1] Defence lawyer Jiri Solil opposed removing the case from its lawful
judges, invoking the constitutional guarantee that no one may be removed from
their lawful judge. [@1] Prague's High Court set a public hearing for December
to decide the prosecutor's request to quash the September acquittal. [@1]

Toul is charged over eleven alleged marijuana deals in 2018 and 2019 in which
prosecutors say he supplied Thomas Zens, who carried the drugs to Germany, while
Toul says the trade ran in the opposite direction. [@1] The same municipal court
had originally sentenced Toul in January 2023 to eight years in prison and a
450,000-koruna fine, a judgment the High Court quashed, after which repeated
acquittals were also quashed on appeal. [@1] The September judgment accepted
that objective evidence proved Toul's relationship and contacts with Zens,
Zens's trips to the Czech Republic for marijuana, and Toul's contact with the
seized shipment's packaging. [@1] The court said DNA traces on several packages
undermined Toul's account of buying only a small amount for personal use but
alone did not establish the direction of the transaction. [@1] The court found
that no objective evidence captured an actual handover, an agreement on price
and quantity, a payment for a consignment, or communication clearly showing Toul
as the seller. [@1] The defence says years of police monitoring captured no
concrete marijuana deal, a later house search was negative, no sample of the
alleged drug was secured in ten of the eleven cases, and the prosecution did not
establish the origin of about twenty kilograms Toul allegedly handled. [@1]

The case is unfolding as the government backed a criminal-procedure amendment
that would stop an appellate court after quashing a judgment from dictating how
the first-instance court evaluates evidence and makes factual findings, while
tightening removal of a case from its original judge. [@1] Justice Minister
Jeronym Tejc said higher courts should not bind lower courts on guilt after
supplementing evidence themselves, presenting the change as strengthening
first-instance independence. [@1]
