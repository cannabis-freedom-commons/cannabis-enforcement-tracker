On 24 September 2026 the court of first instance in Mouila, the capital of Ngounié province, sentenced a prison officer and two civilians over cannabis. [@2] Terence Eyeghe, an officer of the prison security service, and Abdoulaye Bobo and Clarek Mombo Boussougou each received twelve months' firm imprisonment and a fine of 100,000 CFA francs. [@1,2] Two other officers tried with them, Vinique Kassa Kassa and Ndembi Aworet, were acquitted. [@1,2]

The case began in February 2026, when the Ngounié office of the central anti-drug service arrested Bobo and Mombo Boussougou in possession of cannabis. [@1,3] Investigators then went to the home the two men shared with Eyeghe and found cannabis in his room. [@1,2] Eyeghe told them he had bought it on a trip to Libreville. [@1,2] L'Union reports that the cannabis was in fact taken from court seals held at the Mouila registry; Focus Groupe Media and TV+ Afrique say the investigators established that. [@2,3,4] Gabon Review attributes the same claim to a well-informed source. [@1]

At the hearing Bobo admitted taking part in the sale, telling the court "I have no answer. It was a favour." [@2] Mombo Boussougou said he had been using cannabis for about a year. [@1,2] Kassa Kassa and Aworet denied any part in it. [@2] The prosecutor asked for ten years against Eyeghe and Aworet, one year and a 100,000-franc fine for each of the two civilians, and the acquittal of Kassa Kassa. [@1,2] The court did not follow that: it cleared both Aworet and Kassa Kassa and gave the other three one year each. [@1,2]
