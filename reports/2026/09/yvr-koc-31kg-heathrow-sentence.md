Canada Border Services Agency officers intercepted Alaaddin Koc, 56, of Windsor, Ontario, at
Vancouver International Airport on 28 October 2024, finding about 31 kilograms of cannabis in his
suitcase as he was about to board a flight to London's Heathrow Airport. [@1] RCMP Federal Policing
Pacific Region border integrity officers arrested him, and he was released while the investigation
continued. [@1]

Charges were approved under the Cannabis Act, but Koc failed to appear in court and a Canada-wide
arrest warrant was issued. He was later located in Ontario. [@1] On 27 July 2026 he pleaded guilty
to possession of cannabis for the purpose of exportation, and on 21 September 2026 he was given a
one-year prison term and a lifetime firearms prohibition. [@1]

Richmond Provincial Court, where the case was heard, told the Richmond News that after credit for
time served the remaining term is 251 days, and that the firearms ban covers restricted and
prohibited weapons and ammunition. [@2] Staff Sgt. Rhonda Hislop, acting officer-in-charge of the
RCMP's Border Integrity Program (Air & Marine), said that while cannabis is legal in Canada there
is still an active illicit market, and that the outbound flow of illicit drugs remains a priority
for the program. [@1]
