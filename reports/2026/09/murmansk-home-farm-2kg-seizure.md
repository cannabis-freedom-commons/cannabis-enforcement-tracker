In Murmansk, police acting on a tip-off uncovered a home-based drug farm. [@1]
During an authorised search, officers seized about 2 kg of marijuana from the
fully equipped flat. [@1] The flat and the farm belong to a 48-year-old
previously unconvicted local man. [@1] He told police he kept the marijuana
exclusively for himself. [@1] Investigators are now checking that account. [@1]
A criminal case was opened against the man and is being investigated. [@1] He is
now under house arrest. [@1]

The room where the cannabis bushes grew had forced ventilation and powerful
phytolamps. [@1] Ready-to-use batches lay in the freezer and refrigerator, while
more bushes were still drying. [@1] Eleven expert examinations found he kept
about 300 grams of cannabis at home, not counting the discovered plants. [@1]
Officers also seized equipment for using, making and growing drugs. [@1]
Nord-News, citing the regional police, reports that the search found a specially
equipped growing room in one room of the flat. [@2] Plant parts were in the
fridge and freezer and some plants were drying. [@2] Examination identified the
substance as cannabis. [@2] The total seized weight was about 2 kilograms. [@2]

The case concerns storage of a large batch of prohibited substances, carrying
three to ten years in a penal colony. [@1] Nord-News reports the case was opened
under part 2 of Article 228 of the Russian Criminal Code. [@2] The outlet says
he faces up to ten years in prison. [@2]
