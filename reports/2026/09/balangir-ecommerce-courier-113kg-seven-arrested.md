Balangir police busted a cannabis-smuggling operation that used courier services
and arrested seven people. [@1] Acting on intelligence inputs, officers raided a
lodge near the railway station area and arrested seven people, including two
alleged masterminds from the M. Rampur belt of Kalahandi district. [@1] Police
named Chandrakanta Tandi and Bimal Tandi of Singhpur village under M. Rampur as
the principal accused, and also arrested Jagatram Tandi, Pradeep Suna, Ratna
Bhoi, Chhabilal Dharua and Tarachand Tandi. [@1] Police arrested seven men and
seized 113 kg of ganja hidden in saree boxes as they were on their way to the
nearest logistics centre. [@3]

Police seized 113 kg of ganja, two cars and nine mobile phones. [@1] The ganja
was recovered from 10 boxes weighing a total of 113 kg. [@2] Police intercepted
the accused travelling in two vehicles and seized a Hyundai Creta and a Maruti
Suzuki Swift Dzire. [@2] A case has been registered at Adarsha Police Station
and the accused have been produced in court. [@1]

The smugglers used an e-commerce company’s courier network to send ganja from
western Odisha to New Delhi and the NCR region. [@1] The accused registered with
the e-commerce company as a saree centre and handed over ganja boxes at the
Balangir point shown as household appliances and other goods. [@1] The courier
point had no scanner, and only the QR code on consignments was scanned before
dispatch. [@2] Investigators allege the group sourced ganja from Kandhamal,
packed it in Mohangiri, moved it to Balangir and dispatched it to Ghaziabad.
[@1] Delhi-Ghaziabad handlers had earlier trained Kalahandi businessmen and
helped them start the trade. [@1] The cover was an online registration as
Sambalpuri Saree Centre, CCTV allegedly showed similar bookings at least four
times in about six months, and police questioned the e-commerce company’s Odisha
head and searched the Balangir facility. [@1] Further investigation is underway
to identify receivers in Ghaziabad and remaining supply-chain links. [@1]
