Kazakhstan's interior ministry reported interim results of the Qarasora-2026
operation after its first three months, saying officers had stopped more than
2,000 drug offences and shut down four organised crime groups. [@1] The ministry
said it prevented 21 tonnes of drugs from reaching illegal circulation, without
giving a cannabis-only weight. [@1] Police recorded 256 cases of cultivating
drug-bearing plants and destroyed dozens of drug plantations. [@1] Officers
dismantled 25 indoor laboratories fitted out for growing drug-bearing plants,
the police said. [@1]

The ministry said the Shu Valley, a tract of more than 60,000 hectares, draws
drug criminals, and that the sandy areas of the southern regions suit
cultivation of drug-bearing plants. [@1] Police said they pulled forces and
resources into those regions and used aircraft, aviation and satellite
monitoring to find the plantations. [@1] The Qarasora-2026 operation, the
interior ministry's annual anti-cannabis drive, was still under way when the
interim results were published. [@1]

The ministry's own statement carried the same figures for the three months,
including four organised groups shut down and 21 tonnes kept out of circulation.
[@2]
