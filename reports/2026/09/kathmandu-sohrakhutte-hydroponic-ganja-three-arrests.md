Nepal's Narcotics Control Bureau arrested a Myanmar woman, Jin Jin Phyo, at a
hotel in Sohrakhutte, Kathmandu, where she was staying, and also arrested
Barkatullah and Yusuf Khan, both from Gorakhpur in India, in the same ganja
case. [@1] Officers found hydroponic ganja in Phyo's suitcase. [@1] She had
reached Kathmandu on a Pacific Airways flight from Vietnam, via Hong Kong. [@1]
The two arrested Indians, Barkatullah and Yusuf Khan of Gorakhpur, had been
given the job of taking the ganja on to Delhi and the National Capital Region.
[@1]

The arrest features in a wider account of smugglers flying higher-priced
hydroponic ganja from Thailand to Kathmandu and then bringing loads by road
across the open border at Sonauli and other Maharajganj and Siddharthnagar
crossings towards Delhi and Punjab. [@1] Jagran reports that the trade has
picked up since Nepal's Supreme Court ordered limited licences for hemp
production in May 2025, with a detailed order issued the previous week under the
1976 law. [@1] Nepal's western hills, central Nepal and the Tarai districts
bordering India are still used for cultivation without permits, according to the
newspaper. [@1]

Both countries' authorities met in Kathmandu on a strategy to stop the
smuggling, according to the page. [@1]
