Colombian Navy coast guard units rescued two Panamanian citizens from the sea
and detained them after their boat sank with a marijuana cargo near
Buenaventura. [@1] Two suspected Panamanian traffickers were captured in
Colombian Pacific waters when the launch carrying them sank with almost a tonne
of marijuana. [@2] The sinking happened near the Valle del Cauca coast, close to
the district of Buenaventura. [@2]

The Navy said naval intelligence had warned of a launch moving suspiciously in
the central Pacific, and Buenaventura Coast Guard boats went to inspect it. [@2]
When the coast guards reached the vessel it was sinking and its two crew members
were at risk of drowning. [@2] The acting defence minister, Pedro Sánchez, said
the Navy intercepted a go-fast boat in the central Pacific with international
air support. [@5]

The traffickers had filled the boat with 801 kilograms of marijuana in 34 sacks,
plus 10 plastic containers of fuel for a long trip. [@2] Coast guard units
recovered the 34 sacks of the drug floating at sea along with the 10 plastic
containers of fuel. [@3] A preliminary judicial police test confirmed the
material was marijuana, according to CNC Tuluá. [@4]

The Navy attributed the sinking to structural failures caused by excess weight,
worsened by bad sea conditions that undermined the vessel's stability. [@2] The
Navy said the operation kept 8 million dollars from entering criminal
structures' finances and 132,000 doses of narcotics off the world's streets.
[@2]

The two alleged Panamanian traffickers were placed at the disposal of the
judicial authorities for drug trafficking. [@3] The detainees and the seized
drug were handed to the competent authorities while investigations continued
into the cargo's origin and destination. [@1]
