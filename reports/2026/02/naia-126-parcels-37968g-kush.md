The Bureau of Customs at Ninoy Aquino International Airport seized 126 abandoned
inbound parcels containing dried leaves believed to be marijuana between 3 and 6
February 2026. [@1] The parcels held an estimated 37,968 grams (about 38 kg) of
suspected marijuana with a street value of P56,952,000. [@1]

The Inquirer's account names no arrests and does not say who sent or was meant
to receive the parcels. [@1] The Manila Times reported that customs officers
worked with PDEA and the NAIA Inter-Agency Drug Interdiction Task Group on the
operation. [@2]

The Bureau of Customs said the drugs were declared as ordinary consumer goods,
including clothing, magazines and household items, and described the parcels as
an attempt by criminal groups to use postal and courier channels. [@1] The
shipment was held to violate the Comprehensive Dangerous Drugs Act of 2002 and
the Customs Modernization and Tariff Act. [@1] All the seized items were turned
over to the Philippine Drug Enforcement Agency for laboratory examination, case
build-up and proper disposition. [@1]

Customs Commissioner Ariel Nepomuceno said the multi-day interdictions showed
frontline units denying syndicates the use of ports and courier channels. [@2]
The Bureau of Customs said its total value of illegal drugs confiscated at the
NAIA port in 2026 had exceeded P377 million with this seizure. [@1]
