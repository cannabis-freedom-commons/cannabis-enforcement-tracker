Chilean prosecutors and detectives of the PDI's Brigada Antinarcóticos Sur
dismantled part of an international ring that brought large loads of cannabis
into Chile through the north of the country. [@2] The operation, called
Operación Aguas Escondidas, ended with the detention of several suspects, one of
them a Chilean citizen. [@2] The PDI reported the arrest of a Chilean man caught
transporting the marijuana in the commune of El Bosque. [@3] The head of the
PDI's national organised-crime unit, Erick Menay, said the suppliers were
Bolivian and the drug entered through unauthorised crossings in the Antofagasta
region. [@3] The load was then taken to the Metropolitan Region and stored in
"security depots" in El Bosque for sale to microtraffickers. [@3] The
organisation moved the drug from Antofagasta into Santiago-area safe houses in
El Bosque for sale in the capital's southern sector. [@1]

Officers seized around 1,200 kilos of cannabis sativa, the vehicles used to move
the drug, mobile phones and firearms. [@1] Fernando Anais, the chief prosecutor
of the Fiscalía Oriente's criminal-analysis unit, said the operation led to the
inspection of a tonne and 200 kilos of drugs. [@2] Menay said the haul kept more
than one million doses off the streets and put its value at more than 6,000
million pesos, about USD 7 million. [@1]

At a press conference Menay said transnational crimes would be faced jointly
with the Public Prosecutor's Office and the Undersecretariat for Public Security
to give residents the security they want. [@2] Anais said the first phase of the
operation ended with the detentions but the investigation would continue in
order to reach the top of the criminal structure. [@1] Prosecutors said they
would ask a court to remand the detainee in pre-trial detention. [@3] Acting
Public Security Minister Rafael Collado said the country's police forces seized
134 tonnes of illicit substances in 2025, 135 per cent more than in 2024. [@1]
