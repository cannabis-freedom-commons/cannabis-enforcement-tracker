Drug-control officers of the Amursky district police department in Khabarovsk
Krai identified two cases of illegal storage of large amounts of plant-based
drugs. [@1] In the first case police stopped a foreign car driven by a
23-year-old resident of Ukrainka village. [@1] Officers found a white sack of
green plant material in the boot and a black plastic bag with similar contents
on the back seat. [@1] Examination established that the material was marijuana
weighing more than one kilogram. [@1] The detained man admitted he had gathered
the drug in a forest in the autumn for his own use. [@1] By his account he hid
the dope grass in his car from his wife. [@1]

In the second case police received information that a 68-year-old resident of
the workers settlement of Elban was keeping a consignment of cannabis at his
home. [@1] The information was confirmed when officers found dried plants in the
attic of the man's house. [@1] Testing showed the material was marijuana with a
total mass of about 10 kilograms. [@1] The man explained he had found bushes of
wild cannabis in a forest strip the previous autumn and brought them home for
his own use. [@1]

The investigative department of the Amursky district police opened criminal
cases over signs of an offence under part 2 of article 228 of the Russian
Criminal Code. [@1] Both suspects were placed under a written undertaking not to
leave and to behave properly. [@1] The Rambler relay of the police statement
says the maximum penalty under that part of the article is up to 10 years of
deprivation of liberty. [@2] The Rambler relay confirms officers opened storage
cases against Amursky district residents after finding the white sack in the
boot and the black bag on the back seat. [@2]
