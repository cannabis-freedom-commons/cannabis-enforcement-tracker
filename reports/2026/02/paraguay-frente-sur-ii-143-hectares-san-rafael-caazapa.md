Paraguay's National Anti-Drug Secretariat (Senad), with the Internal Defence
Command (CODI) and the Public Ministry, ended eight consecutive days of
eradication in protected forests on Tuesday 3 February 2026. [@1] The operation
was called Frente Sur II and ran in the San Rafael reserve, in Alto Vera
district, Itapúa department, and in Caazapá National Park. [@1] Over the eight
days the joint force, supported by Paraguayan Air Force helicopters, eliminated
about 143 hectares of marijuana plantations in the San Rafael reserve area. [@1]
The teams also destroyed about six tonnes of processed, chopped marijuana ready
for distribution. [@1] Five camps were found and dismantled, fitted with tanks,
irrigation systems, electric generators and other advanced equipment. [@1] The
Senad put the economic loss to the trafficking organisations at USD 65 million
and said the marijuana produced was destined for Brazil and Argentina. [@3]

Prosecutor Rocío Valdez said several raids were carried out in the Itapúa area,
covering 18 parcels and 148 hectares of crops. [@1] No arrests were recorded
during the operation. [@1] The units that took part were Senad Regional 3
Encarnación, the Joint Task Force (FTC Sur) and the anti-drug unit for Itapúa
and Caazapá, and the raids were carried out by air. [@1]

Officials said each hectare of marijuana represents one hectare of cleared
native forest, giving the operation what they described as a strong
environmental component. [@1] The Senad said the raids in the Caazapá park and
the San Rafael reserve were coordinated with the Public Prosecutor's Office,
CODI-FTC and the Paraguayan Air Force. [@2] The Senad valued the 143 hectares
and six tonnes it destroyed at more than 434 tonnes of marijuana kept out of
circulation, according to ADN Digital. [@4]
