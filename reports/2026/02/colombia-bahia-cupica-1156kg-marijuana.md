Army and navy troops found 46 sacks and a suitcase of marijuana in the Cupica
area of rural Bahía Solano, Chocó. [@1] The navy said the material weighed 1,156
kg and was packed in 1,190 packages. [@4] The find followed a naval intelligence
warning about a possible illegal stash in the general area of Bahía Cupica. [@3]
Marines from Battalion No. 23 and the Bahía Solano coast guard station went to
the site to check it. [@3]

Defence Minister Pedro Sánchez announced the find on X. [@1] His post put the
load at more than 1.1 tonnes. [@2] He called the result a contundente golpe
contra el narcotráfico en el Pacífico colombiano. [@1] The navy valued the load
at more than US$12 million. [@3] It said the find prevented more than 190,000
doses of drugs from reaching illegal markets. [@3] The material was handed to
the competent authorities. [@3] The navy attributed the load to the Clan del
Golfo and said it was to be moved along Pacific routes used to send drugs
abroad. [@1]

Two weeks before the announcement, authorities captured alias el Panameño, a
member of the Clan del Golfo's Pacific substructure described as a suspected
person responsible for the load. [@1] The authorities called the Pacific Chocó a
strategic zone for drug trafficking because of its location and the presence of
illegal armed groups. [@1] The government said it would keep joint operations in
the region to weaken criminal finances and strengthen the state's presence. [@1]
