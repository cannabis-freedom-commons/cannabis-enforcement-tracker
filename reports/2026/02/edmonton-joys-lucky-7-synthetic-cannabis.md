Rui Ho, 60, and Aaron Ho, 32, were arrested in late January, and with the corporation that owns
the Joy's Lucky 7 convenience stores they face a combined 63 charges, Edmonton police said on
23 February 2026: four counts of drug trafficking, four of contraband tobacco trafficking, two of
weapons trafficking and fraud under $5,000. [@1] No court dates had been set for either man. [@1]
The allegations have not been tested in court. [@2]

The charges follow a series of searches by the Edmonton Police Service convenience store task
force, beginning on 19 November 2025 at a Joy's Lucky 7 near 67 Street and 118 Avenue, where
officers seized roughly $60,000 of synthetic cannabis, nearly 45 bottles of psilocybin edibles,
nearly 1,000 contraband cigars, 70 grams of contraband chewing tobacco, brass knuckles and more
than $1,000 in cash. [@1] A second store near 156 Street and 106 Avenue was searched on
1 December 2025, where police seized nearly $4,800 of synthetic cannabis, packaging material,
nearly 40 spring-assisted knives and nearly 500 contraband cigars. [@1] A third warrant, on
18 December at a home near Lessard Road and Dalhousie Way, turned up nearly $15,000 in cash,
brass knuckles and a large selection of designer goods, including $25,000 in purses. [@1]

Follow-up inspections at both stores on 7 January 2026 seized nearly 200 packages of synthetic
cannabis and more than 300 contraband cigars carrying illegal "tax paid" stamps. [@1] Across the
searches, police valued the drugs, weapons, contraband tobacco and designer goods at more than
$109,400. [@1] Const. Michael Davis said the team was glad to have taken illegal drugs, contraband
tobacco and weapons out of shops that serve the community, including areas close to schools. [@1]
Heartland News reported the first store's seizure as more than 1,100 packages of synthetic
cannabis, with the drugs there put at almost $65,000 in street value and tax avoidance together.
[@3] Richelle Booker, a forensic pharmacologist with Criminal Intelligence Service Alberta, part
of ALERT, said synthetic cannabinoids are unregulated in Canada and may be more potent than
natural cannabis, causing effects that can include cognitive impairment, cardiovascular
complications and severe psychotic symptoms. [@2]
