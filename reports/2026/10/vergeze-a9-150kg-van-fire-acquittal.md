On 28 October, a 37-year-old man appeared before the Nîmes criminal court for
drug trafficking after his van was found burned out on the motorway. [@3] The
defendant lived in Belgium and worked for a parcel-transport company there, and
he said he had not known drugs were in the vehicle. [@3] Described as previously
unknown to justice, he was acquitted on the benefit of the doubt and left the
Nîmes courthouse free. [@1]

A man in his thirties had been tried the previous week at the Nîmes criminal
court over the van that caught fire near Vergèze on 25 October. [@2] On 25
October, a van caught fire on the A9 at Vergèze in the Gard and was found
destroyed, with no occupants present. [@1] Inside the passenger compartment,
rescuers found cigarette cartons and perfumes alongside about 150 kilos of
cannabis resin that was burning. [@1] At the same time, a Brussels resident at
the Montélimar rest area repeatedly called rescuers about his burning van,
investigators linked the call to the Vergèze fire, and the driver was placed in
custody. [@1]

He said he had collected the vehicle at Sète to drive it toward Lyon and then
Belgium, believing he was carrying olive oil, for 500 euros plus 1,000 euros in
expenses. [@1] He said he gave investigators the names of the people who had
ordered the transport. [@1] The prosecutor sought a one-year suspended prison
sentence plus confiscation of the seized items. [@3] The court acquitted him on
the benefit of the doubt for lack of sufficient proof that he knew about the
load. [@1]
