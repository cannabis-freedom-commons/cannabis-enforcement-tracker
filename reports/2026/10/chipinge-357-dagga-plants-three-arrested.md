Police in Chipinge arrested 48-year-old Maria Mawacha after 118 dagga plants
were found in her maize field. [@1] Mawacha appeared before Chimanimani
Magistrate Regis Mawarire on 29 October 2026 for plea recording and pleaded
guilty. [@1] Mawarire convicted her and sentenced her to 36 months’
imprisonment, suspending 18 months on condition of good behaviour. [@1] The
remaining 18 months were wholly suspended on condition she perform 580 hours of
community service at Nyambeya Primary School. [@1] Mutema told officers that the
plants were linked to her husband Simon Sithole, who was away from home. [@1]
The two accused persons were arrested and appeared before Chipinge Magistrate
Tariro Hove. [@1] The state withdrew charges against Mutema after plea, while
Sithole pleaded guilty and is awaiting sentence. [@1]

On 27 October 2026 around 4 pm, officers from Cashel Valley Police Station
received information that Mawacha was cultivating dagga in her maize field. [@1]
Mawacha voluntarily led officers to the field, where 118 plants ranging from 70
centimetres to two metres were discovered. [@1] Officers asked her to produce a
permit to cultivate dagga and arrested her when she had none. [@1] Three
officers travelling to Negwani Village in Chief Mutema passed through Nduku
Village and found dagga plants in Mutema’s garden very close to her homestead.
[@1] Police found 213 plants about 1.4 metres tall in that garden and 26 plants
in Sithole’s garden at Negwani. [@1] The second Chipinge case was reported as
involving 239 dagga plants. [@1] A second account said a joint operation by the
police and the National Anti-Corruption Association of Zimbabwe recovered 118
suspected dagga plants. [@2] That operation took place on 27 October in
Nyamashanga, Mushambi Village under Chief Mutambara. [@2]

NACAZ members led by Thomas Ngirazi worked with officers from ZRP Cashel under
Officer-in-Charge Inspector Ariberito Mbuwa. [@2] Mawacha and the recovered
plants were taken to ZRP Cashel for further procedures. [@2] Police said the
arrests are part of intensified efforts to curb drug and substance abuse by
targeting suppliers of illegal intoxicating substances. [@1] The second account
identified the suspect as Maria Mawacha arrested for cultivating dagga, the
local name for cannabis. [@2]
