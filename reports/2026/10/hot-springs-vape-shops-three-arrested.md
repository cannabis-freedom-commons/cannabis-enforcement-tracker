Three employees of two tobacco and vape shops in the Hot Springs area were
arrested on the afternoon of Thursday 29 October 2026, after an undercover
investigation into marijuana being sold illegally at the businesses. [@1,6] The
local drug task force made the arrests on multiple felony charges. [@1] One of
the shops was Ameer's Tobacco and Vape Shop at 1607 Albert Pike Road in Hot
Springs. [@1,5]

Garland County jail records name one of the three as Hemyar Mahubemulhi Abdulla,
booked on 30 October 2026. [@3] A second record names Ammar Aldailam, booked the
same day. [@4] A third names Sidnee Michelle Jones, booked on 29 October. [@2]

The jail records list the drug task force as the arresting agency. [@3] The
charges listed include possession of a Schedule VI controlled substance with
purpose to deliver. [@2] Aldailam and Jones also face simultaneous possession of
drugs and firearms. [@4]

DFA Regulatory Enforcement agents seized more than 6,400 illegal products in search and seizure warrants at Ameer's Tobacco and Vape at 1607 Albert Pike in Hot Springs, Ameer's Vape and Cigar at 4656 N Highway 7 in Hot Springs Village, and Loyal Vape at 3907 Central Avenue in Hot Springs. [@6] The seized products included intoxicating hemp-derived substances, synthetic cannabis, suspected controlled-substance compounds and illicit or unregulated vape products. [@7] Agents also seized 29 grams of marijuana flower, $4,298 in US currency and one Glock 9mm handgun, and found three concealed compartments behind false walls holding suspected narcotics and illegal vape products. [@7] At Loyal Vape the count included 1,356 grams of intoxicating THC products, alongside 1,339 illegal vape devices. [@6] The Sentinel-Record reported that the raids took place the previous week and that the figures came from a DFA news release issued on 5 November 2026. [@8] DFA Secretary Jim Hudson said the agents had made thousands of visits to retailers across Arkansas and that the arrests should serve as a warning. [@6] David Potter, director of the Regulatory Enforcement Division, said the retailers knew they were selling narcotics and took steps to conceal them, and that sellers may face criminal charges and loss of their permits. [@7]
