Members of the police Anti-Gang Unit raided a Rastafarian family's home in
Lentegeur, Mitchells Plain, at about 3.30pm on 10 October 2026. [@1,2,3] Police
spokesperson Colonel André Traut confirmed the operation, saying it ended in one
arrest and the seizure of dagga, the local term for cannabis. [@1,2,3] The amount
seized was not stated in any account. [@1,2,3]

## The family's account

The family says masked male officers arrived in several vehicles, broke into a
separate dwelling with a crowbar without showing a search warrant, and damaged
furniture and cupboards. [@1,2,3] The complainant said six women were on the
property but no female officers were present, and that young children were nearby
while her 11-year-old daughter was out. [@1,2]

She said officers took R2,500 in rent money from her 58-year-old mother's handbag
and forced the mother to eat a cannabis gummy against her will. [@1,2,3] "They told
my 58-year-old mother to eat the gummy against her will. She doesn't take edibles;
she never has," she said. [@1] The family says it holds documents for medicinal
cannabis use and herbalist recognition, plus a permit for a small clothing business,
and asks why the cannabis was seized. [@1,2] It also says Rastafarian households are
repeatedly targeted in such operations. [@1,2]

## A case against the police

Traut said a criminal case was registered against SAPS members over alleged cash
theft during what he called an allegedly unauthorised search, and that the claims are
under investigation. [@1,2,3] "The allegations are serious and are being investigated,"
he said. [@3] The family opened theft and malicious-damage cases at Lentegeur police
station and plans to approach the Independent Police Investigative Directorate. [@1,2,3]

Private adult cannabis use in South Africa is protected under the 2018 Prince court
judgment, while the 2024 Cannabis for Private Purposes Act has still not commenced. [@4]
