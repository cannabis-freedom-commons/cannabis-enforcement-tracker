Ukraine’s Criminal Cassation Court closed the criminal proceedings against a man
who had been prosecuted for producing 11.6 grams of cannabis for personal use.
[@1] The case materials say that at the end of August 2023 he uprooted cannabis
plants, moved them to his garage, dried and crushed them, producing cannabis
weighing 11.6 grams in dry-substance equivalent. [@1] On 7 February 2024 an
investigating judge authorized a search of his residence for the specific
purpose of finding and seizing a mobile phone with a specified number that
allegedly contained video threats in a separate threats case. [@1] Immediately
after the ruling was announced and a copy handed over, the man voluntarily gave
investigators the phone named in the warrant, whose number officers checked and
matched before packing it. [@1] Officers then continued to inspect the premises
and found cannabis, which became the basis for the drug charge. [@1]

The Shpola District Court in Cherkasy region initially acquitted the man,
finding the search protocol and resulting evidence inadmissible, but on 9
December 2025 the Cherkasy Court of Appeal overturned the acquittal and
convicted him of illegal acquisition and possession of cannabis without intent
to sell, fining him 17,000 hryvnias. [@1] On 13 October 2026 the Supreme Court
overturned that guilty verdict and closed the proceedings for lack of sufficient
admissible evidence of guilt. [@2] It held that once the phone was handed over
and verified, the search’s purpose was achieved, so the continued inspection
exceeded the warrant and amounted to a “blind search” or fishing expedition.
[@1] The court therefore ruled the search protocol, its results and derivative
evidence including expert conclusions inadmissible, noting the prosecution had
offered no other proof despite having the opportunity. [@1] It stressed that it
did not find that cannabis was absent or that its possession was lawful, only
that evidence obtained beyond the warrant’s defined purpose could not sustain a
conviction. [@1]
