Detectives of the PDI's Brigada Antinarcóticos y Contra el Crimen Organizado
(Briant) in Iquique, working with the Supraterritorial Prosecutor's Office,
dismantled an organisation that brought large consignments of drugs into
northern Chile to sell in the south of the Santiago Metropolitan Region and in
southern Chile while hiding the origin of its earnings. [@1] Officers detained
12 people: six Colombians, five Chileans and one Ecuadorian. [@3] PDI director
general Eduardo Cerna said the investigation had run for at least six months.
[@2] Vilas Radio reported that detectives raided premises in La Pintana, Lo
Espejo, Paine and Rancagua and arrested the 12 there. [@6]

Subprefect René Camilo, head of the Iquique anti-narcotics brigade, put the haul
at 2,711 kilos 600 grams divided between cannabis sativa and cocaine
hydrochloride. [@1] Four of the firearms came from abroad, one of them from the
police of Tucumán, Argentina. [@1] Cooperativa reported the seizure of eight
firearms, one of them identified as stolen from the police of Tucumán in
Argentina. [@3] Emol reported high-value vehicles and cash taken and commercial
activity set up through front men to conceal the profits of bringing in,
distributing and selling the drugs. [@2] T13 reported that the seized substances
were valued at 14,500 million pesos in total, cannabis and cocaine together.
[@7]

Subprefect Camilo said two Colombian brothers led the organisation and that
eight bank accounts used to move its money were frozen, one of them linked to
the leader of Los Monarcas de Cali. [@1] T13 named the brothers as Ferney and
Jefferson Camacho Suárez, who had entered Chile regularly more than three years
earlier and installed two barbershops in the Pablo de Rokha neighbourhood of La
Pintana as a commercial front. [@7] T13 reported that money transfers went to
William Ramírez, former leader of Los Monarcas de Cali, who was arrested in
Colombia in 2025 on an Interpol red notice and is being extradited to Chile.
[@7]

Prosecutor Marcelo Vargas Leiva said the 12 accused would be brought before a
court on Wednesday 28 October and formalised under the drugs law (Ley 20.000)
and the arms law. [@1] Vilas Radio reported the detainees would face charges of
criminal association, drug trafficking and illegal possession of firearms and
military-controlled items before Santiago's 15th Guarantee Court. [@6] El Dínamo
reported that the Supraterritorial Prosecutor's Office and the PDI led the
operation, named Operación Cafeteros, which the authorities described as the
year's largest illicit-substance seizure in Chile. [@4] La Tercera reported the
formalisation would cover people involved across the whole structure of the
organisation, not only those who transported the drugs. [@5]

Security minister Martín Arrau called the operation good news against drug
trafficking while saying border control and investigation remained pending
tasks. [@1]
