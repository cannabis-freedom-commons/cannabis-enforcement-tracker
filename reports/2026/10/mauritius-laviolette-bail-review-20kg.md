Mauritius's Supreme Court on 21 October 2026 rejected a judicial review brought
by Marie Nathalia Laviolette, a 37-year-old bakery owner from L'Escalier, and
kept her in pre-trial detention. [@1] The Supreme Court upheld the Mahébourg
court's refusal to release her on bail on 10 March 2026. [@1]

The case began in January 2026 with a cannabis cargo brought by sea from
Réunion, valued at 24 million rupees and hidden in a house at Baie-du-Cap. [@1]
Police arrested three suspects in that operation on 31 January 2026, among them
Laviolette's husband Jean Berty David Kardaree. [@1] Another man arrested in
mid-February 2026, Terry Toussaint, identified Laviolette by the alias Sorenza
as Kardaree's wife. [@1]

Since 24 February 2026 she has faced two provisional charges: aggravated drug
trafficking over 20 kg of cannabis intended for sale, and inciting Toussaint to
collect the parcel at Baie-du-Cap and bring it to her at Rose-Belle. [@1] Her
lawyer, Sanjeev Teeluckdharry, argued that no drug or suspicious money was found
at her home, that she had no criminal record, ran a bakery and was mother to
four minor children. [@1]

Judges Iqbal Maghooa and Renuka Devi Dabee found the offences grave and the
risks of flight, reoffending, witness pressure and destruction of evidence too
high, citing the disappearance of the accused's phone and of the 20 kg of
cannabis. [@1]
