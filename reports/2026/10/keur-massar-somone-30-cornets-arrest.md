The Keur Massar Research Brigade, a unit of Senegal's National Gendarmerie,
arrested two men suspected of belonging to a drug-trafficking network operating
between Guinea-Bissau and Senegal. [@2] Gendarmes first arrested a suspect in
Keur Massar on 28 September 2026 after finding him carrying 89 grams of cocaine.
[@1] Investigators then traced another suspected member of the network to Somone
and arrested him with support from the Somone proximity brigade, the local
gendarmerie unit. [@1] One suspect was placed in garde à vue, police custody, at
about 7 p.m. on 2 October 2026 for alleged criminal association and possession
and trafficking of chanvre indien, the French term for Indian hemp or cannabis.
[@1]

In Somone, gendarmes seized a bag containing 30 cornets, small retail wraps, of
chanvre indien. [@1] The Keur Massar seizure comprised 89 grams of cocaine,
mentioned here only to explain the mixed case and excluded from cannabis
figures. [@1]

According to the gendarmerie, the operation followed information about a large
trafficking network moving drugs between Guinea-Bissau and Senegal, after which
investigators identified several possible participants. [@1] The gendarmerie
said the inquiry was still continuing to establish the network's scale and the
roles of those identified. [@2]
