Sheffield Crown Court sentenced Kieran Murphy and Brandon Horne on 30 October
2026, giving Murphy another six years and Horne another four years. [@2] Serving
prisoners Brandon Horne, 23, and Kieran Murphy, 30, were jailed with Horne's
mother Michelle Midgley and Murphy's partner Lydia Pinnington for their roles in
the conspiracy. [@1,3,4] Horne was sentenced to four years and Murphy to six years
after both pleaded guilty to a number of drugs charges. [@1,3,4] Pinnington, 28, of
Clieves Road, Liverpool, was sentenced to five and a half years, while Midgley,
48, of Halifax Road in Ripponden was jailed for 32 months. [@1,3,4]

South Yorkshire Police said the group used "any items" they could to smuggle
ketamine, cannabis and spice into prisons across the UK. [@1,4] Members of the
gang smuggled drugs into prison hidden inside hot dog tins, mouthwash and
packets of washing powder. [@1] The gang exploited the system used for
forwarding on inmates' possessions when they were transferred from one prison to
another to get the items into jail. [@1,4] The group's items were found to contain
ketamine and cannabis as well as synthetic drugs such as spice and nicotine, in
packages totalling tens of thousands of pounds. [@2,5]

Using a contraband mobile phone inside prison, Murphy conspired with Pinnington
to smuggle items inside and bring Midgley and Horne into the circle. [@2] Murphy
was already serving a jail sentence for his role in another major smuggling
conspiracy, while Pinnington had received a suspended sentence for money
laundering linked to the activity. [@1] Detective Sergeant Gareth Gent said
those involved in smuggling drugs into prisons destabilise the prison
environment and fuel violence, intimidation and criminality. [@1]
