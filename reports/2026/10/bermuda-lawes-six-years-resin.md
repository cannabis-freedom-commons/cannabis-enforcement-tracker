Ty Lawes, 21, was jailed for six years on 7 October 2026 for importing cannabis resin
into Bermuda as a 17-year-old. [@1,2] He arrived at LF Wade International Airport on
BA flight 159 on 20 July 2023, and customs officers found the resin concealed in items
in his baggage during a secondary inspection triggered by a canine alert. [@1]

The seizure weighed 16,855.4 grams, with a street value of up to $3,371,080. [@1] Born
on 25 August 2005, Lawes turned 18 almost exactly five weeks after the offence and was
not placed in court until 25 February 2025, 19 months after his arrest. [@1] He
pleaded guilty on 27 July 2026, after a trial date had already been set. [@1]

Puisne Judge Alan Richards said an adult convicted after trial would have received 15
to 18 years, and even on a guilty plea more than ten. [@1] Following guidance from
England and Wales on offenders who cross a significant age threshold, he set the
starting point at the date of the offence: nine years, close to the ten-year maximum a
Family Court could have imposed on him as a young person. [@1] He discounted that by
about a fifth for the guilty plea and a tenth for the delay before Lawes first appeared
in court, and called six years the least sentence he could properly impose. [@1]

The judge noted that Lawes had no previous convictions and that young people are
selected as couriers because they are less likely to arouse suspicion. [@1] Time spent
in custody counts toward the sentence. [@1]
