Two Vietnamese men found tending a large cannabis farm claim they were
modern-slavery victims. [@1] The pair, aged 38 and 34, were arrested when police
executed a magistrates’ warrant at the unit on Seaham Grange Industrial Estate
on Wednesday, October 7. [@1] Both men were detained at the scene and arrested
on suspicion of cultivating cannabis. [@1] They have remained in custody since
and appeared by video link from Holme House Prison in Stockton for a plea
hearing at Durham Crown Court. [@1] Both defendants faced a joint charge of
being concerned in the production of cannabis, a class B drug, and pleaded not
guilty. [@1]

Officers found a significant cannabis-growing operation using a network of
lights and watering equipment. [@1] Police photos show some of the cannabis
plants found being cultivated in the unit at Seaham Grange Industrial Estate on
October 7. [@1] Speaking via a Vietnamese interpreter, the 38-year-old followed
his plea by saying he was forced to work there. [@1] Both men’s defence is that
they were victims of modern slavery. [@1] The prosecutor said the Home Office
has yet to provide documents confirming their immigration status. [@1]

The hearing was adjourned so the Home Office can assess their claims that they
were forced to work at the unit. [@1] The court set a contingency trial date of
March 15 next year, with the hearing expected to last two days. [@1] The
prosecution must serve its evidence by November 27 and the men must help their
lawyers prepare defence statements by late December. [@1] Judge Richard Bennett
remanded both men to remain in custody in the meantime. [@1]
