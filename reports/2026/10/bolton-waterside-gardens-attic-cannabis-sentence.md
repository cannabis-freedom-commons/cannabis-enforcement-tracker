Bolton Crown Court sentenced Jonathon Hall, 35, to 12 months in prison,
suspended for two years, for allowing his Waterside Gardens flat to be used for
cannabis supply. [@1] Hall had been arrested and interviewed after the attic
find and eventually pleaded guilty to allowing his premises to be used for the
supply of cannabis. [@1] The defence said Hall had no previous convictions, had
a positive work history with several positive references, and had done
everything possible to address his issues. [@1]

Officers arrived at Waterside Gardens on February 5 last year where they found
cannabis being cultivated across two rooms in one of the flats. [@1] Around 53
plants were in the process of being cultivated. [@1] Officers seized the keys to
a vehicle where similar packaging to that found at the flat was found. [@1] That
flat had been the home of David Jarvis, 38, who has since pleaded guilty to
producing cannabis and abstracting electricity. [@1] The keys and car led
officers to Hall's nearby flat, also at Waterside Gardens, where some of the
cannabis Jarvis had grown was drying in the attic. [@1] Officers found more than
7,200 grams of cannabis in Hall's property, which prosecutors said would have
been worth between £18,970 and £72,670 at street level depending how it was
sold. [@1]

The pre-sentence report on Hall was described as very positive, with the court
told he was unlikely to commit further crimes. [@1] The judge nevertheless
reminded the court there had been a very significant quantity of cannabis in his
attic. [@1] Alongside the suspended sentence, Hall was ordered to complete 240
hours of unpaid work and a three-month curfew between 8pm and 7am each day. [@1]
