Touho gendarmes identified a father, his son and his son's wife, all residents
of the Koé tribe, as the family behind the case. [@1] The three admitted running
the cannabis traffic from early 2022 through 30 September 2026. [@1] They were
held in custody and then brought before the prosecutor's office under the
guilty-plea procedure of comparution sur reconnaissance préalable de
culpabilité, in which they admitted the facts. [@1] The penalties proposed by
the prosecution were accepted by the three defendants and then approved by a
judge. [@1] Two of them received 18 months' imprisonment with 6 months
suspended, with one ordered to pay a 400,000-franc fine and the other placed on
a two-year probationary suspension including 140 hours of community service plus
work or training and fine-payment obligations. [@1] The third defendant received
18 months' imprisonment with 8 months suspended plus a 400,000-franc fine. [@1]
The custodial portions were not enforced immediately, with the prosecution
linking that decision for two of the convicted to their lack of prior
convictions. [@1]

The investigation began with a tip about herbal cannabis dealing conducted
mainly around the Touho high school, which put gendarmes on the three suspects'
trail. [@1] During the search, gendarmes found 988 cannabis plants along with
growing equipment. [@1] Officers also seized 12,395 francs in cash. [@1] The
prosecution estimated the resale attributed to the three at 3.66 million francs,
and a motorboat and other equipment were seized and then confiscated as criminal
assets. [@1]

The three were convicted on Friday 2 October. [@1] Nearly 1,000 plants were
discovered during the search. [@1]
