Norwalk police arrested a Bronx-based employee of A-to-Z Tobacco Vape during a
search-and-seizure raid at the shop. [@1,2] The employee was charged with multiple
counts of drug possession after the search. [@1]

Officers executed the warrant on Wednesday at A-to-Z Tobacco Vape on Connecticut
Avenue in Norwalk. [@1] They seized more than 10 pounds of unlicensed marijuana
and THC products at the shop. [@1,2] Officers also seized $1901 in cash and a
range of Kratom products in the same search. [@1,2] Police said they also found 19
packages of Kratom pills and roughly two liters of liquid Kratom divided into
smaller containers at the shop. [@1] Police photos of the seizure show pop-top
style containers and prerolled marijuana cigarettes found at the shop. [@1,2]

Lt. Ramon Tejada said via press release on Monday that the raids were part of
ongoing investigations into smoke shops throughout Norwalk suspected of selling
cannabis without required licenses. [@1] Connecticut law requires a state-issued
license to sell products containing a total THC concentration greater than 0.3%,
while cannabis itself must be sold in a licensed dispensary. [@1]

The shop is at 108 Connecticut Avenue in Norwalk. [@2] Officers found the marijuana behind the counter and inside a concealed compartment in a rear storage room closed by a fake electrical box with a remotely operated magnetic locking device. [@2] The latest search seized 10.2 pounds of marijuana flower and THC products. [@2] The arrested employee was identified as 35-year-old Abdo A. Al-Areqi of Bronx, New York. [@2] He was charged with sale of over 1 kilogram of cannabis, sale of a narcotic substance, possession of over 8 ounces of cannabis and possession of a controlled substance. [@2] He was held on $150,000 bond and scheduled to appear in court on Oct. 29. [@2] The latest investigation followed a separate search and seizure warrant at the same location on Oct. 13. [@2]

Norwalk police said the raids at two local smoke shops were part of ongoing investigations into shops suspected of selling cannabis without the required state-issued licenses. [@3] Lt. Ramon Tejada gave that account in a press release issued on the Monday before the article was published. [@3]
