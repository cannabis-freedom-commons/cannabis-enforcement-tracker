The European Anti-Fraud Office completed an enquiry into gummies containing THC,
the main psychoactive substance in cannabis, sold in counterfeit packs
infringing a very well-known European gummy brand. [@2] French Customs reported
two consignments originating in the United States and destined for the
Netherlands in support of the enquiry. [@2] The consignments contained 3,560
packs of gummies, of which 3,213 copied the look of a popular brand using
altered lettering to resemble its original name. [@2] All of the packs were
seized and destroyed. [@2]

The investigation also led to the identification of multiple online vendors
selling this type of product. [@2] OLAF's intelligence enabled the rights-holder
to take appropriate action against the sellers concerned. [@2] OLAF also
identified a number of websites and physical retail outlets in France offering
illicit psychoactive or narcotic products. [@2] That intelligence was
transmitted to the French authorities for assessment and possible action, and no
arrests or criminal charges are named in the accounts. [@2]

Products of this kind are produced abroad without any regard to the safety,
labelling and quality rules that apply in the European Union. [@2] Consumers
have no reliable information about the fact that the gummies contain THC, or
about the other ingredients or the conditions in which the gummies were made.
[@2] The familiar packaging gives a false impression of a safe, ordinary
product, and a single portion may contain a dose of THC that the consumer
neither expects nor is able to assess. [@2] Gummies are consumed widely by
children, and OLAF Director-General Petr Klement warned that such counterfeits
can put health and safety at risk, especially for children. [@2] A Taiwan LIFE
report summarised the episode as gummies from the United States containing fake
THC found in the European market, raising cross-border food-safety and
drug-trafficking alarms. [@1]
