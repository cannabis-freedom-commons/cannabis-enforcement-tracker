A federal judge in Wisconsin ruled that a sheriff's deputy violated a driver's
Fourth Amendment rights by extending a traffic stop by 17 seconds for a drug-dog
inspection that led to marijuana and a firearm being found. [@1] In an October
29 ruling, U.S. District Judge J.P. Stadtmueller granted partial summary
judgment for Brian Burnside, finding Marquette County Sheriff's Deputy Brendan
Cutrell unlawfully prolonged the stop without reasonable suspicion. [@1] The
judge rejected the deputy's claim of qualified immunity, leaving only financial
damages for trial. [@1] Burnside had faced state criminal charges that were
dismissed after he challenged the search, and he filed a federal civil rights
lawsuit against Cutrell and Marquette County in February 2025. [@1]

The case arose from a December 9, 2023 stop on Interstate 39 in Marquette County
after Cutrell saw Burnside driving about 89 miles per hour. [@1] Cutrell issued
citations for speeding and illegal window tint but also called a K-9 unit to
pursue suspected drugs in the vehicle. [@1] The dog began inspecting at 10:57:16
p.m., Cutrell finished explaining the citations at 10:57:24 p.m., and the dog
alerted at 10:57:41 p.m., creating the 17-second extension. [@1] The later
search found a pistol, ammunition, an open bottle of whiskey and a bag of
marijuana that tested positive for THC. [@1]

The judge rejected the deputy's cited grounds including speeding, tinted
windows, cologne smell, nervousness and the Wausau to Wisconsin Dells route as
mostly common behaviors showing little criminal activity. [@1] The decision
applied the U.S. Supreme Court's 2015 Rodriguez v. United States rule that
police generally cannot extend a completed stop for a dog sniff without
independent reasonable suspicion. [@1] The judge found the stop's legitimate
purpose ended when citation explanations finished, making the roughly 17-second
continued detention unconstitutional. [@1] The court held the protection was
clearly established by the 2023 stop, so Cutrell could not use qualified
immunity, and damages will be decided at trial. [@1]
