Estonia's Tax and Customs Board (MTA) stopped nearly 79 kilograms of narcotic
and psychotropic substances from crossing the border illegally and reaching the
black market in the first nine months of 2026. [@1] Cannabis and cannabis
products were the largest share of what the board seized, alongside GHB and its
analogues, new psychoactive substances and cocaine. [@2]

In the same period the board seized nearly 20 kilograms of GHB and its
analogues, 5.3 kilograms of new psychoactive substances and 4.9 kilograms of
cocaine. [@1] Vitali Zajarin, head of the board's drug enforcement unit, said
seizures of new psychoactive substances had risen from about 2.4 kilograms in
the first nine months of 2025 to nearly 5.3 kilograms this year. [@3] Zajarin
said users may not know the composition or strength of the new substances, which
he linked to a significant risk of poisoning and overdose. [@2]

By the board's estimate, the quantities seized this year, depending on the
substance, its purity, dosage and method of use, could have supplied hundreds of
thousands of individual doses. [@2] Zajarin said each intercepted consignment
keeps dangerous substances from crossing the border and reaching the black
market, and that the board works with law enforcement in Estonia and other
countries to detect and investigate cross-border smuggling. [@2] The board asked
the public to report suspected drug smuggling and suspicious activity through
anonymous tips on its website. [@2]
