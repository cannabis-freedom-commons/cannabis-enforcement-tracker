Mahmood and Sarhan, along with two others, were sentenced at Swansea Crown
Court. [@1] Three members of the gang were jailed at Swansea Crown Court, though
the ringleaders remain at large. [@2] Police launched an investigation into a
Kurdish gang believed to be recruiting asylum seekers to sell drugs via the
Pasha line in Swansea. [@1]

On 11 August 2026 officers saw a suspected drug exchange in Carlton Terrace in
the Mount Pleasant area of Swansea. [@1] They arrested Sirwan Aland, aged 22,
from a black Audi which contained cannabis, cash and drugs equipment. [@1] Omer
Mahmood was spotted driving a Ford Focus in Swansea later the same day and was
arrested. [@1] The investigation led officers to a property in Swansea where
they found Barzan Sarhan in a bedroom. [@2] In the house officers found bags of
cannabis, cash, identity documents, a lock knife, cocaine, car keys and phones,
including the one holding the Pasha line number. [@2] One of the keys opened a
VW Golf parked nearby, where officers found bags of cannabis and cash in a
hidden footwell compartment. [@2] Mohammed Yasin was identified as another
member of the gang and was arrested the following day. [@2] The court was told
police seized 17 grams of cocaine worth up to £1,000 and 1.2 kilograms of
cannabis worth up to £8,000, along with cannabis vapes, edibles and a quantity
of cash. [@2]

Mahmood, Sarhan and Yasin all admitted being concerned in the supply of cocaine,
being concerned in the supply of cannabis and possessing criminal property. [@1]
Sarhan further pleaded guilty to possessing cocaine with intent to supply. [@1]
Aland admitted being concerned in the supply of cannabis, possession of cannabis
with intent to supply and possession of criminal property. [@1] With discounts
for his guilty pleas, Mahmood was sentenced to 28 months' detention in a young
offenders' institution. [@2] With discounts for his guilty pleas, Sarhan was
sentenced to four years in prison. [@2] Yasin was sentenced to 28 months for the
drugs matters and two months for a Treorchy burglary, with three months of an
earlier suspended sentence activated, making 33 months in all. [@2] Aland was
given an 18-month community order with a probation requirement and 150 hours of
unpaid work. [@2] In interview Mahmood claimed he was being paid £30 a day to
supply cocaine and cannabis. [@1] Mahmood, the 20-year-old living in West
Bromwich, is an Iraqi national with no leave to remain in the UK. [@1]

Judge Huw Rees said the court was dealing with serious offences of trafficking
Class A and Class B drugs. [@2] Sarhan has a previous conviction for conspiracy
to supply cocaine, for which Swansea Crown Court sentenced him to four years in
2024. [@2]
