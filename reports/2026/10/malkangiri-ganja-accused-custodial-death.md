Haryana police suspended five CIA Hansi personnel after ganja-smuggling accused
Tanup Dey, also called Rahul and Bapun, died in police custody in Odisha. [@1,4]
Hansi Superintendent of Police Vinod Kumar ordered the suspensions and a
departmental inquiry into the death. [@1,2,3] The inquiry will examine the
circumstances and all related aspects of the incident. [@1]

The case began on 27 March, when a CIA Hansi team arrested four men near the
canal bridge on the Garhi to Bhatol road and seized 63 kg 700 grams of ganja
from them. [@1,2,5] Police identified the four as Rakesh and Manveer of Igrah in
Jind district and Vinod and Ravindra of Puthhi. [@1,2,3] Investigators said
disclosure statements by Manveer and Vinod named Odisha resident Tanup Dey as
the ganja supplier. [@1,2,3] A CIA special team arrested Dey on 25 October with
local police assistance and, after a court granted five days of transit remand,
stayed with him in Odisha's Malkangiri district. [@1,2]

Police said Dey went to the washroom on the morning of 26 October and hanged
himself there with a gamcha, a cotton cloth. [@1,2] The escort team informed local
police, who completed the required legal process under National Human Rights
Commission guidelines. [@1,3] The suspended men are Assistant Sub-Inspector
Sammat, PSI Kapil, EHC Joginder, EHC Arjun and Constable Mukesh, who must report
at the Hansi police lines during suspension. [@1,2]

Police gave the four accused's parentage as Rakesh son of Krishna, Manveer son of Jora, Vinod son of Lahna and Ravindra son of Kuldeep. [@2]

The CIA-1 Hansi team had earlier arrested four men near a canal culvert on the Garhi-Bhatol road, police said. [@3]

The Haryana team checked into a hotel in Malkangiri town instead of taking Dey to a local police station, a decision a protester questioned. [@6] Dey's family alleged that the Haryana police personnel beat him to death and tried to pass the death off as suicide. [@7] Dey's relatives and local residents gathered outside Malkangiri police station and demanded an impartial inquiry into his death. [@7] Protesters blocked National Highway 326, which connects Asika in Odisha with Chinturu in Andhra Pradesh. [@6] The Haryana police personnel who had come for Dey stayed inside the police station while protesters remained outside in the evening. [@7] The Haryana team reached the area in a vehicle after a two-and-a-half-day journey and arrested Dey the next day. [@6] The Hansi CIA in-charge said police from another state are not ordinarily permitted to keep an accused overnight at a local police station, which is why the team stayed in a hotel. [@6] Malkangiri's sub-divisional police officer said the Haryana team was staying at a hotel in Malkangiri town and was to leave for Haryana with Dey on Monday. [@7] The Hansi CIA in-charge said Haryana police teams had visited Dey's village three times before without finding him. [@6]
