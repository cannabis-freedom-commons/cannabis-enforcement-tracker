Portugal's Judicial Police ran Operation Erva Daninha, with about 300 inspectors, against a group
accused of putting large quantities of cannabis on European and African markets. [@2,6] In the May 2025
raids officers executed 64 search warrants across mainland Portugal and Madeira, with further searches in
Spain, Bulgaria and Cyprus, and detained five people, all reported as Portuguese. [@2] A 48-year-old man
described as one of the leaders was detained in the Netherlands and extradited to Portugal, apparently on
11 September 2025, for a first judicial interrogation. [@3] He was described as a specialist in moving
cryptoassets on the dark web, in a money-laundering structure with its own cell; other suspects include
bank directors, lawyers and accountants. [@1]

## A medical-cannabis front

Medical cannabis cultivation and export have been allowed in Portugal since 2019. [@1] The group allegedly
bought pharmaceutical firms and set up licensed medical-cannabis wholesale, import and export companies,
then used false documents and certificates to send thousands of kilos to illicit markets. [@2,5] Infarmed,
the medicines regulator, approved tonnes of medical cannabis for export to Kenya, Congo and Guinea-Bissau,
countries with no law on the matter. [@1,5] The network is described as a Costa del Sol organisation that
moved its base to the Algarve and spread across Portugal to exploit weaknesses at Infarmed. [@1]
Authorities suspect profits may have funded terrorism through informal money-transfer systems. [@1]

Investigators seized about 1.78 tonnes of cannabis at premises in Sardoal and more than 3 tonnes of
biomass and flower in warehouses. [@5] A laboratory dismantled in central Portugal yielded one tonne of
amphetamines, the largest such seizure in the country. [@1,4]

## The accusation

On 22 May prosecutors charged 24 defendants, 13 people and 11 companies, with criminal association for
trafficking, aggravated narcotics trafficking, money laundering and document forgery. [@4,5,6] They
describe a network with great logistical and financial power, established in Portugal since 2021 [@4] and
led by Dutch and Danish citizens who settled there in 2020. [@6] It allegedly bought companies with
medical-cannabis licences and changed their officers repeatedly to hide its leaders, diverted cannabis to
the European black market, made fraudulent exports to the Democratic Republic of Congo, Kenya and
Guinea-Bissau, imported amphetamines for European laboratories, and forged licences, customs declarations
and import certificates. [@4] Profits were allegedly shared through sham commission contracts, laundered
through property, tourism and trading companies with Malaysian financiers, and passed through
cryptoassets before returning to banks. [@4]

Through Smart Nature, whose legal representative Aldo Vidinha is named as a central figure, the group
allegedly moved at least 6,751 kg of cannabis flower under a medical cover, and through Linnaeuspharma it
bought Sofex — Farmacêutica, a medicines maker in Massamá, in 2021. [@6] Geert Caers allegedly moved more
than 30.4 million dollars in cryptoassets on Binance between 2019 and 2024; the indictment documents more
than 11 million euros moved through 17 Malaysian front companies, and a forged Guinea-Bissau import
certificate dated to expire on 30 February 2024 that passed Infarmed unnoticed. [@5] Until May 2025
Infarmed had no systematic checks with authorities abroad to validate import certificates; it says it has
since tightened control and inspection. [@6]

Two individual defendants, including the man extradited from the Netherlands, are in pre-trial detention
[@4,5], two are at large under European and international arrest warrants, and the rest await trial at
liberty. [@4]
