Officers of the Interior Ministry's Central Criminal Police Department detained
eight people in Georgia on charges of illegal acquisition and storage of drugs
in an especially large amount, their import into Georgia and facilitation of
sale. [@1] Those detained included citizens of Georgia, Turkey and Japan. [@3]

Searches of the suspects' homes, personal searches and investigative experiments
recovered especially large batches of narcotic and psychotropic substances
prepared for sale from caches the suspects indicated. [@1] The seizure list
included cocaine, methadone, buprenorphine, methamphetamine, opium, amphetamine,
marijuana and cannabis plants, plus about 300 pregabalin tablets. [@1] Part of
the suspects distributed drugs through dead-drop caches known as zakladki. [@3]
During operational measures police found the dead-drop caches equipped by them
with prohibited substances intended for further sale. [@3] Other suspects were
linked to an international supply channel in which prohibited substances arrived
in Georgia in foreign postal parcels made out in the suspects' names. [@3] Those
parcels were intercepted by customs officers at the criminal police's request.
[@3] Some detainees were exposed through a special operation by the Central
Criminal Police jointly with the Customs Department of the Revenue Service using
controlled substitution of postal packages addressed to them. [@2] Police also
found materials intended for packaging drugs during the investigation. [@2]

The alleged offences carry up to 20 years in prison or life imprisonment. [@1]
