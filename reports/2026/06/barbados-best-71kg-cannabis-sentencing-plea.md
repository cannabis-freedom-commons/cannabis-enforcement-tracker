A defence attorney urged the High Court to impose a $30 000 fine rather than a
custodial sentence on a fisherman convicted of trafficking more than 70
kilogrammes of cannabis more than seven years ago. [@1] Defence attorney Kristin
Vanderpool told the High Court that a fine would meet justice in the case of
Troy Decoursey Best. [@1] Best, of Inch Marlow, Christ Church, previously
admitted his guilt before Justice Anthony Blackman in the No. 3A Supreme Court.
[@1]

Within Barbados’s territorial waters, he had in his possession 71.6 kilogrammes
of cannabis. [@1] He also confessed to trafficking and importing the drug. [@1]
The offences occurred on January 18, 2019. [@1]

Vanderpool told the court that the amount of the drug was an aggravating factor.
[@1] To Best’s credit, there was no high degree of sophistication in the crime,
he was not the mastermind and there was no attempt to flee when the boat was
spotted by the Coast Guard. [@1] Best had one previous dated conviction, had
pleaded guilty, had shown remorse and had been cooperative. [@1]

Suggesting a six-year starting point, Vanderpool said mitigating circumstances
should reduce this to five years and urged that a non-custodial sentence of a
fine be imposed. [@1] The sentencing hearing continues on June 10, when
Principal State Counsel Joyann Catwell will make her submissions. [@1]
