On 11 June 2026, the Tokyo District Court sentenced Yuki Nishimiya, 45, known as
Micro of the music duo Def Tech, to two years in prison suspended for four years
for violating the Narcotics Control Act. [@1] The court also ordered the
forfeiture of two cannabis items, matching the two-year prison term and
confiscation prosecutors had requested. [@2] Judge Hideki Murohashi said
Nishimiya possessed multiple illegal drugs and used them selectively for casual
motives such as staying awake while driving, indicating dependence and weak
respect for the law. [@4]

According to the ruling, Nishimiya possessed 3.517 grams of dried cannabis at
his home in Shibuya Ward, Tokyo, on 2 February 2026, and used cocaine and other
substances in a car in Chiba Prefecture around the same day. [@1] The drugs
ingested in the Chiba vehicle were cocaine and MDMA. [@3] Agents of the
Narcotics Control Department of the Kanto-Shinetsu Regional Bureau of Health and
Welfare found the dried cannabis during a search of his home and arrested him in
the act. [@4] Tokyo District Public Prosecutors indicted him in the Tokyo
District Court on 20 February for violating the Narcotics Control Act. [@6] By
13 March, the Narcotics Control Department had referred an additional allegation
to prosecutors accusing Nishimiya of cocaine use. [@7] He was released on bail
and attended counselling to prevent drug dependence under supervision from
family members living with him. [@8]

At his first hearing on 1 June, Nishimiya admitted the charges while his defence
asked for a suspended sentence. [@8] During questioning, he testified that he
used cannabis brought from Hawaii to escape excessive expectations and pressure
ahead of Def Tech's 20th-anniversary events. [@8] His arrest six days before a
scheduled 20th-anniversary concert at Nippon Budokan forced the show to be
cancelled. [@8] The court suspended the term because Nishimiya admitted the
indictment, showed remorse, was attending a medical clinic, and lived with his
mother, who checked his mobile phone history. [@4] On the day of the verdict,
his management agency announced the termination of his artist contract, and
Nishimiya posted an apology on Instagram. [@5] He said he would base his future
work at his personal company, Wergshire, and continue both Def Tech and solo
activities after leaving 2VOX. [@5]
