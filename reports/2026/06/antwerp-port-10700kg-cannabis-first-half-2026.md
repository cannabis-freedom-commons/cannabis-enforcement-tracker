The cannabis seizures were made in the port of Antwerp. [@1] Cannabis seizures
there increased in the first half of the year. [@1] Customs made 13 marijuana
interceptions, compared with six in the same period of 2025. [@1] The total
volume seized was 10.7 tonnes, compared with 12.3 tonnes a year earlier, with
smaller individual loads. [@1] The figures come from a mid-year review published
by SPF Finances, the federal finance service overseeing Belgian customs. [@1]

Most of the intercepted marijuana came from Canada, with which cooperation has
been strengthened, while the seized hashish came mainly from Morocco. [@1]
Customs administrator-general Kristian Vanderwaeren attributed the rise in
Canadian seizures to conditions in the local market there. [@1] He said Canada
has legalised cannabis nationwide for domestic production and consumption, but
export is banned, and overproduction leaves sellers able to profit by exporting
it to the European Union. [@1]

In the same half-year review, customs reported 5.4 tonnes of cocaine seized at
Antwerp, down from 16.7 tonnes a year earlier, a separate drug total excluded
from the cannabis figures. [@1]
