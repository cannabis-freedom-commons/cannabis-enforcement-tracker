Police in Ceará found about 290,000 marijuana plants on a farm in Acopiara, in
the state's interior, during an operation on 25 June 2026. [@1] The Civil Police
of Ceará (PCCE) said the plantation covered about three hectares, with some
160,000 plants still growing and 130,000 already harvested. [@1] The state's
Civil Police described it as one of its largest plantation seizures in recent
years, and no suspects were found at the site. [@1]

On 3 July, federal deputy André Fernandes (PL) said he had returned to the farm
and dug up marijuana plants that were buried rather than incinerated, and he
filmed a video at the site. [@2] Brazil's drug law requires drugs seized without
an arrest in the act to be destroyed by incineration within 30 days, keeping a
sample for the definitive forensic report. [@2] On an earlier visit on Saturday
27 June, the deputy reported finding bags of marijuana abandoned, more than half
the plantation not destroyed, notebooks and a mobile phone left behind, with no
security at the site. [@2] Fernandes said the operation had been halted at the
order of some powerful person, without presenting evidence for the claim. [@2]

The Civil Police said the destruction and controlled incineration was concluded
on Thursday 2 July, using a Fire Brigade technique of digging trenches, burning
the plants with petrol and diesel and covering the residue with earth. [@3] The
police said what the deputy found were the remains of that plantation and of
other plants destroyed on the land, and a police director said the material was
burned and not marijuana. [@3] Fernandes said the plantation had held about 5
tonnes of the drug, and that tractors from the state public works agency had
turned over the whole area. [@3]

The PL party announced it would take a legal offensive, filing additions to
complaints already sent to the Federal Prosecution Service, the Federal Police
and the Ceará state prosecution service. [@2] Governor Elmano de Freitas (PT)
said he had ordered immediate and rigorous investigations by the Civil Police
and by the CGD, the state body that disciplines security officers, and that
anyone involved would answer for it. [@2] The case became a strain on the
re-election campaign of the governor, and the Civil Police said investigations
were continuing to identify those responsible for the plantation. [@2]
