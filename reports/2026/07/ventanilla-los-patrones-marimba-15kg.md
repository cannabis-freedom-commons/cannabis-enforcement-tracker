Peru's National Police stopped a suspected drug-trafficking band called Los
Patrones de la Marimba in Ventanilla after finding 15 kilos of marijuana buried
in the patio of a house used as a hideout. [@1] Police said the group supplied
micro-dealers in the Pachacútec area and was detained as it was about to make a
delivery. [@1] The Escuadrón Verde police unit detained the alleged ringleader
Rubén Cahuascanco Yucra, 36, alias Caracortada, his partner Lady Torres Rosas,
28, alias Bizcochito, and Andrés Atto Arteaga, 18, alias Moquillo. [@2] The
operation began when officers spotted a mototaxi, a three-wheeled taxi, that the
suspects allegedly used to hand out the drug. [@2]

Officers found the 15 kilos in three separate places in the house: part inside
the mototaxi, part stored in the bedrooms, and the largest share buried in the
back patio, where officers had to dig the packages out with shovels. [@2] A
pitbull belonging to the suspects guarded the packages buried in the patio. [@1]
Police said the marijuana was already divided by kilos to make distribution
easier. [@1] Officers also seized mobile phones, and the detainees were handed
to the Callao anti-drug unit Dirandro. [@1] Police said the suspects promoted
the marijuana in videos sent to a list of clients. [@1]

Colonel Carlos Alcántara, head of the Escuadrón Verde, said the marijuana was
buried in a hole dug in the patio so the smell would not spread and give the
activity away. [@3] The group distributed marijuana by delivery from its
Ventanilla base to micro-dealers across Callao. [@3] Cahuascan told officers he
was paid 50 soles for each distribution run and said he did not buy the drug,
only moved it. [@3] The three detainees were taken to the Callao prison and left
at the disposal of the judicial authorities. [@3]
