The Bureau of Customs through its Port of Clark intercepted four shipments
containing marijuana resin and high-grade marijuana known as kush with a
combined estimated value of P10.15 million. [@1] The operation was carried out
with the Clark Inter-Agency Task Force Against Illegal Drugs after intelligence
from the Philippine Drug Enforcement Agency flagged the cargo. [@3]

Two shipments from California arrived on July 1 and were consigned to recipients
in Caloocan City and Valenzuela City. [@2] Declared as a custom-printed wedding
photo and a letter notebook, they were examined on July 3, yielding two pouches
of suspected marijuana resin in each shipment. [@2] The resin weighed a total of
1,618 grams, comprising 810 grams and 808 grams, and was valued at P2.427
million. [@2] Two shipments from Thailand arrived on July 3 and were addressed
to the same recipient in Taguig City. [@2] Declared as used clothing, they held
eight plastic bags of suspected high-grade marijuana or kush weighing 5,142
grams and valued at P7.723 million. [@2]

All four shipments had undergone X-ray scanning and K-9 sniff tests that gave
positive indications for drugs, prompting 100 percent physical examinations.
[@1] Initial testing with a Rigaku spectrometer indicated the presence of
cannabinoids. [@2] Samples were turned over to the PDEA for confirmatory
laboratory examination. [@2] Warrants of seizure and detention were issued
against the shipments for violations of the Customs Modernization and Tariff Act
in relation to Republic Act No. 9165. [@1] BOC Commissioner Ariel Nepomuceno
said air express shipments remain a frequent target for drug smugglers and
stressed tighter monitoring and training at air express warehouses. [@3]
