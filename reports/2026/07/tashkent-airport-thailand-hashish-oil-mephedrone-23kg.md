Customs officers at Tashkent International Airport broke up a scheme to bring
narcotics into Uzbekistan from Thailand, seizing 34.58 kg of substances in a
series of multi-stage operations run with the State Security Service in June and
July 2026. [@2] The haul was split between 11.62 kg of mephedrone, a synthetic
stimulant, and 22.96 kg of hashish oil, a cannabis resin extract. [@2] One
person, described as the recipient who was to take delivery of the substances
and organise their onward distribution, was detained. [@2]

The customs service described the four interlinked attempts as the work of a
transnational criminal group that used foreign citizens arriving in Uzbekistan
for the first time and complex air routes through several countries. [@4] The
couriers were flown on roundabout routes through Vietnam, South Korea, the UAE
and India instead of direct flights from Thailand. [@1] The baggage passed
checks at several foreign airports undetected and was found only on arrival in
Tashkent. [@1] The substances were hidden in packaging for everyday goods,
including packets of powdered milk and spices, jars of chocolate spread, ketchup
and shampoo bottles. [@1]

Criminal cases were opened under part 2 of article 246 of Uzbekistan's Criminal
Code, the large-scale smuggling provision, and the investigation was continuing.
[@2] The accounts came from Uzbekistan's customs service, which said customs
officers at the airport prevented the attempt to smuggle a large consignment
from Thailand. [@3]
