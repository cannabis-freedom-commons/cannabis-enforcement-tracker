A client in South Korea was booked for violating the Narcotics Control Act after
allegedly sending payment from a virtual-asset exchange to a seller's wallet and
collecting cannabis hidden in a residential alley, completing two cannabis
purchases and attempting the same method a third time but failing when no
cannabis was found. [@1] The client admitted all of the charges, while the
investigative agency viewed the repeated purchases as a matter that could not be
treated lightly. [@1]

The issue in the case was whether a purchase case, which is treated more
seriously than simple smoking, could avoid a formal trial. [@1] The defence
highlighted that the client had fully confessed to the alleged facts and showed
remorse. [@1] The defence asked for the client's first-offender status with no
record of punishment, together with the motive, age and circumstances after the
offence, to be taken into account. [@1] The defence argued that a narcotics
education programme would be more effective than criminal punishment in
preventing reoffending. [@1]

Prosecutors granted deferred prosecution on condition that the client complete a
drug-prevention education programme, so the client was not sent to trial. [@1]
