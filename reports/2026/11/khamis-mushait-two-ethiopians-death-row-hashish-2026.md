Two Ethiopian men from Tigray, identified by the pseudonyms Dawit Tesfay and
Adam Berhe, are being held on death row at the Khamis Mushait detention centre
in Saudi Arabia's Asir region after being sentenced to death for transporting
hashish. [@1] Dawit Tesfay, 24, said he was sentenced after a trial lasting
about two minutes in which he had no lawyer and no interpreter, and that he was
made to sign documents in Arabic he could not read. [@1] Adam Berhe, 34, said he
knew he was carrying hashish and agreed because he was destitute in Yemen, and
that he has been awaiting execution for three years. [@1] Tesfay said he had
never heard the word hashish until his arrest, having been recruited as a
herdsman shortly after arriving in the kingdom. [@1]

Tesfay said his wing of the prison held 68 death row prisoners when he arrived
and holds 55 now. [@1] Berhe said eight prisoners were beheaded in the same
prison the week before the interview. [@1] Human Rights Watch documented the
executions of at least 20 Ethiopians for drug trafficking between January and
August 2026, the last three on 31 July, and said at least 76 more faced imminent
execution. [@1] Amnesty International recorded 96 executions in Saudi Arabia
between 1 January and 22 June 2026, 61 of them for drug offences and 39 of those
of foreign nationals. [@1] Amnesty researcher Dana Ahmed said at least 36
Ethiopians were executed in 2025 and 20 in 2026, all for smuggling hashish. [@1]

Human Rights Watch researcher Joey Shea said documented cases showed hearings of
a few minutes, some held by videoconference, with no legal representation, and
that appeals had to be written in Arabic by prisoners who could not verify they
were sent. [@1] Shea said many of the men fled the 2020-2022 war in Tigray,
crossed the Gulf of Aden to Yemen and were recruited there by trafficking
networks that did not tell them what they would carry. [@1] Tigray's former
youth affairs bureau head and the men's families asked for legal counsel, fair
trials, repatriation and release, and the African Commission on Human and
Peoples' Rights urged a review of the sentences. [@1] The UN Working Group on
Arbitrary Detention called death sentences for drug crimes in Saudi Arabia
incompatible with international law and urged the kingdom to restore its
moratorium. [@1]

Ethiopia's foreign ministry said in a 13 July release that 1,971 Ethiopians had
received royal pardons and that their repatriation had begun. [@1]
