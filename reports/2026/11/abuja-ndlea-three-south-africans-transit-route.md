Nigeria's National Drug Law Enforcement Agency (NDLEA) said three South African
nationals had been arrested in recent operations in which large quantities of
heroin, cocaine and synthetic cannabis, known in Nigeria as Loud, were
recovered. [@1] NDLEA Assistant Director for Media and Advocacy Francis Okogun
gave the figure during a joint security briefing in Abuja on Thursday, 5
November 2026, referring to "the period under review". [@2] Okogun said the
arrests had drawn attention because of the negative impression they could create
about Nigeria internationally. [@1]

He said the agency's findings showed foreign nationals increasingly facilitating
the movement of drugs through Nigeria to other parts of the world, and others
bringing such substances into the country. [@1] Okogun said the quantity and
variety of the drugs recovered suggested links to international trafficking
networks and that the suspects were suspected of working for international
cartels. [@1] He said the agency was investigating where the shipments were
destined and which cartels were responsible. [@1]

One of the three, South African national Gebashe Sithandiwe Jessie, was arrested
at Port Harcourt International Airport in October 2026 on suspicion of importing
32.74 kg of Thai Loud. [@1] The second, 38-year-old Will Jessica Ann, was
arrested at Nnamdi Azikiwe International Airport in Abuja on 6 July 2026 after
5.75 kg of heroin was allegedly found concealed in 14 blocks in her luggage.
[@1] She was arrested during the clearance of passengers arriving on a Qatar
Airways flight from Doha to Abuja. [@1] Nigeria's NDLEA presented the airport
arrests as evidence that foreign nationals are using the country as a transit
route for drugs headed elsewhere. [@2]
