Six adults—three men and three women—were accused in a federal narcotics case
after two raids dismantled two suspected retail drug sale points in Cipolletti's
Barrio 1200 Viviendas. [@1] Police carried out the procedures on Monday
afternoon between 4 p.m. and 8 p.m. at two homes in the northern part of the
city, with the COER special operations group carrying out the entries and
providing security. [@2] All six people were formally notified that a case had
been opened against them for suspected violation of Law 23,737, the narcotics
statute, and remained subject to forthcoming judicial rulings. [@1] No one was
reported to have been detained in the operation. [@3]

Inside the homes, police found cocaine and marijuana divided into wrappers for
sale and seized two scales, cutting and portioning equipment, and ten mobile
phones for analysis. [@2] Officers also seized a .22-calibre Bersa pistol,
cartridges of various calibres and more than 700,000 pesos in cash in mixed
banknotes, placing all items at the disposal of the federal courts. [@2] During
one of the searches, officers found three wild animals kept in captivity—a
parrot and two tortoises—and provincial wildlife staff confiscated them under
Provincial Law 5,796. [@1]

The inquiry began with information submitted to the 0800-333-4124 hotline of the
Secretariat of Narcocriminality, part of Río Negro's Ministry of Security and
Justice, prompting Cipolletti's Alto Valle Oeste anti-narcotics unit to
investigate. [@1] Investigators spent three months gathering intelligence and
evidence under the direction of the Federal Prosecutor's Office in General Roca,
through its Simple Cases unit, before a guarantees judge authorised the search
warrants. [@3] The operation resulted from coordinated work among the Río Negro
Police, the provincial Secretariat of Narcocriminality and the Roca federal
prosecutor's office. [@2]
