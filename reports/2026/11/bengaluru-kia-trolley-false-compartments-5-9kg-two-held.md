Customs officers at Kempegowda International Airport in Bengaluru arrested two
men in separate cases this week after hydroponic ganja was found hidden in false
compartments built into their trolley bags. [@1] Ritesh Vijay Thadani, a
30-year-old resident of Mumbai, was the first, caught on Monday after arriving
from Bangkok. [@1] Officers found the ganja in sealed plastic bags inside
check-in luggage that had been deposited at Bangkok airport. [@1] Customs
estimated the 2.9 kg haul at Rs 1 crore according to approved valuation
guidelines. [@1]

A day later, on Tuesday, Rahul Kumar, a Bihar resident, was stopped as he tried
to leave KIA's Terminal 2 with 3 kg of hydroponic ganja. [@1] In his case the
drug had been packed in vacuum-sealed pockets to save space. [@1] Customs put
the value of the 3 kg seizure at Rs 1.05 crore. [@1] Both men were arrested by
Customs under the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act, 1985. [@1]

A senior customs officer told The Times of India that x-ray scanners alone
cannot reveal ganja and that only experienced, trained officers find a hidden
compartment. [@2] Officials said the technique of hiding ganja in false
compartments was not new, though similar smuggling acts had not been reported at
the airport in recent times. [@2] Customs counted the two seizures together at
5.9 kg of hydroponic ganja worth roughly Rs 2 crore. [@2]
