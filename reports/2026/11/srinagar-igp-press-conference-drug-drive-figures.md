Jammu and Kashmir Police arrested 1,941 people and registered 1,651 cases under
the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances (NDPS) Act in the Kashmir Valley
between 1 January and 31 October 2026, Inspector General of Police Kashmir Sujit
Kumar said at his first major press conference after taking charge. [@1] Police
said they seized 1,711 kilograms of contraband and attached or forfeited
properties worth about 1.54 billion rupees (Rs 154 crore) belonging to drug
traffickers. [@1] Officers froze 567 bank accounts and recovered cash in the
anti-drug drive. [@1] Illicit crops grown on about 2,116 kanals of land, a kanal
being a local unit of about 505 square metres, were destroyed, which works out
at roughly 107 hectares. [@1]

The press conference was held at the Police Control Room in Srinagar. [@3] Kumar
said the earnings of drug kingpins, operating locally or across the border, are
distributed to overground workers, though he said the exact volume of money
involved was hard to specify. [@2] He said narcotics entering Kashmir came both
from across the border and from other states, with investigations establishing
links to multiple supply routes. [@2] Kumar said police were pursuing the wider
network of a large drug racket uncovered in downtown Srinagar and had sent teams
to Chandigarh and other states in connection with it. [@2]

Kumar said people fighting drug addiction could approach any police station and
would be helped without their identities being compromised, and that the police
rehabilitation centre charged patients only for food and medicines where
applicable. [@2] Kumar said the purpose of the figures was to show that police
teams were working on the ground but that enforcement alone was not enough, and
he urged young people to play a constructive role in society. [@3] Kumar said
police were working with the government and society towards making Kashmir
drug-free and cyber-safe. [@4] The drive runs alongside the larger Nasha Mukt
Jammu Kashmir and Nasha Mukt Bharat anti-drug campaigns. [@1]
