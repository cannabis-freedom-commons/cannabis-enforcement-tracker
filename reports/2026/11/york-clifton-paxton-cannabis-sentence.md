Deborah Paxton, 51, denied selling the Class B drug to users until the day of
her trial at York Magistrates' Court, when she changed her plea. [@1] Paxton, of
Spalding Avenue in Clifton, admitted being concerned in the supply of cannabis
between September 2024 and December 2024. [@1] The court gave her a 12-month
prison sentence, described as the maximum possible at a magistrates' court,
suspended for 12 months on condition she complete 12 months' probation
supervision and commit no further offences. [@1]

She had been arrested after police raided her home in Clifton and found mobile
phones and £1,521 in cash. [@1] When police inspected the phones they found
evidence of her involvement in drug supply, the court heard. [@1]

Her solicitor Andrew Craven said she knew a man who used several drugs and had
run up drug debts, with the people owed the money demanding payment. [@1] Craven
said the man suggested that, since they both knew cannabis users, Paxton sell
cannabis to pay off his debts or sell it on behalf of those who supplied him.
[@1] Paxton had denied the charge after being charged because she said the man
was more involved than she was. [@1]

Craven told the court the man had promised Paxton throughout the proceedings
that he would make a statement to police but had not done so. [@1] Deputy
district judge Nick Hayles said it had been a conspiracy between the two and
that Paxton had taken the blame because the man was more vulnerable. [@1] Craven
said the man had mental health problems and had never worked. [@1] Paxton had
not worked for 20 years and said through her solicitor that she had various
medical problems. [@1] The judge noted that she did not produce any evidence of
her medical issues. [@1]
