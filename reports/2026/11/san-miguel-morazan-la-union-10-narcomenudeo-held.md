El Salvador's Attorney General's Office and the National Civil Police dismantled
a structure allegedly dedicated to distributing cocaine and marijuana in the
departments of San Miguel, Morazán and La Unión. [@1] Ten people were captured
on court orders after a ten-month investigation into three interconnected cells
that supplied and sold drugs in eastern El Salvador. [@2] The cells used
auto-tinting workshops, food outlets, vehicle-rental businesses, corner shops,
fruit stalls and a parcel-shipping agency to the United States as fronts for
storing, moving and selling the drugs. [@2]

The FGR named Diógenes de Jesús Benítez Escobar as the alleged leader and main
supplier, working with his son Anthony de Jesús Benítez Benítez, who handled
logistics, weighing and deliveries in San Miguel and La Unión. [@2] The other
eight detainees were alleged wholesale suppliers, route intermediaries and local
distributors in San Miguel, Corinto, Lislique, Sociedad and Cacaopera, with
Reina Ester Medina described as storing and selling in Lislique. [@2] Officers
carried out 15 searches, concentrated in the Miralvalle subdivision, colonia
Milagro de la Paz and Ciudad Jardín in the city of San Miguel and in the
districts of Corinto in Morazán and Lislique in La Unión. [@2] The searches
seized $8,928.13 in cash, 18 mobile phones, seven vehicles and several portions
of cocaine, and the cash from the alleged leader alone, $4,100, tested positive
for cocaine traces. [@2]

The ten detainees were to be handed to the courts to face proceedings for
illicit trafficking, proposal and conspiracy to commit illicit trafficking, and
illicit association. [@2] The prosecution said the network ran from February to
November 2026, with surveillance, monitoring and police-controlled purchases
used to identify its members. [@3] No weight of marijuana or cannabis seized in
the operation was published in any of the accounts. [@1]
