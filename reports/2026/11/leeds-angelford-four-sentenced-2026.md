Four people were sentenced after an investigation into drug supply in east Leeds
examined as Operation Angelford. [@1] Christopher Laking, 35, of Ramshead Drive,
Seacroft, was jailed for 14 years. [@1] Laking was convicted of being concerned
in cannabis supply from May 2023 to December 2025. [@1] Chris Wilson, 35, of
Bradford, was jailed for six years and three months. [@1] Luke Cockerill, 36, of
no fixed abode, was jailed for four years and six months. [@1] Jordan Parker,
33, of Inglewood Drive, was given a two-year community order for being concerned
in cannabis supply. [@1]

Operation Angelford began in May 2023 with a warrant at Laking’s home on
Ramshead Drive in Seacroft. [@1] Officers found over £14,000 in cash at that
address. [@1] Enquiries into Laking’s seized phone and banking material showed
he had been running a drugs line. [@1] In December 2025 further warrants were
executed at Laking’s home and addresses on Inglewood Drive and Skelwith Walk.
[@1] Laking, Parker and Cockerill were arrested at Laking’s address, where
jewellery suspected to have been bought with crime proceeds was seized. [@1] The
Inglewood Drive address was uninhabited and used as a drugs stash. [@1] Inside
officers found cocaine police valued at over £13,000 and cannabis police valued
at over £17,000. [@1] A further £2,850 worth of cocaine was found at the
Skelwith Walk property. [@1] Phone enquiries pointed to a ring-and-bring service
with Laking in charge and Wilson and Cockerill assisting. [@1]

At Leeds Crown Court prosecutors described Laking directing Wilson and Cockerill
in a dial-and-delivery operation. [@2] Bank records showed suspected profits of
£130,000 and £197,000. [@2] The judge said Laking had a directing and organising
role while Wilson and Cockerill had subordinate but significant roles. [@2]
Police said drugs, cash and assets were taken out of the supply chain and that
assets will be pursued in a later Proceeds of Crime Act hearing. [@1]
