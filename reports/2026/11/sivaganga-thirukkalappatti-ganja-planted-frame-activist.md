Five people were arrested near Tiruppattur for allegedly planting a packet of
ganja at the grocery shop of Rajkumar, an activist who had campaigned against a
local liquor outlet, to frame him. [@1] According to CCTV footage reviewed by
Rajkumar, two men posed as customers and dropped the packet at his shop, and two
plainclothes prohibition officers arrived in a private vehicle about five
minutes later. [@1]

Rajkumar of Thirukkalappatti, who runs a grocery and e-service shop, led
protests against a Tasmac liquor outlet and its attached private bar that
villagers said harmed students, women and farmers. [@2] Villagers blockaded the
road on November 4, 2026, demanding the shop's closure, and it was temporarily
closed after the protest. [@2] Officers from the Sivaganga prohibition wing
searched Rajkumar's shop and found a packet of ganja and tobacco lying by the
pavement stone in front of it, then questioned his wife and mother about selling
banned goods. [@3]

Dinamalar reported that when Rajkumar showed the officers the camera footage,
they took copies and left the scene. [@3] Rajkumar, accompanied by CPI(M)
District Secretary Mohan, party cadres and villagers, besieged the Sivaganga
district police headquarters and complained to Superintendent of Police Abhishek
Gupta. [@1] Among the five arrested was 55-year-old Rajapandi of Karendhalpatti,
who reportedly admitted during interrogation that the packet was planted to aid
the bar operators. [@1]

Dinamalar described the man who posed as a customer as Rajapandi of
Karendhalpatti and the second as Asai, who worked at the Thirukkalappatti bar.
[@3] Additional Superintendent of Police (Prohibition) Francis has opened an
inquiry into the conduct of the local police personnel on Gupta's instructions,
while police representatives said patrolling officers had merely responded
promptly to information. [@1] A senior police official told The Times of India
that a case had been registered, that five people had been arrested and that
further investigation was under way, and denied that police were involved. [@2]
