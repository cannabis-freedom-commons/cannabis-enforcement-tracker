Bolivia's former deputy Laura Rojas told prosecutors on Thursday 5 November 2026
that the suitcases brought into the country from the United States through Viru
Viru airport in Santa Cruz belonged to Romer Saucedo, president of the Supreme
Court of Justice. [@1] Rojas gave the statement to the Public Ministry with a
bulletproof vest on, saying she had kept silent out of fear for her safety and
that of her children. [@1] La Razón reported that Rojas arrived at the Special
Substances Prosecutors' Office in Santa Cruz de la Sierra shortly before 11:30
and said she was appearing voluntarily. [@2]

The former judge Hebert Zeballos then said the 31 suitcases carried marijuana
produced in California, and that he was told the luggage had the cover and
protection of the then attorney general Roger Mariaca. [@1,4] Zeballos said he
went to Viru Viru to help Rojas out of friendship, and that he did not report
what he learned because Mariaca was the top authority of the Public Ministry.
[@1] He also said that evidence had been planted on him and that he had spent
eight months in detention in the case. [@1] Zeballos first said Rojas told him the marijuana belonged to a man nicknamed El Alemán, supposedly Romer Saucedo Gómez, and that he doubted El Alemán was Saucedo. [@4] In a later video, Zeballos said
Rojas had told him at the airport that a passenger on the flight told her the
marijuana suitcases belonged to a man nicknamed El Alemán, who was Saucedo, and
asked the authorities for protection for himself and his family. [@1,4]

The case concerns the more than 30 suitcases that entered the country on 29
November 2025, and Rojas, who is under house arrest for using her diplomatic
passport to bring them in, says she does not know what they contained. [@1] The
suitcases arrived at Viru Viru airport on a charter flight from Miami, and Rojas
went to customs a day later to collect 31 pieces of luggage registered as
unclaimed. [@2]

Saucedo, who has an arrest warrant for drug trafficking and criminal
organisation, says he is abroad in exile, while Zeballos remains in preventive
detention at Palmasola prison. [@1] Mauricio Requena, a former adviser to Saucedo, was also apprehended in the same case after he presented himself to give a statement. [@5] He said former judge Hebert Zeballos was supposedly in charge of setting up an ice factory as a front for that process. [@5,8,11] Quezada said a search of the company's premises found an ice-crushing machine that he said was really used to process the marijuana. [@8] Quezada said Saucedo explained that an industrial cold process turned the marijuana into a liquid for vapes, to be sold at a higher price in Brazil and other countries. [@5,8,11] Quezada said Saucedo told him the withdrawal of the suitcases was supervised and supported by a man he called Chino, meaning then attorney general Roger Mariaca. [@5,8] Quezada said Saucedo told him there was 'the necessary protection' and that El Alemán would have no problem. [@8] Asked by the Fiscalía who El Alemán was, Quezada said Saucedo had identified himself with that alias, a version that coincides with former deputy Laura Rojas's statement naming Saucedo as the man known by that nickname. [@12] He said Saucedo told him that 32 suitcases which arrived at Viru Viru from California on a private jet were Saucedo's and contained genetically modified marijuana. [@5,11,12] In his expanded statement, Quezada also described a stop by the private flight in San Ramón and an alleged movement of 200,000 US dollars linked to the operation. [@11] Quezada said Saucedo named former deputy Laura Rojas as the person who transported the suitcases on the private jet from California. [@9] In his statement, Quezada said the disclosure came on 26 November 2025 during a trip he and Saucedo made to Mexico to receive an honorary distinction. [@5] Quezada said the conversation happened at a dinner after the honorary ceremony in Mexico, where they drank alcohol. [@10] The day before, after giving his statement, he had been released. [@7] He was taken into FELCN custody at about 15:00 that Wednesday in the suitcase investigation. [@7] The FELCN and a commission of prosecutors searched Quezada's home on Avenida Alemana in Santa Cruz. [@6] He is investigated for suspected trafficking of controlled substances, criminal association and conspiracy. [@5,11] Quezada said he called Laura Rojas to tell her the permit would come out the next day. [@11] Quezada said that on the day the permit was processed he was at home and that Romer Saucedo Gómez called and ordered him to give it course. [@11] On Wednesday 11 November 2026, former Santa Cruz departmental court president Aldo Ismael Quezada was apprehended after a search of his home and taken to the FELCN in Santa Cruz. [@5] Rojas's lawyer Joadel Bravo said she
travelled from Santa Cruz to Miami for a political conference that never took
place, was invited by Zeballos, and flew back on a Uruguayan friend's chartered
flight. [@1] La Razón reported that the marijuana found in a warehouse where the
suitcases are said to have been stored made the case more prominent. [@3]
