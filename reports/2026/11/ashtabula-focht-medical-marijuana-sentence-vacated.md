Amy Arlene Focht was placed on two years of community control in August 2024
after pleading guilty to failing to comply with a police officer's order and
assault. [@1] As part of that sentence, she was prohibited from possessing or
consuming alcohol, marijuana or THC, even if those substances were otherwise
legal. [@1] During a March 2026 hearing, Focht acknowledged testing positive for
marijuana on three occasions. [@1] She explained that she was not using
marijuana recreationally but had received a doctor's recommendation for medical
marijuana, which she mistakenly believed constituted a prescription. [@1] Focht
apologized, accepted responsibility and told the court she was participating in
a 12-step program and maintaining daily contact with her sponsor. [@1]
Prosecutors nevertheless requested at least 36 months in prison, noting that she
had repeatedly been informed that marijuana use violated her probation
conditions. [@1] The trial judge revoked her community control and imposed
concurrent prison sentences of 36 months and 18 months for her original
offenses, resulting in a three-year prison term. [@1]

In a November 2 ruling, the Ohio Eleventh District Court of Appeals vacated the
36-month sentence imposed on Focht by the Ashtabula County Court of Common
Pleas, ordering a new sentencing hearing. [@1] On appeal, Focht argued that the
judge had failed to consider Ohio's required sentencing factors, including the
seriousness of the offenses and the likelihood of reoffending. [@1] The appeals
court agreed, pointing to statements indicating that the judge had effectively
offered Focht a choice between 36 months in prison if she admitted the
violations and 54 months if she contested them. [@1] The appellate judges found
that the trial court's statements indicated Focht could receive additional
punishment for requiring prosecutors to prove the violations rather than
admitting them. [@1] The court also determined that the sentencing record
provided no indication that the judge reconsidered the statutory sentencing
factors required when imposing a prison term following a community control
violation. [@1] The appeals court emphasized that the original sentencing
hearing did not eliminate the requirement to consider those factors again when
imposing a new sentence. [@1] The judges ultimately concluded that the
three-year sentence was contrary to law and ordered the case returned to the
lower court for a complete resentencing hearing. [@1]

The decision does not invalidate the prohibition on marijuana use imposed as a
condition of Focht's community control. [@1] Her admission that she violated
those conditions also remains in effect, meaning the lower court must reconsider
the appropriate punishment rather than determine whether the violations
occurred. [@1]
