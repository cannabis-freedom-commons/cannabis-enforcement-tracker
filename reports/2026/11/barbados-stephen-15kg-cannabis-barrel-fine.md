Venice Keith McKenzie Stephen, of Sealy Land, Government Hill, St Michael, was
fined $10,000 and spared jail for trafficking 15.5 kilogrammes of cannabis into
Barbados. [@1] Justice Pamela Beckles ordered him to pay $4,000 of the fine
immediately, with the remaining $6,000 due in six months, and he will spend
three years and 182 days behind bars if the payment is not made. [@1] He was
convicted, reprimanded and discharged on the possession and importation counts.
[@1] The case will be reviewed on 7 March 2027. [@1]

Justice Beckles identified the nature and gravity of the offences, the large
quantity of drugs, the sophistication and planning involved and the
international, cross-border nature of the case as aggravating factors. [@1] She
set a six-year starting point, reduced by two years because Stephen had no
previous convictions and was deemed at low risk of reoffending. [@1] She said
people who trafficked drugs should expect a custodial sentence, but was
persuaded not to impose one by the submissions of both the prosecution and
defence. [@1] Defence counsel Sade Harris represented Stephen, while acting
Principal State Counsel Kevin Forde prosecuted. [@1]

A jury had convicted Stephen at the end of a trial before Justice Beckles in the
No. 5 Supreme Court of possessing, trafficking and importing the cannabis on 15
July 2020. [@2] He had pleaded not guilty at the start of the trial. [@3] He
remained on bail after the verdict. [@2] He was arrested and charged after a
customs officer at EHS Freightline Limited searched a barrel consigned to him
and found 40 packages of cannabis. [@3] The barrel bore the name Williams
Phillips and a Canadian address. [@1] Stephen denied any knowledge of the drugs,
saying he had ordered food. [@1] He said he had asked a man named Barry, whom he
had met when he lived in Canada, to send him a barrel during the COVID-19
pandemic. [@1]
