Turin's investigating judge ordered precautionary measures against six people —
three Moroccan citizens, one Albanian and two Italians — at the close of the
'Driver' investigation. [@1] The measures ranged from prison to house arrest to
a daily obligation to report to the judicial police. [@1] Three of the six had
already been arrested in flagrante during the wider investigation. [@1] Across
the whole investigation, 21 people were arrested, including an Italian man
caught during house searches carried out when the measures were executed. [@1]

Investigators seized about 700 kilograms of hashish, 70 kilograms of marijuana,
14 kilograms of cocaine and 500 ecstasy pills. [@1] Officers also recovered four
pistols with ammunition that came from a theft. [@1] The Italian man arrested
during the final searches held about 500 grams of hashish, 250 grams of
marijuana and 7 grams of cocaine for dealing. [@1] Earlier in the investigation,
officers had already arrested 20 people in flagrante. [@1]

The organisation was based in Turin province with branches in Liguria, Lombardy,
Veneto and Lazio, and ran an established supply and distribution network for
hashish, marijuana and cocaine. [@1] Turin's Squadra mobile developed the
investigation between 2024 and 2025 through wiretaps and surveillance,
reconstructing a structured criminal organisation dedicated to importing and
distributing large quantities of drugs. [@1] The inquiry, coordinated by the
Turin public prosecutor's office, ended with a precautionary order by the city's
investigating judge against six people seriously suspected of jointly
trafficking large quantities of drugs. [@1] The central anti-crime directorate's
central operations service and the central directorate for anti-drug services
supported the investigation. [@1]
