In a November 4 decision, the Montana Supreme Court held that a district court
abused its discretion by treating a phlebotomist's certification and hospital
employment as enough to establish the supervision and direction the blood-draw
statute requires. [@1] The case was State v. Rogers, an appeal by Donald Paul
Rogers from a Ravalli County jury verdict finding him guilty of operating a
vehicle with a THC concentration of 5 ng/ml or more. [@2] The court ruled that
prosecutors cannot rely solely on a phlebotomist's certification and hospital
employment to prove a blood sample was collected under the medical supervision
state law requires in a marijuana DUI case. [@1]

Rogers was stopped in March 2022, admitted smoking marijuana, and was arrested
after field sobriety tests before a certified phlebotomist drew his blood at a
hospital. [@1] The stop occurred on March 18, 2022, when a Montana Highway
Patrol trooper stopped Rogers on U.S. Highway 93 in Ravalli County. [@2] Testing
by the Montana State Crime Lab found Rogers had a delta-9 THC concentration of
8.9 nanograms per milliliter of blood, above the state's 5-nanogram limit for
driving. [@1] At trial the phlebotomist described her qualifications and the
blood collection but identified no physician or registered nurse supervising or
directing her work. [@2] The district court had overruled the objection,
reasoning that the statute did not require a physician or nurse in the room and
that her hospital employment implied supervision. [@2]

The Supreme Court nevertheless upheld Rogers's conviction because his attorneys
failed to raise the objection in a timely manner. [@1] The court found counsel
did not challenge the phlebotomist's testimony or the admission of the
blood-collection form when she testified, and never moved to strike that
evidence. [@2] It also upheld the use of Rogers's certified driving record to
establish at least three prior DUI convictions, affirming his sentencing as a
fourth or subsequent DUI offender. [@1] The jury found Rogers guilty of the THC
per se offence and related misdemeanours, and acquitted him of failing to have a
taillamp. [@2]
