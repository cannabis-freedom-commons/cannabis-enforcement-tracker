Bahrain's High Criminal Court sentenced a man in his forties to five years in
prison and a BD3,000 fine for promoting narcotic drugs. [@1] The case began when
the Anti-Narcotics Directorate received information that the defendant held
drugs for promotion. [@1] Police assigned a secret source to buy hashish, and
the source called the defendant by phone in a policeman's hearing, agreeing to
buy about BD80 worth. [@1] The source went with a policeman to the agreed place,
and the defendant handed over a dark-coloured piece that a laboratory confirmed
was hashish. [@1]

The arrest force then raided the defendant, who tried to flee, and detained him.
[@1] In Public Prosecution investigations, he confessed to holding the seized
material for resale. [@1] He said he had agreed that day to sell the hashish for
about BD80 and handed it over at the appointed time, having obtained it through
a dead drop, a secret spot where something is left for collection without the
two sides meeting. [@1]

Prosecutors charged him with supplying hashish for use for payment outside the
cases licensed by law, and with possessing and holding hashish outside the cases
permitted by law. [@1]
