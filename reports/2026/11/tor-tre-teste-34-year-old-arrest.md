A neighbour dispute over renovation noise in Tor Tre Teste turned into a State
Police anti-drug intervention in Rome. [@1] Officers of the Porta Maggiore
police station and the Flying Squad arrested a 34-year-old man of German origin
on strong suspicion of possession for drug dealing. [@1] The intervention began
after a renovation worker sought help saying a neighbour annoyed by the noise
had threatened him and confronted his entrance door. [@1] The man's arrest was
later validated by the judicial authority at Piazzale Clodio. [@2]

A second worker near the car park then saw a blue bag fall from the building.
[@1] The bag held more than 150 doses of cocaine and two bars of hashish. [@1]
Officers traced the bag's trajectory to the bedroom window of the man involved
in the earlier dispute. [@1] Inside the flat, officers found stocks of cocaine,
hashish and marijuana in the kitchen refrigerator instead of food. [@3] Another
bar of hashish had been thrown into the waste bin. [@1] Officers recovered
additional cocaine in the bathroom. [@1] Officers also found equipment for
weighing, cutting and packaging doses. [@1] Officers seized more than 5,000
euros in cash believed to come from dealing. [@1] Police put the total seizure
at about one kilogram of drugs comprising cocaine, hashish and marijuana. [@2]

Investigators described the flat's contents as a genuine drug-dealing store
spread across its rooms. [@1] Police said the evidence concerns the
preliminary-investigation phase and the suspect remains presumed innocent until
any final conviction. [@2]
