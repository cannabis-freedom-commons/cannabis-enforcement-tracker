West Bengal police arrested 378 people in 212 cases registered under the Narcotic Drugs and
Psychotropic Substances (NDPS) Act during a statewide special drive against drug trafficking and
illicit cultivation. [@1,3,6,8,9,10,11,12,13] The drive ran for a week, from 31 October to 7 November
2026 [@6,7,9], and Chief Minister Suvendu Adhikari announced the results on its last day in a post on
social media. [@2,3,6] International and state borders were placed under closer watch, and vehicles
were let through only after thorough checks. [@10]

Officers destroyed illegal ganja cultivation spread over 300 bighas of land. [@1,3,6,8,10,12,13] The
chief minister's post gave this as 99.17 acres, with an estimated yield of 10,000 kg. [@5,11] Agencies
also seized Rs 17 lakh in cash and disposed of seized narcotics worth around Rs 101 crore during the
period. [@1,3,5,8,10,11,13]

Police valued the narcotics seized in the drive at about Rs 64 crore, a figure that covered heroin,
brown sugar, morphine, yaba tablets and codeine-based cough syrup as well as cannabis. [@1,6,7,10]
They attached property worth Rs 1 crore and began 68 financial investigations against 110 accused.
[@1,3,9,10,11] The drive also included 215 public awareness campaigns, and Adhikari linked it to the
central government's Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyan, its campaign for a drug-free India. [@1,4] He said his
government kept a zero-tolerance policy against illicit narcotics and commended the police. [@1,3,8]
