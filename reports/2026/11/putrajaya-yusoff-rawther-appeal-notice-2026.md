The Court of Appeal in Putrajaya was told on 4 November 2026 that prosecutors
had still not physically served their appeal notice on former political aide
Muhammed Yusoff Rawther, who is in the United Kingdom. [@1] Deputy Public
Prosecutor How May Ling said authorities in the United Kingdom and Ireland had
failed to trace Yusoff or serve the notice despite a request for Interpol
assistance. [@1] She said prosecutors were told last month that tracing had
failed and the authorities had no information about his asylum application,
during case management before judges Azman Abdullah, Hayatul Akmal Abdul Aziz
and K. Muniandy. [@1] Prosecutors are appealing the High Court decision that
acquitted Yusoff of cannabis trafficking and possessing imitation pistols
without calling his defence. [@1] The prosecution sought another case-management
date to continue tracing and service and asked for Zoom proceedings so Yusoff
could appear online to confirm his presence in the United Kingdom. [@1]

Judge Azman Abdullah refused an exception for Zoom, saying the criminal
respondent must attend court physically and the court did not want to create an
unnecessary precedent. [@1] He said if prosecutors trace Yusoff they should
bring him before the court and asked counsel to advise him to return to
Malaysia. [@1] Defence counsel Muhammad Rafique Rashid Ali accepted that
physical service is required under Section 314 of the Criminal Procedure Code
but said his client had not fled and is in the United Kingdom seeking asylum.
[@1] The court fixed 3 February 2027 as the next case-management date. [@1]

On 12 June 2025 High Court judge Muhammad Jamil Hussin had acquitted Yusoff,
then 33, after finding prosecutors failed to prove a prima facie case. [@1] The
court found prosecutors failed on witness evidence to prove the accused had
control, custody and possession of the drugs and pistols. [@1] Yusoff had been
charged with trafficking 305 grams of cannabis in a vehicle in front of the
Kuala Lumpur police contingent headquarters surau at 10.15am on 6 September
2024. [@1] The trafficking charge under Section 39B(1)(a) of the Dangerous Drugs
Act 1952 carried a maximum of life imprisonment and at least 12 strokes of the
cane on conviction. [@1] He was also charged with possessing imitation pistols
by a roadside near a condominium on Jalan Bukit Kiara at 9.25am the same day
under Section 36(1) of the Arms Act 1960. [@1]
