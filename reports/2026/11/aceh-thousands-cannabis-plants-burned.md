Officers found thousands of cannabis plants in an area of Aceh during a
narcotics-eradication operation. [@1] Officers carrying out the action pulled
the plants up to their roots and then destroyed them by burning at the discovery
site. [@1] Several officers were present in the area to pull up the plants. [@1]

The plants grew in an expanse in an open area surrounded by trees and dense
undergrowth. [@1] After collection, the plants found were destroyed on site.
[@1] The step was presented as an effort to cut off the narcotics production
source before the crop could be harvested and enter illicit distribution. [@1]
By pulling the plants up to the roots, officers sought to prevent them from
growing back. [@1] Destruction on site was intended to keep the seized plants
from being misused or reused by irresponsible parties. [@1]

Action against cannabis planting sites was described as part of narcotics
eradication aimed not only at dealers and distribution networks but also at
production sources. [@1] The account says officers still need to trace those
suspected of managing the land, supplying seeds and possible links to
trafficking networks. [@1] It adds that public tip-offs to officers can help
detect illegal planting activity early. [@1] By the time the account circulated,
no precise discovery location, exact plant count, field area or suspected
manager had been disclosed. [@1]
