30-year-old Xiaochen Han, of Griffith’s Close, Birmingham, appeared in custody
at Cork Circuit Criminal Court at Anglesea Street in Cork, and his mother
attended the hearing. [@1] Han was living in the United Kingdom and is from
China. [@1] He confirmed a guilty plea at Cork Circuit Criminal Court and was
remanded in custody for sentencing in February 2027. [@1] Defence senior counsel
Jane Hyland asked for a probation report so his background history and
circumstances could be outlined before sentencing. [@1] Judge Helen Boyle
ordered a probation report and a prison governor’s report. [@1] The court
extended free legal aid for a Mandarin interpreter for further consultations.
[@1] Sentencing was adjourned until 24 February 2027 with Han remanded in
custody. [@1]

A court heard that 80 kilos of cannabis with a street value of €1.6 million were
discovered after officers went to a storage unit at Little Island in July. [@2]
Han pleaded guilty to possession of cannabis and possession for sale or supply,
where the drug value exceeded €13,000, the threshold for a 10-year minimum
sentence unless exceptional circumstances apply. [@1] The charges refer to 6
July at U Store It, Unit 24, Euro Business Park, Little Island, County Cork.
[@1] The case involved a joint investigation by the Cork City Divisional Drugs
Unit, the Garda National Drugs and Organised Crime Bureau, and Revenue and
Customs, through a controlled delivery to the business-park unit. [@1]

Detective Garda Derry O’Brien said gardaí had objected to bail because of the
seriousness of the allegations and their belief that Han was a flight risk with
no ties to Ireland. [@1] Gardaí said Han had no ties to the jurisdiction and
admitted travelling from the United Kingdom to Belfast and by train to Cork.
[@1] He travelled that way to avoid flagging immigration at airports because he
had no visa and had entered the country illegally. [@1]
