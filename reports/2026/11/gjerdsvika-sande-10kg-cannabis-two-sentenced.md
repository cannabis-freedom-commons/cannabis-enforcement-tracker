Sunnmøre District Court sentenced a man in his 20s to two years in prison for
his role in storing cannabis found in a vehicle and on a rural farm property in
Gjerdsvika. [@2] In October 2026, Sør-Rogaland District Court had already
sentenced a co-defendant in his 30s to one year and 11 months in prison in the
same case after he admitted criminal liability. [@3] Both convicted men appealed
their sentences. [@2]

The two men were arrested in Volda in June 2026 after police intercepted their
car and uncovered ten vacuum-packed packages containing 4.8 kilograms of hashish
hidden inside a bag. [@2] During a coordinated search of a farm property in
Gjerdsvika in Sande municipality, officers found traces of cannabis production
across a dwelling house, a barn, and a garage. [@4] Police seized approximately
5.5 kilograms of marijuana from the property along with hashish plants and
cultivation equipment, bringing the total cannabis seizure to more than 10
kilograms. [@2] Local newspaper Vestlandsnytt confirmed that the cannabis
plantation had been uncovered during the June operation. [@1]

The investigation began in February 2026 when police stopped the men during a
routine speed check and noted that neither possessed a Norwegian national
identity number, registered employment, or a registered residential address.
[@2] Subsequent telecommunications tracking and toll station records revealed
repeated journeys between Søre Sunnmøre, Lillestrøm, and Nordmøre, prompting
police to establish covert camera surveillance at the farm. [@2] Although the
younger man denied criminal guilt and no matching DNA linked him to the seized
drugs, the court concluded that he had aided the operation, noting his travel to
Norway had been booked by the farm owner. [@2]
