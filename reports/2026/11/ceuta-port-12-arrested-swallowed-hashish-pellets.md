Guardia Civil officers arrested 12 people at the Port of Ceuta on Tuesday, 10
November 2026, after discovering they were attempting to board a ferry to
Algeciras with swallowed hashish pellets. [@1,2,4] Health sources reported that all
12 detainees had ingested the drugs internally and had to be taken directly to
the Hospital Universitario de Ceuta for medical attention. [@1,2] Some of those detained needed urgent medical help because the hashish in their digestive tracts posed a serious risk to their health. [@3] At the
hospital's emergency ward on Tuesday night, X-ray examinations confirmed that
the pellets of hashish were clearly visible inside their bodies. [@1,2] Hospital checks found hashish inside the bodies of 11 of the 12 detainees. [@4] Doctors at the hospital in Ceuta oversaw the controlled expulsion of the swallowed drug while the suspects remained under police guard. [@3] They carried between one and two kilos in pellets to later expel. [@4]

The intervention took place as the group tried to board one of the passenger
vessels connecting Ceuta across the Strait of Gibraltar to mainland Spain. [@1,2]
Officers stationed at the port terminal grew suspicious of the group's behaviour
as they approached the boarding gates. [@1,2] Detection dogs assigned to port
duties reacted to the individuals and detected the presence of narcotics,
prompting officers to stop and detain the travellers. [@1] Authorities did not
immediately release the total weight of the seized hashish, as the suspects
remained under medical supervision. [@1] After they were discharged, the arrested people were handed over to the judicial authorities, accused of a suspected drug-trafficking offence, and the police investigation continued into possible distribution networks. [@3] Another detainee carried just over 2,000 grams valued at 16,238 euros and was sentenced to 10 months and 20 days in prison. [@4] One detainee carried 1,073 grams with indicatively over 4% THC, valued at 7,575 euros. [@4] All sentences were two years or less and were suspended except for one detainee with a previous drug-trafficking conviction issued by an Algeciras court in 2024. [@4] All of those detained accepted agreed penalties after admitting involvement in a public-health offence. [@4]
