The Bayonne judicial court tried a 21-year-old man from Pau, living in Jurançon,
in immediate proceedings for possession, offering and transport of drugs. [@1]
The court imposed a total of two and a half years of firm prison on the repeat
offender. [@1,2] Police had arrested him on 31 October at about 8:30 p.m. in a
street in Bayonne. [@1,2]

Investigators seized 166 grams of cannabis herb and 183 grams of cannabis resin,
alongside one gram of cocaine. [@1] Officers also found 2,250 euros in banknotes
in the glove box and small drug sachets each marked with a town name: Tarnos,
Bayonne, Seignosse and Saint-Paul-les Dax. [@1] The sachets each carried the
name of a town: Tarnos, Bayonne, Seignosse and Saint-Paul-les Dax. [@1]

The courier said he acted for a handler who supplied a car, a list of recipients
and cash for fuel and tolls, paying 200 euros per day. [@1] He said he needed
money and was driving without a licence. [@1] The prosecutor called the case an
example of the uberisation of drug trafficking and requested two years in prison
including 18 months suspended. [@1,2] The court went further, imposing one year of
firm prison and revoking 18 months of a prior suspended sentence, making two and
a half years firm in total. [@1,2]

A second man arrested with him in the car was cleared by the defendant and released without prosecution. [@2] The defendant said he had answered a neighbourhood job offer for a 200-euro courier run and told the court he acted because he needed money. [@2] The court heard he already had six convictions and lived with his mother and nine-year-old sister after his father's death. [@2] Sud Ouest names Anglet alongside Tarnos and Bayonne among the town names written on the sachets. [@2]
