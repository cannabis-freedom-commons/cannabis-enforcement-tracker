A South Korean court sentenced a defendant to 1 year and 6 months in prison,
suspended for 3 years, for smoking cannabis and taking part in growing it. [@1]
The law firm that handled the case said a prison term had been expected because
cultivation was involved. [@1] The defendant, identified only as A, had come to
cannabis through a friend's recommendation during a period of psychological
drifting. [@1] A was tried for the cultivation as well as the smoking, not only
for smoking or possession. [@1] Because the case had co-defendants, separating
how the offence came about, how far each defendant took part and who led it was
the central issue. [@1]

Hwang Won-yong, the head of law firm Inyul who represented A, argued that A did
not know the tools or the method needed to grow cannabis and had joined on a
friend's suggestion with no aim of distributing or selling it. [@1] Family and
friends submitted petitions describing A's daily conduct, social ties and the
family's commitment to supervising and guiding A. [@1] The court weighed the
sentencing factors presented by the defence in fixing the term, and A avoided
prison. [@1]
