Nashaun Joseph, 21, of Huddersfield, had been given a 12-month prison sentence
in August, suspended for a year, for possessing a blade in public and possession
of cannabis. [@1,2] Joseph, formerly of Batley, was arrested on 14 October for
unrelated matters and was found in possession of a small bag of cannabis. [@1]
Magistrates were told that he was in breach of the suspended sentence and had
not yet done the unpaid work he had been ordered to do. [@1]

The court heard that Joseph had been smoking cannabis since he was 12 and that
his mental health had suffered since his release from custody. [@1] He was about
to receive help from the substance misuse charity Change Grow Live. [@1]

Magistrates decided not to activate the prison sentence, saying there was a
"chink of light" for him to stay out of custody and work with the Probation
Service. [@1] They told him it was likely to be his last opportunity and said:
"I can't guarantee you will be going home next time. Good luck. You are free to
leave the court." [@1] Joseph was told to continue with the previous unpaid work
order and was given a six-month drug rehabilitation requirement added to his
suspended prison sentence. [@1] He was also ordered to pay a fine and costs
totalling £197. [@1]
