Cork Circuit Criminal Court jailed 49-year-old motorist Piotr Szczepanik for two
years and six months for drug-dealing. [@1] Detective Garda Dylan Murphy charged
Szczepanik, of Woodview, Pinecroft, Grange, Cork, with having cannabis for sale
or supply at his home on 11 June 2025 and a similar offence in a vehicle on
South Douglas Road the same day. [@1] Szczepanik pleaded guilty to the charges.
[@1] Detective Garda Dylan Murphy had arrested the then 48-year-old Szczepanik
at South Douglas Road, with a follow-up search at his Grange home then alleged
to have found about €10,000 worth of cannabis. [@2] Judge Helen Boyle imposed
three years and six months with the last year suspended. [@1]

On 11 June 2025 members of the Cork county divisional drugs unit stopped
Szczepanik while he was driving at South Douglas Road. [@1] When told there
would be a search under the Misuse of Drugs Act, he removed clingfilm-wrapped
cannabis from his pants and handed it over. [@1] There were two more
clingfilm-wrapped cannabis packets in the steering wheel and a fourth was found
in the search. [@1] The cannabis in the car had a total value of €348. [@1]
During a search of the bedroom at the Grange house, a suitcase held fifteen
individual clingfilm-wrapped cannabis packets with a total value of €6,972. [@1]
The total value of cannabis in the car and the house amounted to €7,310. [@1]
Also found in the wardrobe with the case were two industrial rolls of clingfilm,
and his passport. [@1] He cooperated with gardai and gave them the key to the
property where most of the cannabis was located. [@1]

He denied dealing, saying he gave cannabis to one friend and would have smoked
the rest himself, but gardai doubted this account. [@1] Judge Boyle said the
quantity was too large to be explained in terms of personal consumption. [@1] He
had previously been convicted and sentenced to two years with the last nine
months suspended for having €7,000 worth of cannabis. [@1] His counsel said he
was doing courses in relapse aversion, personal development, piano lessons and
haircutting in prison, and the judge acknowledged he was trying to better
himself. [@1]
