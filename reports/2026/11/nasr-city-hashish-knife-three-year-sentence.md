The Cairo Criminal Court's Seventh South Circuit, sitting at the Fifth
Settlement, sentenced a defendant identified as Ahmed M. in absentia to three
years of strict imprisonment and fined him 10,000 Egyptian pounds for possessing
hashish and an unlicensed knife in the Second Nasr City police district. [@1]
The judicial panel was headed by Counsellor Mohamed Rushdi Abu El-Naga,
alongside Counsellors Khaled Mohamed Ali and Ahmed Mahmoud Saeed, with
prosecutor Mostafa Ahmed Khalil and court secretary Tareq Fathi Kamel present.
[@1] The referral order in Case No. 2971 of 2026 Felonies Second Nasr City,
registered as No. 1053 of 2026 New Cairo Total, indicated that the incident
dated to 3 April 2026 following investigations by an assistant detective at the
Second Nasr City police station. [@1]

After obtaining a search and arrest warrant from the Public Prosecution, the
investigating officer and a police squad moved to the Seventh District in Nasr
City and arrested the man behind a building near his residence. [@1] A physical
search uncovered a transparent plastic bag containing 27 pieces of a brown
substance, confirmed by the forensic chemical laboratory to be hashish, along
with a wooden-handled knife bearing traces of the drug on its blade. [@1] The
ruling noted that the defendant admitted possessing both the narcotics and the
bladed weapon, claiming he carried the knife to protect the seized drugs. [@1]

The court stated it was unconvinced that trafficking intent existed because he
was not apprehended selling drugs and carried no tools typically used in drug
dealing, determining instead that he possessed the hashish for personal
consumption. [@1] Judges based the judgment on the arresting officer's
testimony, the search results, and the forensic authority's chemical laboratory
report confirming the 27 pieces of hashish and the knife residue. [@1] The court
convicted the defendant of possessing hashish for personal consumption and
carrying an unlicensed bladed weapon, treated the two offenses as indivisible,
and imposed the penalty for the more severe offense. [@1] The judgment ordered
the confiscation of the seized hashish and knife, sentenced him in absentia to
three years of strict imprisonment with a 10,000-pound fine, and required him to
pay criminal court costs. [@1]
