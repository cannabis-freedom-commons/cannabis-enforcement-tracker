The Traffic Routes Control Unit of the Darmstadt Main Customs Office checked
numerous parcels of a mail-order business sent from Spain to Germany last week.
[@2] Officers used a mobile X-ray unit to examine the parcels in a targeted way.
[@2] All of the checked consignments came from Spain and were destined for
Germany. [@3]

The officers came across a large number of prohibited or improperly declared
goods. [@2] Among them were several kilograms of marijuana or hashish, as well
as shisha tobacco and a firearm, the officers said. [@1] The narcotic was often
present in comparatively large quantities within the individual parcels. [@2]
The firearm was a hunting rifle suitable for hunting medium-sized game. [@2]
Cross-border shipment and import of firearms require special permits and import
documents. [@2] In this case those documents could not be produced. [@2]
Although cannabis has been partly legalised in Germany, imports from abroad
remain generally prohibited, so the rules do not allow cross-border shipment or
import of cannabis. [@1]

Customs spokeswoman Tanja Ackermann said every find counts and that it is
customs' duty to protect the public and consistently stop illegal flows of
goods. [@2] In all cases the authorities opened criminal or
administrative-offence proceedings. [@2] The goods found were seized and are
being legally assessed in the course of further investigations. [@2] Those
investigations are still ongoing. [@3]
