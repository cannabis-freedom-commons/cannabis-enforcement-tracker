Qasim and Ahsan Rasool of 4 Cloverwell, Edgeworthstown, county Longford, who are
dual Irish-Pakistani citizens, appeared for a preliminary hearing at Longford
District Court recently. [@1] Each brother faces two charges in connection with
the seizure. [@1] The two defendants were released on bail in July under
conditions requiring independent sureties of €50,000 and €40,000 respectively,
with an order that half must be lodged in cash. [@1] The judge remanded both men
on continuing bail and adjourned the case until February 2, 2027 for direction.
[@1] Direction had not yet been received from the Director of Public
Prosecutions. [@1]

The seizure was described as controlled drugs worth an estimated €2 million with
north of 100,000 vapes seized. [@1] Gardaí said there are north of 100,000 vapes
seized in the investigation and 40 different types of vapes and cannabis
products or products believed to contain cannabis. [@1] Gardaí seized what was
described in court as massive, massive quantities of products containing HHC and
THC, two cannabinoids regulated under the Misuse of Drugs Act. [@1] According to
Gardaí, the products seized included edibles, vapes, and pre-rolled cannabis
joints. [@1] The investigating officer called the products all vapes and
cannabis products in a case that had not been dealt with before. [@1]

The seizure came from a joint search operation between Gardaí and Revenue which
targeted Candy Cloud vape shops situated in Longford, Mayo, Louth, and Carlow.
[@1] A warehouse in Longford’s Townspark Industrial Estate was also searched
under warrant, as were two residential properties in Mayo and Louth. [@1] The
searches were conducted on July 2 and July 8 last. [@1] Forensic Science Ireland
has had difficulty testing the vapes due to safety issues with regard to the
lithium batteries within the vapes. [@1] The defence said its instruction was
that some of those vapes are legal products. [@1] Defence counsel cited the new
Single Use Vapes Act grace period between July 24th and January 2027. [@1]
