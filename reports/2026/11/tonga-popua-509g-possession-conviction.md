‘Atunaisa Kali, 50, was found guilty of cannabis possession at the Supreme Court
in Nuku’alofa after a trial on 23 October, while his co-accused Mele Vakatapu,
24, was found not guilty. [@1] Acting Justice Langi convicted Kali on two counts
including possession of cannabis branches and acquitted Vakatapu. [@1] Kali was
found guilty on both counts and is expected to be sentenced in December. [@1]

On 5 December 2025 police searched Kali’s residence in Popua without a warrant
after receiving information that cannabis was being kept or sold there. [@1]
Officers found a package containing 508.78 grams of cannabis wrapped in a
blanket and placed inside a plastic bag on a chair in Kali’s bedroom. [@1]
Police also found cannabis branches concealed behind a box of clothes in the
same bedroom. [@1] Both defendants were present in the bedroom when police
arrived. [@1] The court ordered the seized drugs to be destroyed by police. [@1]

Kali claimed the package belonged to his brother and denied knowing that it
contained cannabis or knowing about the cannabis branches. [@1] Vakatapu said
she did not live at the residence and only occasionally visited Kali. [@1] The
Crown argued the cannabis was in Kali’s private bedroom, was visible and known
to both defendants, pointing to inconsistencies about when the brother allegedly
placed the package on the chair. [@1] The judge found Kali had custody and
control over the package and knew or ought to have known that it contained
cannabis. [@1] On the second count the judge accepted police evidence about the
branches and rejected Kali’s denial for lack of convincing evidence that an
officer planted the cannabis. [@1] The judge acquitted Vakatapu on both counts
because the evidence was insufficient to prove beyond reasonable doubt that she
had custody and control over the drugs. [@1]
