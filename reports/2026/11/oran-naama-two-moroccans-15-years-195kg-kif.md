The criminal court of appeal in Oran recently sentenced two Moroccan nationals
to 15 years in prison each for involvement in a drug-trafficking network
dismantled in Naâma wilaya, a province. [@1] The case involved six accused and
covered importation, possession, transport and sale of drugs, membership in a
criminal organisation and unauthorised carrying of weapons. [@1] The six
defendants received prison terms ranging from 10 to 20 years, with the two
Moroccan nationals each receiving 15 years. [@1] At the hearing the accused
admitted the acts, citing their social conditions and asking for leniency, while
the public prosecutor had requested life imprisonment. [@2]

According to the referral order, the investigation started from information
about a drug shipment through a border crossing in the commune of Mekmen Ben
Amar in Naâma. [@1] Two suspects were then caught with 13 kg of treated kif, the
local term for processed cannabis, while a third accomplice fled. [@1]
Investigators then arrested two other alleged members accused of collecting the
drugs and distributing them to traffickers. [@1] In a second night operation
near the border, officers caught the two Moroccan nationals moving 1 quintal and
82 kg — 182 kg — of treated kif on pack animals. [@1] The two men were carrying
two hunting rifles when caught. [@1]

The two Moroccan nationals told interrogators they worked under a compatriot
nicknamed Cheikh for 40 to 50 million centimes per load. [@1]
