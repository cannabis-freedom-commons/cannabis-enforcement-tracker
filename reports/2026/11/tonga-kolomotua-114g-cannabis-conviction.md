Polotu ‘Aho Halalilo was found guilty on three charges by Lord Chief Justice
Malcolm Bishop KC in a written verdict delivered last month. [@1] His sentencing
was adjourned pending an appeal expected to be heard in March 2027. [@1]

The charges included possessing 1.14 grams of cannabis. [@1] Police found that
cannabis on a shelf on the veranda of his residence in Kolomotu’a. [@1] The
other charges involved supplying 3.97 grams of methamphetamine and possessing
11.93 grams of methamphetamine. [@1] A search of his vehicle had uncovered
further methamphetamine weighing 7.96 grams concealed behind the steering wheel.
[@1]

The case arose from a police operation on 27 April 2020 at a car wash in
Kolofo’ou, next to the residence of Nasif Ali, who worked there. [@1] Ali told
police he had purchased methamphetamine from Halalilo. [@1] Police then asked
Ali to telephone Halalilo and order another consignment, and Ali requested four
grams of methamphetamine. [@1] Inspector Vi saw Halalilo arrive in a yellow car
and hand Ali a transparent plastic bag containing two loaves of bread before
driving away. [@1] The package was handed to Inspector Vi, who found four small
bags of methamphetamine concealed inside one loaf. [@1] Police followed Halalilo
and apprehended him in the Basilica Church car park with assistance from a
police dog team. [@1] The court rejected the entrapment argument, finding
Halalilo had not been pressured or coerced and was unaware of police
involvement. [@1] The court also rejected the argument that the delay between
the 2020 offences and the 2026 decision justified stopping the prosecution. [@1]
The judge accepted the drug analyst’s evidence that the substances had been
correctly identified as methamphetamine and cannabis. [@1]
