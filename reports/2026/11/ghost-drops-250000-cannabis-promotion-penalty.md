The Federal Court upheld penalties totalling $250,000 imposed on a cannabis
marketing company for unlawful cannabis promotion and event sponsorship. [@1]
Health Canada issued Ghost Drops two notices of violation in March 2024 over
non-compliant promotion and sponsorship at an all-ages event where young people
were present. [@2] The notices initially carried combined penalties of $500,000.
[@2] Ghost Drops requested a review, completed in March 2025, that found it had
committed the violations and reduced the total penalty to $250,000. [@2] Health
Canada said the notices were the first administrative monetary penalties it had
imposed under the Cannabis Act. [@2]

Ghost Drops argued that Canada's cannabis marketing restrictions unfairly
limited its freedom of expression and ability to compete. [@2] Ghost Drops said
the event was open to the public and included promotional displays from several
cannabis companies in an age-gated area. [@2] Ghost Drops said it believed it
had not engaged in rule-violating activities. [@2]
