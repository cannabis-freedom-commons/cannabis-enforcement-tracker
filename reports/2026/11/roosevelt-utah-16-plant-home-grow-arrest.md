Officers discovered a marijuana grow operation on Monday in the Roosevelt home
of a man who pleaded guilty to production of marijuana in 2024. [@1] According
to an affidavit of probable cause, officers arrived at the home near 300 North
600 East to serve court paperwork on Danny Lee Cooper, who came to the door.
[@1] Because of an odor from inside the home, the officers checked with the
Duchesne County Attorney and found that a plea-in-abeyance agreement from his
prior conviction requires him to permit monthly law-enforcement searches to
verify that he is not cultivating marijuana. [@1]

Cooper directed them to the kitchen, where there was suspected raw and burned
marijuana. [@1] He then led them into the basement, where two indoor grow tents
contained approximately 16 large suspected marijuana plants. [@1] The basement
contained an extensive cultivation setup of grow lights, digital temperature
gauges, fans, heaters, blowers and a ventilation system. [@1] The ventilation
system was routed through the basement wall and into the chimney. [@1] In a
separate upstairs room, officers located equipment consistent with marijuana
processing and packaging, including small baggies, new plastic containers, empty
pill capsules, scales, grinders, a press and a dehydrator. [@1] Throughout the
residence, officers also observed suspected marijuana products, including
gummies, chocolates, baked goods, rolled blunts and multiple containers of a
green leafy substance consistent in appearance with marijuana. [@1]

Although held in abeyance, Cooper's prior guilty plea to marijuana production is
a qualifying prior offense for penalty enhancement. [@1] Seventy-eight-year-old
Danny Lee Cooper was arrested and charged with second-degree felony production
of marijuana with a prior and misdemeanor use of drug paraphernalia. [@1]
