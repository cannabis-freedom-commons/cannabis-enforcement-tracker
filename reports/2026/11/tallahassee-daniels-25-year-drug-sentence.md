Christopher L. Daniels, 47, of Tallahassee, Florida, was sentenced to 25 years
in federal prison after a jury found him guilty of possessing controlled
substances with intent to distribute, including more than 50 grams of
methamphetamine, and possessing a firearm as a convicted felon. [@1] United
States Attorney John P. Heekin announced the sentence on 10 November 2026. [@3]
Daniels had 12 prior felony convictions dating back to 1996, and his sentence
was enhanced because of multiple prior convictions for serious drug felonies.
[@2]

Evidence presented at trial showed that a Tallahassee Police Department
drug-trafficking investigation led officers to execute a search warrant at
Daniels' home on 21 January 2026. [@1] Officers seized a pound (about 0.45 kg)
of marijuana along with approximately 2.75 ounces of methamphetamine, an ounce
of cocaine, half an ounce of crack cocaine on an ironing board, about an ounce
of synthetic cathinones sold as molly and 64 tablets confirmed to be low-purity
methamphetamine. [@1] Investigators also recovered four pistols inside the
house, including a Draco AK-47-style pistol near the crack cocaine, and evidence
on Daniels' cellphone showed he was engaged in drug trafficking. [@1]

Federal court records list the case as USA v. Daniels, No. 4:26-cr-00024, in the
Florida Northern District Court. [@7] Identified in an April indictment as
Christopher Lamar Daniels, he was initially charged with possession with intent
to distribute multiple controlled substances, possession of a firearm in
furtherance of a drug-trafficking crime and possession of a firearm by a
convicted felon. [@6] He was arraigned before U.S. Magistrate Judge Martin A.
Fitzpatrick in Tallahassee, with trial initially set for 15 June 2026 before
U.S. District Judge Robert L. Hinkle. [@6] A federal jury found Daniels guilty
on 29 July 2026. [@5] The Federal Public Defender's Office represented Daniels
in the federal case. [@5] At the time of his conviction, sentencing was set for
15 October 2026 before Judge Hinkle, when Daniels faced 25 years to life in
prison, 10 years to life of supervised release and up to $20 million in fines.
[@4]

The Tallahassee Police Department and the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms,
and Explosives investigated the case jointly with assistance from the Drug
Enforcement Administration, and Assistant U.S. Attorney James A. McCain
prosecuted it. [@1] Prosecutors handled the case under Project Safe
Neighborhoods, a federal initiative coordinating law enforcement against violent
crime. [@3]
