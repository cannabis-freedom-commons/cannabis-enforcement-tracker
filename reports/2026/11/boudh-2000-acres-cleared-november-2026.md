Every year between November and February, police, excise and forest personnel
carry out extensive operations to find and destroy illegal cannabis plantations
across Boudh district. [@1,3] With the annual eradication drive already under way,
another 2,000 acres had been cleared in Boudh district as of 5 November 2026.
[@1,3] Officials expected the area cleared to touch 10,000 acres by the end of the
month. [@1,3]

District police data put the area where illegal cannabis was found and destroyed
at nearly 10,000 acres across different police-station jurisdictions in 2024.
[@1,3] That figure rose by 50 per cent to 15,000 acres in 2025. [@2,3] The accounts
say growers increasingly use remote and inaccessible forest areas for
concealment, making cannabis cultivation a significant contributor to forest
degradation in the district. [@1]

During his 5 November visit to Boudh, Inspector General of Police Himanshu Lal
called for strengthening the Anti-Narcotics Task Force and held a joint meeting
with police, district administration, excise and forest departments on a
coordinated strategy to rid Boudh and Odisha of illegal narcotics. [@1] The
initiative is in line with Union Home Minister Amit Shah's target of making
India drug-free by March 2029. [@2] An additional five platoons of police
personnel have reportedly been deployed to intensify the crackdown. [@1]
