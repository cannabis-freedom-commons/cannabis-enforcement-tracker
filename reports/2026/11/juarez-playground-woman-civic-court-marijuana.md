A father in colonia Juárez, in the borough of Cuauhtémoc, Mexico City,
complained that a woman was smoking marijuana in a public space in front of
children. [@1] He asked her to stop using marijuana in a children's play area
where minors were present, and a video of the argument spread on social media.
[@1] The complainant said she started the physical aggression. [@1] The
complaint also said she threw a swing toward a child who was playing. [@1]
During the police procedure she said she had been smoking and had not known she
was not allowed to smoke there. [@1]

Officers of the Secretaría de Seguridad Ciudadana (SSC) went to the site and
took her before a civic court. [@1] Neighborhood reports identified her as
Mexican and living abroad, although some social-media users described her as a
foreigner. [@1] During the administrative procedure she repeatedly refused to
speak Spanish with the officers and with court staff. [@2]

Mexico City's civic-culture law treats consuming, ingesting, inhaling or
aspirating narcotics, psychotropics or toxic substances in public places as an
infraction against citizen security. [@1] The penalties set for that category
are a fine of 21 to 30 times the Unidad de Medida y Actualización, detention of
25 to 36 hours, or 12 to 18 hours of community work, and the civic authority
decides which applies. [@1] Designated cannabis tolerance spaces, known as
puntos 4:20, do not mean marijuana may be smoked freely in any park, pavement or
play area. [@1]
