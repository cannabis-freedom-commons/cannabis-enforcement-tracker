Days after voters replaced all three Republican justices on the Texas 15th Court
of Appeals, the court canceled oral arguments in four cases and told lawyers the
cases would be reset at a later date. [@1] One of the four is the hemp
industry's challenge to Texas Department of State Health Services rules limiting
the sale of hemp-derived products containing THC. [@1] The challenge will go to
three Democrats who take office in early January. [@2]

The court was due to hear oral arguments in the hemp case on December 8 and
canceled its December 8 and 9 arguments in all four cases. [@1] Justice Scott
Field told Bloomberg Law the court could not finish opinions on cases argued
before the end of the year, so it did not want to waste the lawyers' time on
arguments the new court might want to repeat. [@1] The outgoing justices still
have 39 cases awaiting opinions after oral or written arguments. [@1]

The rules, which were to take effect at the end of March, included
child-resistant packaging, new labelling, testing and bookkeeping requirements
and a legal purchasing age of 21. [@1] The rules raised licence fees for
manufacturers of hemp-derived THC from $258 to $10,000 per facility and retail
registrations from $155 to $5,000, and industry leaders said the fees amounted
to a ban that would force businesses to close. [@1] The other canceled cases,
all non-cannabis, concern a homeless shelter in South Austin and tax disputes
over about $3 million and $2.58 million. [@2]
