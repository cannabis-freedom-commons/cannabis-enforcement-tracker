Thirty-six people have been charged in a multi-state federal investigation into
drug trafficking and money laundering, the U.S. Department of Justice announced.
[@1] Nine of them are in the country illegally, prosecutors said. [@3] The
organization is based in Mexico and has operated in South Carolina since at
least 2011. [@1] An April 2026 indictment charged 18 people. [@1] A 42-count
superseding indictment unsealed in November charged 16 more. [@1] Two others
were previously charged in July 2025. [@2] Six defendants have pleaded guilty so
far. [@1]

During the investigation, the Drug Enforcement Administration seized 34 pounds
(about 15.4 kilograms) of marijuana. [@2] It also seized large quantities of
fentanyl, methamphetamine and cocaine. [@1] The department said the organization
had supplied South Carolina with hundreds of kilograms of fentanyl and cocaine
since at least 2021. [@1] Investigators traced a distribution network spanning
the Lowcountry, Pee Dee, Grand Strand and Midlands. [@1] Its members were active
in Mexico, other states, and inside the Federal Bureau of Prisons and the South
Carolina Department of Corrections, the department said. [@1]

U.S. Attorney Bryan Stirling said the Homeland Security Task Force investigation
had an impact on 'every region of our state, and beyond'. [@1] The DEA, Homeland
Security Investigations, the FBI's Columbia field office and the South Carolina
Law Enforcement Division investigated the case with numerous state and local
agencies. [@1] Assistant U.S. Attorneys Mike O'Mara and Cole Shannon are
prosecuting it. [@1] The Justice Department said an indictment is merely an
allegation and all defendants are presumed innocent. [@1]
