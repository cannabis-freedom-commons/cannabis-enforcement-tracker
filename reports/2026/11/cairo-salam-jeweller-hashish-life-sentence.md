Cairo's appeals criminal court was due on Wednesday to deliver its ruling on the
appeal of a jewellery-shop owner, his son and a third man against life sentences
imposed at first instance. [@1] That first-instance verdict had punished each of
the three with life imprisonment and a 100,000-Egyptian-pound fine for
manufacturing and trafficking drugs and holding unlicensed firearms in Al-Salam.
[@1] An earlier hearing set 4 November for the appeal ruling. [@2] The three are
a 45-year-old jewellery-shop owner, his 22-year-old son and a 19-year-old man,
all sent for criminal trial. [@1]

The case began when an officer of the General Administration for Drug Control
received information, confirmed by his inquiries, that the accused were holding
quantities of drugs, and obtained prosecution permission to arrest them and
search their persons and home. [@1] Acting on the warrant, the officer went to
where the accused were, knocked, and detained the first man when he opened the
door, who said the other two were in a room. [@1] A search of the home found
quantities of drugs later confirmed by laboratory testing as narcotics,
alongside firearms, ammunition and two knives. [@1]

Prosecutors charged the men with manufacturing the narcotic hashish for the
purpose of trafficking. [@1] They also charged them with possessing heroin,
hashish, methamphetamine, indazole and the narcotic clonazepam for trafficking
outside legally authorised circumstances. [@1] Prosecutors further charged them
with unlicensed possession of a shotgun, a second shotgun-type firearm and 10
cartridges for use in the two weapons. [@1] The hashish charge is the cannabis
component recorded here, while the reported drug finds were described only as
quantities with no separable cannabis weight. [@1]
