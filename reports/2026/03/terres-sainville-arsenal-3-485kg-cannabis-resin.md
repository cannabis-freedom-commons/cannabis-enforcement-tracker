Fort-de-France's correctional court on 23 March 2026 jailed two men for four
years each and a third for two years, one of them suspended, over the arsenal
and drugs found in a Terres-Sainville house. [@1] José A.G., 38, a Dominican
national, was sentenced to two years with one year suspended and will serve his
term at home under an electronic bracelet. [@1] Frédérick T., 34, holds French
and Dominican nationality, and Weston G.C., 34, is Venezuelan; the first was
arrested on 26 January 2026 and the second on 12 February 2026. [@1] Two of the
three had previous convictions on their records, which the court treated as
legal reoffending. [@1] The prosecutor had sought between 18 months and five
years for weapons possession, acquiring drugs and participation in a criminal
association. [@1] Defence lawyers argued the investigation breached the strict
rules for flagrant-offence inquiries, and Frédérick T. kept silent at the
hearing. [@1] José A.G.'s lawyer said he would not appeal because his client
would sleep at home rather than in detention. [@1]

The case began on 12 November 2025, when about thirty police officers from the
RAID, the territorial judicial police service and specialist units searched a
house in Terres-Sainville. [@2] The house was empty when officers entered, after
a sniffer dog from the canine brigade reacted outside it. [@2] The search found
3.485 kg of cannabis resin, reported at the time as 3.5 kg, along with 1.8 kg of
cocaine. [@3] Officers also seized a ballistic vest, a stock that turns a
handgun into a shoulder weapon, a silencer, about fifty 9 mm rounds, 5.56 mm
ammunition, a laser sight, balaclavas and packaging material. [@2] Police
described the house as a logistics site for a drug-trafficking operation, used
to prepare and pack drugs for sale. [@3] Investigators took fingerprints and DNA
profiles from the seized material to identify who had handled it. [@2]
