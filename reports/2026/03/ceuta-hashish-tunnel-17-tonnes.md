Spanish police uncovered a drug-smuggling tunnel beneath an industrial warehouse in Ceuta, Spain's North African exclave, and said it was used to move hashish from Morocco into Spain. [@1,2]

The structure extended over three levels, with a descent shaft, an intermediate chamber for storing pallets, and the tunnel itself, equipped with a rail system and underground cranes. [@1,2]

Police seized 17 metric tons of hashish and €1.4 million (about $1.6 million) in cash and arrested 27 people in the operation. [@1,2] The police did not disclose the street value of the haul. [@1,2]

Hashish, made from cannabis resin, is usually smuggled into Spain by sea in speedboats. [@1,2] Spain is a major entry point for hashish into Europe, and Ceuta and Melilla form the European Union's only land borders with Africa. [@1,2] In 2023 Spain accounted for 68% of cannabis resin seizures in the EU, according to EU drugs agency data. [@1,2]
