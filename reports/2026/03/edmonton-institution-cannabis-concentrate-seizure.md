Staff at Edmonton Institution found packages of contraband during their duties
on 30 March 2026, and no inmate, visitor or employee was named in connection
with them. [@2]

The packages held methamphetamine and cannabis concentrate, which the
Correctional Service of Canada also calls THC concentrate, a concentrated
cannabis product rather than dried flower. [@2] In its French release the agency
described the same items as "du concentré de THC". [@3] The Correctional Service
of Canada put the institutional value of everything seized at $57,700. [@2] The
agency says the institutional value is its own estimate of the drugs' worth
inside the prison. [@4]

The police were notified and the institution opened an investigation. [@2]

The Correctional Service of Canada says it uses ion scanners and drug-detector
dogs to search buildings, personal property, inmates and visitors for drugs
entering federal prisons. [@2] The agency also runs an anonymous toll-free tip
line for information on drug use or trafficking that could threaten visitors,
inmates and staff. [@2]

CTV News reported the same 30 March seizure and the same $57,700 estimate at the
Edmonton jail. [@1] CTV News also carried the agency's statement that police had
been notified and the institution was investigating. [@1]
