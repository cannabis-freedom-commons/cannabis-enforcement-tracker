Tony M., a 38-year-old Martinican nicknamed “Biggy”, was the inmate at the
centre of the case. [@1] The court sentenced him to six years in prison for
active corruption of two Fresnes prison officers. [@1] The trial was held from 3
to 5 March 2026 in Évry-Courcouronnes. [@4] The two officers were identified in
court as L. and “JF”, both in their twenties, and were tried for corruption and
drug trafficking. [@3] Both had been suspended from their jobs at Fresnes. [@3]
The court sentenced one guard to one year in prison and the other to two and a
half years. [@6] The one-year term was the firm part of a two-year sentence with
one year suspended. [@1] The prosecution had sought eight years for M. [@2]

Between summer 2023 and January 2024, the guards brought bars of cannabis resin
into Fresnes on several occasions for two detainees who were drug traffickers.
[@3] M. was held at Fresnes after a cannabis conviction in Créteil. [@7] From
inside the prison, he organized the deliveries and corrupted the two guards.
[@7] He paid the guards €100 per delivery to bring in cannabis resin and
cigarettes. [@5] An anonymous denunciation had accused him of bringing cannabis
into the prison during his detention. [@2] Phone taps led investigators to M.
and the two guards. [@2] Other defendants were tried alongside them in a wider
drug-trafficking case. [@3]

One guard admitted a foolish act and expressed shame at the hearing. [@1] The
other denied the charges and said he had only given a few cigarettes to
detainees to keep the peace. [@2] Defence lawyers argued that conditions at
Fresnes were particularly difficult, with overcrowding, violence and threats
against staff. [@1]
