At least eleven young French nationals have been held in Thailand on cannabis
trafficking accusations since the start of summer 2026. [@1] Thai customs
officers found cannabis in their luggage at airports and handed them to the Thai
police, who placed them in pre-trial detention to await a judge. [@3] Their
luggage held several kilograms of cannabis, according to a police source cited
by BFMTV. [@4] Two more French nationals were held in Hong Kong after arriving
from Thailand and being stopped in transit with cannabis in suitcases. [@1]

One Hong Kong suitcase held 16.8 kilograms of cannabis, according to a relative
of one of the two detainees quoted by AFP. [@2] A customs fine of 30,000 baht
(about 790 euros) per kilogram of drug seized has applied systematically in
Thailand since 18 June 2026. [@1] Non-payment of the fine leads to police
custody and detention awaiting trial, according to the French foreign ministry's
France Diplomatie travel site. [@1]

A French diplomatic source said consular services in Thailand and Hong Kong were
mobilised and in contact with the families. [@1] The Parquet national
anti-criminalité organisée (Pnaco), France's national prosecutor's office for
organised crime, said it had investigations under way into the "plans Thaïlande"
recruitment. [@1] Recruiters publish advertisements on apps such as Snapchat
offering fully paid holidays in Thailand in exchange for bringing a suitcase
back to Europe that supposedly holds phones but in reality holds 10 to 20 kg of
cannabis, the Pnaco said. [@1] The Pnaco said its aim was to identify those
behind the operation and the networks, and to understand how the young people
were recruited. [@1]

The lawyer for the two Hong Kong detainees said these cases involving young
French or other European nationals were rising appreciably, and that he was
handling four such files. [@1] The lawyer said the people involved were often
middle-class boys from rather disadvantaged backgrounds. [@1] The sister-in-law
of one Hong Kong detainee said he thought he had been recruited online to bring
smartphones back for a French company in exchange for free holidays and pay of
up to 3,000 euros. [@1]
