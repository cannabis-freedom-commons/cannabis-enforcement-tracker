On 14 August 2026, Marcos Emiliano Cayupán, a repeat offender serving more than
two years for multiple robberies at the Alcaidía UR-I in Santa Rosa, La Pampa,
returned from his second 48-hour day-release and presented himself for the
mandatory re-entry search. [@1] Guards found five small black nylon packets in
his mouth containing compact marijuana, which he had hidden to evade the search.
[@1]

Judge Mónica Rivero, the substitute execution judge, immediately revoked his
day-release benefit, ruling he had gravely violated the absolute prohibition on
possession and use of drugs imposed as a condition of his release. [@1]

His official public defender Juan José Hermúa appealed the revocation to Sala B
of the La Pampa Penal Tribunal de Impugnación, arguing there were no
surveillance cameras in the search room and no seizure records, photographs or
medical examinations to prove the substance. [@1] Sala B of the La Pampa Penal
Tribunal de Impugnación, with judges Piombi and Frigerio, rejected the appeal
and upheld the revocation, reasoning that it is normal for privacy reasons that
intimate-search rooms have no cameras, and that returning to the institution in
a way that shows an intention to introduce drugs and evade regulatory controls
constitutes a concrete negative impact on the progressive sentencing regime.
[@1]

Cayupán must now serve the remainder of his sentence continuously in prison; his
sentence ends in November 2027. [@1]
