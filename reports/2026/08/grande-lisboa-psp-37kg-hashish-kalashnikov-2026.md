Two Portuguese men aged 28 and 30 were detained in the Lisbon metropolitan area
on Monday 10 August 2026 over a hashish supply network. [@2] The arrests were
made by the Divisão de Investigação Criminal, the criminal investigation
division of the PSP, Portugal's public security police, after a year of evidence
gathering. [@2] Police said the men were caught as they made another drug
transport intended for sale in the Lisbon area. [@2]

Sub-intendant Nelson Silva said the cell exported and delivered drugs that were
then meant for direct sale. [@1] Officers seized 37,108.95 grams of hashish,
which the PSP equated to 74,217 doses. [@2] They also took three light vehicles,
185 rounds of ammunition of various calibres and two ballistic vests. [@2] The
firearms seized were an AK-47 Kalashnikov machine gun and two 7.65 mm pistols.
[@2] 7,614.35 euros in cash was seized. [@2]

After their arrest, the two men appeared before a first judicial interrogation
and a court ordered them held in preventive detention, Portugal's most serious
coercive measure. [@2] They are suspected of drug trafficking and of possessing
a prohibited weapon. [@2] At a press conference at the division's Lisbon
headquarters, the PSP said the two men bought the hashish in Spain and sold it
on in Portugal. [@3] Investigators suspect the hashish came from Morocco and
reached Europe through Spain. [@3] Both men had criminal records and were
established in Portugal. [@3]

The operation was carried out on Monday by the Divisão de Investigação Criminal
de Lisboa. [@1] Nelson Silva said the AK-47 showed the group was prepared,
calling the dismantled cell more prepared than other trafficking structures the
PSP had investigated. [@3] Silva said the investigation was not closed and that
the material gathered could identify other people in the distribution chain.
[@3]
