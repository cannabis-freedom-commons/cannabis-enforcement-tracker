Belfast Crown Court jailed two Afghan nationals for their involvement in a
cannabis factory in North Belfast worth up to £500,000. [@1] Faroz Ahmed, 34, of
Cromwell Road in south Belfast, had pleaded guilty to cultivating cannabis and
possessing £2,280 in criminal-property cash. [@1] Shahidullah Arghandabi, 27, of
Popes Lane in London, had pleaded guilty to cultivating cannabis. [@1] Judge
Philip Gilpin imposed a 24-month determinate sentence on each man, split equally
between custody and supervised licence after release. [@1]

In February 2024 the owner of a large commercial premises on Limestone Road in
North Belfast rented it to a London company called Just Meet and Eat. [@1] The
landlord regained access on 7 June 2024, found an industrial-scale cannabis
factory and alerted police. [@1] Officers found 553 healthy flowering cannabis
plants and 357 seedlings, plus hundreds of soil-filled pots and 47 black bags of
cut cannabis leaves. [@1] Police described the site as a sophisticated
cannabis-growing operation and valued the plants at between £110,000 and
£497,000. [@1]

Ahmed was stopped in a Renault van at Holywood train halt in County Down with
cannabis plants in five bags and £2,280 in cash. [@1] Arghandabi drove a Ford
Transit van from Cairnryan to Belfast on 29 March 2024. [@1] The van carried
electric boards, fans and filters for use in cannabis cultivation. [@1] Ahmed
admitted his role on the basis that he did not set up the factory, rent the
premises or share in profits and acted at the direction of others. [@1]
Arghandabi accepted he was not an organiser of the operation but was assisting
in cultivation. [@1] The judge ordered destruction of all the drugs and other
items seized at the factory. [@1]
