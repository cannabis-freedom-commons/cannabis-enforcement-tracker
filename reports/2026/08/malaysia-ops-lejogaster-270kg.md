Malaysia's federal narcotics department said it broke up an international syndicate that used
terrace houses near airports as transit points for cannabis buds bound for Europe, announcing
the results on 11 August 2026. [@1,2] Operation Lejogaster ran from 12 July to 3 August 2026
after intelligence work by Bukit Aman NCID. [@1,2]

Nine men aged 22 to 51 — one Malaysian and eight foreigners — were arrested in three raids in
Penang, Negri Sembilan and Selangor. [@1,2,3] Police said they also removed four foreigners from
exploitation as mules. [@1,2,3] On 12 July officers arrested two men outside a Bayan Lepas hotel
and found about 31.7 kg of cannabis buds in luggage in a car, then about 31 kg more at a nearby
house. [@1,2] On 3 August a search in Mantin, Negri Sembilan, yielded about 175.8 kg in ten bags;
five more foreigners were arrested in Sepang, four of them described as mules, and another man
was stopped at Penang International Airport with about 31.6 kg. [@1,2]

In total the force seized 270.27 kg of cannabis buds, which it valued at about RM21.62 million,
along with 0.5 g of methamphetamine and assets including jewellery, phones, a laptop and a
vehicle. [@1,3] Investigators said the group had been active since about April, bringing buds in
through Malaysia's northern border and staging them near Bayan Lepas and Nilai before airport
hand-offs; tighter KLIA screening had pushed the network toward Penang. [@2] The case is under
Section 39B of the Dangerous Drugs Act 1952. [@1,3]

Five men believed to be Portuguese passport holders were among eight foreigners recruited to transport cannabis from Malaysia to Europe. [@3] The other foreigners arrested were a Myanmar national and two Singaporeans, while the ninth suspect was a Malaysian. [@3] Police said the Portuguese group's leader is a Portuguese national who coordinated from Europe the people brought to Malaysia to act as drug mules. [@3] The suspected drug mules did not know one another and were housed in separate hotels while in Malaysia. [@3] The mules were believed to transport the drugs to London before they were distributed to other European countries. [@3] Various assets worth an estimated RM117,172 were also seized, including jewellery, mobile phones, laptops and a Honda CR-V. [@3]
