Department of Border Operations teams carried out the action during Operation
Protetor. [@1] A 27-year-old road military police soldier and a 19-year-old
student were arrested in flagrante for drug trafficking on the afternoon of
Monday 10 August on highway MS-164 near Vista Alegre district in Maracaju. [@1]
The car was driven by military police officer Luiz Rhuan Horiguchi, who said he
was returning from Ponta Porã to Campo Grande, where he lives. [@1] The other
named passenger was 19-year-old Ingra Laylla Machado de Medeiros of Belo
Horizonte, who seemed nervous and could not detail how long she had stayed in
the border region. [@1] The third occupant, a 23-year-old student, had his
belongings checked with nothing illicit found and was heard as a witness. [@1]

Officers found 14 packages of skunk totalling 4.1 kg in the young woman's
backpack during the vehicle inspection. [@1] The loss to crime from the seizure
was estimated at R$101,300. [@1] She admitted to police that she knew about the
drug and said she had agreed to carry it from Ponta Porã to Belo Horizonte for
R$3,000 after a friend invited her to travel and she was left without money to
return. [@1] She said the driver did not know about the illicit contents. [@1]
DOF officers reported that before the drug was found the driver had said he
inspected all passengers' bags before boarding. [@1] Questioned again after the
seizure, the officer chose to remain silent. [@1]

At Defron in Dourados the officer changed his account, denying a full search and
saying he had only squeezed the bag. [@1] He repeated that he used a platform to
offer rides and had no knowledge of the drug. [@1] Civil Police ratified the
flagrante arrest of both suspects. [@1] The delegate sought conversion of
Ingra's flagrante into preventive detention, citing interstate logistics and the
quantity of drugs. [@1] The order noted no concrete risk to public order or
obstruction, highlighting his fixed address, active service ties and voluntary
handover of his phone with its password for examination. [@1] At the 12 August
custody hearing the court converted Ingra's detention to preventive detention
and granted the soldier provisional liberty. [@2] The judge authorized forensic
examination of the phones seized from those involved. [@2] The court also
ordered incineration of the seized drug while preserving a sample for possible
counter-proof. [@2]
