## The executions

Saudi Arabia executed three Ethiopian nationals on 21 April 2026 after convicting them of
"participating in smuggling hashish" into the kingdom, the interior ministry announced that
day. [@1] The official statement records the sentences as discretionary death penalties
carried out in the Asir region. [@4]

## The men

The three men were Kibrom Gebremariam, Tsigabu Hagos and Kidane Angesom, all from
Ethiopia's northern Tigray region, and they were put to death at the Khamis Mushait
detention centre in Asir Province. [@2] Kidane Angesom was 32 and had spent nine years in
Saudi Arabia, four of them behind bars. [@3] His mother, Tsehay Wolay, learned of his
execution from his cellmate, and said she never knew what her son had been accused of.
[@3] Ethiopian prisoners at the facility said they had been convicted on drug charges after
being forced to sign documents written in Arabic without interpreters or adequate legal
representation. [@2]

## A wave of cases

Human Rights Watch warned at the time that at least 65 Ethiopian migrants were at imminent
risk of execution for drug offences in Saudi Arabia. [@1] The organisation said executing
foreign migrants for non-violent offences after proceedings that fail basic standards of
due process reflects a profound disregard for their rights and lives. [@2] Many families
are still waiting for the return of the bodies of relatives already executed, while parents
of detainees keep appealing for international intervention. [@2]
