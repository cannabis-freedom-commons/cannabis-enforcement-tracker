Thirty-five-year-old American Gia Fields, a ramp agent from the Bronx, New York,
arrived in Belize on 23 April 2026 aboard a Delta Airlines flight from Atlanta.
[@2] Customs officers at the Philip Goldson International Airport detected a
strong odour of cannabis during a routine check, and police responded to assist.
[@1] A police search found a plastic container concealed in her bosom and three
additional packages hidden in her pelvic area. [@2] Fields was detained and
escorted to the Ladyville Police Station, where the drugs were weighed in her
presence. [@2] She pleaded guilty at the first opportunity and apologised,
saying she had wrongly assumed bringing cannabis was acceptable because she is a
smoker. [@2]

The cannabis weighed 52 grams. [@2] The court started from a fine of $3,000 for
the offence. [@2] She received a one-third discount for her early guilty plea,
reducing the fine by $1,000, plus a further $500 reduction for remorse and
mitigation. [@2] She was ordered to pay the remaining $1,500 forthwith. [@2]

Importation of a controlled drug carries penalties similar to drug trafficking,
including fines of up to $10,000 and imprisonment of up to three years. [@1] In
this case the court exercised leniency based on the circumstances. [@1]
