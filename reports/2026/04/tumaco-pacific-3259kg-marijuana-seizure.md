Colombia's Navy intercepted a go-fast boat in the South Pacific that was
carrying 3.2 tonnes of marijuana valued at 3.4 million dollars. [@1] The
procedure formed part of maritime control and security operations with the
Pacific Coast Guard Group and a surface unit of the United States Southern
Command's Joint Interagency Task Force South. [@1] Naval intelligence located
the launch, which had three outboard motors. [@1] During the inspection, Navy
personnel found three Colombian citizens aboard with 109 blue and yellow sacks
containing the drug. [@1]

The boat and its crew were taken to the Tumaco Coast Guard Station in Nariño,
where CTI officers' preliminary test confirmed 3,259 kilograms of marijuana.
[@1] Officers also seized about 600 gallons of foreign fuel in the procedure.
[@2] The three detainees were placed at the disposal of the competent
authorities, which began judicialization proceedings. [@1] The Navy said the
seizure kept more than 540,000 doses from reaching illegal international
markets. [@1] The Navy described the result as a blow to the illicit economies
of criminal organizations operating in the Colombian Pacific. [@1] The Navy
invited the public to report suspicious activity to line 146, available 24 hours
a day. [@2]
