South Korea's Korea Customs Service said its first-quarter joint crackdowns with
the customs agencies of Thailand and Cambodia intercepted 657 kg of narcotics in
32 cases between January and March 2026, a quantity it said could supply about
1.8 million people. [@1] Marijuana was among the drugs seized alongside
methamphetamine and etomidate, an anaesthetic used to induce sedation. [@1]

Twenty-eight of the cases, holding 651.4 kg of narcotics, came from operation
Trident with Thai customs, the largest haul in a single such operation since the
two agencies began them in 2022. [@1] The five Thai joint operations to date
have produced 184 cases and 1,036.9 kg of drugs, according to the customs
agency. [@3] The first operation with Cambodia, Lionstone, ran from 26 January
to 28 February 2026 and intercepted 5.7 kg of drugs in four cases, the Korean
customs agency said. [@1] The agency said it would pursue the supply chains
behind the intercepted shipments and seek custody of the main suspects through
follow-up enforcement with the two customs agencies. [@3]

Customs chief Lee Myeong-ku said his agency would cooperate with other
governments to trace smuggling routes, concealment methods and overseas senders,
vowing that "not a single grain" of drugs would cross South Korea's border. [@3]
The agency framed the operations as a way to stop drugs at their overseas
departure points before they reach South Korea. [@2]
