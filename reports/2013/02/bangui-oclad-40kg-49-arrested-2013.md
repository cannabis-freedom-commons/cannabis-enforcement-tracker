Officers of the Central African Republic's anti-drug office, the Office
centrafricaine de lutte anti drogue (OCLAD), seized 40 kilograms of cannabis in
Bangui on Wednesday 27 February 2013. [@1] The seizure came during an operation
the office called "coup de poing", a French phrase for a sudden strike. [@1] In
the same operation police arrested 49 people for trafficking the drug: 29
Congolese women and 20 men of Congolese and Central African nationality. [@1]

The operation covered the Bangui neighbourhoods of Km5, Fodé, Bibalé, Miskine
and Mamadou-Mbaïki, the last two written Mamadou-Mbaïki in the account; Mamadou
is a separate neighbourhood. [@1] The officer commanding the operation said his
teams had carried out house searches and made incidental seizures. [@1] The
police commander said the operation also turned up other drugs besides the
chanvre indien, the French term for Indian hemp, or cannabis. [@1]

The raids followed complaints residents of those neighbourhoods made to local
media about growing insecurity they blamed on the arrested men. [@1] The
operation commander said proceedings were already under way to bring the
detainees before the Bangui high court (tribunal de grande instance), the city's
court of first instance. [@1]
