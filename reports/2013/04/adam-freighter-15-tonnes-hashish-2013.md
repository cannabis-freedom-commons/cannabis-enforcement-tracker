Investigators of Italy's antidrug unit, acting on a telephone tip that an enormous freighter loaded with hashish was plying international waters south of Sicily and bound for Libya, sent two Italian Navy warships to stop the freighter Adam off the Libyan coast on 12 April 2013. [@1] Aboard they found a terrified Syrian crew and 15 metric tons of hashish, a stash many multiples larger than Italian officials had ever seen. [@1,2]

Senior investigator Francesco Amico, who waited in the Sicilian port of Trapani for the escorted ship, said there was so much of the drug that officers had to rent a warehouse to store it. [@1] The seizure exposed a new trafficking route stretching from Moroccan fields along the North African coast to Libya, and the Adam was the first of 20 ships intercepted on it over the following 32 months, carrying more than 280 tons of hashish valued at 2.8 billion euros. [@1,3] Investigators later traced the shipments onward from Libya overland through Egypt toward Europe, and the Spanish Tax Agency lists the Adam among the Eastern Mediterranean route seizures by Italy, France and Spain. [@1,3]
