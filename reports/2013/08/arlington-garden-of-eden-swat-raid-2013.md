Arlington police stormed the Garden of Eden organic farm in south Arlington,
Texas, at around 7 a.m. on 2 August 2013. [@5] More than 50 officers, armed with
military-style weapons, cut through the chain-link gate. [@5] Most of the
residents were still asleep. [@5] The officers rounded up and handcuffed all the
adults, including five visitors. [@5] The ACLU said residents were held at
gunpoint for at least half an hour. [@1] A guard stayed with a sleeping toddler
and newborn while officers took another resident downstairs. [@5] The farm's
account said all eight adults present were handcuffed at the gunpoint of heavily
armed SWAT officers. [@4] NBC 5 reported that six adults lived at the farm. [@2]
One resident, 30-year-old Quinn Eaker, was arrested on an outstanding warrant
over unpaid parking tickets. [@2] Police removed the handcuffs within about 30
minutes. [@2] The residents said police did not show a warrant until two hours
after the raid began. [@3] The raid lasted about 10 hours. [@1]

Police had a search warrant for marijuana plants. [@2] In June an anonymous
source told police Eaker was growing marijuana in a garden surrounded by bamboo.
[@2] On 30 July a Texas Department of Public Safety aircraft photographed the
garden, and an officer thought the plants looked like marijuana. [@2] Eaker said
the plants police mistook for marijuana were likely tomatoes. [@2] No marijuana
or other drugs were found. [@5] Officers and city code inspectors instead took
away 17 blackberry bushes, 15 okra plants, 14 tomatillo plants, native grasses
and sunflowers. [@1] A city abatement team then mowed part of the garden. [@5]
The farm covered 3.5 acres at 7325 Mansfield Cardinal Road. [@6]

The residents demanded an apology from police. [@2] They also sought
compensation for the damage done during the raid. [@2] In July 2015 the owner
and four other members sued the city. [@8] The case was filed in federal court
in Fort Worth. [@7] The suit said police had no probable cause and based the
search largely on an anonymous tip and a flyover that spotted what looked like
marijuana plants. [@7] The city said it was reviewing the lawsuit and that the
factual recitations appeared not wholly accurate. [@7] The case was later stayed
pending the owner's bankruptcy, and no outcome is reported. [@8] The ACLU, then
running a 25-state investigation into police militarization, cited the raid as
an example of routine drug enforcement conducted like a war. [@1]
