Five Manchester men were jailed on 18 October 2013 for running a cannabis supply network that brought the drug into England from the Netherlands. [@1] Carmello Anthony Abela, 53, received 18 years and his brother Geno Abela, 42, received 15 years; Michael Levvy, 44, was jailed for 12 years, Ian Thompson, 42, for five years, and Rizwan Safdar, 30, who had pleaded guilty to conspiracy to supply, for 20 months. [@1] The judge called it the largest prosecution for cannabis supply ever brought in Britain. [@1]

## Operation Bizarre

West Yorkshire Police launched Operation Bizarre in 2011 against gangs distributing cannabis across the north of England. [@1] In December 2011 officers stopped a vehicle on the M67 and seized about 50 kilos of cannabis worth £422,450, which led them to a supply premises on the Roe Cross Industrial Estate in Mottram. [@1] A search there pointed to a cold-storage company in County Durham, where officers found a 1.8-tonne shipment valued at £7,819,645 hidden in a cover load of frozen meat. [@1]

Police linked the Abelas to a fake transport company behind 13 earlier shipments carrying as much as 20 tonnes of cannabis, which the authorities valued at more than £80 million. [@1] The prosecution put the drugs' conservative value above £100 million. [@2] After an eight-week trial at Minshull Street Crown Court, a Manchester businessman defended by James Gregory and Dan Thomas was the only defendant acquitted. [@2]
