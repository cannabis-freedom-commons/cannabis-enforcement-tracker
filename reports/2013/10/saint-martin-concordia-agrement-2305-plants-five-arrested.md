Law enforcement in Saint-Martin carried out an operation on 15 October 2013 that
seized more than 2,000 cannabis plants and dismantled a drug trafficking
network. [@1] Five people were arrested in the operation; they had been growing
cannabis on a large scale. [@1]

A customs helicopter and customs officers first spotted several cannabis-growing
sites at Concordia and Agrément during an overflight of the island in May 2012.
[@2] The Saint-Martin gendarmerie research brigade, with air support from the
Pointe-à-Pitre gendarmerie air section, located three production sites in the
hills of Concordia and Agrément among dense vegetation that was hard to reach.
[@2] The sites were equipped with a generator running a water pump and a
rainwater collection system that, after chemical treatment, irrigated the
plants. [@2]

A long investigation led to the discovery of large-scale cannabis production and
the dismantling of the trafficking network. [@1] After months of investigation
and discreet surveillance, gendarmes identified the main grower of the largest
site and the main participants in the cannabis-selling network. [@2] The largest
site alone held about 1,700 cannabis plants and, according to estimates,
represented a production of 50 kilograms since December 2012. [@2] On 15 October
2013 a joint operation by the Saint-Martin research brigade, the Melun mobile
gendarmerie intervention platoon and the gendarmerie surveillance and
intervention platoon led to the destruction of the production sites. [@2]
Searches at the suspects' homes produced the seizure of 305 more cannabis
plants, growing equipment including a drying cabinet with ultraviolet lamps and
ventilation, cannabis seeds and fertiliser, account books and a .22 calibre long
rifle. [@2]

After the detentions, the main suspect was brought before the Republic's
Prosecutor and placed in pre-trial detention at Basse-Terre prison pending
trial. [@2] Three others were released and summoned to appear in court on 6
December 2013, and the last individual was placed under judicial supervision.
[@2]
