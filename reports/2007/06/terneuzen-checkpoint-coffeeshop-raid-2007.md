On 1 June 2007 Dutch police acted against Checkpoint, a coffeeshop in Terneuzen
near the Belgian border described as one of the largest in the Netherlands, finding
almost 100 kilograms of cannabis in the shop and a related storehouse. [@1] The
haul came to 5.1 kilograms in the shop and 92.2 kilograms in a storehouse close by,
many times the 500 grams a coffeeshop may officially keep on hand. [@1] Checkpoint
served 2,000 to 2,500 visitors a day, most of them Belgian or French. [@1]

## Warning

Terneuzen mayor Jan Lonink said he was not surprised at the size of the stock and
called for a serious rethink of Dutch soft-drugs policy, in which the municipality
licences the shop's front door while the supply remains a matter for the justice
authorities. [@1] Checkpoint received a formal warning: if caught again it would be
closed for the time being. [@1] Prosecutors later put the 2007 haul at 120 kilograms
when the owner and sixteen staff and suppliers went on trial in Middelburg. [@2,3]
