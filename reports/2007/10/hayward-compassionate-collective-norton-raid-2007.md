Federal agents arrested brothers Winslow Norton, 26, of Lafayette and Abraham Norton, 23, of Oakland early on Tuesday 30 October 2007 after indicting them for running a multimillion-dollar drug operation from a Hayward-area medical marijuana collective. [@1] At 6 a.m. that morning DEA and IRS agents raided the Compassionate Collective of Alameda County at 21222 Mission Boulevard near Blossom Way, just north of Hayward. [@1] The brothers were indicted for conspiring to distribute more than 220 pounds of marijuana alongside money-laundering counts. [@1]

The collective had opened in Hayward under a county ordinance licensing it with two other dispensaries, and paid about 4 million dollars in sales tax on 26.3 million dollars of revenue in a 2007 business year cut short by the raid. [@2] The raid also seized several hundred pounds of marijuana and 385,000 dollars the collective had banked to pay sales tax, according to its manager Brian Everett. [@2] The owners faced 21 felony counts including intent to distribute more than 100 kilograms of marijuana, maintaining a drug-involved premises, money laundering and a firearm count tied to their mandated armed guard; their father Michael Norton and Everett were indicted later too. [@2]
