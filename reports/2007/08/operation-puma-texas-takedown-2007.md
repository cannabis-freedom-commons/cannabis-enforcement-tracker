Federal agents swept through Dallas, McAllen, Laredo and San Antonio starting about
6 a.m. on Thursday 16 August 2007, executing 19 search warrants and arresting more
than 30 people linked to the Gulf Cartel's drug distribution network in a 2.5-year
DEA investigation called Operation Puma. [@1] A U.S. diplomatic cable reporting on
the operation counted 32 Gulf Cartel operatives captured in Texas. [@2]

In the federal Southern District of Texas, covering Houston, Laredo and the Rio
Grande Valley, the operation produced 27 arrests and seizures of 2,622 kilograms of
cocaine, 15,945 kilograms of marijuana and $5.5 million in cash. [@1] The cable gave
the same overall tally of 2.6 tonnes of cocaine, 15.9 tonnes of marijuana and $5.5
million in cash. [@2] The North Texas branch added 277 kilograms of cocaine, 900
pounds of marijuana and nearly $2.5 million in currency, with the Thursday raids
contributing 2 kilograms of cocaine and $80,000 to that total. [@1]

Among those detained was Sergio Maldonado, 33, of McKinney, described as the
cartel's cell leader for North Texas and arrested without incident in Laredo on
indictments for conspiracy, drug trafficking and money laundering. [@1] The
investigation was named for Carlos Landin-Martinez, described as a Gulf Cartel
second-in-command in Reynosa, who had been arrested on 14 July while shopping in
McAllen. [@1] A later federal release confirmed Jason Paul Cantu was arrested in
Brownsville in August 2007 as part of the same conspiracy. [@3]

## Cross-border operation

Mexican federal police joined the August effort, launching raids in Mexico whose
targets had been forewarned and fled before officers arrived. [@2] U.S. officials
called the cooperation between U.S. and Mexican agencies unprecedented, while one
official cautioned it was unclear how much the arrests would affect the cartel and
its Zetas enforcers. [@1] Prosecutors also moved to forfeit homes in McKinney and
Dallas and 13 vehicles seized from the defendants. [@1]
