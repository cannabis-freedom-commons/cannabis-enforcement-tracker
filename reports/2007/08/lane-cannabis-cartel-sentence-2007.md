Queensland grazier Alexander Malcolm Lane was sentenced in Cairns Supreme Court on 16
August 2007 to thirteen and a half years' jail for heading a multi-million-dollar
marijuana-growing cartel. [@1,2] Lane, a Cairns father of two, had presided over one of
Australia's most sophisticated marijuana cartels for more than a decade. [@1,2] Justice
Stanley Jones said Lane was second to none in the organisation, responsible for marketing
the drug, distributing profits, converting cash into gold and keeping accounts. [@1,2]

Lane and four other syndicate members were sentenced the same day after a three-year joint
police and Australian Crime Commission investigation that traced the syndicate across
Australia and to Europe. [@2] Officers said that at the time of the arrests on 30 May 2006
it was one of the biggest and longest-running drug operations found in several
decades. [@1,2] The syndicate used helicopters to ferry men and supplies into remote bush
camps on an outback cattle station near Chillagoe, producing tonnes of high-grade cannabis
each year. [@1,2] The helicopters carried each crop, worth between three and four million
dollars, to waiting semi-trailers that drove it down inland roads to the Sydney and
Melbourne markets between 1995 and 2006. [@2] Helicopter pilot Peter Pantovich, the former
manager of Bolwarra Station 75 kilometres south-west of Chillagoe, was sentenced to eight
years for his role. [@1,2]
