Malaysia's Federal Court, sitting in Kota Kinabalu, unanimously dismissed on Monday 19 March 2007 the appeals of two death-row prisoners, including 36-year-old Basil Omar. [@1,2] Omar had been sentenced to death by hanging by the Sandakan High Court on 26 September 1994 for trafficking 1,241 grams of cannabis found in a taxi in front of the general market in Sandakan on 31 January 1990. [@1] He was charged under section 39B(1)(a) of the Dangerous Drugs Act 1952, which carries a mandatory death sentence, and the Court of Appeal had affirmed the conviction and sentence on 3 June 2002. [@1]

His co-appellant Mohamed Hj Muslimin, whose death sentence was for a 1992 firearms offence against a policeman, also lost his appeal; that non-cannabis case is not covered here. [@1,2]
