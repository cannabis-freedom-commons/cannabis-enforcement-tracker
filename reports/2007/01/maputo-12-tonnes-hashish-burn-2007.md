Nine Pakistani citizens travelling aboard a vessel from Mombasa, Kenya were
detained and a year later tried and convicted of drug trafficking. [@1] In
December, an agent of the Rapid Intervention Force and senior local
law-and-order figures were tried and given heavy prison sentences over the
episode. [@1] They included the former director of the Criminal Investigation
Police and the former commander and logistics chief of the Rapid Intervention
Force. [@1]

The case began in July 2000 when a shipwreck washed drugs concealed in coffee
tins onto the coast of Inhambane in southern Mozambique, where authorities
collected them. [@1] Mozambique was finally destroying 12 tonnes of hashish
seized accidentally off the coast some years earlier. [@1] About a month before
the January 2007 report, the remaining hashish had been moved to Maputo for
immediate destruction. [@1] The operation was supervised and witnessed by
Inhambane provincial prosecutor Américo Mazenga, who told Radio Mozambique that
12.800 tonnes of hashish had been weighed. [@1] Mazenga said incinerating the
drugs in Maputo reflected past experience and ended the case of the drugs
recovered from the waters off Bazaruto in Vilankulo. [@1]

The seized hashish should have been destroyed earlier but stayed in storage,
allowing about three tonnes to disappear. [@1] Senior officials were implicated
in the disappearance of just over two tonnes of the seized drugs. [@1] The
disappearance of 2 tonnes led to the conviction of several police agents. [@1]
