A federal jury in San Francisco convicted marijuana advocate Ed Rosenthal on
30 May 2007 of three charges of illegal cultivation and conspiracy for growing
cannabis for medical patients, after a day of deliberations. [@1] Rosenthal, 62,
of Oakland was acquitted on a fourth charge while the jury hung on a fifth, and
he faced no punishment for the felony convictions apart from the one day in jail
already served. [@1]

An earlier jury had convicted him of similar charges in 2003, but an appeals
court overturned that verdict after a juror telephoned a lawyer for advice
during deliberations. [@1] For the retrial prosecutors added money-laundering
and tax charges, but Judge Charles Breyer dismissed them, finding the government
was retaliating for Rosenthal's criticism and his successful appeal. [@1]
Americans for Safe Access likewise records the March 2007 dismissal of the tax
and money-laundering charges and the retrial conviction with another one-day
time-served sentence. [@2]

Rosenthal had been arrested in February 2002 after federal agents seized more
than 3,700 marijuana plants at an Oakland warehouse, where he grew for supply
to Bay Area medical marijuana dispensaries. [@1] In both trials the judge barred
evidence that the marijuana was for medical use under Proposition 215, the 1996
state initiative, or that Oakland had deputized Rosenthal, so his lawyers called
no witnesses at the retrial. [@1] His lawyers said they would ask the judge to
throw out the convictions. [@1]
