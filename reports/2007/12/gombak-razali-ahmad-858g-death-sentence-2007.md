Courier company clerk Razali Ahmad, 33, of Gombak was sentenced to death by the High Court on 18 December 2007 for trafficking in cannabis.[@1] He was found guilty of trafficking 858 grams of cannabis found in his sling bag in a house in Jalan Dewan Gombak Setia, Gombak, at about 5.45am on 6 August 2003.[@1] Judicial Commissioner Abang Iskandar Abang Hashim ruled that the prosecution had proved its case, and the charge carried the mandatory death sentence.[@1]

The prosecution called eight witnesses while Razali was the only witness for the defence.[@1] He was represented by counsel James George while deputy public prosecutor Shoba Venu Gobal appeared for the prosecution.[@1]

## Later attention

Razali's mother Siti Zabidah Muhammad Rasyid said her son had been on death row for two decades after being caught with cannabis, and that a friend had forced him to carry the drugs before making him a scapegoat.[@2] She said a judge had rejected an earlier attempt by the family to appeal the sentence.[@2] After parliament passed reforms to scrap the mandatory death penalty, prisoners facing death or life imprisonment including those who had exhausted appeals could seek a sentencing review.[@2]
