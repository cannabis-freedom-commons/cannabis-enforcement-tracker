San Bernardino County sheriff's deputies shot and killed a camouflage-clad gunman
on Monday 10 September 2007 during a shootout with three people protecting a
marijuana crop in the mountains north of Fontana. [@1,2] A manhunt for the two
others continued into the night, with deputies searching the rugged terrain on
foot and in four-wheelers, until the search was called off with no arrests made.
[@1,2]

The shots were fired as deputies working with the United States Forest Service
were preparing to destroy a large marijuana field near Lytle Creek in the San
Gabriel Mountains, an area of steep hills and manzanita, sheriff's spokeswoman
Jodi Miller said. [@1,2] At least three people dressed in camouflage and armed
with automatic weapons were camping at the site and fired on the deputies as they
approached the field at about 7.30am, said Sgt. Rick Ells of the California
Highway Patrol. [@1,2] The deputies returned fire and fatally wounded one gunman,
who had climbed up a hill apparently to seek a better shooting position; he was
taken to a ranger station, where he was pronounced dead. [@1,2]

About 40 deputies aided by a helicopter searched for the two other gunmen, and
highway patrol officers closed Lytle Creek Road to aid the search. [@1,2] The
authorities estimated the field held about 2,500 marijuana plants with a street
value of $6 million. [@1,2] A Forest Service spokeswoman said marijuana had been
found in the Lytle Creek area before, but this was the first time deputies had
been shot at there. [@1,2]
