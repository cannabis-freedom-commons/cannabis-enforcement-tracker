On Tuesday 13 November 2007 Attorney General Jerry Brown announced a record haul of
illegal marijuana pulled from secluded fields in rural California: 2.9 million plants
eradicated from public parks and state lands, worth an estimated $11.6 billion.
[@1,2] The results were announced jointly by the state Justice Department, the US
attorney's office and the Drug Enforcement Administration. [@2]

In the Sacramento district the program produced 36 indictments, and US Attorney
McGregor Scott said prosecutors were moving beyond garden caretakers to the
trafficking hierarchy, which faces a minimum of ten years for producing more than
1,000 plants under federal law. [@2] Mid-season, the campaign had already reached
2.49 million plants with a few weeks still to go, after more than 475 raids and 27
arrests the year before. [@3]

## Raids push growers indoors and onto public land

Officials blamed large cartel-run grows for environmental damage on public land,
where guards stood watch heavily armed; Brown said hikers had heard gunshots and
agents found gang graffiti carved into trees. [@2] A National Drug Threat Assessment
released 8 November found indoor cultivation up 85 percent between 2000 and 2006, and
legalization advocates said eradication was pushing growers into suburban homes:
police in Elk Grove had unravelled two indoor networks, raiding 21 homes and seizing
14,000 plants valued at $56 million. [@2] Researcher Jon Gettman estimated the
state's 2006 outdoor crop at more than 17 million plants, meaning the record season
still took under a fifth of it. [@1]
