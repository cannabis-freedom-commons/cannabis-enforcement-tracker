Police and customs officers seized several tonnes of cannabis from premises in Newtownards, County
Down, on 10 October 2006, in what police called the largest drugs haul recorded in Northern
Ireland. [@1,3] Three tonnes of the drug, with an estimated street value of 18 million pounds,
were found during a joint operation by the Police Service of Northern Ireland and officers from
the Revenue and Customs Service, and two men were arrested. [@1] A police spokesman called it a
highly significant seizure and the largest cannabis seizure made in Northern Ireland. [@1]

The drugs were understood to have arrived as a single shipment brought to an industrial unit in
Newtownards, and police said a paramilitary link to the smuggling was not suspected. [@3] The
seizure was presented as part of the Organised Crime Task Force commitment to crack down on
illegal drugs. [@1,3]

## Charges and pleas

Martin Kerfoot, 40, of Bangor, later pleaded not guilty to importing 3.5 tonnes of cannabis said
to be worth 18 million pounds, as well as supplying cannabis and possessing it with intent to
supply. [@2] Stephen James McGivern, 32, of Sunderland, pleaded not guilty to possessing cannabis
with intent to supply. [@2] Both men had been arrested at the premises in October 2006, and the
resin was found in a removal-firm lorry packed with wooden doors and in a hire lorry at the
Quarry Heights warehouse. [@2] McGivern was remanded in custody while Kerfoot was released on
continuing bail. [@2]
