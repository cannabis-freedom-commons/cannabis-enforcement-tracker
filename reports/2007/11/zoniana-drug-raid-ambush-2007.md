On Monday 5 November 2007, gunmen with assault rifles opened fire on a convoy
of about 12 police jeeps outside Zoniana, a remote village in Rethymno
prefecture on Crete, as the officers drove in to search for a major hashish
stash. [@1] Three officers on the drug raid were injured, one left in critical
condition with a serious gunshot wound to the head and neck area while the
other two were slightly hurt. [@1,3] The raid had been set in motion by the
arrest of a 24-year-old Zoniana man on drug-trafficking charges in Rethymno
the previous Friday. [@1]

## Sweep of the village

At 7:15 on Wednesday 7 November, 75 heavily armed EKAM special-forces officers
descended on the village with local police, 130 officers in all, setting up
roadblocks and searching houses, farms, sheds and shops with sniffer dogs and
two helicopters overhead. [@2] The Supreme Court prosecutor had ordered a
preliminary investigation to bring the gunmen to justice, and 75 police
commandos were flown to Crete to join the operation. [@1] Officers detained
more than 25 suspects for weapons, explosives and small quantities of drugs,
and confiscated weapons and ammunition, though no quantities were announced
and the search for hashish plantations in the wider Mylopotamos area
continued. [@2] The seriously wounded special guard remained in intensive care
in critical but stable condition, and opposition leader George Papandreou
visited the injured officer at the hospital and expressed support to his
family. [@3]
