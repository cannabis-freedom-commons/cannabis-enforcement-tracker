Baltimore County police shot and killed Cheryl Noel during a January 2005 raid on her home. [@1,2,3,4,5]

Just before 5 a.m. on Friday 21 January 2005, officers of the Baltimore County
Police tactical unit served a search-and-seizure warrant tied to a narcotics
investigation at a home in the 8100 block of Del Haven Road in the Gray Haven
area. [@4] The officers had obtained the warrant after finding evidence of
personal drug use in the trash cans behind the Noel home, and they chose a
no-knock entry because some residents had criminal histories and two owned
registered handguns. [@4,5]

After breaking down the front door and detonating a flash-bang grenade, seven
officers entered the house and four ran upstairs to the bedroom where Cheryl
Noel, 44, and her husband Charles were sleeping. [@5] Hearing the commotion,
Cheryl Noel grabbed her handgun and stood up out of bed, holding it pointed
slightly downward at waist level as two officers kicked open the bedroom
door. [@5] The officers said they had yelled "Police, police, police" throughout
the raid as procedure required. [@4]

Fearing for his life, Officer Carlos Artson fired three shots, striking and
killing Noel. [@4] Artson first fired twice as she stood by the bed, then fired
a third shot into her chest after she moved her hand toward the gun lying about
eight inches away; the coroner found that only the third shot was immediately
fatal. [@5] The officer was placed on administrative leave pending investigation
by the county's homicide and internal affairs divisions, and police did not
name him at the time. [@4]

On the same day, police charged Charles Noel, 51, with two counts of possessing
more than five pounds of black powder along with single counts of possessing
marijuana and drug paraphernalia. [@4] Nineteen-year-olds Matthew Noel and
Sarah Betz were each charged with possessing drug paraphernalia and possessing
marijuana, and all three were later released on their own recognizance. [@4]
Neighbours and a co-worker described Noel as a religious, hard-working woman
and said her alleged actions with the gun did not fit her character, with one
suggesting she had come to the door thinking someone was breaking into her
house. [@4]

Her family filed a civil rights lawsuit in 2006, and weeks later the department gave [@1] Artson its Silver Star for his actions during the raid. [@1]

In March 2009 a federal jury returned [@1,2] a verdict for the police, and a federal appeals court upheld it in 2011. [@1,2]
