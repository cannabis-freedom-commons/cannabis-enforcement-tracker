Provincial criminal-investigation police in Namibe detained 123 people — 117 men
and six women aged 14 to 35 — as alleged traffickers, sellers and consumers in
91 cleared drug cases. [@1] The six women were already facing justice for
involvement with light and heavy drugs in the province. [@1] Police sent 80 of
the cases to the Public Prosecution Service for further judicial proceedings.
[@1] During a work visit on 25 June, Deputy Attorney General Pascoal António
Joaquim spoke with women held in the Namibe comarca, the district jail. [@1] He
said he had seen three or four women held there because they had sold liamba,
the local name for cannabis, which he described as a prohibited narcotic and not
tobacco. [@1]

As a result of the detentions, police reported seizing 257 kilograms of liamba,
along with 206 grams of crack, 330,000 kwanzas and 5,100 U.S. dollars said to
come from sales of heavy and light drugs. [@1] On the previous Sunday, officers
had destroyed 257 kilograms of liamba and 206 grams of crack derived from
cocaine. [@1] The seizure and destruction were presented during commemorations
of the International Day Against Drug Abuse, when police also displayed cash
described as proceeds of illicit drug buying and selling. [@1]

The results cover the work of the DPIC-Namibe, the Provincial Directorate of
Criminal Investigation, from July 2010 to June 2011, with 48 more cases than the
previous period. [@1] The destruction took place at the directorate's premises
in Namibe in the presence of its deputy director, Superintendent Eduardo Luís,
and other provincial police officers. [@1] Provincial anti-drug chief
Inspector-Chief João Domingos Ana said the fight against drugs must be a joint
task for churches, civil society, traditional authorities and families. [@1]
