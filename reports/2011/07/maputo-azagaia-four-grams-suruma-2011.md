Mozambican rapper Azagaia was detained on Saturday 30 July 2011, the day he was
to present the video "A Minha Geração" from his new album Aza-leaks, Lusa
reported. [@2] Azagaia was held by the Polícia da República de Moçambique hours
before he was to launch one of his albums and was released to answer to the case
at liberty. [@1]

The city police command's spokesman, Arnaldo Chefo, said officers intercepted
the rapper with four grams of suruma, the local name for cannabis sativa, in his
car during the weekend. [@2] The rapper was accused of possessing and consuming
narcotics after a Polícia de Investigação Criminal team allegedly found four
grams of cannabis sativa in his vehicle. [@1] VOA Português reported the find as
three small balls of suruma weighing about three to four grams. [@1]

Azagaia spent about 48 hours in police custody, first at the 12th PRM station
and then at the 6th station, where he was transferred for the weekend. [@1] He
was released on an identity and residence term on Monday 1 August, meaning he
would answer to the case at liberty, his lawyer Salvador Kamate confirmed. [@2]
VOA Português reported on 2 August that he had been released and would stand
trial at liberty. [@1]

Azagaia denied the police account, and Lusa reported that the detention drew a
wave of reactions from fans, some accusing officers of forging evidence. [@2] In
a statement the same day he thanked his supporters and said he had slept two
nights on the floor. [@2] Global Voices described him as one of Mozambique's
brightest and most polemical hip-hop musicians, followed by the authorities
since he began using his songs for social intervention. [@3]
