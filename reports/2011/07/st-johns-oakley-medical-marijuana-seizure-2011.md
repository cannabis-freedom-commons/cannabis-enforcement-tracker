Richard Oakley, a St. John's man who tested positive for HIV 25 years ago, had $1,500 worth of medical marijuana confiscated when he went to collect a package at Purolator and was met by the RCMP instead. [@1,2] Oakley had moved back from British Columbia three months earlier; his first package from his designated B.C. grower arrived without trouble, but the second, marijuana and marijuana-laced chocolates, was held after Purolator told him to come back Monday. [@1,2]

Oakley holds a Health Canada authorization for medical marijuana, which the RCMP said it believed was properly obtained; police were checking whether his supplier was registered under federal guidelines before deciding whether to hand the package over. [@1,2] No charges were being considered, RCMP spokesman Sgt. Boyd Merrill said. [@1,2] Purolator said it cannot tell legal medical packages from illegal ones and must notify police about suspicious or damaged parcels. [@1,2]

Oakley said the cannabis eases nausea from his HIV drug cocktail, pain and neuropathy in his feet, and that without it he could not keep his pills down. [@1,2]
