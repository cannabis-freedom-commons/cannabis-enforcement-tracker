Federal and El Cajon authorities arrested about 60 people tied to an
Iraqi-community trafficking ring after a months-long investigation centered in
El Cajon, east of San Diego. [@1] Many suspects were Iraqi Chaldeans, and police
said at least some were suspected of affiliation with the Detroit-based Chaldean
Organized Crime Syndicate. [@1] Investigators said the suspects operated out of
an Iraqi social club in El Cajon and shipped Sinaloa-cartel-supplied drugs to
Detroit. [@1] Four federal indictments unsealed on 18 August 2011 charged nine
people with federal narcotics and weapons trafficking and unlawful possession of
firearms and explosives. [@1] One defendant, Nofel Noel Suleyman, was also
charged with engaging in a continuing criminal enterprise, which carries a
20-year mandatory minimum. [@4] About half of the 60 arrested faced federal
charges and half faced state charges. [@1] Officials said the investigation was
continuing and more arrests were possible. [@1]

Authorities seized more than 3,500 pounds of marijuana that officials attributed
to the Sinaloa cartel. [@1] Investigators also seized more than $630,000 in
cash, three luxury cars, 34 firearms and four improvised explosive devices. [@2]
In April, a DEA undercover operative was shown a hand grenade and told
additional grenades were available from a Mexican military source. [@1]

El Cajon police said neighbours and club members' spouses had complained for
years about attempted murder, meth and marijuana sales, gambling and illegal gun
sales at the club. [@1] Officials said the weapons and explosives were meant for
local sale, with no indication the ring was supplying the cartel's war in
Mexico. [@1] The DEA said Sinaloa operatives supplied the El Cajon traffickers
with large amounts of marijuana that sold in Detroit for up to three times its
purchase value. [@3]

The club's managers were allegedly aware of the criminal activity and demanded a cut of the money. [@5]
