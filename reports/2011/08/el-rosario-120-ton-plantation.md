On 10 August 2011, a federal judge in Baja California ordered 57 farmworkers held for trial on drug charges after the army found a 120-hectare cannabis plantation near El Rosario, in the municipality of Ensenada. [@8] The workers had been transferred to El Hongo prison in Tecate. [@7] Authorities detained 58 people in connection with the operation and released one because he was a minor. [@1,6,7]

## The discovery

Soldiers found the field on 12 July in the desert south of Ensenada. [@1,3,8,9] Black shade cloth concealed the plants, and the plot had a well and irrigation hoses. [@2,3,6] Workers fled when the soldiers approached. [@1,3,5] Reuters initially reported six checkpoint arrests, while later accounts reported 58 people detained. [@1,5,6,7] The army said 16 of the detainees were workers from Sinaloa. [@9]

Brigadier General Alfonso Duarte Mújica estimated the crop could have yielded 120 tonnes worth about US$160 million. [@1,3,5] The army called it Mexico’s largest known cannabis plantation and later attributed it to Joaquín Guzmán and the Sinaloa cartel. [@1,4,5,9] Soldiers burned the crop, and President Felipe Calderón congratulated the defence ministry on the operation. [@1,4,6]
