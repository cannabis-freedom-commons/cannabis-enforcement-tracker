A jury in Bermuda's Supreme Court found Mary Johnson, 53, of Edgeway Lane,
Pembroke, guilty of conspiring to import millions of dollars' worth of cannabis
into Bermuda. [@1] US drug enforcement agents detained her at Newark Liberty
International Airport on 21 October 2010, just after she boarded her return
flight to the island. [@1] The 49kg of wrapped cannabis bricks hidden in her
luggage could have sold for $2.4 million on Bermuda's streets. [@1] The cannabis
was in the false bottoms of four pieces of luggage delivered to her Newark hotel
room the night before her flight home. [@1]

Johnson told the jury she never knew what the bags contained and feared being
targeted by gangsters if she did not bring the "illegal" luggage back from the
United States. [@1] She said she accepted $2,570 in cash from a Bermudian drug
dealer known as "KJ", of the Deepdale area of Pembroke, who booked her trip and
paid for her air ticket. [@1] She told the jury two Bermudians, a man described
as a "Deepdale man" and a woman, delivered the bags to her hotel, and that she
felt threatened when the man photographed her with them. [@1]

Prosecutor Larissa Burgess told the jury in closing that the first question
Johnson asked the US special agent who detained her was whether she would be
arrested before the bags were searched. [@1] Defence lawyer Larry Mussenden
argued the Crown had not proved a conspiracy and that Johnson should be
acquitted if the jury found she acted under duress. [@1] The jury deliberated
for less than an hour before returning its verdict. [@1] Johnson whispered "I
told you, they'll kill me" before being led from the dock, and was remanded in
custody until sentencing in November. [@1]
