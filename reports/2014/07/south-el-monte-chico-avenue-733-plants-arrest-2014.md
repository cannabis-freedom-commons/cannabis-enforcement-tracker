Covina police arrested two people on Thursday, 24 July 2014, after finding more
than 700 marijuana plants growing inside a vacant business in South El Monte.
[@1] George Almaguer, 26, of Pico Rivera and Jenny Ly, 31, of Rosemead were
arrested at the scene, police said. [@1] Both were booked on suspicion of
cultivating marijuana and theft of electricity. [@1]

Covina police Sergeant Jim McDonough said officers served a search warrant at
about 1 p.m. at the secluded, seemingly vacant business in the 2600 block of
Chico Avenue in South El Monte. [@1] The operation used three rooms for
different stages of growth, and police seized 733 pot plants from the building.
[@1] The sergeant said the hydroponic operation was wired to bypass the
electrical meter and draw power directly from the Southern California Edison
grid. [@1] Police estimated the grow had stolen at least $10,000 worth of
electricity from the utility over the past year. [@1] Investigators seized about
$2,000 in suspected drug money that Almaguer was carrying, McDonough said. [@1]

The building sat at the rear of a lot secured with a padlocked gate. [@1] Police
said Thursday's arrests were related to another investigation in which a grow
house was found in Covina on 26 May 2014. [@1] In that earlier case, officers
called to downed power lines smelled marijuana from a house in the 200 block of
North Larkin Drive and found more than 200 plants. [@1] That investigation led
to the South El Monte search, though McDonough declined to say how detectives
identified the address, and the investigation was ongoing. [@1] A suspect had
been identified in the Larkin Drive grow house, but no arrests had been made in
that case, McDonough said. [@1]
