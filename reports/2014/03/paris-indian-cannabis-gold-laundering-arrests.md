French police broke up a trafficking network that turned cannabis from Morocco
sold in France into gold resold in India. [@1] Ten people were arrested in Paris
and three others in Belgium. [@2] At its centre was Sayed, a 32-year-old Indian
man who lived an unremarkable life with a child in a modest flat in Tremblay,
north of Paris. [@1] Behind closed doors he allegedly ran companies based in
France, Morocco, Dubai, Belgium and India that collected and laundelled the drug
money. [@1]

The proceeds were sent to Belgium and converted into gold bracelets or fine gold
powder mixed into ground coffee. [@1] Accomplices then recruited students as
couriers to carry the gold on express weekend trips to Dubai. [@1] In Dubai the
gold was made into jewellery and sent to India for sale, with the profits shared
between the French and Indian launderers and a Moroccan organiser. [@2]

The 32-year-old Indian man who led the organisation in Paris admitted laundering
36 million euros since 2010 and sending 200 kg of gold from Belgium to India.
[@2] In custody Sayed admitted four years of illegal activity amounting to 200
kg of gold sent to the Indian market, worth 25 million euros in cash. [@1] The
network as a whole was said to collect about 170 million euros per year. [@2]
Investigators described it as one of the biggest money-laundering operations
ever uncovered in France. [@1]
