Spanish Customs Surveillance boarded the 24-metre Portuguese-flagged fishing vessel
Eiskos about 40 nautical miles south of Almería on Sunday 23 November 2014 and seized
about 20 tons of hashish from its hold. [@1,2] The boarding party worked from the Navy
patrol vessel Cazadora with Portuguese authorisation and found the hold full of bales
of the kind typically used to traffic hashish. [@1] As officers boarded, the crew set
fire to a can of petrol in an attempt to burn the boat and destroy the drugs; officers
put the fire out, but two crew members were injured in the blast and evacuated by
Maritime Rescue for hospital treatment. [@1,2]

Ten crew members were arrested: five Portuguese, two Senegalese, one Spaniard, one
Guinean and one Ghanaian. [@1] The first official estimate put the haul at 10,000 to
15,000 kilos before weighing; the Portuguese Judicial Police later put it at about
20 tons. [@1,2]

The seizure was the sea end of Operation Adiada, a months-long investigation by
Portugal's Judicial Police and DCIAP prosecutors with Spain's Tax Agency, Civil Guard
and National Police, supported by CITCO, MAOC-N and the Spanish Navy and Air Force.
[@1,2] The alleged leader of the network was arrested later alongside eleven searches,
in which officers also seized a 50-metre refrigeration ship meant for future
transports and two cars. [@2] The Tax Agency said Adiada was its eighth large hashish
seizure on the Eastern Mediterranean route since June 2013, passing 100 tons in
total. [@1]
