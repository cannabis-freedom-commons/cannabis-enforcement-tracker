Moses Adejumo, a 23-year-old Canadian university student, was sentenced to eight
months in prison after admitting importing cannabis worth about $40,000 into
Bermuda. [@2] He arrived on an Air Canada flight from Toronto in May 2014, and
customs officers sent him for a secondary inspection at LF Wade International
Airport. [@2] In his single black suitcase they found two brown packages hidden
under the cloth lining, which analysis showed held 885 grams of cannabis. [@2]

Bermuda customs charged him with importing more than $40,000 worth of cannabis.
[@1] When he first appeared in Bermuda's Magistrates' Court on 22 May, Adejumo
denied importing cannabis and possessing it with intent to supply. [@1] Senior
Magistrate Archibald Warner remanded him in custody and set a trial date for 5
June. [@1] The charge set the drugs' street value at $44,000. [@1]

On 5 June Adejumo pleaded guilty to the importation charge, and prosecutor
Takiyah Burgess entered no evidence on the possession charge. [@2] Defence
lawyer Arion Mapp said Adejumo was a vulnerable young man who had been offered
the opportunity to bring the drugs in by an unnamed person. [@2] Mapp asked for
a psychiatric report before sentence, but Warner refused it, saying "at the end
of the day it is blatant importation". [@2]

Warner sentenced him to eight months' imprisonment, taking into account the time
he had already spent in custody. [@3] The court heard that the cannabis had an
estimated street value of $44,250. [@3] Adejumo, who did not speak during the
proceedings, never revealed who he imported the drugs for, and his mother and
aunt flew to Bermuda from Nigeria for the hearing. [@3] Warner jailed him for
eight months, taking his time in custody into account. [@4]
