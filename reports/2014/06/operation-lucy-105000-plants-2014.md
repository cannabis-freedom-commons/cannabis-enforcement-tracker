New Zealand Police announced on 30 June 2014 the results of Operation Lucy, that year's
national cannabis operation: about 105,000 plants destroyed, 112 firearms seized and
640 suspects arrested. [@1,2] Detective Senior Sergeant Scott McGill said destroying
more than 100,000 plants before harvest had prevented significant social damage,
calling the cannabis trade a destructive force in New Zealand society. [@1]

Northland accounted for the largest share with 46,224 plants, followed by Bay of Plenty
with 16,620 and Eastern District with 14,657; the remaining districts each yielded
between about 1,400 and 8,300 plants. [@1] Sixty-four of those arrested were patched
gang members or associates, which McGill said showed the strong connection between
organised crime and the cannabis trade. [@1,2]

Officers also recovered an estimated $195,000 of stolen property and shut down three
clandestine drug labs, two in Northland and one in Eastern District. [@1,2] Police said
the labs showed organised groups often manufactured and supplied a range of illegal
drugs, and asked the public to keep reporting suspicious drug activity. [@1]
