Errol Wallace, a 49-year-old Jamaican, was sentenced to six years in prison
after admitting that he grew more than 30,000 marijuana plants for sale in East
Grand Bahama. [@1] His alleged accomplice, 38-year-old Daron Anderson of
Freeport, pleaded not guilty to a single charge of conspiracy to possess
dangerous drugs with intent to supply and was to stand trial on July 8. [@1] The
two appeared before Magistrate Andrew Forbes in Court Eight in New Providence.
[@2]

Wallace pleaded guilty to conspiracy to possess dangerous drugs with intent to
supply and four other charges: cultivation of dangerous drugs, possession of
dangerous drugs with intent to supply, possession of a prohibited weapon and
possession of ammunition. [@1] The charges stem from a marijuana field found in
East Grand Bahama on May 31, 2014. [@1] The prosecutor told the court that
officers acting on information went to the Bell Channel Inn around 5.25am on May
31, questioned Wallace and, on his directions, went to a site in East Grand
Bahama where they found the suspected marijuana field. [@1] Inside a tent on the
property, officers found $350 in cash, 16 bullets, a Kel-Tec semi-automatic
pistol and a passport belonging to Wallace, the court heard. [@1]

Wallace was convicted of all five charges and given two years for conspiracy,
cultivation and possession of ammunition, five years for possession of dangerous
drugs with intent to supply and six years for the prohibited weapon. [@1] The
sentences were to run concurrently, and Magistrate Forbes told Wallace he could
appeal to the Court of Appeal. [@1]

On June 5, 2014, the day after Wallace was sentenced, the Tribune reported that
both men had been charged and had pleaded not guilty, and that they were
remanded to Her Majesty's Prison until July 30 for trial. [@2]
