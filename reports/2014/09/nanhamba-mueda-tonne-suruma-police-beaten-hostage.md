Two policemen were severely beaten and one was taken prisoner after a drug raid
in Cabo Delgado province. [@1] Police raiding homes in Nanhamba, in Mueda
district, confiscated about a tonne of cannabis, known locally as suruma, in 52
sacks. [@1] The raid took place on a Friday and the ambush followed two days
later. [@1]

Villagers reacted angrily to the raid and there was a shootout, which Cabo
Delgado police spokesperson Abdul Chaguro blamed on three villagers he said were
responsible for growing the cannabis. [@1] The villagers beat up two policemen
and seized a police pistol; one officer escaped and the other was held in a hut
for many hours until colleagues found him alive. [@1] The villagers made the
officers' release conditional on the return of the suruma. [@2]

Both officers were treated at the hospital in the provincial capital, Pemba, and
the officer who had been held prisoner was transferred to the central hospital
in Nampula province, where he was under intensive care. [@1] The cannabis had
been seized by PRM officers attached to the administrative post of Ngapa. [@2]
Chaguro said the men responsible for the kidnapping and beating were on the run
and would be caught and held responsible. [@1] Police said they were working
with local community leaders to calm villagers and pursue only those responsible
for cultivating the cannabis. [@1]

Villagers said the police operation had been violent and had injured several
people, including children. [@2] On the Mueda plateau the drug law is widely
disregarded because cannabis is grown as a cash crop. [@1] Cannabis grows
readily in Mozambique, but possessing and smoking it are offences under national
drug legislation. [@1]
