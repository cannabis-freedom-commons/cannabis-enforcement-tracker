Tampa police sent a SWAT team to the Seminole Heights home that Jason Westcott, 29, shared with
his boyfriend Izzy Reyes on the night of 27 May 2014 to execute a search warrant for
marijuana. [@1,2] Officers entered through an unlocked door after knocks went unanswered and
pulled Reyes from the couch, shouting that they had a search warrant. [@2] Westcott, who was in
his bedroom, picked up his pistol and moved toward the hallway; two SWAT members fired five
rounds, killing him. [@1,2] He died later at a hospital after treatment by a SWAT medic. [@2]

The search found 0.2 grams of marijuana, a fraction of the pound the police informant had
claimed to see. [@1,2] The informant, 50-year-old felon and addict Ronnie "Bodie" Coogle, had
made buys totalling under $200 over four months and later told the Tampa Bay Times he invented
the claims that Westcott carried a gun during deals and had a heroin connection. [@1] A
detective's report that Westcott carried his pistol during every drug deal turned it into an
armed-trafficking flag that triggered the use of the SWAT team. [@1] Westcott had armed himself
after a guest threatened to rob and kill him months earlier, and a responding officer had told
him to grab his gun and shoot to kill if anyone broke in, his family said. [@2]

Police first said undercover officers made the buys, then admitted the case rested on Coogle;
Chief Jane Castor vouched for his credibility in July and reversed herself after he went to the
FBI. [@1] An internal investigation cleared the officers, and Castor said the team acted within
its training; the department maintains Westcott aimed a loaded firearm at officers. [@2] The
Times investigations found the raid was built on the informant rather than neighbour
complaints. [@3,4]
