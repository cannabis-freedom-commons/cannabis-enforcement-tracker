Nkhotakota police arrested two Malawi Defence Force private soldiers, Grey
Chimbulumata, 26, of Parachute Battalion and Mike Jusab, 25, of Mafco Barracks,
at the Kaombe police roadblock. [@1] Police said the two came to the roadblock
looking for a lift to Salima, grew suspicious of their movements and found the
drug in their travelling bags; each of their two bags held some. [@2] Malawi24
reported that the soldiers were stopped at the roadblock on the night of
Saturday 20 December 2014. [@2]

In a separate case at the same roadblock two days earlier, police arrested three
women, Milliam Chafera, 30, Cecelia Nkolimbo, 20, and Maria Winstone, 22, all
from Mphandazaola village in Ntcheu. [@1] The women were travelling in a Toyota
Passo driven by Emmanuel Chiwaya, and their travelling bags were found to
contain Indian hemp totalling 32 kilograms. [@1] The driver, in transit from
Tanzania to Blantyre, said his passengers boarded at Dwangwa; the women could
not produce a licence and were arrested at once. [@1] Police charged all five
with possessing cannabis sativa without a licence. [@1]

The soldiers were taken before the First Grade Magistrate Court in Nkhotakota,
pleaded guilty and were each fined K40,000 or, in default, two years'
imprisonment with hard labour. [@3] State prosecutor sergeant Ngupasye Muyira
told the court the two were arrested at the Kaombe checkpoint on 20 December
2014 with cannabis and no licence. [@3] In mitigation the soldiers said they
supported their families and paid school fees for orphans they look after, and
in sentencing Magistrate Fred Juma Chilowetsa called the offence serious and
Indian hemp cases common in Nkhotakota. [@3]
