Wellington police have broken up a commercial cannabis growing and dealing
network across the lower North Island, arresting eleven people aged from 20 to
68. [@1] Under Operation Yesterday, officers executed fourteen search warrants
in Wellington, Porirua, Manukau, Peka Peka and Whanganui. [@1] Those arrested
face charges including cultivating cannabis and possessing and selling it for
supply, and were due in courts over the following days. [@1]

Officers found cannabis growing at five addresses, including a whole house in
Whanganui set up solely for cultivation with more than 200 plants. [@1] They
also searched an industrial-sized shed in Whanganui bought by the group and
being fitted out for growing, and seized large amounts of dried cannabis and
cash at other addresses. [@1] Police said the network had sold hundreds of
thousands of dollars of cannabis over several years. [@1]

Police said the group imported cannabis seeds from Europe and LED grow lights
from China, and would work with Customs to block such imports. [@1] One alleged
ringleader was also arrested on old warrants over serious family-violence
charges from two Wellington home invasions nearly ten years earlier, having
used false identities to avoid arrest. [@1] Detective Senior Sergeant Tim
Leitch, who led the drug squad operation, warned commercial growers that police
would keep targeting the trade. [@1]
