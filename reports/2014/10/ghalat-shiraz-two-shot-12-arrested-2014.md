On 27 October 2014 two local youths were smoking marijuana outside in Ghalat
when local basijis confronted them and sprayed them with pepper spray. [@1] A
fight broke out and other villagers came to defend the youths, and the basiji
called Shiraz police for backup. [@1] The basiji shot and injured two youths.
[@1] Later that day villagers went to protest outside Shiraz’s police station,
where clashes again broke out. [@1] In the end 12 Ghalat residents were
arrested. [@1] The basiji who shot the youths left the village. [@1] That
account listed the accusations as actions against national security, attacking a
police post and forming a mafia group to disrupt security. [@2]

The village, which lies in a mountainous area near Shiraz in Fars province,
became known across Iran as a cannabis-growing and -selling place and was
nicknamed “Little Amsterdam”. [@1] Farmers there turned to planting marijuana
after a decade of serious droughts made wine production difficult, and growers
hid the plants in the woods. [@1] Marijuana from the village sold for about
3,000 euros a kilogram, against about 5,500 euros for marijuana brought in from
outside Iran. [@1] A Ghalat resident said the cultivated land could not be
measured precisely because the plots were scattered but estimated it at about 2
to 5 hectares, with more than 500 kilograms of dried cannabis a year. [@2]

Cannabis is illegal in Iran, where possession of any amount carries at least 50
lashes and a fine, with prison terms and, for repeat offences, the death penalty
for larger quantities. [@1] Residents also allege that arrested people are
quickly released and that police take bribes from dealers, a claim France 24
said it could not verify and for which it received no police reply. [@1]
