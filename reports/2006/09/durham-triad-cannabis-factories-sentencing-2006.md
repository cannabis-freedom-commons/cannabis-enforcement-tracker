Eight Vietnamese people who ran three large cannabis factories in County
Durham, believed to be linked to London-based triads, were jailed at
Newcastle Crown Court on 29 September 2006. [@1,2] Each factory had the
potential to yield crops worth £200,000 a year. [@1,2]

Police were alerted by a member of the public reporting suspicious activity
at a house in Murphy Crescent on the Woodhouse Close estate in Bishop
Auckland, which officers raided on 10 September 2005 to find a sophisticated
cultivation operation. [@1,2] The house was jointly owned by Thai Thi Vu, 45,
of Collingwood Street, Coundon, and Than Van Liu, 42, of Brancepeth Road,
Ferryhill, who had bought it that March intending to convert it into a
cannabis farm worked by Hai Van Tran, a 55-year-old illegal-immigrant
sailor. [@1,2] Within a week police found almost identical factories at Haig
Terrace in Ferryhill and High Street in West Cornforth, plus more than 400
seedlings and 1.8 kilos of cannabis at Thai Thi Vu's home with her husband
Quang Xuan Vu, 45. [@1,2] Thien Van Nguyen, 24, had worked as a driver's mate
delivering equipment, 17-year-old Nam Nguyen as a gardener repaying a family
debt, and forensic evidence led police to a ninth man, Phuc Danh San, 47, of
Eden Close, Coundon, with growing equipment in his garage. [@1,2]

Judge Michael Cartlidge split the group into administrators, who bought cheap
houses to convert, and gardeners employed to tend the crops, warning that
more important persons might still be arrested. [@1,2] Thai Thi Vu, Kuay Khin
Dip, 47, and Michael Quan, 43, each received six and a half years, Liu five
and a half years, and Quang Xuan Vu five years in his absence. [@1,2] Tran
received 24 months and Thien Van Nguyen 15 months, while Nam Nguyen received
four months in a young offenders' institution; the three gardeners were
expected to be deported. [@1,2]
