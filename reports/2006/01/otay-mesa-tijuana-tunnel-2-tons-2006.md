Acting on tips, Mexican authorities found the entrance of a 2,400-foot tunnel
running from a small warehouse near Tijuana airport to a large warehouse in
Otay Mesa, San Diego, and agents entered the passage on Wednesday 25 January
2006. [@1,2] US agents had hunted the tunnel for over a year with classified
military detection equipment without finding it. [@1]

The tunnel ran nearly half a mile through sandstone and compacted sand,
fitted with lighting, ventilation and groundwater drainage, with chest-high
water at its deepest point more than nine stories down. [@1] Investigators
called it the longest cross-border tunnel in US history and the work of a
well-funded smuggling group. [@1] The head of the San Diego DEA office named
the Arellano-Felix Tijuana cartel as the usual suspects. [@1]

## Two tons seized, no arrests

Mexican agents seized two tons of marijuana on the Mexican side while their
US counterparts found 200 pounds at the Otay Mesa end. [@1,2] Neither side
reported any arrests. [@1] Agents said their work was just beginning, poring
over the site for fingerprints, DNA, phones and computers to link the builders
and operators. [@1] Activity at the 48,222-square-foot Otay Mesa warehouse had
stopped abruptly the previous October after busy months of trucks moving
through its twelve docks. [@1]
