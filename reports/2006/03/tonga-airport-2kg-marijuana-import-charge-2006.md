Pangi Puloka, 33, appeared in the Nuku'alofa Magistrate's Court on 15 March 2006
charged with importing two kilograms of marijuana into Tonga from Fiji. [@1]

The police prosecutor said Puloka arrived on an Air Pacific flight from Fiji on
4 February 2006 and left a suitcase containing the marijuana on the inward
carousel at Fua'amotu International Airport. [@2] Passengers on the flight were
searched as part of a border security exercise by Tongan customs officers. [@2]
Lee Degan, the head of Tongan customs, called the seizure a great success for
the Border Security operation, which had been launched two weeks before the
suitcase was found. [@2]

Puloka first appeared in court the previous week and pleaded not guilty to two
counts of importation and possession of marijuana. [@2] His counsel, Siosifa
Tu'utafaiva, applied for the case to be dismissed on the ground that there was
no case to answer. [@2] Police Magistrate Peau Pifeleti held that there was a
case to answer and directed that the case be heard in the Nuku'alofa Supreme
Court on 24 April. [@2] The magistrate found that Police Prosecutor Viliami
Tulua had given enough evidence to show that the defendant may have committed
the crime. [@2]

The prosecutor said Puloka was on the flight, and that the airline tag on the
abandoned suitcase, addressed from Fiji, resembled the tag on Puloka's own
luggage. [@2]
