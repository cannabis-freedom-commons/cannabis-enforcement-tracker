Toronto police went to the 13-storey highrise at 2600 Jane St with warrants for
five apartments and ended up raiding 22 units across nine floors of the 117-unit
building, finding about 6,000 marijuana plants worth more than $6 million and 30
pounds (about 13.6 kg) of dried cannabis worth about $45,000. [@1,2] Initial
reports spoke of two arrests, and by Friday three men faced charges: building
superintendent Daniel Wallace, Tat Thang Nguyen and Dinh Pham. [@1,3] Police
said they expected to make more arrests. [@1]

Detectives called it one of the largest grow operations in Toronto's history, an
extremely lucrative business growing high-quality marijuana under 1,000-watt
bulbs with heavy wiring, ventilation to hide the smell and hoses hooked to
shower heads for watering. [@1] Investigators said toxic fertilizer drained
through kitchen sinks into the sewers, and mould and moisture damage left the
apartments unlivable and raised health concerns for the rest of the
building. [@1,2]

## Aftermath for tenants

About 200 residents attended a Saturday meeting organised by city councillor
Giorgio Mammoliti to ask about the health risks, after a mould inspector who
saw police videotape of the grow-ops described walls with visible mould growth
up to five feet high. [@2] Clean-up was estimated at $250,000 to $500,000, and
Mammoliti pressed the management company to refund part of the tenants'
rent. [@2] Toronto Public Health told residents not to panic, saying sensitivity
to mould varies by person, while acknowledging hallways and distant units could
be affected by water leaks and airborne particles. [@2]
