Police across Kentucky cut and burned 557,276 marijuana plants in 2006, up
nearly 50,000 on the 2005 total and the most since 1995. [@1,2] Arrests rose
to 475 from 452 in 2005, and at an estimated $2,000 per pound per plant,
police said about $1 billion was kept from the illegal drug market. [@1]

The US Drug Enforcement Administration brought in several helicopters and an
aeroplane for six weeks over the summer so airborne spotters could find more
patches, Lt Ed Shemelya, head of the Kentucky State Police
marijuana-eradication program, told the Lexington Herald-Leader. [@1] The
statewide effort drew in local officers, state police, the National Guard,
the DEA, the US Forest Service and the Appalachia High Intensity Drug
Trafficking Area task force, using National Guard helicopters whose computers
mapped plot locations at the click of a cursor. [@1] It was 20 years since
state police and the Guard's first coordinated one-day eradication sweep in
1986, and growers had adapted by shrinking plots and scattering them through
woods and hills. [@1]
