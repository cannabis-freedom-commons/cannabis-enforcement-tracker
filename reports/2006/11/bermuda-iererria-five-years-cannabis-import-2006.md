Teeteta Iereria, a 26-year-old cruise-ship cleaner from Kiribati, was sentenced
to five years in jail for importing heroin and cannabis into Bermuda. [@1] She
is single, with two daughters aged five and seven, and had faced a maximum
sentence of life imprisonment. [@1] She pleaded guilty to importation of
cannabis and diamorphine. [@1]

On Friday 6 October, plain-clothes officers from the Narcotics Cruise Ship
Enforcement Team were on duty at Kings Wharf, Dockyard, as passengers and crew
left the cruise ship Norwegian Spirit. [@1] The ship had arrived in Bermuda that
morning, and one officer saw Iereria acting suspiciously as she disembarked.
[@1] Officers followed her into the Calypso clothing store in the Clock Tower
Mall and detained her for a search under the Misuse of Drugs Act. [@1] She ran
out of the mall, officers chased and caught her a short distance away, and she
struggled until they subdued her. [@1]

After her arrest she was taken to Somerset Police Station, where she said she
had no money, only drugs, and that she believed the packages around her waist
were weed. [@1] Officers found eight packages on her person, seven containing
cannabis and one containing heroin. [@1] The Crown said tests found 1,577 grams
of cannabis, with a street value of $78,850, and just over 60 grams of heroin,
with a street value of $232,220. [@1] The Royal Gazette reported that the
cannabis and heroin together were valued at more than $311,000. [@1]

Chief Justice Richard Ground sent her to prison after the court heard that she
had given assistance to the police. [@1] The Judicial Committee of the Privy
Council later recorded that her five-year sentence was reduced on appeal to two
years under section 27E of the Misuse of Drugs Act 1972, in recognition of her
testimony against a former co-defendant. [@2]
