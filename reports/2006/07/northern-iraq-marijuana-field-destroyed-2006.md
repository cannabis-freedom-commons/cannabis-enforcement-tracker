US troops and Iraqi police seized and destroyed a bumper crop of marijuana plants in northern Iraq in early July 2006, acting on a US military press release reported by Stars and Stripes. [@1] Soldiers from the 172nd Stryker Brigade Combat Team, responsible for most of northern Iraq, discovered the field at an undisclosed location. [@1]

The field held juvenile marijuana plants grown in a series of furrows, and the owner claimed he was growing sesame. [@1] Police put the value of the field at $2 million. [@1] The crop was cut down and destroyed, and the owner was arrested. [@1]

## A wartime first

Drug use and trafficking were rare under Saddam Hussein, but the chaos since the 2003 invasion increased drug use and made the country more attractive to smugglers, the account notes. [@1] Hamid Ghodse, head of the International Narcotics Control Board, told the BBC that weakened border controls turn countries into convenient transit points for traffickers as well as militants. [@1]
