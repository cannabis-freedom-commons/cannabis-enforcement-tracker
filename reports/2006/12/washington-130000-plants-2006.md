Drug-enforcement officials in Washington said they destroyed more than 130,000
marijuana plants in 2006 as part of a joint state and federal eradication
effort. [@1] That was nearly the same as the year before, when a record 135,000
plants were destroyed under the Washington Domestic Cannabis
Eradication/Suppression Program, the Drug Enforcement Administration said in a
news release. [@1]

The 2006 seizures led to more than 340 arrests and 190 weapons seizures. [@1]
Officials attributed the number of destroyed plants to the movement of drug
traffickers from Canada to Washington in an effort to avoid tightened border
security. [@1] "Washington state is susceptible to both outdoor marijuana
growers, who typically use and damage public lands to ply their illegal trade,
and previously Canadian-based indoor cultivators attempting to avoid cross-border
detection," said DEA special agent in charge Rodney Benson. [@1]

Agents said growers had dammed streams, clear-cut forest lands, used large
quantities of insecticide and killed wildlife to aid the outdoor growing
operations. [@1]
