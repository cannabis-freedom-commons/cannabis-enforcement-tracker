Australian Federal Police and customs officers seized almost three tons of hashish from two shipping containers at the Port of Melbourne on Tuesday 14 August 2001. [@1,2] A team of more than 50 officers arrested five men as they opened the suspect containers, ending a four-day stake-out that police called one of Australia's ten biggest cannabis hauls. [@1]

Officers had begun covert surveillance the previous Friday when the container ship Mol Waratah docked at Melbourne. [@1] The containers were loaded with marble floor tiles laid over dozens of parcels of hashish, and police said the cannabis had come from the Middle East without naming a specific country. [@1,2,3] Police conservatively estimated the wholesale value at $15 million ($7.8 million), or up to ten times that if sold in small amounts on city streets. [@1]

The five Melbourne men appeared briefly in Melbourne Magistrates Court on Tuesday, each charged with possessing a prohibited import and being knowingly concerned with a prohibited import. [@1] Those charges carry a maximum penalty of life in jail. [@1]

A Victorian County Court jury later convicted all five men of possessing the cannabis resin. [@3] Franco received nine years' imprisonment with a six-year non-parole period, while Cetrangolo, Ciampoli, Pratico and Crnac each received seven years and six months with five-year non-parole periods. [@3] On 20 December 2006 the Court of Appeal allowed their sentence appeals, cutting Franco's term to seven years with a four-year non-parole period and the other four men's terms to five years and six months with three-year non-parole periods. [@3,4] The sentencing review recorded that all five had played roles at the lowest level of the enterprise, with Franco slightly more culpable than the rest. [@3]
