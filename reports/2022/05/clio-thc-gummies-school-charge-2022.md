Melinda A. Gatica of Mt. Morris Township was arraigned on Wednesday, May 4,
2022, on one felony count of second-degree child abuse. [@2] She turned herself
in before the hearing and pleaded not guilty before Genesee County District
Court Judge William H. Crawford. [@2] She was taken into custody on Wednesday
after turning herself in. [@1] The judge set a $25,000 bond barring marijuana,
marijuana products and alcohol from her home and requiring random drug testing.
[@2]

According to Genesee County Prosecutor David Leyton, her 6-year-old child took
homemade THC gummies stored in a Life Savers package to Edgerton Elementary
School in Vienna Township and shared them with kindergarten classmates. [@1]
More than a dozen students fell ill on Friday, April 29. [@2] The school
building of more than 400 students was evacuated as a precaution. [@2] Four
kindergarten students were hospitalized; three were discharged the next day and
the fourth after two days, and all four were back at school. [@1] A later
account said five kindergarten students from one classroom were taken to Hurley
Medical Center in Flint. [@2]

Gatica purchased liquid THC in 2021 and made her own marijuana-infused gummy
edibles with up to 30 milligrams of THC per gummy, according to Sheriff Chris
Swanson. [@2] In announcing the charge, authorities said Gatica left the
marijuana edibles in a LifeSavers package on a shelf inside her refrigerator.
[@2] Leyton said making and possessing such edibles is legal in Michigan, but
that leaving them where a child could reach them was her error. [@2]
Second-degree child abuse is a felony punishable by up to 10 years in prison.
[@1] Her attorney said she understands the seriousness of the allegations and
wanted to answer to the court right away. [@2] A probable cause conference was
scheduled for Thursday, May 12. [@2]
