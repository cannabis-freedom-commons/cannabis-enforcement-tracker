Judiciary Police officers arrested a 27-year-old photography director surnamed
Sou and a 28-year-old photographer surnamed Sam, both local residents, on 18 May
2022. [@1] Officers stopped Sam at 5:40 p.m. as he collected a parcel at an
express pick-up point in the central district and found 377.1 grammes of
cannabis in boxes of chocolates and biscuits. [@1] A search of the studio found
seven grammes of cannabis and 9.92 grammes of psilocin and psilocybin, the
active substances of the magic mushrooms grown there. [@1] The police valued the
cannabis and mushrooms at about 300,000 patacas. [@1]

The Judiciary Police said the parcel had been sent from a country in Oceania
through Hong Kong after an intelligence exchange with Hong Kong Customs. [@2]
The same statement said the parcel held cannabis weighing around 337.1 grammes.
[@2] The two men told police they had bought the drugs online and had grown them
in the studio, which had operated for about a year. [@2]

The police sent both men to the Public Prosecutions Office, Macau's prosecution
service, on charges of illegal production of narcotic drugs, illegal trafficking
and improper possession of utensils or equipment. [@2] The Macau Post Daily
reported that the men were held on trafficking and drug abuse charges, while
police said Sou and Sam refused to cooperate and the case was still under
investigation. [@1]

Macau's Court of Final Appeal cut Sou's combined sentence to three years and six
months, treating the mushroom growing as the lesser offence of production and
trafficking. [@3] Macau's Court of First Instance convicted Sou of illegal
production of narcotic drugs over the mushroom growing and gave him a combined
seven-year prison term. [@3] The top court reasoned that Macau's daily-dose
reference table does not list psilocybin or psilocine, so police could not show
how much of the controlled substances the mushrooms contained. [@3]
