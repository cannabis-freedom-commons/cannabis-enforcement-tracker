Macau's Judiciary Police detained a 40-year-old local travel agent over a
cannabis parcel sent to Macau by post from abroad, after a joint investigation
with Hong Kong Customs. [@1] On 24 May 2022 officers intercepted the man at a
parcel pick-up point in the Central district as he came to collect the parcel.
[@2] A police spokeswoman identified him as a 40-year-old local man surnamed
Cheong who works in tourism. [@3]

The first reports described six packets of cannabis wrapped in tin foil weighing
140.45 grams, with a black-market value of about MOP148,000 (US$18,306). [@1]
Laboratory examination confirmed the parcel held 147.456 grams of marijuana
worth approximately MOP148,000. [@2] A local Chinese-language report also put
the parcel at six foil-wrapped packets of 140.45 grams. [@4] The
Chinese-language newspaper Zheng Bao said the cannabis was worth MOP147,000 and
that the suspect tested negative for drugs. [@5] Officers then searched his home
in Taipa and seized a marijuana grinder, a glass bottle with a bong and storage
containers. [@2] Police in Macau said grinders and smoking tools were found at
his residence in Taipa and the investigation would continue. [@1] Va Kio Daily
reported that the haul was found in one package and described six foil-wrapped
packets weighing about 147 grams. [@6]

The Judiciary Police said intelligence exchange with Hong Kong's Customs Drug
Investigation Bureau showed the parcel had been sent to Macau via Hong Kong from
an American country. [@2] The submitted account instead said the suspect
admitted buying the drugs online and sending them to Macau from the "Americas",
without police naming the country of origin. [@1] The Macau Post Daily reported
that during the joint investigation with Hong Kong police, officers found Cheong
had bought the drugs in South Africa and had them posted to Macau via Hong Kong.
[@3] Cheong told police he bought the marijuana abroad for personal use and had
been taking the drug for a year and a half. [@3] Macau Today News reported that
the suspect said he had bought cannabis online from overseas to relieve stress
because the pandemic had cut visitor numbers. [@7] He admitted buying the drugs
online, and the Chinese-language report says he refused to say whether he had
been dealing. [@4]

He was transferred to the Public Prosecutions Office on charges of illegally
trafficking narcotics, illegally consuming them and improperly possessing
utensils or equipment. [@2] Zheng Bao likewise reported that the man was sent to
the Public Prosecutions Office suspected of drug trafficking and drug use. [@5]
