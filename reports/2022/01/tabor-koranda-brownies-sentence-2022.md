Michael James Koranda, 46, of Tabor, South Dakota, was arrested on 5 January
2022 for possession of a controlled substance, a day after the Bon Homme County
Sheriff's Office began investigating reports of possible poisonings at the Tabor
Community Center. [@1] Bon Homme County dispatchers received several calls
around 8 p.m. on 4 January about possible poisoning at the Tabor Senior Center,
and every patient had been at a card game there earlier that day. [@2] The
deputy who wrote the affidavit concluded that all the patients were under the
influence of THC from a batch of brownies that Koranda's mother brought to the
community center. [@1] Koranda's mother told Sheriff Mark Maggs that her son had
baked a pan of brownies and she took them to the community center card game
without knowing they were made with THC. [@1]

At Tabor Elementary School on 5 January, Koranda admitted bringing one pound
(about 0.45 kg) of THC butter back to Tabor from Colorado, where recreational
marijuana is legal for adults. [@2] He said he used half a pound of the butter
for the brownies and went to bed, leaving his mother to take the rest to the
game unaware. [@2] He handed the remaining half pound of THC butter to
investigators, who placed it into evidence. [@2] He was released on $3,000 bond,
and the county clerk's office said he had not retained an attorney when the
arrest was reported. [@1] Koranda taught in the Bon Homme School District, which
still listed him on staff at the time of the arrest reports. [@2]

On 15 March 2022, Judge Cheryle Gering gave Koranda a suspended imposition of
sentence and two years of probation for the Class 5 felony of possessing a
controlled substance, which carried up to five years in prison and a $10,000
fine. [@3] She also sentenced him to 60 days in the county jail in three 20-day
periods starting 1 July, 1 September and 1 December, which could be waived if he
met court conditions. [@3] Koranda was ordered to pay $1,116.50 in fine and
court costs and $2,312.82 in restitution to victims, and to write a letter of
apology. [@3]

At sentencing Koranda said he was sorry and that so many people getting sick had
not been his intention. [@3]
