Two Venezuelan men were each sentenced to six years of hard labour for
trafficking marijuana when they appeared virtually before a Port of Spain
magistrate. [@1] Carlos Emilio Gomez Herrera and Carlos Alexis Salazar Barcelo
pleaded guilty to trafficking 14.3 kilogrammes of marijuana in June 2020 after
entering a plea deal with the Office of the Director of Public Prosecutions.
[@1] The two appeared virtually before magistrate Rehanna Hosein. [@1]

The two men were held by the Coast Guard on June 23, 2020, in the waters off
Point-a-Pierre. [@1] They were charged by PC Nandesh Nanan two days later. [@1]
The two men were in a pirogue stopped by the Coast Guard, which had eight fuel
kegs on board. [@1] The men, the boat and its contents were taken to Coast Guard
Headquarters at Staubles Bay, where police examined the contents of the kegs.
[@1] Fourteen marijuana packages were found when the second keg was cut open.
[@1]

One of the men, questioned with the help of an interpreter, allegedly told
police he had no knowledge of what was on the boat. [@1] Assistant DPP Nigel
Pilgrim told the court there were no mitigating factors, pointing out that the
drugs had entered Trinidad and Tobago through Venezuela. [@1] The men were
represented by attorney Adrian Thompson. [@1]
