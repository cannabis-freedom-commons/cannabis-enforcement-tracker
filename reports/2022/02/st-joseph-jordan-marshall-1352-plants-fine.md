Jordan Marshall, 29, of The Morne, St. Joseph, pleaded guilty to cultivating
1,352 cannabis plants and was ordered to pay $7,500. [@1] He appeared in court
with his brothers Shanly Marshall and Linson Marshall, who had been jointly
charged with him but pleaded not guilty. [@1] All three brothers were arrested
on 10 February 2022 when police executed a search warrant at the home where
Jordan lived with them. [@2] Jordan told the officers that all of the
cultivation was his, while his brothers made no reply. [@1] The prosecution
offered no evidence against Shanly and Linson, so their cases were dismissed.
[@1]

On 9 February 2022 Constable Lambert Hilaire of the Commonwealth of Dominica
Police Force obtained a warrant to search the premises where Jordan resided for
cannabis. [@1] At 4:30 a.m. the next day, Drug Squad and Task Force officers in
battle dress uniforms carrying pistols and rifles left police headquarters to
execute the warrant. [@1] In the first ground-floor room officers found an
indoor cannabis nursery with three wooden stands holding fifteen wired black
seedling trays partly covered with clear plastic. [@1] A second room held
another wired stand with eight trays of more apparent cannabis plants. [@1] More
cannabis was being cultivated on the northern side of the premises under lights
powered from the house via solar panels. [@1] Officers uprooted 130 plants at
the scene and took seven sample plants in a nylon bag. [@1] At the Drug Squad
office officers counted 1,222 plants in the seedling trays. [@1] Together with
the plants uprooted at the scene, the seizure totalled 1,352 plants. [@2]
Samples sent to the government analyst tested positive for cannabis. [@1] Police
put the amount of cannabis at 72.75 pounds when the brothers were formally
charged with cultivation. [@1] The defence disputed that weight, saying the
plants had never been weighed. [@1]

The defence said Jordan, a father of a 3-year-old and a 9-year-old, had no
previous convictions. [@1] His lawyer said earlier incidents had left him
disabled, with iron in his legs and recurring pain. [@1] She said his disability
prevented him from being gainfully employed. [@1] The magistrate said he had to
depart from the sentencing guideline because the prosecution had not presented
an accurate weight. [@1] In sentencing, the magistrate said he weighed Jordan's
guilty plea, age, family ties and the public interest. [@2] Jordan was fined
$7,500, payable as $1,500 by 30 June 2022, $2,000 by 31 December 2022 and $4,000
by 31 October 2023. [@1] If he misses any payment he faces six months in prison.
[@1] Cultivation of cannabis carries a penalty of a $100,000 fine, three times
the street value, or three months in prison. [@1]
