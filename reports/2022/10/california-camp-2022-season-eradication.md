On 11 October 2022 Attorney General Rob Bonta announced that California's Campaign Against Marijuana Planting (CAMP) had eradicated nearly a million illegally grown cannabis plants during the 2022 season, along with more than 200,000 pounds (about 90.7 tonnes) of processed cannabis. [@1,2] CAMP teams conducted 449 operations across 26 counties, recovering 184 weapons and removing nearly 67,000 pounds of cultivation infrastructure such as dams, water lines and toxic chemicals. [@1,2]

Mendocino County had the largest haul with 190,018 plants at 18 sites, followed by Riverside with 159,287 plants at 77 sites and San Bernardino with 138,815 plants at 41 sites. [@1,2] Teams also worked in Lake, Kern, Siskiyou, Trinity, Monterey and eighteen other counties, with Santa Barbara and Santa Cruz limited to reconnaissance. [@1]

## From seasonal raids to a year-round task force

Bonta said illegal grows undercut the state's legal market and damage public lands with banned pesticides such as carbofuran, and announced that the 13-week seasonal program would become the year-round Eradication and Prevention of Illicit Cannabis (EPIC) task force. [@1,2] The new task force pairs the justice department with federal land agencies to pursue civil and criminal cases over the environmental, economic and labor harms of unlicensed cultivation. [@1,2]
