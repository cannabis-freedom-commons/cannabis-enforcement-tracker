A 33-year-old citizen of Moldova acting in concert with other people tried to
bring almost 62 kg of hashish from the European Union into the Eurasian Economic
Union. [@1] He was driving a Nissan car that arrived from Poland and passed
through the Brest checkpoint where customs sent it for inspection with a
scanning complex. [@1]

The scan showed hidden attachments inside the fuel tank of the vehicle. [@1]
During the follow-up search customs officers removed 96 briquettes in plastic
packaging from the tank. [@1] A service dog indicated the presence of drugs in
the briquettes. [@1] Forensic experts of the Brest Region directorate of the
State Committee of Judicial Examinations concluded that the seized substance was
hashish. [@1]

Brest Customs opened a criminal case for large-scale illegal movement of
narcotics across the border, an offence punishable by restriction of liberty for
up to five years or imprisonment for five to ten years. [@1] The citizen was
detained while further operational-search and investigative measures continued.
[@1]

Customs described the load as the fourth large batch of drugs and psychotropic
substances taken out of circulation by Belarusian customs officers since August
of that year. [@1]
