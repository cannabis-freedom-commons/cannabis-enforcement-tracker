Craig Carrol, a 40-year-old businessman from Harcroft Meadow in Douglas, was
jailed for four years at the Court of General Gaol Delivery. [@2] Deemster
Graeme Cook imposed the term on the afternoon of 1 June 2022, with the BBC and
Isle of Man Today reporting the hearing that day. [@2] In court, Carrol pleaded
guilty to fraud by false representation and two counts of making articles for
use in fraud, Isle of Man Today reported. [@3] The BBC reported that his defence
advocate said he had panicked after learning his previous convictions could
block the application and had only used "Tipp-Ex and a photocopier" to alter the
documents. [@1]

In July 2021 he submitted the application forms and a business plan to the
Gambling Supervision Commission (GSC), which processes the island's medicinal
cannabis licences, through a company called Cannamann that listed him as a
director and his partner as the sole shareholder. [@1] Commission staff found
the copy of his passport had been altered to change his middle name and date of
birth, and that bank statements showing balances of £42,000 to £54,000 covered
an account actually holding between £2 and £102. [@1] The application was halted
and the police were called, and Carrol was arrested on 7 September 2021. [@4] In
August 2021, before the alteration was discovered, Carrol had returned to the
commission's office and signed a document confirming that all the information he
had supplied was true. [@1] The investigation also found he had changed his
electoral register details to an address in Ramsey where he did not live to hide
his true identity, and forged his partner's signature on the application. [@1]
Isle of Man Today reported that the Ramsey address belonged to a woman who had
done part-time accountancy work for him and knew nothing of the application, and
that police raided her house. [@3] Isle of Man Today reported that his partner
was also initially arrested and released without charge because she had no
knowledge of the application. [@3]

The BBC reported that prosecutors argued it was a sophisticated and sustained
fraud. [@1] Isle of Man Today reported that Carrol told police he changed the
documents to cover up previous convictions in Scotland that would have made him
ineligible for a licence. [@3] The BBC reported that the court heard his 2021
business plan indicated profits of £2.2m for the venture. [@1] Isle of Man Today
reported that the business plan estimated the venture would make £22 million
over three years and that bank statements claiming a £2 million credit balance
were untrue. [@3]

Deemster Cook said the potential embarrassment and public perceptions could only
damage the industry the government was trying to create, and that a deterrent
was necessary. [@3] The BBC reported the deemster saying he was handing down a
"deterrent sentence" so that nobody else would attempt to falsify licence
applications in the future. [@1] Manx Radio republished the BBC's account of the
sentencing under a report credited to BBC Isle of Man, rather than adding a
separate account of its own. [@5]
