Police officers from city, regional, provincial and federal forces swept down on
home-grow marijuana operations from Nova Scotia to British Columbia on Wednesday 30
January 2002. [@1] By half past three in the afternoon Eastern time, police had executed
149 search warrants, made 136 arrests and laid 289 charges, seizing a total of 46,796
plants. [@1] About 500 officers from more than 20 agencies took part, also seizing growing
equipment worth about $3.2 million; police reported no violence but said some weapons
were seized. [@1,3] The joint-forces sweep went under the name Operation Green Sweep. [@2,3] Further raids were planned that evening in Quebec and British Columbia
with about 40 warrants still to be executed, and officers found 28 children in the raided
homes, placing them with relatives or children's aid services. [@1]

## Guelph and Wellington County

Wellington County OPP and Guelph Police raided four houses and one apartment that
Wednesday, confiscating $1.2 to $1.5 million worth of marijuana and more than $100,000
in growing equipment at three of the houses. [@2] Three people were arrested and police
sought a fourth. [@2] Every room but the bathroom and kitchen of a Fife Road house was
filled with marijuana at various stages of growth, about 800 plants with an estimated
$800,000 in marijuana confiscated there. [@2] The Fife Road homeowner, 52-year-old Hung
Manh Nguyen, was released on $30,000 bail facing charges of possession for trafficking,
cultivation and theft of electricity. [@2] Van Luyen Tran, 35, described by police as the
caretaker of the County Road 124 growing operation, stayed in jail on charges of unlawful
production, possession for the purpose of trafficking and theft of electricity. [@2]

Police said growers routinely bypassed electricity meters to power heat lamps, creating
fire and carbon-monoxide hazards for neighbours and emergency crews. [@1,2]
