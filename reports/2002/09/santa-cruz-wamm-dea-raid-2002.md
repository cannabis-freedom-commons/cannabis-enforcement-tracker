Federal agents raided the Wo/Men's Alliance for Medical Marijuana (WAMM) garden near Santa
Cruz on Thursday 5 September 2002, meeting no resistance as they broke in, arrested three
people and cut down 150 cannabis plants. [@1] The agents also seized a small amount of
hashish, three rifles and a shotgun in the raid. [@1] Those arrested, founders Mike and
Valerie Corral and collective member Suzanne Pfeil, were released that afternoon after being
held on suspicion of possessing marijuana with intent to distribute and conspiracy. [@1]

WAMM had operated openly for six years with the cooperation of city, county and sheriff's
officials, who helped define who qualified as a medical user, issued identification and let
the group supply organically grown cannabis free of charge. [@1] The Corrals had helped draft
Proposition 215, California's 1996 medical marijuana initiative. [@1] The collective served
300 seriously ill patients, many of them terminal. [@2] DEA spokesman Richard Meyer
acknowledged the local cooperation but said marijuana remained illegal under federal law no
matter what local law enforcement tolerated. [@1]

Twelve days later, advocates joined by city leaders gave marijuana to about a dozen sick
people in the courtyard of Santa Cruz City Hall before more than 1,000 supporters. [@3]
Mayor Christopher Krohn called the city a sanctuary from federal authorities and said its
leaders were taking a stand. [@3] The DEA spokesman said he was appalled by the giveaway,
while Mike Corral, who helped distribute the marijuana, said the only message was that
marijuana is medicine. [@3]
