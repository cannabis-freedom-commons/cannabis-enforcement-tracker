Thirty Drug Enforcement Administration agents raided the garden of the Wo/Men's Alliance for
Medical Marijuana on a Santa Cruz hillside on 5 September 2002, holding Valerie Corral, her
husband Mike and two other residents at gunpoint before arresting the Corrals. [@2] Agents
chainsawed the collective's cannabis plants; the agents said they took 167 plants, less than
one for each of the collective's members. [@1]

WAMM had grown its own supply for seriously ill and dying members through the 1990s, and
Valerie Corral had co-authored Proposition 215, the 1996 ballot initiative that made California
the first state to legalize medical cannabis. [@2] As news of the raid spread, members used a
phone tree to gather at the garden's security gate, padlocking it and parking cars across the
driveway until the Corrals were freed from the federal courthouse in San Jose. [@1,2] Less
than two weeks later the Corrals handed out free marijuana on the steps of Santa Cruz City
Hall beside the mayor, and WAMM sued the Justice Department and won a federal injunction
against further enforcement. [@2]
