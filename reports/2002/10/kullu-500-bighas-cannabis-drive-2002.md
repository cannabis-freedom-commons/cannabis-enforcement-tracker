A combined operation by the Narcotics Control Bureau's Chandigarh zonal unit, the Amritsar
Customs Commissionerate with its local prevention station, and the police destroyed illicit
cannabis growing on 500 bighas in the Anni, Khanag, Manikaran and Nagar areas of Kullu
district between 27 and 31 October 2002. [@1] The drive was launched on the initiative of
Customs Commissioner Bhattacharjee, and the 104-member team comprised 53 customs officials,
20 NCB officials from Chandigarh and Delhi, and 31 police officials. [@1]

NCB Zonal Director Rakesh Goyal and Superintendent of Police N. Venu Gopal said at a joint
press conference that local people had volunteered to help educate growers, who were urged
to shift to agro-horticulture crops suited to the area's soil and environment. [@1] Goyal
said the NCB had offered to finance a sustainable alternative-livelihood scheme through an
international agency if villagers stopped growing cannabis, and that the NCB Director-General
had asked the state government for such a proposal, which was still awaited. [@1]

Goyal said action against officials who failed to report illicit cultivation could be taken
under Section 47 of the NDPS Act, but the agencies preferred education over direct action
for now. [@1] He added that no action had been taken against anybody so far for growing
cannabis on private or government land. [@1]
