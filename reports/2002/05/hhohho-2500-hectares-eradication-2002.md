Swazi police destroyed 2,500 hectares of marijuana fields in the mountainous
northern Hhohho region in a series of raids early in 2002. [@1, 2] South
African police helicopters identified the fields, and Swazi police sprayed the
plants with defoliants while the cultivators fled to the hills. [@1, 2]

Police said the eradication drive was at its most successful yet, helped by new
roads from the Maguga Dam construction reaching previously inaccessible
growing areas where South African drug lords had secured supplies. [@1] A year
later Swaziland claimed that cooperation with South African police had let it
destroy half of the crop, though cultivation continued. [@3]

The Times of Swaziland reported that rains after some raids washed the
chemicals off the plants and killed competing weeds instead, and that the
financiers' agents taught farmers how to wash the plants clean. [@1] Police
Superintendent Jerome Ndlangamandla gauged the destroyed share of the crop by
a 40 percent local price rise: a matchbox of insangu cost E10 (about US$1), a
compressed block E500 (about US$50), and 25 kilos E1,500 (about US$150). [@1]
