Police in Moroni detained a man described as a notable who owned a parcel
containing 119 kg of cannabis. [@1] The seizure and detention took place about
two days before 8 November 2012. [@1] The case was handled by the national
police anti-drug brigade operating at the port of Moroni. [@1] Officers said the
arrest followed a denunciation by an African woman who had entrusted the man
with goats to sell. [@1] They said she turned him in after he failed to hand
over the sale money. [@1]

Officers recorded the seizure as 119 kg of cannabis. [@1] They said traffickers
hide cannabis in coconuts, chips, oil cans, freezers and even doors to evade
checks. [@1] The brigade officers said they mostly rely on intuition and tips
from accomplices. [@1] They said a dispute over keeping a market alone often
leads one trafficker to denounce another. [@1]

Officers described the brigade as having neither sniffer dogs nor X-ray imaging
equipment nor detectors. [@1] They said the officers keep working empty-handed,
relying on the luck of the day. [@1] Officers warned that in view of large
seizures, the Comoros risked becoming a transit hub for drug traffic between
East Africa, Madagascar and Mauritius. [@1]
