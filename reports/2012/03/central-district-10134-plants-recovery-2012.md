New Zealand police recovered 10,134 cannabis plants across the Central District in their annual cannabis recovery operation, up from 6,374 plants the year before. [@1,2] The operation combined an intelligence phase with an aerial phase using Air Force Iroquois helicopters to winch officers into plots, plus a ground phase with more than 70 search warrants against cultivators and dealers. [@1,2] Of the total, 5,346 plants came from the aerial operation and 4,788 from 78 search warrants. [@1,2]

Cannabis grows were found at 87 plots, and police said 165 offenders had been identified, resulting in 336 charges. [@1,2] Officers also recovered firearms and ammunition, a large sum of cash, quantities of methamphetamine and cannabis oil, and stolen property. [@1,2] The district's aerial results were broken down as Taranaki with 1,953 plants, Ruapehu with 1,816, Whanganui with 754 and Manawatu with 823. [@1,2]

Operation coordinator Detective Senior Sergeant Keith Borrell credited public tip-offs, including calls to Crimestoppers, for the rise, especially against indoor grows in sheds, spare bedrooms and even an underground bunker. [@1,2] Plot sizes ranged from five to 150 plants, and a damp growing season was also blamed for the higher numbers. [@1,2]
