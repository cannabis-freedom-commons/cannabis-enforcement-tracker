Singapore's Court of Appeal set aside the 2010 death sentence of Azman bin Mohamed Sanwan for
trafficking 1,525.7 grams of cannabis, ruling on 6 March 2012 that the evidence against him was
flawed. [@1,2] Azman had been convicted of a capital charge over a 28 April 2007 handover in the
car park in front of Block 108 Yishun Ring Road, where Central Narcotics Bureau officers watched
him move a paper bag holding the cannabis between two cars. [@1,2]

His two companions, Tamil Salvem and Balasubramaniam, were tried with him and acquitted of the
same capital charge, and the prosecution did not appeal their acquittals. [@1,2] The appeal court
ruled the self-incriminating statements Azman gave months after his arrest inadmissible, held he
was no more culpable than the two acquitted men, and noted traces of cannabis were found in
Balasubramaniam's urine but none in Azman's. [@1,2]
