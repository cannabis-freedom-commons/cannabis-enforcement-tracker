The Court of Appeal in Putrajaya dismissed Airiy Azmi Mohamed's appeal on 10 May 2012, affirming his conviction and mandatory death sentence for trafficking 59,911.7 grams of cannabis. [@1] A total of 65 compressed slabs had been recovered from three secret compartments in the car he was driving alone at the Bukit Kayu Hitam Immigration Complex, and chemists confirmed the substance was cannabis. [@1] Airiy, a 31-year-old car dealer from Kelantan, had been sentenced to the gallows on 8 May 2011 for trafficking the 60 kg load at the same complex on 17 April 2009. [@2]

## Appeal

The judges held that concealment in purpose-built vehicle compartments attracted the statutory presumption of knowledge under section 37(h) of the Dangerous Drugs Act 1952, and that carrying the cannabis from Hatyai in Thailand into Malaysia met the legal definition of trafficking. [@1] Airiy's defence that he was an innocent carrier with no knowledge of the hidden drugs was rejected. [@1]
