The Turkish army seized 21 tonnes of marijuana from Kurdish rebels in Diyarbakir province, in what local authorities on 15 December 2012 called one of the biggest ever crackdowns on terrorist income. [@1,2] Diyarbakir governor Mustafa Toprak said the stash had been unearthed along with several cells filled with ammunition and explosives, after hundreds of special security forces were deployed the preceding week. [@1,2]

The authorities valued the marijuana at 17 million euros (about $22.4 million). [@1,2] Toprak said the operation had landed a huge blow to terrorist income, which the authorities attribute mainly to drug trafficking by the outlawed Kurdistan Workers' Party (PKK); the PKK denies drug trafficking. [@1,2]
