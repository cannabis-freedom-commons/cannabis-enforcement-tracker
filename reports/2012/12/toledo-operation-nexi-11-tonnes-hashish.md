Spanish police seized 11 tonnes of hashish hidden in truck fuel tanks and
arrested 35 people in Madrid and Castilla-La Mancha, the National Police
announced on 26 December 2012. [@2] Police called the operation Nexi and
described it as one of the most important of the last decade. [@2]

The gang brought the hashish from Morocco in trucks, which had to refuel
constantly because the bundles of hashish left little room in the tanks. [@2]
The emptied tanks were rewelded in another unit and sent back to Morocco to be
loaded with hashish again. [@2] The hashish was then hidden in legal merchandise
bound for Spain, the Netherlands, France, Belgium and England. [@2] A part of
the hashish was sent for sale in Madrid and the rest was put into trucks with
legal goods, which were driven to Belgium, Britain, France and the Netherlands.
[@1] Most of the hashish was stored in warehouses in the central Spanish
province of Toledo, the BBC reported. [@3]

One truck carried about 1,700 kg of hashish and 65 kg of marijuana among tonnes
of fresh carrots bound for France and the Netherlands, and a second, driven by
the arrested Belgian, hid 8,500 kg of drugs in branded clothing bound for
England, the police said. [@2]

The 35 arrested included 31 Moroccan nationals, three Spaniards and one Belgian.
[@2] The investigation by the Madrid anti-drug and organised crime unit began in
March 2012 with hashish dealing detected in Leganés, Fuenlabrada and Móstoles.
[@2] Officers carried out 17 searches and seized 150,000 euros in cash, 14
vehicles and 109 mobile phones, among the vehicles two articulated trucks valued
at almost 400,000 euros. [@2]

The Vijesti account, quoting the officials' announcement, says the hashish was
smuggled from Morocco in tanker trucks and that those arrested belonged to one
of the largest smuggling chains on the European market. [@1]
