Federal prosecutors in Cincinnati unsealed an 18-count indictment against eleven
men accused of conspiring to bring hundreds of kilograms of marijuana from Texas
into the Cincinnati area and shipping cash back. [@1] Eight of the eleven
charged are from Hamilton and Butler counties in southwest Ohio. [@1] Three of
the eleven charged are from the Brownsville, Texas, area. [@1] Members of an
Organized Crime Drug Enforcement Task Force began arresting the accused men on
the day the indictment was unsealed. [@1] The eight in custody were to appear
before a federal magistrate judge in Cincinnati, and agents were still seeking
the other three. [@1] The indictment had been returned on 25 July and was
unsealed following the defendants' arrests. [@1]

All eleven are charged with conspiracy to distribute and possess with intent to
distribute marijuana, punishable by at least ten years and up to life in prison.
[@1] The indictment alleges that from 2006 Lee Wurzelbacher received shipments
of marijuana concealed in the gas tanks of motor vehicles at Payless Autos on
Pippin Road in Cincinnati. [@1] Lee Wurzelbacher and Bryon Roos operated the
used-car businesses Payless Auto Sales and Ace 1 Auto Sales to launder drug
proceeds and give the appearance of legitimate income. [@1] The marijuana was
shipped to Ohio in crates containing tile and granite countertop bars by
commercial freight companies and delivered to locations in Hamilton and Butler
counties. [@1] Reporting on the case said the marijuana was stored and processed
at a Hamilton County farmhouse and a building in Fairfield, among other places.
[@2] The indictment alleges that the conspirators laundered the trafficking
proceeds by purchasing eleven pieces of real estate and a boat. [@1] Reporting
on the case said $83,990 in cash was stashed inside the tire of a vehicle being
towed to Texas. [@2]

Bryon Roos was a 13-year veteran officer of the North College Hill police force.
[@3] Roos told the North College Hill police department about the allegations
and was placed on unpaid leave pending the outcome of the case. [@2] The
indictment also charges the defendants with conspiracy to commit money
laundering, money laundering, illegally structuring financial transactions and
attempt to possess with intent to distribute marijuana. [@1] The Justice
Department stressed that an indictment is only a charge and is not evidence of
guilt. [@1]
