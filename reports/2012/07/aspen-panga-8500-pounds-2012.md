The San Francisco-based Coast Guard cutter Aspen seized approximately 8,500 pounds of marijuana from a vessel on 25 July, about 160 miles west of Los Angeles. [@1] The 225-foot buoy tender's crew had spotted a small panga-style boat piled high with suspicious bales and heading north, and intercepted it to find the marijuana aboard. [@1,2]

The July report put the load at more than 340 bales worth about $7.7 million, while the Coast Guard's later account gave a street value of about $8 million. [@1,2] The bust marked a 50-ton milestone for waterborne marijuana seizures in Southern California and the Pacific Southwest border region that fiscal year, with the 50 tons valued at about $90.7 million. [@1] Pangas running multi-ton loads of marijuana and migrants north from Baja California had driven a rise in maritime smuggling as far north as Santa Barbara County. [@1]

The Los Angeles/Long Beach sector commander said stopping the loads denied trafficking organisations the money feeding wider smuggling, crime and violence. [@1] The Coast Guard coordinated the patrols with Customs and Border Protection, Immigration and Customs Enforcement and other federal, state and local agencies, alongside Mexican counterparts. [@1]
