Federal officials and sheriffs announced on 26 September 2012 that the
summer-long Operation Mercury had seized more than 400,000 marijuana plants
across Central Valley farmland since March. [@1,2] The six-county effort by
federal prosecutors and agents with sheriffs' offices from Merced to Kern
also brought more than 175 arrests and over 100 confiscated firearms. [@1,2]

The announcement coincided with a morning raid on a 140-acre farm at Jensen
and Sunnyside avenues, just outside southeast Fresno's city limits, which
Sheriff Margaret Mims called one of the biggest marijuana busts in Fresno
County history. [@1] Deputies with federal agents found thousands of
marijuana plants, many ten feet tall, with about 50 acres of cannabis hidden
among rows of peppers, tomatoes, bitter melons and corn. [@1] Fifty people
were detained, and deputies seized several weapons and several pounds of
processed high-grade marijuana; no medical marijuana cards were posted in
the fields. [@1]

US Attorney Benjamin Wagner said the grows were commercial operations tied
to Mexican cartels and sold across the country, not medical gardens. [@1,2]
Federal authorities said most Operation Mercury defendants were not Mexican
nationals, and described the Fresno farm raided that day as a legitimate
operation owned by people from Southeast Asia. [@2] During the campaign,
authorities had sent landowners letters giving them 14 days to eradicate
marijuana on their property before facing land seizure. [@2]
