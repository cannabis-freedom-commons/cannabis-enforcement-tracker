Police in Lilongwe arrested businessman Rashid Adam Sauzande, who runs the
Ambassador Rest House in the city, and said he was found in possession of Indian
hemp, locally known as chamba. [@1] Kingsly Dandaula, the public relations
officer for Lilongwe Police Station, said the arrest was made on 2 February 2015
after the police's Criminal Investigation Department searched Sauzande's office
at the rest house. [@1] Dandaula said the search followed suspicions that
Sauzande could be involved with drugs. [@1]

Dandaula said the search of the office on Devil Street began at around 10 in the
morning and found six cobs of chamba, and that Sauzande was then taken to
Lilongwe station for questioning. [@1] The six cobs were sent to Chitedze
Research Station for tests and police were still waiting for the result,
Dandaula said. [@1] The Maravi Post, which carried the same account, reported
that Malawi News Agency staff on the Tuesday before publication saw proceedings
in the Lilongwe court where state prosecutors described the drug as a dangerous
drug. [@2] Sauzande complained that he was sick and needed special medical
treatment, according to that account. [@2]
