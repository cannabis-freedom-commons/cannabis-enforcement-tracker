Regulators raided Dell's Maraschino Cherries, a family-owned maraschino cherry
factory on Dikeman Street in the Red Hook neighborhood of Brooklyn, on 24
February 2015. [@3] About 25 investigators from the state Department of
Environmental Conservation, the city Department of Environmental Protection and
detective investigators from the Brooklyn district attorney's office arrived
with four warrants to investigate complaints that the plant was dumping syrup
and other cherry waste into the water near the warehouse. [@3] Five hours into
the search, investigators noticed flimsy shelving and a faint smell of marijuana
and told the owner, Arthur Mondella, 57, that they would be back with a warrant.
[@1]

Mondella excused himself to the private bathroom attached to his office, yelled
to his sister "Take care of my kids!" and shot himself in the head. [@1] He died
later at an area hospital of gunshot wounds. [@2]

After the shooting, investigators found three bags holding about 80 pounds
(about 36.3 kilograms) of marijuana and hundreds of thousands of dollars in cash
stashed in the factory. [@2] A search warrant on the secret entrance revealed
what a source called a huge marijuana-growing operation underneath the
warehouse, reached by a ladder down to a basement. [@3] The space below the
plant also held numerous high-end vehicles, including a Rolls-Royce, a Porsche
and a Harley-Davidson motorcycle. [@3] Dell's Maraschino Cherries had recently
completed a $5 million renovation of the Red Hook plant, which employed about a
dozen people. [@2] The company supplied its syrup-soaked and dyed fruits to some
of the largest restaurant chains in the United States, including Olive Garden
and TGI Fridays. [@1]

Investigators returned to the factory the next day, ABC News reported. [@2]
Authorities said they were still questioning whether more would be discovered,
and an unnamed law enforcement source said: "You don't kill yourself over weed.
There has to be more than that." [@1] ABC News reported that the investigation
into environmental crimes continued and that it remained unclear whether anyone
would now face charges. [@2]
