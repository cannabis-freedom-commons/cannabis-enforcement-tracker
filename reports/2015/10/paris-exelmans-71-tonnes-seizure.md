French customs officers seized more than seven tonnes of cannabis from three vans parked in Paris's upmarket 16th arrondissement on the night of 17 to 18 October 2015. [@1] The vans, hired vehicles with French plates, were being used as temporary mobile storage, and a fourth empty van stood nearby. [@1] A local resident had alerted officers after the vans sat parked on the boulevard for more than a week. [@1]

The cannabis lay in big bags in the boots of the vans, packed as resin blocks of about 25 kilograms inside plastic-lined jute bags. [@1,2] In total officers took 7.1 tonnes, which the authorities valued at no less than 15 million euros. [@1] President François Hollande called it the biggest cannabis haul ever made in the capital and visited the customs investigations unit's headquarters to congratulate the agents. [@1,2] He said the bust dealt a setback to criminal gangs with links to other organisations, including terrorists. [@1]

The find capped a weeks-long investigation by the National Directorate of Customs Intelligence and Investigations that had already yielded 320 kilograms seized in Bayonne in September and 200 kilograms in Rouen earlier in October. [@2] No arrests were made when the vans were found. [@2]
