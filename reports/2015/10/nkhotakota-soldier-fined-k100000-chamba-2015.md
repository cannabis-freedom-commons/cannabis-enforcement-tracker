Nkhotakota police arrested a Malawi Defence Force soldier on Monday 19 October
2015 at the Kaombe checking point as he drove on the Dwangwa-Lilongwe road. [@1]
Officers at the checkpoint found the soldier's two travelling bags filled to
capacity with Indian hemp. [@1] Police identified the soldier as Andrew Matochi,
34, of Kamuzu Barracks, who hails from Kanyenda village in the district. [@1]
Matochi could not explain where he got the hemp and was arrested. [@1]

The Nkhunga First Grade Magistrate Court convicted and sentenced Matochi on
Thursday 29 October 2015 for being found in possession of cannabis sativa at the
Kaombe roadblock. [@3] Magistrate Buleya ordered Matochi to pay a K100,000 fine
or, in default, serve two years' imprisonment with hard labour. [@3] The
magistrate told Matochi that the court expected law enforcers to set an example
and that a stiff punishment was required so others could learn a lesson. [@3] In
mitigation Matochi told the court he was remorseful, a first offender and a
family man. [@3] State prosecutor Sub-Inspector James Ngupasye Muyira told the
court that Indian hemp cases are rampant in the district. [@3] Possessing
cannabis sativa without a licence is contrary to section 4(a) of the Dangerous
Drugs Regulation and section 19(1) of the Dangerous Drugs Act. [@2]

Nkhotakota police spokesperson Sub-Inspector Williams Kaponda confirmed that the
hemp had been packed in two military travelling bags. [@2] Kaponda said Matochi
had paid the fine and that cases of people being found with the drug are common
in the district. [@2]
