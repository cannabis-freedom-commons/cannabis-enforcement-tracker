A judge at Luton Crown Court sentenced two men on Friday 13 March 2015 over a
seven-year conspiracy to import more than 28 tonnes of cannabis from the
Netherlands. [@1,2] John Wright, 66, of Hounslow, received nine years and four
months and Steven McDonald, 32, of Chatham, received eight years, and both were
banned from travelling outside Britain for ten years after release. [@1,2] The
pair had admitted conspiring to import a Class B drug at the same court on 16
October 2014. [@1,2]

They were caught after Border Force officers at Harwich intercepted about 300
kg of herbal cannabis shipped from the Netherlands disguised as flooring tiles
on 24 April 2014. [@1,2] Investigators watched the load delivered to an address
in Whetstone, north London, the next day, where McDonald signed for it before
driving it to garages in Wembley with Wright following; officers arrested them
as they began opening the consignment and found another 150 kg in a second
garage. [@1,2] Searches of their homes recovered dealing ledgers and evidence of
bank transfers and frequent trips to the Netherlands, including a rented
apartment in Lisse used by Wright. [@1,2]

Working with Dutch police, investigators tied about a hundred other importations
from three Dutch addresses to two front companies the men ran; one shipment had
been stolen at gunpoint by a rival group, forcing both ends to change company
names and locations. [@1] Detective Inspector Steve Miles said the men smuggled
on an industrial scale worth potentially more than £80 million, and the
prosecution said the sentences showed such organised crime would not go
unpunished. [@1,2] The National Crime Agency said investigators were still
hunting a third man believed to have rented the Wembley garages under the false
name Mark Gamble. [@1]
