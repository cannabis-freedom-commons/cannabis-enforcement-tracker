Jesse Teplicki, 50, grew 46 marijuana plants at his Hollywood home and hid nothing when detectives arrived on a tip about two years before the trial, telling them the cannabis was medicine for the severe anorexia he had suffered since age nine. [@1] He took the stand at trial and admitted smoking a marijuana cigarette that morning for nausea and suppressed appetite, saying pills did not work because he could not keep them down. [@1,2] He told the court he grew so many plants at once only because he grows once a year. [@2]

Manufacturing cannabis was a felony punishable by up to five years in prison, and Teplicki had rejected several plea offers while calling the plant medicine he needs to function. [@1] Prosecutor Kathleen O'Brien attacked his medical history and the high plant count, saying he had no treatment plan and outdated records. [@2] The jury of four women and two men deliberated less than an hour before acquitting him, the first medical-need acquittal by a Florida jury. [@1] A tearful Teplicki told three jurors who stayed behind: "You saved my life." [@1]
