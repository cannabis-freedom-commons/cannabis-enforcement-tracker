More than a tonne of cannabis was seized by Border Force officers at London
Gateway. [@1] Officers discovered 1.1 tonnes of the Class B drug when they
searched a container that had arrived at the port from Ghana. [@1] The load had
been manifested as sacks of corn husks and corn flour, but officers unloading
the sacks found a heap of cardboard boxes buried underneath. [@1]

All 42 boxes were stuffed with shrink-wrapped blocks of cannabis. [@1] Border
Force said the seizure was worth an estimated £2.3 million and had prevented a
substantial quantity of illegal drugs from reaching the streets of the UK. [@1]
The account of the concealment and value was given by Border Force Assistant
Director for London Gateway Mark Kennedy. [@1]

The seizure was made on 12 September 2015. [@1] The case was passed to the
National Crime Agency, which continued to investigate. [@1] Border Force said
its officers use sniffer dogs, carbon dioxide detectors, heartbeat monitors,
scanners and visual searches to find hidden drugs, firearms and tobacco. [@1]
