Fort Hunter Liggett eradicated a record 5,523 marijuana plants on 30 July 2015, at an
estimated street value of $3,000 per plant for a $16.5 million total. [@1,2] Routine U.S.
Forest Service aerial surveys in July located several grows on the base and coordinated their
removal with the Army post. [@1] The Forest Service, Monterey County sheriffs and the
California Department of Fish and Wildlife took part as members of the Campaign Against
Marijuana Program. [@1]

## Troops and toxins

Post commander Col. Jan Norris called the joint effort a deterrent to future grows on federal
property, where cultivation stays illegal regardless of state law. [@1] Police Lt. Robert Look
Jr. said the grows hide in remote ground visible only from helicopters, and that debate over
legalisation in California threatened funding for the eradication programme. [@1]
Conservation manager Liz Clark said cartel growers use banned fertilisers and pesticides that
poison soil and water and damage waterways, wildlife and cultural resources. [@1] The post
said Mexican and Chinese cartel crews employ armed field workers who set animal traps,
endangering soldiers training on the base. [@1]
