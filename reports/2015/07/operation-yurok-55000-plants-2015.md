A two-week multi-agency effort led by the Yurok Tribe eradicated about 55,000
cannabis plants on and near the Yurok Reservation in July 2015. [@1,3] Officers
served 30 search warrants, arrested seven people, seized 1,000 pounds of
processed cannabis and uncovered one hash-oil lab. [@3] The tribe said the main
purpose was returning as much water as possible to the Klamath River and the
community water systems downstream of the grows. [@1]

The plantations were diverting millions of gallons of water from creeks feeding
tribal water systems serving more than 300 families, while the Klamath ran warm
enough to threaten migrating salmon. [@2] Raided sites held abandoned vehicles,
broken batteries, household waste, chemical fertilisers and illegal grading that
one scientist at the scene called an environmental disaster. [@2]

Tribal leaders called the grows the largest ever seen in their territory and
promised charges for every environmental crime. [@2] The tribe had run a similar
operation the previous year, and its vice-chair said the raids would continue
every summer until growers left the area. [@3]
