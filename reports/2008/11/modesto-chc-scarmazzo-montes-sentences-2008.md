Luke Scarmazzo and Ricardo Montes, both 26 at the time, opened the California
Healthcare Collective in Modesto in 2004, running a medical marijuana dispensary
that operated openly and paid taxes for more than a year. [@2,4] The Drug
Enforcement Administration raided the collective on 27 September 2006 after a
34-month investigation that ended with nine arrests and the seizure of 1,000
marijuana plants and $330,000 in cash. [@2,3,4]

At trial the US Attorney argued the collective flouted even state law, saying it
had taken a $9 million profit from 2004 to 2006 while state law required
dispensaries to operate as nonprofits. [@1] The defense answered that the
collective held a Modesto business licence and had paid the IRS over $1 million,
and that neither the city nor the state had ever told it that it was violating
state law. [@1] Modesto's mayor and police chief stood with federal prosecutors
at a news conference after the verdict, where officers said the police chief had
called in the DEA to investigate the business. [@1]

A Fresno jury convicted both men on 15 May 2008 of manufacturing and distributing
marijuana and of operating a continuing criminal enterprise, which carried a
20-year mandatory minimum, along with firearms counts. [@1,2,3] Learning of the
mandatory term, two jurors filed declarations with the federal court in Fresno
recanting their verdict while lawyers sought a retrial. [@2]

On 21 November 2008 the Eastern District of California sentenced Scarmazzo to
262 months (21 years and 10 months) and Montes to the 240-month (20-year)
minimum, with an $8.89 million forfeiture judgment. [@2,3] Montes was granted
clemency by President Obama and released in May 2017, while Scarmazzo served
nearly 15 years before a compassionate release on 3 February 2023. [@4]
