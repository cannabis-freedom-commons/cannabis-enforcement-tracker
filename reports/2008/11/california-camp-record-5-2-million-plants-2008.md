Top narcotics enforcers gathered in Sacramento to announce a record seizure of marijuana plants from public and private lands: raids in 40 of the state's 58 counties yielded more than 5.2 million plants seized and destroyed. [@1] Nearly 70 percent of them were growing on state and federal land, and the eradicated plants would have had a wholesale value of $15.8 billion. [@1] The 2008 eradication season ran from July through October. [@1]

The officers reported more than 221 people arrested and 244 weapons confiscated during the season. [@1] The U.S. attorney said the cartels favor California for its vast rural public land, including the national forests, its temperate climate and local acceptance of marijuana in some areas. [@1] In and around Sequoia National Park, which regularly leads the nation in large-scale cultivation, 524,000 plants were seized. [@1] The counties with the most eradicated plants were Lake with 499,508, Tulare with 395,489, Shasta with 394,375, Mendocino with 231,802 and Humboldt with 145,762. [@1,2]

## CAMP's own count

The Campaign Against Marijuana Planting's season summary counted 2,905,232 plants seized, valued at $11.6 billion wholesale, in 542 raids with 143 arrests and 142 weapons seized. [@2] The larger 5.2 million figure adds the federal partners' seizures to the CAMP total. [@1]
