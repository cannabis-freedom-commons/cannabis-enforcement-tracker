In September 2005 Whitehorse RCMP carried out an eleven-hour drug bust on
three homes in the Copper Ridge subdivision, seizing 2,400 marijuana plants
with a street value of up to $5 million, and searched a fourth Copper Ridge
home three days later.[@1] Three Whitehorse men and two Vancouver residents
were charged after the 23 September raids, and in total police seized 4,500
pot plants from the Copper Ridge grow-ops and charged eight men with
possessing and growing marijuana.[@1,2]

The case went to trial in November 2006, and in April 2007 the territorial
court judge ruled that the RCMP had breached the arrested men's Charter
rights to legal counsel, manipulated court documents, arrested people
without grounds and mishandled the accused after arrest, excluding a great
deal of evidence.[@2] Four of the eight men were acquitted, the Crown filed
an appeal notice in May 2007, but the RCMP could not find the men to serve
it and abandoned the appeal.[@2] Of the other four accused, three pleaded
guilty and served up to a year in jail while one man's guilty plea in
Vancouver was still before the courts.[@2]
