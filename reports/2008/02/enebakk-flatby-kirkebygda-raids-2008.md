On the evening of 27 February 2008, Follo police raided a cannabis nursery
in Flatby in Enebakk, arresting five people, four of them of Vietnamese
background, and seizing around 400 plants.[@1] Two evenings later police
raided a house in Kirkebygda, also in Enebakk, finding about 400 large
plants ready for harvest plus some 100 empty pots, and arrested a man in
his forties who was of Vietnamese origin and held Norwegian
citizenship.[@2]

The raids belonged to a wave of indoor plantations across eastern Norway:
police had uncovered more than 30 nurseries, charged nearly 60 people with
cultivation, and remanded more than 40 in custody, straining remand
capacity.[@1,3] About 10,000 plants had been seized, with an estimated
street value near 150 million kroner, and Kripos, the national criminal
investigation service, was coordinating the districts ahead of a 5 March
meeting on centralizing the investigation.[@3]

The nurseries followed a pattern: rented detached houses with covered
windows, advanced irrigation, lights burning around the clock on stolen
electricity, and a resident "gardener" tending the plants, whom police
believed may have been smuggled into the country.[@3]
