On 18 September 2008 Ontario Provincial Police found some 40,000 marijuana plants at a farm on
B-Line Road in Laurentian Valley Township near Pembroke, about 150 kilometres west of Ottawa,
during the execution of a search warrant. [@1] The provincial police news release called it the
largest grow operation in Canada's history, one-third larger than the 30,000-plant former Molson
brewery operation in Barrie. [@1]

## Hidden in the corn

The plants were hidden between rows of corn on a 43-hectare farm, about half of it cornfield,
with a buffer zone of corn-only field around the five-or-six-football-fields plot where the
six-foot cannabis plants grew. [@1,2] Sgt. Kristine Rae said officers spent the whole weekend
removing the crop by hand. [@1] It took at least six shipping containers on flatbed trucks to
cart the plants away, later buried at a secure site, and police valued the seizure at about $40
million. [@1,2]

Officers said the crop was watered by plastic tubing fed from a pond and an above-ground pool,
and a double garage behind the farmhouse had been set up to dry plants, some already being
harvested. [@1,2] At least 30 police and auxiliary officers took part in the operation. [@2]
Land registry records showed the property at 1970 B-Line Road was bought by Viet Ha for $190,000
on 1 November 2005. [@2]
