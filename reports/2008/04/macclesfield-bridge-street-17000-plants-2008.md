Firefighters answering a Saturday lunchtime blaze at Birch House, a Georgian three-storey former weaver's cottage on Bridge Street in Macclesfield, walked into one of the largest cannabis factories ever found in Britain. [@3] The building stood about 400 yards from the town's police station, and two men in white face masks were seen running from it just before the fire took hold. [@3]

## The perfect factory

Inside, officers found a triple-tiered indoor farm packed wall to wall with cannabis bushes, arranged methodically from saplings to fully grown plants under a sprawling network of heat lamps. [@3] The electricity meters had been bypassed to power the system, and that overloaded wiring is what started the fire that exposed the operation. [@2,3] Macclesfield police Inspector Gareth Woods called it "the perfect cannabis factory", and warned the building could have exploded, with catastrophic results for the densely populated street. [@3] Police believed an organised gang had run the site for about 18 months. [@3]

Officers first estimated about 8,000 plants worth roughly £500,000. [@3] As clearing continued, the count roughly doubled: the local paper later described a £2 million factory, and Macclesfield's MP told Parliament the house had contained 17,000 plants. [@1,2]
