On 30 April 2008 the Florida Department of Law Enforcement, with the North Florida HIDTA and many
other agencies, raided high-potency indoor cannabis grow houses in 48 Florida counties in Operation
D-Day. [@1] The effort, coordinated by the South Florida HIDTA with the Central and North Florida
HIDTAs, aimed at hydroponic grow operations run as organised criminal enterprises and linked to
home invasions, robberies, kidnappings, arsons and homicides. [@2] The U.S. Attorney's Office for the
Southern District of Florida announced the preliminary results on 1 May. [@2]

Officers made 135 arrests and seized 9,249 cannabis plants from more than 150 houses, plants
officials valued at an estimated $41 million. [@1,2,3] In the South Florida region alone they made 74
arrests and took 3,400 plants from 74 houses; in Duval County the sheriff seized 632 plants, seven
firearms and a bulletproof vest and arrested 14 people. [@1,2] The cases were to be prosecuted by
local and federal authorities. [@2]

## Disputed raid

Defence lawyer Norm Kent wrote that sixteen agencies combined on a single day to bust 150 grow
houses, and that only about ten guns and a vest were found in South Florida. [@3] He also described
agents storming the wrong house in Opa-locka, throwing a resident down at gunpoint before realising
the mistake. [@3]
