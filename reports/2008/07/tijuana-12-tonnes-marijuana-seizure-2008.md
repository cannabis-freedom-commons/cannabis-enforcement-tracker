Mexico's army seized at least 12 tonnes of marijuana in the border city of
Tijuana, in what officials called the largest haul since President Felipe
Calderón sent more than 25,000 troops to the region to tackle trafficking. [@1]

Officials said about three tonnes of the drug were found in a stolen car on
the Thursday while the rest was discovered on the Friday. [@1] The seizure came
as the army said it had confiscated more than 500 tonnes of marijuana in the
state of Baja California alone so far in 2008. [@1] Calderón had come to power
in 2006 promising to rid Mexico of organised and drug-related crime. [@1]

The BBC's Americas editor noted that, despite celebrations at the military
barracks in Tijuana, the seizure was a mostly symbolic victory that left the
cartel bosses far from dealt a death blow. [@1] Authorities described the
traffickers as extremely organised and unwilling to give up an enterprise worth
tens of millions of dollars every year. [@1]
