Belfast Crown Court sentenced Martin Kerfoot and Stephen James McGivern in July 2008 in a case involving 3.5 tonnes of cannabis resin seized in Newtownards, County Down. [@5] The sentencing account reports seven years in prison for McGivern, whom it described as a courier. [@5] The judge said Kerfoot had made his transport and storage business available to the organisers, and rejected his claim that he acted under duress. [@5]

## Seizure and trial

Police and customs officers arrested the two men at a warehouse on the Quarry Heights industrial estate on 10 October 2006. [@1,2,3] The resin had been brought in a lorry packed with wooden doors and was being unloaded when officers arrived. [@3,5] Early coverage described a three-tonne seizure and quoted a street-value estimate of £18 million. [@1,4] At trial the quantity was given as 3.5 tonnes, and the sentencing account gave a lower estimate of up to £13 million. [@3,5]

The two men were initially remanded in custody on charges of possession with intent to supply. [@2] On 22 November 2007 both pleaded not guilty at Downpatrick Crown Court. [@3] Kerfoot also denied importation and supply, and was allowed to remain on bail while McGivern was remanded. [@3] At sentencing the judge said the drugs were believed to have come from Morocco via Spain, England and Dublin. [@5]
