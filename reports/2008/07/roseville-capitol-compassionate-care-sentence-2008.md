Richard James Marino ran Capitol Compassionate Care, a storefront medical
marijuana dispensary in Roseville, California, that served about 1,000
registered patients and held a city dispensing licence. [@2] The shop had opened
in January 2004, and federal agents had publicly warned that its openly growing
owner was in harm's way. [@1,2]

On 3 September 2004, Drug Enforcement Administration agents executed search
warrants at the dispensary and at Marino's home in nearby Newcastle. [@1,2] A
shift manager said agents entered at 9:30 a.m. with guns drawn and ordered
everyone to the ground. [@1] No arrests were made. [@1] The later federal
accounting says agents seized 617 live plants, 364 at the shop and 253 at the
residence, plus about 18,805 grams of processed marijuana, about 3,022 grams of
hashish and $105,000 in cash. [@4,5] Marino said by phone that he thought he was
doing everything above board. [@1]

In January 2006 a federal grand jury returned a 19-count indictment charging
Marino with marijuana trafficking and money laundering, alleging a conspiracy to
distribute at least 100 kilograms from January to September 2004 and about
$2,750,849 in proceeds. [@3] On 6 May 2008 he pleaded guilty to conspiracy to
distribute marijuana and money laundering. [@4] Prosecutors said the shop's
records showed $2,275,849 in gross sales between February and August 2004, about
482 pounds of marijuana sold under strain names such as AK-47 and Trainwreck,
and more than $150,000 of shop money spent on Marino's rent, house, housekeeper
and credit cards. [@4]

On 22 July 2008, United States District Judge Lawrence K. Karlton sentenced
Marino, 54, to 51 months in prison, a $2,500 fine and 48 months of supervised
release. [@5] He was remanded into custody immediately. [@5] In related civil
cases he forfeited more than $201,000, including the sale proceeds of the
Newcastle house used to grow marijuana. [@5]
