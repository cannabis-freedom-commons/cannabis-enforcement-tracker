Customs officers impounded 53 boxes at the Aviance Cargo repackaging section
of Kotoka International Airport in Accra on the evening of Thursday 23 October
2008.[@1] The boxes purported to be foodstuff such as plantain and kenkey and
were bound for the United Kingdom.[@1] A search found 50 boxes weighing 664
kg altogether and holding 240 parcels; they broke down into 192 slabs of
compressed dried leaves, 40 plastic bowls and 8 cylindrical containers of a
honey-like substance.[@1] The substances tested positive for cannabis and
negative for cocaine on examination by the Narcotics Control Board unit at
Aviance.[@1]

A Narcotics Control Board official said the honey-like substance was finely
processed cannabis mixed with oil, usable as a bread spread, containing no
honey at all.[@1] A vigilant Customs, Excise and Preventive Service officer
had grown suspicious of the vehicle that brought the undocumented boxes
straight onto the airfield, and the exporter was absent when they were
seized.[@1] The driver sped off as officers scrutinised the goods, and agent
Kennedy Biney was to be invited to help with investigations, though the
exporter's identity and the vehicle's registration remained unknown.[@1]
Customs official Nana Egyin Boadu said the lack of scanners hampered the
outfit, while Aviance export head Dorothy Arhin said the cargo had no export
permit and the airline booked to carry it was not disclosed.[@1]
