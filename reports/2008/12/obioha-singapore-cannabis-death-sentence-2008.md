Chijioke Stephen Obioha, a 20-year-old Ghanaian, was arrested by narcotics
officers in April 2007 after a surveillance operation found him with more than
2.6 kilograms of cannabis. [@1] He had come to Singapore in 2005 to try out for
a football club, was not hired, stayed on as a middleman for electronics goods
dealers, and his immigration pass had expired in January 2007. [@1]

The April 2007 operation that netted him had seized more than 20 kilograms of
cannabis with a street value of about S$410,000. [@4] Zambian Daka Guinea, 21,
arrived from Johannesburg on 9 April with 14 one-kilogram packets hidden in her
luggage, checked into a hotel, and handed the drugs to Obioha, then 29. [@4,5]
Both appeared in court that June, Guinea on a trafficking charge carrying a
possible death sentence and Obioha on three drug charges. [@5]

After a 21-day trial in 2008, the High Court convicted him and, on 30 December
2008, imposed the mandatory death sentence for trafficking; Singapore law
mandates death for trafficking more than 500 grams of cannabis, carried out by
hanging. [@1,2] The precise quantity he was convicted over was 2,604.56 grams. [@3]

He filed an appeal in January 2009, which the Court of Appeal dismissed in
August 2010. [@3]
