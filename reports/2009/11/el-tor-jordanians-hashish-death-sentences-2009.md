A court in El Tor (al-Tor), in the south of the Sinai peninsula, sentenced Jordanian hashish traffickers to death on 12 November 2009 for trying to smuggle hashish into Egypt earlier that year.[@1,2] Security officials had stopped the driver's truck at the Red Sea port of Nuweiba in January and found 173 kilograms of hashish concealed in its tyres.[@1,2]

The driver identified three other Jordanians as his accomplices, and all four were tried on drug-trafficking charges.[@1] Only the driver was present in court for the verdict while the others were sentenced in their absence.[@1,2] The BBC reported four death sentences, while the AFP report said three; the report uses the BBC's figure.[@2,1] The verdicts have to be approved by Egypt's Grand Mufti, Ali Gomaa, before any execution can go ahead.[@1,2]

## Context

In December 2008 another Egyptian court had sentenced three Syrian men to death by hanging for trafficking heroin and opium.[@1,2] Egypt applies the death penalty infrequently: the London-based rights group Amnesty International said only one person was executed in 2007.[@1,2]
