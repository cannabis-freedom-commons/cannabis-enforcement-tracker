Five suspects were arrested on 12 May 2008 after police found hashish packed
in small bags inside two shipping containers at Mong Cai, on the Chinese
border. [@1] Anti-narcotics chief Nguyen Anh Tuan called it the largest amount
of drugs Vietnam had ever discovered. [@1] A US diplomatic cable dates the
seizure operation to 13 May and describes a special interagency task force of
counter-narcotics, provincial, sea-police and customs units. [@2]

The containers had been unloaded at Haiphong port, declared as jeans from
Pakistan, and were being trucked to the Mong Cai border gate for onward
shipment to China; press reports also named Canada as an intended destination.
[@1,2] The five men had entered Vietnam on 10 May and were believed to own the
shipment. [@1] They had been hired in April 2008 by men known as A Giai and
A Tran to move the consignment from Pakistan to Canada. [@3]

After a four-day trial, the People's Court of Quang Ninh Province sentenced
all five, Chinese nationals aged 42 to 52, to death by firing squad for
trafficking nearly eight tons of marijuana by sea. [@3] The court confiscated
19,600 jeans used as cover and HK$1.5 million in assets. [@3] The men
had also been charged with smuggling almost $180,000 in cash. [@4] Vietnam
punishes trafficking 5 kg or more of marijuana resin with death. [@1]
