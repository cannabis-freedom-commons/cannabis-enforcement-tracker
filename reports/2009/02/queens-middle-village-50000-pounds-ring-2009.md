Federal agents broke up a marijuana smuggling ring that moved high-grade
British Columbia cannabis from Canada into Queens, arresting ten people and
seizing more than 50,000 pounds (about 22.7 tonnes) of marijuana.[@1,2] The
suspects were arraigned in Brooklyn federal court on 21 February 2009 and
ordered held without bail.[@1]

The ring had run since 2003, using cars fitted with hidden compartments to
carry the cannabis from the Akwesasne Reserve in Canada across the St. Regis
Mohawk Reservation to a house on Penelope Avenue in Middle Village,
Queens.[@1,2,3] A routine traffic stop broke the case open when police
pulled over alleged drug runner Mersim Kolenovic, who was driving on a
suspended licence with nine pounds of marijuana in the vehicle.[@1,2]

Prosecutors named Canadian David Sunday, 37, of Snye, Quebec, and American
Randolf "Randy" Square, 44, of Hogansburg, New York, as the ringleaders and
charged both with running a continuing criminal enterprise, a racketeering
statute generally used against organized crime.[@2,3] The eight other
defendants were indicted on conspiracy and money-laundering charges for
transporting, packaging and distributing the marijuana.[@1,3]

A federal official put the street value of the seized cannabis at $150
million and called it one of the biggest pot busts in history.[@2]
Prosecutors said the pipeline moved about 50,000 pounds between January 2003
and January 2009 and netted about $100 million, and the investigation was
continuing with more arrests possible.[@3]
