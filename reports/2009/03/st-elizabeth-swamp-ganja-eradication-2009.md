Narcotics police and members of the Jamaica Defence Force began eradicating a
multimillion-dollar ganja cultivation on swampland in St Elizabeth on 10 March
2009, after discovering it the previous day.[@1] Senior Superintendent Carlton
Wilson, head of the Narcotics Division, said it would take at least a week to
complete the task.[@1] He said the operation followed intelligence of a
significant increase in ganja production, which police had anticipated as
people scrambled for money in the economic recession.[@1]

JDF helicopters ferried the lawmen to the swampland, disturbing the usually
quiet farming communities of Slipe and Burnt Savannah.[@1] By late on 10
March they had destroyed 14 acres of ready-to-reap ganja, and a day earlier
police had disposed of 600 pounds (about 272 kg) of the weed.[@1] Deputy
Superintendent Herman Miller, the senior narcotics officer on the ground,
called the find the tip of the iceberg, and officers reported that one farmer
held about four buckets of ganja seeds ready to sow.[@1]
