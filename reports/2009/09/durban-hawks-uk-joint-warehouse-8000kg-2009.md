South Africa's Hawks seized cannabis and heroin worth $70 million (about £42 million)
in Durban over the weekend of 12–13 September 2009, acting on a tip-off from British
police. [@1] The drugs were believed to be destined for the United Kingdom. [@1,2]

Three Britons, brothers Paul and Jon Beazley and Paul Bromley, and two South Africans
were charged with trafficking and due in court on 21 September. [@1] The South African
Cabinet welcomed the joint work between the Hawks and British agencies as the biggest
drug bust in both countries, saying the suspects had been charged with drug
trafficking. [@2]

## The trail from Heathrow to Durban

The week before, more than 150kg of heroin arriving from South Africa had been
intercepted at London's Heathrow Airport, and the Serious Organised Crime Agency found
another 80kg at an address in Dartford, Kent, leading to one arrest in the UK. [@1]
That trail led back to a Durban warehouse holding 8,000kg of cannabis and 115kg of
heroin, which the Hawks said belonged to the same syndicate as the London
consignments. [@1,2]

South African Broadcasting Corporation reporting cited by the BBC said the run began
in Afghanistan, with consignments shipped to Mozambique for repackaging, then moved
through Durban harbour, concealed and repackaged to be sent on to OR Tambo Airport in
Johannesburg. [@1] A US diplomatic cable later dated the Durban seizure to 14
September but recorded six tonnes of hashish and 116 kilograms of cocaine rather than
the Hawks' figures. [@3]
