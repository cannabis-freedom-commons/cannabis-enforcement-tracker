On 22 January 2009 Mexican troops acting on a tip raided a chili-drying
warehouse near Fresnillo and caught people loading marijuana onto trucks; the
Associated Press reported a seizure of more than 10.3 tons. [@1] A later U.S.
diplomatic cable put the haul at 14.5 tons, plus drug seeds and packing molds,
and said two people were detained at the warehouse. [@2] The warehouse belonged
to the family of Ricardo Monreal, a former Zacatecas governor and then federal
senator, and was used to process chile plants; the family said the building
stood empty in winter and the drugs had been planted for political reasons.
[@2] Monreal denied any knowledge, said his brother had reported a broken lock
to police, and accused the state government of a smear campaign; as of 19 May
his brother Cándido had not been charged. [@1] The two people detained at the
warehouse were freed days earlier when gunmen sprang 53 inmates from the
Cieneguillas prison on 16 May. [@2]

On 22 May Cándido Monreal was indicted for harboring more than 14 tons of
marijuana on the property. [@3] The federal attorney general's office said no
charges or inquiries were pending against Senator Monreal himself, who then
took leave from the Senate. [@3] The cable said federal prosecutors were
investigating the family's ties to the Zeta drug cartel. [@2]
