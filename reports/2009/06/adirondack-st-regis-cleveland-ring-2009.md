Eight arrests broke up a drug ring that smuggled Quebec marijuana through
New York's St. Regis Mohawk Reservation to the Cleveland area, Franklin
County District Attorney Derek Champagne said on 1 July 2009.[@1] Five
suspects were from New York and three from Ohio, and all faced felony drug
charges carrying eight years or more.[@1]

Officers executed search warrants on 15 June at homes in the Cleveland area
and at three North Country residences, including a camp on the St. Regis
River in St. Regis Falls and its owner's Hill Top Stop store, seizing $1.3
million in cash, a pound of cocaine, 14 vehicles and a boat.[@1,2]
Investigators said Cleveland drivers rented a car every other weekend, drove
to the camp, and picked up about 100 pounds of marijuana per load valued at
around $500,000, sometimes paying cash hidden inside a computer hard-drive
shell.[@2]

The organization ran for two to three years, with more than 18 confirmed
loads in one year worth an estimated $18 million to $27 million, according
to a law-enforcement press release.[@2] Authorities linked the network to
Russian organized crime; the case began in 2008 when Ohio investigators
looking into mob money laundering found the group was receiving large
marijuana shipments from upstate New York.[@1] Champagne said more charges
and arrests were likely as the investigation continued.[@1]
