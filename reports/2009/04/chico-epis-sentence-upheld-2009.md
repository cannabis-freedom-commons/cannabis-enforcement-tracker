The US Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit affirmed the conviction and 10-year
mandatory minimum sentence of Chico medical marijuana grower Bryan Epis in an unpublished
decision filed on 8 April 2009. [@1] A three-judge panel rejected his challenges to the
conviction and to the denial of safety-valve relief from the mandatory minimum. [@1]

Epis had been arrested by Butte County officers in June 1997 and convicted by a federal
jury in 2002 of conspiring to manufacture 1,000 marijuana plants within 1,000 feet of a
school and of manufacturing 100 plants near a school. [@1,2] Authorities seized 458 plants
from his home, and he said he was growing for a Chico patients group, admitting he sold
some plants at cost to co-op members. [@2] He held a doctor's recommendation for chronic
back and shoulder pain, but the trial judge excluded medical evidence; California's
legislature later cited the case when urging federal medical-cannabis action. [@2,4] Epis
served a little over two years before his release on bail in 2004, and the panel's 2009
decision ordered him back to prison to serve the remainder. [@2,3]
