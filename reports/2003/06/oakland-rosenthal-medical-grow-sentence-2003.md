Federal judge Charles R. Breyer sentenced Oakland marijuana grower Ed Rosenthal to one day in
prison and a $1,000 fine on 4 June 2003, the most lenient sentence allowed under law. [@1]
Rosenthal had faced up to 100 years in prison and a $4.5 million fine for his January conviction
on felony charges of marijuana cultivation and conspiracy. [@1]

Federal agents had arrested Rosenthal in 2002 for growing marijuana to be supplied for medicinal
use under the City of Oakland's medical marijuana ordinance, which California's 1996 proposition
permitted but federal drug law did not. [@1] DEA agents raided medical clubs and grow rooms
across the San Francisco area on 12 February 2002, seizing 3,163 plants in total, some of them
from Rosenthal's Oakland grow room, where the city had made him an official consultant to its
medical program. [@2]

## A trial without a medical defense

Judge Breyer barred Rosenthal from raising medical marijuana as a defense, and after convicting
him several jurors said they had been misled, sought a new trial, and then wrote to the judge
urging leniency. [@1] At sentencing Breyer found Rosenthal could reasonably have believed he was
acting legally, which let the court avoid minimum sentences of at least five years; Rosenthal
left a free man with credit for a day already served, plus three years of court supervision. [@1]
NORML later recounted that the Ninth Circuit overturned his three felony convictions in 2006
because a juror had consulted outside counsel, while signaling it would likely leave the one-day
sentence standing on any retrial. [@3]
