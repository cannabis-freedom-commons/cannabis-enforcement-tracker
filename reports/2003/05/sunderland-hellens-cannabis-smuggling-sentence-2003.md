Newcastle Crown Court sentenced William Hellens, 44, of Mafeking Street, Pallion, Sunderland, to nine years in prison for conspiracy to import cannabis between March 1998 and April 1999. [@1,2] A jury had convicted him on a majority verdict after he pleaded not guilty. [@1]

Customs officers at Tilbury had found 90 kilos of herbal cannabis with a street value of £280,000 concealed in a consignment of 20 tonnes of andalusite, a gravel-type mineral, from South Africa in April 1999. [@1] The shipment was addressed to a Sunderland warehouse and kept under surveillance, but nobody came to collect it. [@1] Further inquiry led to Stephen Vaughan and Robert John Anderson, who pleaded guilty on 7 February 2000 over that shipment and four earlier ones; the judge ruled a total of 360 kilos worth over £1 million had been imported, jailing Vaughan for six and a half years and Anderson for two. [@1]

Hellens had fled to Spain during the investigation. [@1] In December 2002 Northumbria Police stopped him in Sunderland, where he was using a false identity, over a minor motoring offence. [@1] He had served five years for supplying Ecstasy in 1993 and was still on licence when he joined the conspiracy. [@2]
