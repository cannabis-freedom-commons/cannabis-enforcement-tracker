Rusli bin Sembayang, 44, was tried for trafficking 1,100.2 grams of cannabis found in
his possession at Blk 125 Hougang Avenue 1 on 11 April 2002, an amount that attracted
the mandatory death penalty. [@1] Central Narcotics Bureau officers acting on a lead
demanded entry at about 1.20 a.m.; Rusli briefly emerged from the master bedroom,
apologised and withdrew, then climbed down the rear of the block and fled. [@1]
Officers chased him from a taxi outside Blk 114 to Geylang Serai, where he was
arrested after resistance. [@1]

On the ground floor officers recovered a white plastic bag holding a brown slab of
drugs and a broken paper cutter, three more slabs and a square block nearby, plus
pieces of a broken mobile phone; analysis put the cannabis at 1,100.2 grams, and
loose fragments plus transaction records were found inside the flat. [@2] Rusli
claimed the drugs belonged to a childhood friend named Ahmad who had brought them to
the flat, but the trial judge found Ahmad a complete fabrication, noting Rusli could
give no address, number or whereabouts for him. [@2]

## Sentence upheld

The trial judge held that the prosecution had proved trafficking beyond a reasonable
doubt: Rusli's own statements repeatedly described cutting, weighing and packing
cannabis, and his notes used a code word for the drug. [@2] With more than 15 grams
in his possession the presumption of trafficking applied, and the court found him
guilty and sentenced him to death. [@2] The Court of Appeal dismissed his appeal on
12 March 2003, accepting that Ahmad did not exist and that the conviction could rest
on the retracted confessions. [@2]
