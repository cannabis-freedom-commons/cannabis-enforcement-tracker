On Wednesday 10 September 2003, the St. Lawrence County drug task force joined
Drug Enforcement Administration agents, state police, environmental conservation
officers and National Guard troops to harvest and destroy between 75,000 and
80,000 marijuana plants found within about a mile of each other in the northern
part of the county. [@1,2] Crews cut plants some seven feet tall, hauled them
away in dump trucks and burned them. [@2,3]

Officials valued the haul at $30 to $80 million, at $400 to $1,000 a plant, with
one investigator calling it among the largest single seizures he had seen.
[@1] But Sheriff Gary Jarvis said the plants had not been properly cultivated,
and that at $80 million only if they had been. [@3] He said he could not tell
whether they had been sown where found or were growing wild. [@3] A Cornell
Cooperative Extension examination of a sample concluded it was not really
marijuana but some sort of native hemp, and the sheriff agreed the harvest
looked like wild plants with low levels of THC, the compound prized in
marijuana. [@3]

No arrests were made, and the sheriff said none were expected, with no
indication the landowners had planted the crop. [@2,3] Samples were sent to a
Drug Enforcement Administration laboratory to test drug quality and fix a real
street value. [@3]
