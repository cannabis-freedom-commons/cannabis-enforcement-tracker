Mexican soldiers arrested seven members of the country's elite anti-drug
agency in Tijuana on 10 January 2003 after finding nearly five tons of
marijuana hidden unreported in the agency's own building. [@1] The detained
men belonged to FEADS, the Special Prosecutor's Office for Attention to
Crimes Against Health, and included the Tijuana post's commander, Miguel
Angel Uribe. [@1] Soldiers also found two civilians in the building who had
been seized three days earlier and were presumed to own the
marijuana. [@1]

The Attorney General's office said the agents had never reported the seizure
as the law required, raising suspicion they were in traffickers'
pay. [@1] The seven were accused of offering to free the two dealers and
hand back the drugs for a US$2 million bribe. [@2]

FEADS had been created in 1997 after its predecessor agency was disbanded
when its agents were found serving traffickers. [@1] Days after the Tijuana
arrests, soldiers raided FEADS offices in 11 states and questioned all 200
staff; Attorney General Rafael Macedo de la Concha closed the agency
permanently and moved its work to an organised-crime unit. [@2] Macedo said
more than 800 staff had been suspended, fired or charged since December
2000, with 1,300 more under investigation. [@2]

The closure showed the military's growing counter-drug role, a shift that
Amnesty International's Eric Olson called risky for an institution beyond
public scrutiny. [@2]
