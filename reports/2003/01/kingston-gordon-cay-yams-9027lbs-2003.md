Police seized 9,027 pounds (about 4.1 tonnes) of ganja hidden in export boxes of yams
at the Gordon Cay container terminal in Kingston on 5 January 2003.[@1] The Contraband
Enforcement Team found the drug during routine checks of containers waiting to be loaded
onto ships bound for the United States.[@1]

The ganja was packed in cardboard cartons marked "Produce of Jamaica" alongside genuine
yam cartons labelled "Product of Jamaica", 248 boxes in all.[@1] Police were alerted by the
weight: an export box of yams standardly weighs 45 pounds, but these weighed 55
pounds.[@1] The shipment was destined for New York and New Jersey.[@1]

The paper put the street value at $1.4 billion.[@1] No arrests had been made, though the head
of the narcotics police, Senior Superintendent Carl Williams, said officers were following
several leads.[@1] Williams said a US-side discovery of the load would have hurt Jamaica's yam
exports, which at their peak reached over US$12 million a year.[@1]
