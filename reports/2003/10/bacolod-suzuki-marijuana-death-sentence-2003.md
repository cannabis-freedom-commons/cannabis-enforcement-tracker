Hedishi Suzuki, a Japanese national, was arrested on 12 April 1994 as he boarded a
Manila-bound flight at Bacolod airport, where officers found about 1.9 kilograms of
marijuana in a box of biscuits he was carrying. [@2] The Bacolod City Regional Court
sentenced him to death on 7 December 1994, making him the first foreigner condemned
since capital punishment was restored in the Philippines and the fifteenth person
sentenced overall. [@2] The trial judge said Suzuki, having committed a crime lawmakers
defined as heinous, had forfeited his human right to exist. [@2]

Amnesty International warned the trial may have been prejudiced because Suzuki did
not speak or read English and was reportedly interrogated without an interpreter and
made to sign documents he could not understand. [@2] Possession of 750 grams or more
of marijuana then carried the death penalty. [@2]

## Sentence reduced on review

On automatic review, the Supreme Court upheld the conviction on 23 October 2003,
finding the airport search of the box marked "Bongbong's piaya" lawful, but ruled
that 1,547.07 grams of dried marijuana fruiting tops did not merit the maximum
penalty. [@1] It affirmed guilt and modified the sentence to reclusion perpetua,
lowering the fine to one million pesos. [@1]
