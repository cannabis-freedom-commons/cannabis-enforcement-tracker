On 29 October 2003 Attorney General Bill Lockyer announced in San Jose that California’s Campaign
Against Marijuana Planting had eradicated 466,054 cannabis plants during the 2003 season, about
100,000 more than the previous record. [@1,2] The Department of Justice valued the removed crop at
more than $1.9 billion. [@1]

## Season totals

Lockyer said CAMP ran 182 raids in 32 counties from late July through early October, averaging
nearly 2,500 plants per raid. [@1] Officers made 35 arrests and seized 50 weapons. [@1] About 75
percent of gardens were on public land, a 20 percent increase over the prior year, and plantation-
sized grows linked to Mexican drug organizations accounted for 84 percent of plants removed. [@1,2]

## Violence and traps

The attorney general’s office said the 2003 season saw armed garden tenders fire on officers in
Southern California and that four suspects were killed in Northern California after aiming at
law enforcement. [@1] Agents also reported booby-traps including camouflaged barbed wire, bear traps
and hidden razor blades; no officers suffered serious injuries. [@1] CAMP Commander Val Jimenez said
cooperation among state, federal and local agencies made the 21st season successful. [@1]
