The Leningrad Oblast court sentenced a Russian member of an international drug
group to 18 years in a strict-regime colony and a fine of 750,000 roubles for
smuggling hashish and attempting to sell it. [@1] The verdict had recently
entered into legal force when the Interior Ministry published its account. [@1]
The courier who brought the consignment was separately sentenced in Belarus to
14 years in prison. [@1]

Belarusian customs officers found more than 70 kg of hashish in a
Moldovan-registered SUV at the Brest international checkpoint. [@1] The hashish
had been packed into 720 packages hidden in the cavities of the vehicle's sill
panels and moved from Spain to Poland and on to Moldova before the crossing.
[@1] Belarusian investigators asked Moscow colleagues to join the operation
because the consignment was bound for Russia, and the Russian side was led by
Colonel of Justice Grigory Levusenkov of the Interior Ministry's Investigation
Department. [@1]

Investigators replaced most of the hashish with dummies and ran a controlled
delivery under police watch. [@1] The courier drove to a village in Pskov Oblast
and then to a filling station on the Kiev highway in Leningrad Oblast, where he
met the Russian contact. [@1] Their car got stuck on a forest clearing in Vyborg
district, and officers detained the Russian as the packages were handed over.
[@1]

According to the ministry's account, the Russian admitted under questioning that
the group planned regular deliveries of drugs to Russia. [@1] Cases against the
other members of the group were separated out, and Russian police put the
suspects, most of them foreign citizens, on an international wanted list. [@1]
