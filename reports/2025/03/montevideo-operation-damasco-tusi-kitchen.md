Montevideo police raided homes in the Conciliación and Paso Molino
neighbourhoods and detained three people who had outstanding arrest warrants,
then searched a vehicle. [@1] The operation was carried out by the Departmental
Anti-Drug Brigade of the Montevideo police headquarters as Operation Damasco,
aimed at an organisation making and selling synthetic drugs at retail across the
capital. [@1] Police said the production side of the group was led by an
Ecuadorian man who had entered Uruguay shortly before. [@1] Investigators found
a tusi kitchen in one of the homes, equipped with everything needed to make the
pink drug. [@1]

Officers seized 56 grams of marijuana together with the synthetic drugs and the
equipment, vehicle, precision scales, five rounds of ammunition, four spent
casings and five mobile phones. [@1] The marijuana was one item in a haul the
ministry listed as 166 tablets and 9.12 grams of MDMA, eight bags of tusi, a jar
with 276 grams of pink substance and a bag of ketamine; those synthetic
quantities are outside this report's figures. [@1] Teledoce, reporting the
ministry's statement the same day, described the 276 grams as pink cocaine. [@2]

The three detainees were handed to prosecutor Llorente of the second-shift drug
prosecution office. [@1] Judge Islas of the first-shift organised-crime court
then convicted L.M.M.I. as the perpetrator of production of prohibited narcotics
in real concurrence with dealing in them. [@1] The same ruling sentenced
D.E.B.F. and M.G.B.R. to two years and two months in prison each, as
perpetrators of a continuing offence of supplying prohibited narcotics for
payment. [@1]
