Karl Richter, 52, from Fourth Avenue, Clase in Swansea was sentenced to 11 years
in prison at Chelmsford Crown Court. [@1] He appeared by video link for the
sentencing hearing. [@1] He had admitted conspiracy to evade the prohibition on
importing a controlled class B drug. [@1] The cannabis import sentence was two
years and nine months, to run consecutively after an eight-year and three-month
sentence for conspiracy to supply cocaine, making 11 years in total. [@1]

On 1 March 2022, officers at Tilbury Port uncovered 408 kilos of cannabis hidden
in a large plywood crate. [@1] The crate held a precision machine lathe whose
base concealed 408 silver-wrapped 1 kg parcels of cannabis that had come from
South Africa. [@1] The lathe was to be delivered to a textile business used as
the delivery address for the consignment. [@1] Prosecutors said Richter was a
very important link between his UK contacts and the South African importer,
shown by patterns of phone calls and meetings. [@1] The National Crime Agency
said the wider organised group arranged two cannabis importations from Canada
and South Africa totalling 676 kilos. [@2]

Richter was also sentenced for conspiracy to supply 40 kg of cocaine with a
separate group. [@1] That included a July 2023 journey in which a later stop
found a box with 7 kg of high-purity cocaine valued at the time at £189,000.
[@1] After his arrest, a search of his property found £4,000 in cash, mobile
phones and Class A and B drugs. [@1] The judge called the importation a
sophisticated and well-planned attempt and Richter a crucial link between the UK
and South African operations. [@1]
