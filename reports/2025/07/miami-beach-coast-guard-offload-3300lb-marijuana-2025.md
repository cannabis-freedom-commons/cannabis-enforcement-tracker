The U.S. Coast Guard brought about $20.1 million worth of illegal drugs ashore
at Miami Beach on Wednesday 2 July 2025 after stopping four smuggling attempts
in the Caribbean Sea. [@1] Crews seized about 2,200 pounds of cocaine and 3,300
pounds of marijuana, roughly 1,497 kilograms of cannabis, according to the
agency's news release. [@1] The Coast Guard release quoted by Seapower puts the
offload at approximately 2,220 pounds of cocaine and 3,320 pounds of marijuana
worth an estimated $20.1 million. [@2]

The contraband came from four interdictions in the Caribbean Sea by the crews of
the Canadian patrol ship HMCS William Hall and the Dutch patrol vessel HNLMS
Friesland, each with Coast Guard law enforcement detachments aboard. [@2] CBS12
reported that the drugs were found in joint operations with ships from Canada
and the Netherlands, along with U.S. Navy and Coast Guard teams. [@1] The U.S.
Navy destroyer USS Cole, the Coast Guard Tactical Law Enforcement teams South
and Pacific, and Helicopter Interdiction Tactical Squadron Jacksonville were
among the assets involved, according to the agency's release. [@2]

Cmdr. Brian Gismervik, commanding officer of the Northland, said stopping
illicit narcotics from reaching U.S. shores was a team effort. [@1] Joint
Interagency Task Force-South in Key West detects and monitors illegal drug
traffic by air and sea, and control of an operation passes to the Coast Guard
under the Miami-based Seventh Coast Guard District once an interdiction is
imminent. [@2] The Northland is a 270-foot Reliance-class medium endurance
cutter homeported in Portsmouth under Coast Guard Atlantic Area Command. [@2]
