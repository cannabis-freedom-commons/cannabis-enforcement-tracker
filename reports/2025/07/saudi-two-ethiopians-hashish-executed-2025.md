On 10 July 2025, Saudi Arabia's Ministry of Interior announced the execution of two Ethiopian
nationals convicted of drug trafficking.[@1,2] The men, Khalil Qasim Muhammad Omar and Murad
Yakub Adam Siyo, had been found guilty of smuggling hashish, a cannabis resin, into the
kingdom.[@1,2]

## A court confirmed the death sentences

The ministry said the pair were referred to a competent court, which confirmed the charges and
sentenced them to death.[@2] Saudi law makes drug smuggling punishable by death, and both
men were put to death on the hashish charge.[@1,2]

## Foreign executions passed 100 by July

The two deaths brought the year's total to 189 executions, of which 88 were Saudi citizens and
more than 100 were foreigners, according to an Agence France-Presse tally.[@1,2] In 2024 the
100-foreigner mark had only been reached in November.[@2] Amnesty International condemned the
surge, with its Middle East deputy director Kristine Beckerle calling it a horrifying trend of
foreign nationals put to death for crimes that should never carry the death penalty.[@2]
