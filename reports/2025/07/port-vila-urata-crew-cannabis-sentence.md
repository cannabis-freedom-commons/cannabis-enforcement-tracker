Vanuatu's Supreme Court convicted Urata crew members Assak Takau, Martin Kasso
and Raynold Paul Philip after each pleaded guilty to one count of unlawful
possession of cannabis. [@2] Takau was sentenced to 12 months' imprisonment,
suspended for 12 months, with 60 hours of community work. [@2] Kasso was
sentenced to 8 months' imprisonment, suspended for 12 months, with 50 hours of
community work. [@2] Philip was sentenced to 6 months' imprisonment, suspended
for 12 months, with 40 hours of community work. [@2] Each man was placed under
supervision for 12 months. [@2] Each man was ordered to attend a drug and
alcohol rehabilitation programme as part of that supervision. [@2] The court
ordered the seized cannabis to be destroyed immediately. [@2] The sentences were
imposed in Port Vila on 29 July 2025. [@2]

On 23 October 2024 the Police Drugs Unit received information from the owner of
the Urata Riki that crew members were involved in cannabis activity. [@2] Police
searched the men and their personal effects when the ship arrived at the wharf
and found each of them in possession of cannabis. [@2] Takau, the ship's
captain, was found with 0.58 kilograms of dried cannabis plant material in 12
packages inside a black shopping bag. [@2] Kasso was found with 107.5 grams of
dried cannabis in four packages inside a black basket. [@2] Philip was found
with 7.25 grams of dried cannabis in two packages inside a handmade basket. [@2]
Forensic testing confirmed all the exhibits were cannabis, and all three men
admitted possession when cautioned on 24 October 2024. [@2]

Urata Shipping owner John Amos, the Tongoa member of parliament, later addressed
how domestic vessels were being used to transport marijuana around Vanuatu. [@1]
He spoke as someone affected by the trade because his former captain Takau and
two other crew members had been caught in possession of drugs by police in
October the previous year. [@1] He described the shipping routes as a drug
superhighway serving many islands, naming Epi in particular. [@1]
