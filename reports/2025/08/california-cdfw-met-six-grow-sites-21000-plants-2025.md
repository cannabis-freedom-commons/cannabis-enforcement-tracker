California Department of Fish and Wildlife officers arrested eight people during
raids on six illegal cannabis grow sites in July and August 2025. [@1] The raids
took place in Los Angeles, San Bernardino, San Mateo, Santa Cruz and Tehama
counties. [@1] The grow sites were on lands the public can enter, including
Forest Service property, municipal water district land and large underdeveloped
private properties. [@1] CDFW’s Marijuana Enforcement Team led half of the raids
and supported the others, and the agency said the Cannabis Enforcement Program,
the multiagency Eradication and Prevention of Illicit Cannabis (EPIC) program
and local agencies helped. [@1] The eight suspects were arrested on possible
charges including depositing hazardous substances on another's land, cannabis
cultivation that polluted state waters, cannabis cultivation causing substantial
environmental harm to public lands, cultivation with unlawful take, and
resisting arrest. [@1] CBS News reported that CDFW did not identify any of the
suspects. [@3] The governor's office put the same operation at 21,000 plants,
eight arrests, two firearms and banned pesticides across the five counties. [@2]

Officers eradicated 21,000 cannabis plants, which CDFW estimated would have used
up to 9.5 million gallons of water over their growing cycles. [@1] They
destroyed more than 150 pounds (about 68 kilograms) of cannabis already
processed for sale. [@1] Officers seized two illegally possessed firearms and
restored water flow at five places where water had been diverted. [@1] They
removed more than 2,300 pounds of trash and infrastructure and took away partly
used bottles of carbofuran and methamidophos, pesticides banned in the United
States. [@1] CDFW said one suspect at a site where a firearm was found tried to
flee when officers identified themselves and was caught by a team dog. [@1]
Plants collected as evidence at one San Bernardino County site tested positive
for carbofuran, a pesticide confirmed in a soda bottle found there by an
independent laboratory. [@1]

Based on evidence at all six sites and interviews with suspects, the team
concluded that transnational criminal organisations ran the grows. [@1] Three of
the six sites were found after reports from members of the public, including a
hunter scouting for deer. [@1] CDFW describes its Marijuana Enforcement Team,
known as MET, as a 10-officer unit that specialises in raiding clandestine grows
run by transnational criminal organisations on public lands. [@1] Governor Gavin
Newsom said illegal grows threaten the balance of the state's water system and
that violators would be held accountable. [@2]
