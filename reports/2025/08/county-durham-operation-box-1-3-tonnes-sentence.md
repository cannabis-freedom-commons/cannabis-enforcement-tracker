A gang which imported millions of pounds worth of illegal drugs into the
north-east of England was jailed. [@1] The operation involved drug mules
carrying Class A and B drugs from Thailand to County Durham, according to the
North East Regional Organised Crime Unit. [@1] Jordan Stoker, 30, from Coundon
received the largest sentence, a 19-year term from Teesside Crown Court. [@1]
Stoker admitted conspiracy to supply cocaine and conspiracy to fraudulently
evade a ban on importing controlled drugs. [@2] The Sowerby brothers
Christopher, Ian and Shaun operated a safe house where cocaine and cannabis were
stored for onward distribution. [@1] Christopher Sowerby and Shaun Sowerby were
each jailed for 13 years and one month, and Ian Sowerby for ten years and ten
months. [@2]

Matthew Goodwin and Trelawny Brown acted as drug mules travelling to Thailand,
while Brian Stoker and Paul Lamb moved couriers to and from airports. [@1]
Goodwin was jailed for three years and nine months and Brown for three years and
four months. [@2] Brian Stoker was jailed for five years and two months and Paul
Lamb for seven years and two months for the importation conspiracy. [@2]
Stoker's mother Sarah Leighton and half-brother Ethan Smith counted and stored
cash made from the group's criminality. [@1] Leighton and Smith admitted money
laundering and received suspended terms of 22 months and 18 months respectively.
[@1]

The unit worked with UK Border Force under the codename Operation Box to
identify the gang. [@1] Officers seized about £280,000 in cash, 1.3 tonnes of
cannabis and more than 30 kg of cocaine, with an estimated combined street value
of almost £13 million. [@1] All ten defendants admitted offences including
conspiracy to fraudulently evade a prohibition on the importation of controlled
drugs. [@1]
