Homegrown Bar and Grill LLC operates Little Amsterdam Coffee and Cannabis Lounge
at 10 Dronningens Gade on Main Street in St. Thomas. [@3] It held a valid Hemp
Products Retailer License from the Department of Licensing and Consumer Affairs
when the raid occurred. [@2] On 29 April 2025 agents from the Health Department,
Licensing and Consumer Affairs, the Office of Cannabis Regulation and the Virgin
Islands Police Department entered the Main Street store without a warrant and
without advance notice. [@3] The agents seized hemp-derived products from the
retail floor and storage areas, taking what the complaint describes as a
significant portion of active retail inventory. [@3] The complaint values the
seized inventory at approximately 18,000 dollars at wholesale value. [@3]

The retailer says it applied for a licence, received it and bought products from
licensed wholesalers before opening. [@1] Its attorney says hemp-derived
products like those confiscated had been legal under federal law since 2018 and
the territory issued licences to qualified sellers through its own hemp retail
program. [@1] The complaint describes the stock as both non-intoxicating and
intoxicating hemp-derived products that were federally legal when purchased and
stocked. [@2] The complaint alleges the seizure was made without lawful
authority, without statutory basis and without payment of just compensation in
violation of the Fifth Amendment Takings Clause. [@2] The legislature approved a
measure requiring sale of such products only with a Cannabis Regulation licence
and the governor signed it on 23 January 2026. [@1]

In February 2026 the business sued the Virgin Islands government, the attorney
general, the health commissioner, the licensing commissioner and the cannabis
office director, saying the products were legal when seized and no compensation
was paid. [@1] The federal complaint filed on 19 February 2026 raises Fifth,
Fourth and Fourteenth Amendment claims plus a civil rights claim and seeks
declaratory and injunctive relief with just compensation for the seized
inventory. [@3] The business says it still holds at least 60,000 dollars in
intoxicating hemp inventory that would be subject to surrender under the new
law. [@2]
