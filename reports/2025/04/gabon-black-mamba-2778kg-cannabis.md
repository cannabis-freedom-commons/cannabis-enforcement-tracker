Gabon’s National Agency of National Parks, the ANPN, led a joint operation named
Black Mamba with special services, gendarmes, the national police, a special GAS
unit and the immigration and documentation directorate. [@1] The operation
intercepted 130 people described as being in an irregular immigration status,
most of them from Burkina Faso, Togo, Benin and Nigeria. [@1] L’Union reported
that the arrested people were picked up in a series of targeted operations in
sensitive areas of Gabonese territory. [@2]

Authorities seized 2,778 kilograms of cannabis in Operation Black Mamba, a haul
the outlets described as a record. [@1] An investigating judge at the special
tribunal signed a destruction order authorising the public incineration of the
cargo. [@3] L’Union said the cannabis was estimated to be worth more than 500
million CFA francs on the market and was publicly burned on the order of the
Libreville special tribunal’s investigating judge. [@2]

Colonel Hubert Ella Ekogha, the ANPN’s technical director and a special adviser
to the president, praised coordination among the forces and called the operation
a turning point in the fight against illicit trafficking. [@1] The agencies said
the operation formed part of government efforts to secure sensitive zones, in
particular protected areas. [@1]
