Special Task Force officers arrested two men after stopping a truck in forest in
the Jaithari police station area of Anuppur district. [@1] The men were Ankit
Vishwakarma, of Sidhi, and Dhananjay Singh Patel, of Satna. [@1] Both said they
were returning empty-handed from Jharkhand because they had not got any freight.
[@1]

Officers recovered 599 kilograms of ganja, the local word for cannabis, which
the STF valued at about Rs 1.8 crore. [@1] A Jabalpur team led by DSP Santosh
Tiwari tracked the truck from forest near Bilaspur in Chhattisgarh and followed
it for nearly 152 kilometres. [@1] The truck under watch bore registration JH 02
BL 7103. [@1] In the Jaithari area the officers blocked the road with a JCB
machine and intercepted the truck. [@3] Police told the Free Press Journal that
the chase lasted 14 hours, ended in the jungles of Anuppur on Thursday, and
yielded 600 kilograms of ganja. [@2]

On Wednesday at about 11 am, STF SP Rajesh Singh Bhadoria was told that a truck
was carrying marijuana from Odisha to Madhya Pradesh through Chhattisgarh. [@1]
STF DG Pankaj Srivastava ordered two teams and a tighter watch along the Madhya
Pradesh–Chhattisgarh border. [@1] The truck looked empty from the rear, an
appearance the officers treated as a ruse. [@1] The men said a body maker had
built a secret cabin of iron sheets, about two metres long, behind the driver's
cabin. [@1] They said they had copied methods from the film Pushpa. [@1] The STF
SP said the consignment was to be dumped in Maihar and then distributed across
districts in the Mahakaushal, Bundelkhand and Vindhya regions to meet New Year
demand. [@1] A white car, registration MP 65 C 4716, had followed as a support
vehicle; its occupants fled and were being traced. [@1] The STF was still
searching for the truck owner and other members of the network. [@1] Police told
the Free Press Journal that the tip named a truck coming from Sambal in Odisha.
[@2]
