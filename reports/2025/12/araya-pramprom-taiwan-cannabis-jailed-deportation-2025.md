A Taiwanese man posted a public appeal through the popular Facebook page
Drama-addict asking for help for his Thai girlfriend, Araya Pramprom, who he
said was held in a Taiwan prison in poor health and facing delayed paperwork.
[@1] Araya was arrested for smuggling cannabis into Taiwan, and during the
investigation she cooperated fully with Taiwanese police, giving information
that helped them arrest the entire drug trafficking network. [@1]

A Taiwan court then reduced her sentence under Article 66, a Criminal Code
provision that allows a lighter penalty for a suspect who helps investigators,
and she had served about five months at the time of the appeal. [@1]

Her boyfriend said she had served enough time for deportation to begin, but
confusion over paperwork between agencies in the two countries had halted
progress. [@1] Thai authorities said they must wait for approval documents from
Taiwan, while Taiwanese authorities said they need approval documents from
Thailand first, leaving the case in a deadlock. [@1]

Her underlying heart condition worried her boyfriend most, after she began
experiencing severe symptoms including fatigue and panting inside the prison,
and he urged the agencies to speed up deportation so she could receive medical
treatment in Thailand. [@1] Initial reports indicated coordination with the
Mirror Foundation, a Thai charity that helps people detained or stranded abroad,
but those involved were still awaiting clear instructions from government
agencies. [@1]

RTI reported that Taiwanese aviation police and Taipei customs found cannabis
hidden in check-in baggage in an X-ray check at Taiwan Taoyuan International
Airport in early June 2025: a Thai tourist carried 26 parcels weighing 15.039 kg
in total, which was reported to the Taoyuan City prosecutors. [@2] That
traveller was a 27-year-old woman who had flown from Bangkok, and police said
they believed a trafficking ring recruited her to carry the drugs in on a
tourist visa, though she claimed she was only carrying a friend's belongings for
no payment. [@2] The Taoyuan City prosecutors charged her in mid-August 2025
under Taiwan's Narcotics Hazard Prevention Act, an offence carrying life
imprisonment or at least 10 years in prison. [@2]
