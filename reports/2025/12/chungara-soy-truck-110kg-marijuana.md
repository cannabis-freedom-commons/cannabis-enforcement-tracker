Customs officers at Chile's Chungará border crossing, on the frontier with
Bolivia, stopped a truck carrying bulk soybeans in transit to Colombia and
arrested its driver, a Bolivian identified by the initials G.O.R. [@2] The stop
came on the eve of Christmas Eve. [@3] Inspectors noticed an anomaly in the
tarpaulin covering the load, which led them to 48 packages of different sizes
and shapes hidden among the soy. [@2] Infobae reported the marijuana seizure
five days after authorities announced a separate haul of almost 700 kg of
cocaine soaked into export timber sent from Bolivia to Spain. [@1]

A field test established that the packages held 110 kilos 100 grams of
marijuana. [@2] The drug, the driver and all the evidence were handed to
Carabineros' OS7 anti-drug unit in Arica. [@2] Radio Agricultura reported that
the load had been concealed strategically among the bulk soy, which at first
made it hard to detect. [@4] The acting director of the Arica customs office,
César Zamora, said the seizure showed the inspectors' experience in spotting
details of a cargo that had been tampered with. [@2]

The Arica y Parinacota prosecutor's office charged the Bolivian driver and
obtained an order holding him in preventive detention while the investigation
continues. [@4] The charging hearing was held on 26 December, the day the
customs service and La Tercera announced the case. [@3]
