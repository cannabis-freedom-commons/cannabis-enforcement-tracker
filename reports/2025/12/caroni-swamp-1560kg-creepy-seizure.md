The Trinidad and Tobago Police Service recovered a stashed marijuana cargo in
the Caroni Swamp on Wednesday night. [@1] The operation involved the
Multi-Option Police Section of Special Branch, the Coastal and Air Support Unit,
the Special Investigations Unit and other investigative, tactical and
intelligence teams, according to a TTPS release. [@1] Officers navigated the
mangroves overnight to recover the cargo and transported it to the Chaguaramas
Heliport for processing by the SIU and crime-scene investigators. [@1] No
arrests were made and investigations are continuing. [@1]

Officers recovered 1,560 kilogrammes of creepy marijuana, a potent cannabis
variety. [@1] Police gave the estimated street value of the marijuana as
$171,205,320. [@1] The intelligence reportedly came through the United
States-installed radar at Crown Point, Tobago. [@1] The TTPS said this was the
first seizure made using data from the newly installed US-supplied
military-grade radar, described as capable of 360-degree long-range aerial and
maritime surveillance. [@1]

Commissioner Allister Guevarro described the overnight exercise at Chaguaramas
as a significant milestone and warned criminals that the TTPS would be
relentless in searching for them. [@1] Homeland Security Minister Roger
Alexander thanked foreign counterparts for technology provided following an
application from the Prime Minister. [@1] The radar installation had sparked
public debate after being set up without public announcement, with some Tobago
political leaders and civil groups saying they were not consulted. [@1] Former
radar-centre director Norman Dindial questioned whether the Tobago radar could
see into the Caroni Swamp and asked why no suspects were caught, while former
commissioner Gary Griffith said publicising the capability could tip off
criminals. [@1]
