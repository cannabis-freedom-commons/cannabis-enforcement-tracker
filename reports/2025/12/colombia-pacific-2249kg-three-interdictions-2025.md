Colombian Navy units intercepted two boats in Pacific waters carrying more than
two tonnes of drugs, crewed by six Colombians and Panamanians and bound for
Central America, the commander of the Buenaventura coast guard station said.
[@1] On the first boat, sailors found 1,103 kilograms of marijuana and
communications equipment. [@1] In the second interception the Navy seized 739
kilograms of cocaine hydrochloride and 425 kilograms of marijuana. [@2] The
three seizures yielded 84 bales holding 2,397 packages of drugs, Defence
Ministry figures showed. [@1]

In the third interception the crew of a boat fled and threw about 400 packages
into the sea. [@2] Sailors recovered 401.88 kilograms of marijuana from the
water after the third boat's crew fled, according to the Defence Ministry
account. [@2] An intelligence alert let a naval aircraft guide a rapid reaction
unit of the Bahía Solano coast guard station to intercept the boat, its acting
commander, Lieutenant Nicolás Andrés Falon Guzmán, said. [@1]

The three interdictions took the Navy's Pacific seizures for 2025 to 66.3 tonnes
of drugs, of which 41.3 tonnes were cocaine hydrochloride and 25 tonnes
marijuana. [@1] President Gustavo Petro said the operation killed no one and
asked whether more cocaine was leaving through Ecuador, urging that the
neighbouring country be helped. [@1] Bogotá councillor Papo Amín mocked Petro's
interpretation on X, saying the fishing boats were unlikely to be carrying such
cargo. [@1]

El País said the three procedures took place at different points of the Pacific,
a region used by criminal structures to move drugs toward international routes,
mainly to Central America. [@2]
