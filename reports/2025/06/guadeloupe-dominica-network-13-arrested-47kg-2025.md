Between 30 May and 2 June 2025, the ten suspects were arrested in Guadeloupe
during a large judicial operation by several gendarmerie units under the
direction of the JIRS. [@1] After their arrest, the ten suspects were placed
under formal investigation and held in provisional detention. [@1]
France-Antilles reported that ten alleged traffickers were formally investigated
and jailed in Guadeloupe on suspicion of taking part in a supply network that
imported 15 tonnes of various drugs over two years. [@2]

On 15 July 2024, the same research section learned that a drug delivery was
imminent on a beach at Basse-Terre in Guadeloupe. [@1] Supported by the
Guadeloupe GIGN branch, investigators arrested three people landing on the beach
and seized nearly 800 kg of cocaine and 27 kg of cannabis herb that had come
from Dominica. [@1] France-Antilles dates the joint Research Section and GIGN
operation to the night of 14 to 15 July 2024, when three people were arrested on
a beach at Capesterre-Belle-Eau. [@2] It identified the three as two
Guadeloupeans and one Dominican national caught recovering a cargo of 780 kg of
cocaine and 26 kg of cannabis herb from Dominica. [@2] The May-June 2025
operation also seized 20 kg of cannabis herb, a 900-gram cocaine brick, a
category-B handgun, a double-barrel shotgun, ammunition, luxury clothing and
leather goods, cash, jewellery and six vehicles. [@1] France-Antilles lists the
same follow-on seizures as a category-B handgun, a double-barrel shotgun with
various ammunition, a 900-gram cocaine brick, 20 kg of cannabis herb, luxury
clothing and leather goods with jewellery, large cash sums and six vehicles.
[@2]

Investigators established that at least 15 tonnes of drugs, mainly cocaine and
to a lesser extent cannabis, had been transported from Venezuela to Guadeloupe
beaches over the previous two years. [@1] The sea operation off Dominica was run
by Dominican nationals. [@1] Because of the scale of the seizure, the
Fort-de-France Interregional Specialised Jurisdiction took the case and
travelled to Guadeloupe with prosecutors, an investigating judge and a
liberty-and-detention judge. [@1] The investigation also found money-laundering
through fund transfers to Dominica and Venezuela estimated at more than 450,000
euros. [@1]
