The special services reported that 17 people, including eight women, were
arrested in the operation. [@1] The operation took place at the Boulbinet
fishing port in Conakry. [@1] The operation at Bourbinet was conducted over two
days. [@2] The stock was discovered in inhabited houses in Boulbinet, where
people in their forties, fifties and younger lived. [@1] Principal police
commissioner Foromo Soropogui presented the seizure on Monday 30 June 2025. [@1]

Soropogui said officers seized 773 kg of chanvre indien, the French term used in
Guinea for cannabis. [@1] Soropogui described Kush as a substance separate from
the 773 kg of chanvre indien. [@1] Guinéenews likewise reported 773 kg of
chanvre indien alongside a separate quantity of the drug Kush. [@2] Guinéenews
also reported 17 arrests, including eight women. [@2]

Soropogui said the operation had active support from enforcement units and ended
without incident. [@1] Soropogui said 80 percent of the drugs came from Sierra
Leone and that several arrested people were Sierra Leonean. [@1] The special
services conducted the two-day network operation through Operation Épervier.
[@1] Authorities placed the operation within a national Zéro Kush strategy
intended to eliminate Kush from Guinea by 2026. [@1] After the seizure,
authorities announced stronger border surveillance and changes to their
intervention tactics. [@2]
