Chilean customs, navy and police officers arrested a Peruvian man in Santiago
who was trying to send liquid cannabis concentrate to Easter Island by sea. [@4]
Prosecutors in Valparaíso said the man was the leader of the organisation and
had lived on the island for many years. [@4] The case began when the Valparaíso
customs drug unit flagged cargo bound by sea for the island, and the navy's
maritime police identified the sender. [@2] Chile's customs service, the navy's
maritime intelligence and investigation department (DIPOLMAR), the Carabineros
OS-7 anti-drug unit and the Valparaíso organised-crime prosecutor's office
worked the case together. [@2] The case was a multi-month investigation into the
sea route from Valparaíso to Easter Island, and the prosecutor called it a new
trafficking method. [@2]

Officers seized 251 cartridges of tetrahydrocannabinol, the psychoactive
compound in cannabis, in liquid form, and a 2020 Toyota Tundra. [@1] Customs
said each cartridge held one gram of liquid, enough for 200 puffs or doses, a
total of 50,200 doses. [@2] The navy's maritime governor for Valparaíso said it
was the first time this substance had been seized in ampoule form. [@3]
Carabineros' Valparaíso prefecture valued the haul at more than 25 million
pesos, an unusual amount for Rapa Nui. [@4]

The public health institute said the THC concentrations exposed vapers to heart
toxicity from as few as two doses. [@2] The suspect was taken to Valparaíso and
brought before a detention-control hearing at the Valparaíso guarantee court on
12 June 2025, where prosecutors formalised him for drug trafficking. [@3] The
court granted a 100-day investigation period and remanded him in preventive
detention as the prosecution requested. [@3] Drug trafficking carries a minimum
of five years and one day in prison, the classification the court accepted. [@2]
