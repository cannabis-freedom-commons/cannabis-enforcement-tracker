Dominican prosecutors and agents found 3,004 packages of suspected marijuana
wrapped in aluminium foil in 115 boxes inside a container at the Port of Haina
Oriental on 1 May 2025. [@2] The DNCD called it the largest plant-drug haul ever
seized in the country. [@2] The cargo manifest described the container's
contents as cat litter, and DNCD spokesman Carlos Devers said intelligence
alerts led to an X-ray inspection that showed suspicious images. [@2] The load
weighed 6,740 pounds, about 3,057 kilograms. [@3]

The national forensics institute, INACIF, confirmed the substance was marijuana
of the illegal-market variant known as cripy. [@3] Fiscales and agents raided a
home in Residencial Garden City II in Villa Mella, Santo Domingo Norte,
arresting three men and one woman described as linked to an international
trafficking structure. [@4] Authorities were actively seeking José Francisco
Acosta de la Rosa as one of the cargo's consignees. [@2] Dominican officials
said the drug was supposedly loaded at Puerto Santo Tomás de Castilla, and
copies of the customs paperwork were handed to Guatemalan authorities. [@1]

Guatemala's anti-narcotics deputy minister, Claudia Palencia, said her country
was working with Spain and with the Ministerio Público to establish whether the
drug left Guatemala or was contaminated in another port before reaching Spain.
[@1] Palencia said the inquiries would reach public employees at the ports and
airports to establish whether they were involved in drug trafficking. [@1] The
Ministerio Público's Fiscalía contra Delitos de Narcoactividad opened an
investigation of its own and requested information from several institutions.
[@1] The state port company Empornac said it had not been notified of any
investigation involving its staff, and it cited international security
certifications and coordination with K9 units, container-control and customs
bodies. [@1] Congressman Héctor Aldana said two of the shipments reached the
Dominican Republic with drugs and that another with drugs left for Madrid a few
days earlier. [@1] The deputy said the government would sign agreements to
tighten port controls, starting with Puerto Quetzal in the coming weeks. [@1]

Investigators said the Haina container was sealed in Guatemala and the seal
matched the one that arrived at the Dominican port. [@4] DNCD president José
Cabrera Ulloa said only 76 of the container's 194 boxes held the goods the
importer had bought in Guatemala, while the other 118 were full of marijuana.
[@4] Guatemala's interior minister, Francisco Jiménez, rejected the claim that
the drug had left Guatemala and called the Dominican statements irresponsible.
[@4]
