As cases of travellers smuggling cannabis rose, the health minister, Somsak Thepsutin, said it was against Thai law to take cannabis out of the country without permission from the authorities, and that shops would soon be allowed to sell only to customers with a prescription. In March 375 kg had been seized in 22 suitcases at Samui airport and 13 foreigners arrested. [@1,2,3]

Thai officials said the Samui suspects were instructed to carry the suitcases from Samui to Singapore and then to the U.K., where they would be paid 2,000 pounds upon completing the job. [@2]

British authorities said a joint operation with Thailand in February led to more than 2 tons of cannabis being seized from air passengers. [@3] Britain's government said more than 50 British nationals had been arrested in Thailand since July last year for attempting to smuggle cannabis. [@3] It also reported a sharp rise in the amount of cannabis posted from Thailand to the U.K. since decriminalisation in 2022. [@3] Airport officials said most people found with cannabis in their luggage were foreigners, especially Indian and British nationals. [@3] The ruling Pheu Thai Party has promised to criminalise cannabis again, but faced resistance from its coalition partner, which backed decriminalisation. [@3] Thailand decriminalised cannabis in 2022, the first country in Asia to do so, which boosted tourism and farming and led to thousands of shops, but the government faces a backlash over allegations that under-regulation made the drug available to children and caused addiction. [@3] Thai officials said the Samui suspects were hired to travel to Thailand as tourists, then waited at arranged accommodation on the island to receive the suitcases with cannabis before travelling on to Singapore and the U.K. [@3]
