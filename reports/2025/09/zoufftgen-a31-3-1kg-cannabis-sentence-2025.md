The Thionville criminal court sentenced 24-year-old Belgian driver Rayhan Syah
to 18 months of firm prison for importing, transporting and holding 3.1 kg of
cannabis, refusing police orders and driving under the influence of drugs. [@1]
French customs officers stopped him on 10 August on the A31 at Zoufftgen, near
the Luxembourg border. [@1] He first ignored the officers’ order to stop, then
halted briefly on the hard shoulder while his passenger got out and fled on foot
across the motorway. [@1] The customs check was then completed at a rest area
very close to the border. [@1]

Officers found 3.1 kg of cannabis in a bag in the Belgian driver’s car. [@1] The
driver maintained that the bag belonged to the passenger who fled. [@1] He said
a stranger met by chance in Belgium had offered him 200 euros to drop him in
Metz and that he paid no attention to what he was carrying. [@1] He said he did
not smell the herbal cannabis because he had himself just smoked a joint. [@1]
The previously unconvicted defendant repeated that account before the court,
while his lawyer criticised the thin one-month investigation and the absence of
the escaped passenger. [@1] The court kept him in detention after sentencing.
[@1] It also banned him from driving and from being on French territory for
three years. [@1] It ordered him to pay a 20,000-euro customs fine, with ten
days to appeal. [@1]
