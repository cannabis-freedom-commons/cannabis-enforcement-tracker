Portugal's Judicial Police ran Operation Erva Daninha against a group accused of
introducing large quantities of cannabis into European and African markets. [@2]
Officers detained five people, all reported as Portuguese nationals, in the May
2025 raids. [@2] A 48-year-old man described as one of the group's principal
leaders was detained in the Netherlands and extradited to Portugal for a first
judicial interrogation and coercive measures. [@3] The extradition took place on
the Thursday reported on 12 September 2025, placing the transfer on 11 September
2025, when he was to be brought before a judge. [@3] The extradited leader was
described as a specialist in moving cryptoassets on the dark web, in a
money-laundering structure with a dedicated cell. [@1] Other suspects in the
file include bank directors, lawyers and accountants. [@1]

Officers executed 64 search-and-seizure warrants across mainland Portugal and
Madeira, with further searches by authorities in Spain, Bulgaria and Cyprus.
[@2] The group allegedly bought pharmaceutical firms and then created licensed
wholesale, import and export medical-cannabis companies to send thousands of
kilos to illicit markets using false documentation and certificates. [@2] A
laboratory dismantled in central Portugal yielded one tonne of amphetamines,
described as the largest such quantity seized in Portugal to date. [@1] Infarmed
approved tonnes of medical cannabis for export to Kenya, Congo and
Guinea-Bissau, countries described as having no legislation on the matter. [@1]

The network is described as a Costa del Sol organisation that moved its
operating base to the Algarve years ago before spreading across Portugal to
exploit vulnerabilities and weaknesses in Infarmed. [@1] Medical cannabis
cultivation and export have been authorised in Portugal since 2019. [@1]
Authorities suspect trafficking profits may have funded terrorism through
parallel money-transfer systems. [@1]
