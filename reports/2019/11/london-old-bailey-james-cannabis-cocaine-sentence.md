Aidan James, 29, of Formby, Merseyside, was jailed for a terrorism offence and
drug charges at the Old Bailey in London on 7 November 2019. [@1] He had
admitted possessing cannabis at an earlier hearing at Liverpool Crown Court,
along with possession of cocaine with intent to supply. [@1] The judge jailed
James for three years for the drug charges and added a consecutive 12-month
sentence for the terrorism offence, making four years in total. [@3]

James had repeatedly been turned down for recruitment into the British armed
forces on mental health grounds. [@2] The police attended James's home in April
2017 over concerns about his stated intention to travel to the Middle East and
found drugs there. [@1] He admitted possessing cannabis, a Class B drug. [@4]
The cocaine and cannabis offences were both dated 28 April 2017. [@4] He was
arrested on his return to Liverpool airport in February 2018 and charged with
terror offences the following day. [@1]

James was found guilty at a retrial of attending a training camp in Iraq where
the banned PKK was present, and was cleared of attending a YPG training camp in
Syria. [@1]

The court heard that James had spent time in a mental health facility before he
flew to Iraq in August 2017. [@1] James is described as the first Briton who
fought against Islamic State to stand trial for such offences. [@1]
