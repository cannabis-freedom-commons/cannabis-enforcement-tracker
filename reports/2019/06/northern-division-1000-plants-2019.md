Fiji Police uprooted more than 1,000 plants believed to be marijuana during
raids on villages in the North over the preceding few weeks. [@1] Fiji Police
chief of operations Assistant Commissioner Maretino Qiolevu did not reveal which
areas were raided because investigations were continuing. [@1]

Qiolevu warned communities to stay away from illegal drug cultivation. [@1]
Calling the cultivation trend worrying, he said police were concerned about
children while welcoming the government's support. [@1] He reiterated that the
police force would show no sympathy to anyone involved in drug trafficking, drug
trading or cultivation. [@1] Police would ensure that those involved were taken
to task, Qiolevu said. [@1] He noted numerous invitations from schools and said
the government had taken ownership and come down hard on drugs. [@1] Qiolevu
added that police were ready to work with communities to raise awareness and
eradicate drug problems. [@1]

Earlier that week, Qiolevu had urged non-commissioned officers to serve the
public well and not compromise investigations, or leave the police force. [@1]
Warning that some investigations lacked evidence and should not be compromised,
he told officers that people would know and God was watching if they engaged in
misconduct. [@1] Those remarks came at the opening of a week-long management and
leadership training course for non-commissioned officers in Labasa. [@1]
