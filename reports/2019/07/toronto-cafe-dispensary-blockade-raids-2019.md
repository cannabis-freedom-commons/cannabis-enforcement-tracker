Shortly before 10 a.m. on Tuesday 9 July 2019, Toronto police and city bylaw
officers raided the CAFE dispensary on Harbord Street near Spadina Avenue,
clearing out its contents before barring entry to the property. [@1] Jeff
Brodie, who said he lived in the building, was removed shouting that officers
had no right to kick him out of his house, and a CBC camera caught an officer
shoving him after warning he could be arrested. [@1] Cannabis and cash were
seized, and the city's plan to place concrete blocks outside was dropped that
day because overhead wires made the lift impossible. [@1]

The city had turned to giant cement blocks in June after steel doors and
barred entryways failed to keep raided shops closed. [@2] A legal loophole
had stopped officers barring dispensaries that doubled as residences, and a
bill closing it received royal assent that month, while about a dozen illegal
storefronts were still operating. [@2]

That July week, Municipal Licensing and Standards closed all four CAFE
locations on interim orders under the provincial Cannabis Control Act and
stacked 4,000-pound concrete blocks outside. [@3] By Thursday night the
Harbord Street blocks had been hauled away by heavy machinery, which the city
called theft and police investigated as such, while a man had to be rescued
on Wednesday after being trapped inside one blocked shop. [@3] The same
night, the Bloor Street location had its lights on and its garage door open
behind the blocks. [@3]

The blockade battle cost $361,459.49 in labour, engineers and security, paid
from provincial enforcement money rather than city funds. [@4] City officials
said CAFE's owners were repeat offenders believed to be making millions in
illicit sales, and staff kept selling on the sidewalk outside the blocked
stores. [@4] Police eventually raided the shops again and arrested 17 people,
including a 16-year-old boy, on charges including unlawful sale and
distribution of cannabis and unlawful possession of criminal proceeds. [@4]
