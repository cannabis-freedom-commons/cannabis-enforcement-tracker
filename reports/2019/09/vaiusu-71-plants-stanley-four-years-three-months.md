Samoa's Supreme Court sentenced Darren Stanley to four years and three months in
prison for cultivating narcotics. [@1] The sentence followed a police raid on 21
August 2018 on a home in Vaiusu, where 71 marijuana plants were found. [@1]

The raid was carried out at the property of co-defendant Taitaifono Ulugia Sala
Suivai. [@1] Presiding Supreme Court Justice Lei'ataualesa Darryl Clarke said
the prosecution did not lead evidence of the height and size of the plants, so
those particulars of the charge were not proven. [@1] The judge said photographs
showed the plants were not small and that some were well grown. [@1] The court
accepted that Stanley, with Suivai, planted and grew the marijuana, and that he
came to Suivai's land three to four times a week to tend it. [@1]

Stanley was 23, married and had three young children. [@1] The judge listed as
aggravating factors the significant amount of marijuana cultivated and the high
degree of premeditation and planning. [@1] Because the case went to trial and
Stanley had prior convictions, he received no deduction for previous good
character. [@1] The judge set a starting point of four years and six months and
reduced it by three months for Stanley's personal circumstances. [@1]

The judge said the jail term ran less time Stanley had already spent remanded in
custody. [@1] Stanley continued to dispute his role when speaking to the
Probation Service, while expressing remorse, and the judge said the two
positions were inconsistent. [@1] Lawyer Afa Lesa represented Stanley and Fuifui
Ioane, of the Attorney General's Office, prosecuted. [@1]
