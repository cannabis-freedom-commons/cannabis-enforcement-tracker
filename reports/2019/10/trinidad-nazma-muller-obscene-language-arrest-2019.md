Marijuana activist Nazma Muller was arrested and charged for using obscene
language during a protest outside Parliament on Friday, 18 October 2019. [@1]
Muller said she was arrested for chanting, "Free the f---ing weed." [@1] She was
released on own bail at about 8 pm the same evening. [@1] In a Facebook post she
wrote that police tried to keep her for the weekend, that a senior officer told
her she could get station bail, and that she was taken into a room and asked to
raise her top. [@1]

Global Voices reported that she was arrested under Section 49 of the Summary
Offences Act, which deals with "violent language and breach of the peace", and
that the offence carries a $200 fine or 30 days' imprisonment. [@2] Muller is a
Rastafarian and had announced that morning that she would be outside Parliament
from noon to 3 pm reminding the prime minister of his promise to decriminalise
cannabis by June 30. [@2] Images she posted show her being led away by a police
officer, who also took away her sign, which read "Honk your horn for the herb."
[@2] She was released from police custody at around 7:30 that evening, after
being held at the Central Police Station to be charged. [@2] One of her lawyers,
Emir Crowne, said the case raised serious constitutional implications for
freedom of expression, assembly and association. [@2]

Muller, 46, of Cane Farm, Trincity, pleaded not guilty before Magistrate Sarah
de Silva in the Port-of-Spain Magistrates' Court on 21 October 2019. [@3] Police
prosecutors said two officers would testify against her, and the magistrate
ratified the station bail a justice of the peace had granted and adjourned the
case to 13 November 2019. [@3] At her hearing she pleaded not guilty and told
the court she would retain an attorney for the next hearing. [@3]

Muller is the founder of the Caribbean Collective for Justice, a registered NGO,
and had presented the Prime Minister with a petition to decriminalise marijuana.
[@1] She criticised the Government's failure to table decriminalisation
legislation in Parliament by June as the Prime Minister had assured, after the
Attorney General said in July the legislation would have been ready by
September. [@1] The Attorney General, Faris Al-Rawi, had led public
consultations on the issue, and a Caribbean Marijuana Commission report had
called for member states to decriminalise. [@3] No cannabis was seized in the
arrest; the charge concerns her language at the protest rather than any cannabis
offence. [@2]
