The Kern County Sheriff's Office, with the California Department of Fish and
Wildlife and the FBI, served search warrants on 11 hemp cultivation fields
covering more than 459 acres near Arvin on 25 October 2019. [@1] Narcotics
units seized and eradicated about 10 million marijuana plants, which the office
valued at over $1 billion on the black market. [@1]

The office said the gardens were grown under the guise of legitimate hemp
production. [@1] Hemp must contain less than 0.3 percent THC, and the office
said preliminary testing showed levels in these fields well over the legal
limit, making the crop cannabis. [@1] No arrests were announced, and the
office said the investigation was continuing. [@1,2]

## Why the figure is disputed

Hemp experts rejected both the classification and the valuation. [@2] The
plants grew packed tightly like fibre hemp, not the bushy flowering plants of
drug cannabis, and the photographed crop did not look like smokable hemp
either. [@2] At prevailing prices for CBD biomass, even generous assumptions
put the crop near $100 million, far below the claimed billion. [@2] The
sheriff's office did not answer questions about the estimate or the case
after its announcement. [@2]
