On 18 April 2019 the cyber department of the State Attorney's Office filed
indictments against 27 defendants at the Central District Court in Lod over the
Telegrass Telegram drug network, which prosecutors call case 420. [@2] The cyber
department of the State Attorney's Office filed the indictments with the Central
District Court, and Maariv reported that they set out offences under the law
against criminal organisations, brokering and selling dangerous drugs, inducing
a minor to use drugs, possession not for personal use, obstruction of justice,
money laundering and tax evasion. [@4] Twenty-seven of the 42 people arrested in
March were indicted in April, charged with tax evasion, money laundering and
helping others carry out drug deals for a criminal organisation. [@1]

Prosecutors asked that 22 of the defendants be held until the end of
proceedings. [@2] The indictment described the platform's managers and staff as
brokering deals between more than 3,000 drug dealers, at least 250 of them
minors, and about 200,000 buyers across the country. [@2] Prosecutors put the
organisation's income from brokering drug sales at no less than NIS 30 million.
[@2] According to the indictment, most of the drugs sold through Telegrass were
cannabis in various forms, alongside LSD and MDMA. [@3]

Defendant number one was Bar-El Levi, 28, described as Telegrass' co-founder and
head of its Israeli operations. [@1] Prosecutors placed the network's founder
Amos Silver, who ran it from the United States, above Levi; Ynet reported that
Silver, held in Ukraine for extradition, was not among those indicted in these
charges, while Channel 13 listed him as a defendant. [@2] Yossi "Gal" Meshi, the
network's finance chief, was outside Israel, according to the indictment. [@2]
The brothers Ran Buganim and Ofer Rehima, senior members of the network, began
cooperating with police on 19 March, a week after the arrests. [@1]

According to a police report the news site cited, 4,659 dealers sold 4.12 tonnes
of marijuana through the platform in 505,680 deals between March 2017 and
November 2018, worth NIS 282 million. [@1] The investigation took testimony from
90 people involved in the network, 50 of them questioned under caution, and 33
suspects were held in custody for varying periods, Channel 13 reported. [@3]
