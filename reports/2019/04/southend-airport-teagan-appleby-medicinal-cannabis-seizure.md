Emma Appleby and her nine-year-old daughter Teagan were stopped at Southend
Airport after flying from Amsterdam. [@1] Border Force confiscated medicinal
cannabis that Appleby had tried to bring into the United Kingdom illegally for
her daughter, who has severe epilepsy. [@1] The family, who are from Aylesham in
Kent, was released from the airport after the seizure. [@1] The three-month
supply of medicines was confiscated after the family landed at Southend airport
in Essex. [@2]

She had bought 4,000 pounds worth of THC oil capsules in the Netherlands after
being refused a prescription in the United Kingdom. [@1] She bought a
three-month supply of THC and cannabidiol, using money raised through
crowdfunding, at a pharmacy in The Hague. [@1] The family got the medicine
prescribed by a paediatric neurologist at Erasmus Hospital in Rotterdam,
collected it from a pharmacy and paid using fundraised money. [@2] Teagan
suffers from a rare chromosomal disorder called Isodicentric 15 as well as
Lennox-Gastaut syndrome, which causes up to 300 seizures a day. [@2]

It was illegal to bring the drug into the United Kingdom without a prescription,
which doctors have been able to issue legally since 2018. [@1] Doctors in the
United Kingdom have refused to prescribe Teagan THC, a psychoactive compound
found in cannabis. [@1] A government spokesman said Border Force has a duty to
enforce the law and stop the unlawful import of controlled substances into the
United Kingdom. [@1] Emma Appleby was bringing 4,600 pounds worth of THC
capsules back to Britain after buying them in the Netherlands for nine-year-old
Teagan. [@3]
