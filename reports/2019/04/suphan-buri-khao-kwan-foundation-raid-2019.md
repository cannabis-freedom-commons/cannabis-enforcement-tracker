A combined force of police, military and anti-narcotics officials raided the
offices of the Khao Kwan Foundation in Suphan Buri on Wednesday 3 April 2019,
seizing about 200 marijuana plants along with cannabis oil, dried marijuana
seeds and marijuana powder. [@1] Some foundation staff were held in police
custody on charges of illegal possession of narcotics, and a summons was issued
for foundation president Decha Siriphat to report to police in Suphan Buri on
the same charge. [@1] Decha was in Laos on a study tour at the time. [@1]

Decha, a prominent practitioner of traditional medicine, returned to Bangkok on
9 April and called the arrest of his aide Pornchai Chulert an act of bullying.
[@2] He said he had been making cannabinoid medicines for more than a decade
and distributing them to patients in need, and that he had sent a coordinator
to register with the Food and Drug Administration before leaving for Laos.
[@2] The registration was turned down because officials said he could not prove
he was a traditional medicine practitioner, and officers raided the office and
seized the medicines instead. [@2]

The raid drew a backlash during the amnesty period for medical cannabis users,
which ran until 19 May 2019. [@2,3] The Office of the Narcotics Control Board
later returned the seized plants and extracts and advised the foundation to
reorganise its distribution as a proper research project. [@4] Prime Minister
Prayut Chan-o-cha instructed the public health ministry and educators to
support the foundation's research, in a rebuke of the raiding officials. [@4]
