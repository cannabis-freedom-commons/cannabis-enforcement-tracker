Federal, state and local officers arrested 42 people over three days from 22 to
24 May 2019 in one of the largest black-market marijuana enforcement actions in
Colorado's history. [@1,2] Teams executed 255 search warrants in eight counties,
raiding 247 homes and eight businesses and seizing more than 80,000 plants and
4,500 pounds of finished marijuana. [@1,2]

The raids closed a North Metro Task Force investigation running since the summer
of 2016 into cultivators growing large amounts of marijuana for illegal sale in
other states. [@1] The grows hid in the basements of newer houses in upscale
suburban neighbourhoods, using sealed rooms, large charcoal air filters and
late-night venting to hide the smell. [@1] About five percent of the sites
bypassed their electricity meters, cutting bills and raising fire risks. [@1]

Of those arrested, 26 became state defendants and 16 federal defendants, with
the federal cases carrying mandatory minimums of five to ten years. [@1,2]
Officers also moved to forfeit 41 homes, about 2.16 million dollars in cash, 25
vehicles and jewellery. [@1] Colorado's US attorney said the state had become
the epicentre of the country's black-market marijuana, and warned the action
might be only the tip of the iceberg. [@1]
