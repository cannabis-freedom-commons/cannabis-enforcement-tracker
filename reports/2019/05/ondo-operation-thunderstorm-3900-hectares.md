On 20 May 2019, NDLEA chairman and chief executive Col. Muhammad Abdallah (retd) said in a statement that the agency had never supported growing cannabis for medical purposes or economic gain. [@1] As evidence he cited Operation Thunderstorm, undertaken by the Ondo State Command, which destroyed 3,900.73 hectares of cannabis sativa planted in forest reserves. [@1,2]

The statement answered Ondo State governor Rotimi Akeredolu, who had called on the federal government to consider cannabis as a thriving industry that would create jobs, saying Ondo was the hotbed of cannabis cultivation and pointing to an estimated legal marijuana market worth $145 billion by 2025. [@2] Abdallah said the governor had given a wrong account of their joint visit to Thailand, and that at no point had he backed the commercial or medicinal value of cannabis. [@1,2]

Abdallah said the Thailand trip was to study how that country moved farmers from illicit crops to coffee, tea, fruit and crafts through projects such as the Doi Tung development scheme, and that Nigeria had restated its opposition to legalising cannabis at the Commission on Narcotic Drugs roundtable in Vienna in March 2019. [@1]
