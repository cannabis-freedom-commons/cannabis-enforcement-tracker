Hampshire police raided a farm on the outskirts of Kingsclere after reports of excess
compost being delivered to the property. [@1,2] Officers found thousands of plants
being grown in rows in barns on the farm, which was protected by guard dogs, and put
their street value at about £1.5 million. [@1,2] They also found two crossbows in one
of the three barns used to grow and dry the drug. [@1,2]

On the Monday of that week officers arrested five men: a 56-year-old of Kingsclere,
a 48-year-old of Thatcham, a 47-year-old of Reading, a 29-year-old listed as of Nebury
and a 20-year-old of Manor Park in east London. [@1,2] All five were released under
investigation after being arrested on suspicion of being concerned in the supply of
cannabis. [@1]

The raided farm was on Union Lane in Kingsclere, according to a Hampshire police
spokeswoman. [@1,2] Sergeant Carl Holmes, who oversaw the raid, called it the biggest
cannabis farm he had ever seen during a bust. [@1,2] An unnamed source quoted in the
reports claimed the farm was worth about £6 million a year and that it took officers
days to clear the plants. [@1,2]
