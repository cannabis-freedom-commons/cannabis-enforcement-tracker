A construction worker who was charged with cultivating and trafficking cannabis
was denied bail after appearing before a city court. [@2] Mark McLean was
remanded to prison after denying charges of possessing narcotics for trafficking
and cultivating plants in his yard. [@1] Stabroek News reports that the charge
alleged he had 200.11 grammes of cannabis for the purpose of trafficking on 28
February 2019. [@2] Stabroek News says the second charge alleged that on 11
March at Toucan Drive he cultivated a cannabis plant he knew to be prohibited.
[@2] Kaieteur News reports both charges as covering the same date and the Lot
681 Toucan Drive, South Ruimveldt address, with 211 grams of narcotics. [@1] He
denied both charges before Magistrate Faith McGusty. [@2]

The charges were read at the Georgetown Magistrates' Courts, where his lawyer
George Thomas applied for reasonable bail. [@1] The prosecutor said police
searched his home and found the suspected narcotic in a music speaker belonging
to him. [@1] Stabroek News says the bag of cannabis was found on a music speaker
on the stairway leading to his second-floor flat. [@2] His lawyer told Stabroek
News that several people lived in the house and the cannabis could have belonged
to any of them. [@2] The lawyer also said police did not find him tending the
plant. [@2] The prosecutor told the court that a witness said he threw seeds in
the yard after smoking and began tending the plant once he saw it growing. [@2]
The prosecutor also said he had made an oral statement admitting to both
charges. [@2]

Stabroek News reports that he was remanded to jail with his next court date set
for 27 March 2019. [@2] Kaieteur News also records the remand and the
adjournment to 27 March 2019. [@1]
