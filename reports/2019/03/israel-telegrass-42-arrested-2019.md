Israel Police said it arrested 42 people suspected of holding top management
positions in the Telegrass cannabis distribution network, which operated on the
encrypted messaging app Telegram. [@1,3] The 42 arrests were carried out in
Israel, the United States, Ukraine and Germany, police said. [@1,3] The head of
the network was among those detained and was arrested during a visit to Ukraine,
Reuters reported. [@2] Police said the suspects included the network's head,
three deputies and other administrators. [@2]

Detectives from Lahav 433, Israel Police's major crimes unit, led the arrests,
with the national cybercrime unit, the Northern District's central unit, the tax
authorities and the State Attorney's Office's cyber and international affairs
departments. [@2] Police said the operation was carried out with the organised
crime division of Ukraine's security services and with enforcement bodies in the
United States and Germany. [@2] The arrests followed a months-long undercover
investigation, police said. [@1] Police said those arrested face allegations of
drug trafficking, running and funding a criminal organisation, money laundering
and tax-related offences. [@1]

Telegrass let users order drugs anonymously through a secure messaging app and
was used mainly to buy and sell marijuana, but also for cocaine, MDMA and
ecstasy, police said. [@1] According to Reuters, the network had more than
100,000 members in Israel, including dozens of cannabis suppliers, and vendors
were paid in cash, drugs or bitcoin. [@2]

As part of the operation, police raided dozens of homes and offices and seized
money, drugs and technology used by the organisation. [@1] Police said they
sought to extend the suspects' detention at the Nazareth and Rishon Lezion
Magistrates' Courts, and lawyers for the suspects said their clients denied
involvement. [@2]

Police designated the ring "Crime Organization 420" and published its organizational chart. [@3] The network's founder, Amos Dov Silver, a dual Israeli-US citizen who had been living abroad, was arrested in Ukraine. [@3] Israeli Chief Superintendent Avi Maiberg, who runs the police cyber-crime unit leading the investigation, said officers saw the number of dealers jump from hundreds to thousands. [@3] Maiberg said the suspects dealt not only in marijuana but also ecstasy, LSD, cocaine and other dangerous substances. [@3]
