Sal Agro, 67, a retired autoworker and youth sports coach from Lake Orion, died of a heart attack on 2 September 2010, his family said. [@1,3] He had undergone hip surgery two days earlier. [@1,3]

Eight days before his death, on 25 August, officers of the Oakland County Sheriff's Narcotics Enforcement Team raided Agro's home, tore apart beds and clothing looking for marijuana, and tore out and confiscated several marijuana plants he grew in a basement bedroom. [@1] Agro said both he and his wife held state-issued medical-marijuana cards, and his sons Nick and Tony ran Clinical Relief, a Ferndale dispensary at 362 Hilton; his son Tony was arrested at the clinic during the raids. [@1]

The raid on Agro's home was part of a county-wide sting targeting what Sheriff Michael Bouchard called abuse of Michigan's medical-marijuana law, acting on tips about illegal manufacture, possession and sale at Clinical Relief in Ferndale and Everyday Cafe in Waterford, with connected homes including two in Orion Township. [@2] Fifteen people were arrested across the locations, and Bouchard said some $750,000 in marijuana was confiscated and put on display at the sheriff's office alongside guns, hash oil, scales and jars of processed marijuana. [@2]
