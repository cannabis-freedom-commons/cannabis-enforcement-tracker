The federal Drug Enforcement Administration with Las Vegas police raided at least five medical
marijuana dispensaries across the Las Vegas valley on 8 September 2010. [@1,2] The dispensaries
were Happiness Consultant, Salvation Haven, Nature's Way, Organic Releaf and Holistic Solutions,
and agents seized patient and financial records without making arrests. [@1,2] U.S. Attorney's
office spokeswoman Natalie Collins said the search warrants and supporting affidavits were
sealed by federal court order as part of an ongoing law enforcement operation. [@1,2]

## A policy dispute

The raids came nearly a year after an October 2009 Justice Department directive deprioritising
enforcement against medical marijuana patients and providers, and less than two months after
federal raids on state-compliant patients in California and Michigan. [@1,2] Americans for Safe
Access said the federal government should never enforce local or state medical marijuana laws
and called on the DEA to follow the Justice Department policy. [@1,2] Las Vegas mayor Oscar
Goodman had said publicly that allowing dispensaries was a legitimate goal, while Nevada law
made no provision for distribution beyond stringent designated-caregiver rules. [@1,2] The DEA
was then led by Bush appointee Michele Leonhart, whose re-nomination drew objections from
medical marijuana advocates after more than 200 federal raids during the Bush years. [@1,2]
