Federal agents seized more than 20 tons of marijuana on the US end of a smuggling
tunnel in one of the largest drug seizures in the country, the Drug Enforcement
Administration announced on 3 November 2010. [@1] Agents of the San Diego Tunnel
Task Force, drawn from the DEA, Immigration and Customs Enforcement, the Border
Patrol and the California Bureau of Narcotic Enforcement, uncovered the
crawlspace-sized tunnel running 600 yards. [@1] About 30 tons with a street value of
$20 million were seized in the US and Mexico and linked to the tunnel. [@1,2]

The passageway connected a warehouse in the 9000 block of Via de la Amistad in
Otay Mesa with a similar building in Tijuana, and was fitted with rail, lighting
and ventilation; authorities believed it had been completed recently. [@1] While
watching the warehouse on Wednesday, agents followed a tractor-trailer to the
Border Patrol checkpoint in Temecula, where a search found 10 tons of marijuana
packed in large cargo boxes; the driver and passenger were taken into custody for
federal drug smuggling prosecution. [@1] A later search of the warehouse that
Tuesday night found a second cache of about 15 tons and the tunnel entrance, and
the Mexican military recovered another four tons at the Tijuana end. [@1]

## A cartel tunnel, the second-biggest US seizure

ICE Director John Morton called the seizure obviously the work of a cartel, and
DEA acting administrator Michele Leonhart said it would strip millions in
potential profits from the responsible organization. [@1,2] Officials called it
the largest seizure ever in California and believed it the second-largest in the
United States, after 33 tons DEA agents seized in Oregon in 2008. [@2] The
12-hour operation followed a monthlong investigation, and Morton credited
cooperation with Mexican authorities; it came a little over a week after
Mexico's record 134-tonne seizure in Tijuana, though officials had not determined
whether the same traffickers were behind both. [@2]
