A multi-agency federal task force raided the Covelo property of 68-year-old Joy Greenfield on
7 July 2010, the first collective to apply under Mendocino County's new cultivation permit
ordinance. [@1,2] Greenfield had paid the $1,050 application fee in the name of her collective,
Light the Way, and her property had passed a preliminary inspection by sheriff's deputies, whose
zip-ties were meant to mark her plants as legal. [@1,2] In the days before the raid she had seen
a helicopter over her property, and the sheriff told her it belonged to the DEA. [@1,2]

Agents entered with guns drawn, tore out the collective's 99 plants and took Greenfield's
computer and cash while she was away from home. [@1,2] Reached by phone during the raid, she
told the DEA agent in charge that she was a legal grower under the sheriff's program, and he
answered that he did not care what the sheriff said. [@1,2] She returned to find her house in
disarray, with soda cans strewn on the floor. [@1,2]

## Fallout

Her attorney Bob Boyd called the raid a slap in the face of Mendocino's government, and said
neither he nor others believed the sheriff had tipped off the DEA. [@1] California NORML
director Dale Gieringer called the raid evidence that the DEA was out of control and urged a
new federal policy toward state medical marijuana laws. [@1] The DEA said little about the raid
beyond calling it part of a larger investigation involving other suspects. [@1,2]
