About 450 officers from 21 agencies spent the 2010 season targeting large marijuana grows on public
land in Fresno, Madera and Tulare counties under Operation Trident. [@1] On 11 August 2010 the
White House drug-policy director Gil Kerlikowske joined the three county sheriffs and U.S. Attorney
Benjamin Wagner to report that the work had seized 432,271 live plants, 499 pounds of processed
marijuana, 97 arrests, 33 weapons and miles of irrigation tubing removed from grow sites. [@1]

## Public land and reclamation

Wagner said traffickers operating in the Sierra foothills damaged watersheds and endangered hikers
and campers. [@1] Fresno County Sheriff Margaret Mims said the targets were armed smugglers tied to
organized crime, not only summer growers. [@1] Teams also hauled away 15.5 tons of trash and 4,580
pounds of fertilizer from about 270 acres disturbed by cultivation, work the release priced at up to
$11,000 per acre to restore. [@1]

The California National Guard Counterdrug Task Force, working with the same partner list in July
2010, described short-haul removals from forest grows and youth prevention outreach in nearby
communities. [@2] Its summary pointed to more than 100 arrests and nearly half a million plants
with the same roughly $1.7 billion street-value estimate federal speakers used. [@2]
