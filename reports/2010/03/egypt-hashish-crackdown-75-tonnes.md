General Mostafa Amer, director of the Interior Ministry's anti-narcotics
bureau, announced in late March 2010 that 7.5 tonnes of hashish and 25kg of
heroin had been seized in raids over the previous three months. [@1] More than
300 distributors were detained in the same operation, he said. [@1] Ennahar's
April account, citing the authorities, put hashish seizures since the beginning
of the year at more than six tonnes. [@2]

The crackdown was followed by an unprecedented shortage: a 150g block that
normally sold for 1,500 Egyptian pounds doubled in price, and smokers told
reporters they could not find the drug. [@1] A Cairo resident said a quantity
that cost 2,600 pounds a few months earlier then cost 3,500 pounds and took
months to obtain. [@2] Most hashish on the Egyptian market arrives illegally
from Morocco through the porous border with Libya, while Lebanese shipments
come through Sinai; some users turned to local bango marijuana, whose prices
also soared. [@1,2] Deputy Interior Minister Hamdi Abdel Karim said the fight
was never over and the ministry hoped to keep curbing trafficking. [@2]
Several smokers and analysts quoted disputed that the raids caused the
shortage, blaming corrupt officials or internal power struggles. [@1,2]
