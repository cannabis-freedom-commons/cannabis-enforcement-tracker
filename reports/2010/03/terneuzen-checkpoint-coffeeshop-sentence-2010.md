Checkpoint in Terneuzen, near the Belgian border, was the biggest cannabis coffeeshop in
the Netherlands, selling about 10 kg a day to some 3,000 customers before police forcibly
closed it in May 2008. [@1] Dutch tolerance rules let coffeeshops keep no more than 500 g
on hand, but police found about 200 kg of cannabis in raids on the shop in 2007 and 2008.
[@2,3] At five grams per customer, a full day's trade meant shifting up to 15,000 grams
through a back door the law pretends does not exist. [@3]

## Conviction

Owner Meddy Willemsen stood trial in 2009 with 15 employees and suppliers on trafficking
and criminal-organisation charges, with prosecutors treating 28 million euros allegedly
earned between January 2006 and May 2008 as criminal assets. [@1] On 25 March 2010 the
court convicted all sixteen and ordered Willemsen to pay 10 million euros, less than
prosecutors had sought because the municipality had helped the shop grow, putting up road
signs pointing to it and building a nearby car park. [@2,3] Willemsen also received a
16-week prison term and walked free, credited with time served after his arrest. [@2]
