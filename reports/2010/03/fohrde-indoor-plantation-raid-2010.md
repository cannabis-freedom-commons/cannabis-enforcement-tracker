Police found more than 2,500 cannabis plants in a hall in the village of
Fohrde near the city of Brandenburg in March 2010, after a routine traffic
check on Highway 102 turned up the smell of cannabis. [@1] Commissioner Marco
Stein traced the sweetish scent to a hall that had once belonged to a
construction supply shop, and two hours later officers of the criminal
investigation department returned with a search warrant and broke down the
doors. [@1] Inside, cannabis sativa plants stood in neat rows under artificial
light, watered by a system of tubes, from small shoots to bushes two metres
tall. [@1]

Investigators first looked at the 35-year-old tenant and several helpers, then
three weeks later called on the car dealer K. and confiscated two Porsches, an
Aston Martin and ten motor yachts; the public prosecutor suspected K. and his
accomplices of earning at least 824,000 euros from the plantation since
January 2009. [@1] The site was one of the largest indoor plantations
discovered in Germany to date. [@1]

## Context

Germany's Federal Criminal Police Office had counted 26 large-scale growing
operations with more than 1,000 plants in the previous year, up from 18, plus
316 smaller sites. [@1]
