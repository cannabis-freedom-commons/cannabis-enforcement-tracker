On 10 September 2006, customs officers at Felixstowe inspected a container of
tables and chairs shipped from South Africa and found about 7.5 tonnes of
cannabis inside. [@2] The load, reported as eight tonnes with a wholesale
value of £12 million, was the Metropolitan Police's largest ever seizure of
the drug. [@1] The container was the 26th in a series of shipments behind two
conspiracies to import cannabis and cocaine into Britain, a trade running
since 2001. [@2]

Robert Flook, 46, of Eltham, was convicted at Blackfriars Crown Court in
August 2007 on both conspiracy counts. [@1,2] In February 2008 he received
26 years overall, with a 13-year concurrent term for the cannabis load. [@1]
The Court of Appeal ruled the conviction unsafe in July 2009 and ordered a
retrial. [@2]

At the retrial at Isleworth Crown Court, Flook pleaded guilty to two counts,
admitting concern in the supply of 8.1 tonnes of cannabis alongside 150 kg of
cocaine, and was sentenced to 18 years. [@3] The court heard the ring had
moved eleven cannabis containers worth £132 million wholesale between 2001 and
2006. [@1,3]
