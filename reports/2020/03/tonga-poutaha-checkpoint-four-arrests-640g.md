While enforcing the National Lockdown Order, police and His Majesty's Armed
Forces officers stopped a rental car at the lockdown checkpoint in Poutaha,
Tofoa, on the afternoon of Monday, 30 March 2020, and arrested three men. [@2]
The three men, all reported to be from Tofoa, were arrested with 128 packs of
cannabis weighing 165.37 grams and described as ready for distribution. [@2]
Drug utensils were also seized from the stopped car. [@2] Following the
checkpoint arrests, the Police Drug Enforcement Taskforce with the Tactical
Response Group and Detector Dog Unit searched the accused's residence in Tofoa
and arrested a woman there with 474.31 grams of cannabis and cash. [@2] All four
accused persons were reported to be in police custody. [@2]

Police put the total cannabis seized in the checkpoint operation and house
search at 639.68 grams. [@1]

Police said the four were among 81 people arrested since Police Operation
COVID19 began on 28 March 2020, with 41 held for curfew breach and other related
criminal activity. [@2] Acting Deputy Commissioner Tevita Vailea said police
would continue to target those who sought to profit from supplying and
distributing illicit drugs during the national lockdown. [@2]

One of the three checkpoint arrests produced a Supreme Court sentence: Sosiua Totau, 25, was jailed for two years on 6 November 2020 for possessing 400.37 grams of cannabis found when police searched his Tofoa home. [@3] The co-driver at the checkpoint, Likamani Fa'aoso, was sentenced on 30 October 2020 to 16 months' imprisonment, with the final eight months suspended, for possessing the 165.37 grams found in the vehicle. [@4]
