Toomas Liiv, an Estonian who has lived in Tonga since about 2003, was sentenced
by the Nuku'alofa Supreme Court to two years' imprisonment, fully suspended, on
charges arising from a police search at Puke in 2019. [@1] Justice Niu sentenced
him on 18 December 2020 after he pleaded guilty to three charges: possession of
cannabis, cultivation of four cannabis plants and possession of two .12 rifle
bullets without a licence, all dated 12 April 2019. [@2] Police searched his
home after a tip that he had drugs there and found 67.12 grams of cannabis and
the bullets, which he said were his. [@2]

The court heard that Liiv, who came from Estonia with his father at about 18 and
stayed on, works as a plumber and motor mechanic to support his partner and two
teenage children. [@2] A doctor who examined him at the court's order confirmed
a back problem consistent with osteo-arthritis, and Liiv told the judge he used
cannabis because it relieved the pain, having grown his own after earlier
sentences for possession. [@2]

Justice Niu said possession for a high deserves punishment but that this case
concerned relief of pain, suspended the two-year term in full for three years
and warned that any imprisonable offence in that period would activate it. [@2]
The court ordered the cannabis destroyed by police and the rifle forfeited to
the Crown. [@2]
