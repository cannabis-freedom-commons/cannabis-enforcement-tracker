Samoa Customs and the police ministry seized more than 2,000 grams of marijuana
and more than 500 grams of methamphetamine imported from the United States in a
joint operation on 22 December 2020. [@3] Police valued the high-grade marijuana
at $50,000 tala and the methamphetamine, known in Samoa as ice, at over $570,000
tala; the joint release headed the two together as more than $600,000 tala. [@1]
The drugs came in as personal effects on a cargo ship that originated in
California and arrived in Apia via American Samoa on Thursday 17 December 2020.
[@1] Customs chief executive Matafeo Avalisa Viali-Fautuaalii said a K9 unit
officer and his canine partner identified the drugs during a random checking
process. [@1] The marijuana was concealed inside seven boxes of laundry powder
and a can of Stax, while the methamphetamine was hidden in one box of cereal.
[@3] The search ran more than fourteen hours at the Customs Headquarters at
Matautu-tai. [@2]

Police Commissioner Su'a Fuiavailiili Egon Keil said that was over half a
million tala worth of drugs now off the streets of Samoa. [@1] He said the
Criminal Investigation Division was working closely with Customs and that proof
beyond reasonable doubt had to be attained before an arrest was made. [@1] Keil
warned that a premature arrest would expose the government and the officers
involved to liability and could lead to civil rights violations. [@1] The
ministries said the police would continue investigating and file a joint legal
case once the evidence was finalised. [@3] Keil said 229 grams of cocaine had
also been confiscated at the border that year, alongside the methamphetamine and
cannabis imports. [@1]

Police arrested a customs officer on 2 February 2021 over a separately
confiscated consignment of drugs worth about $500,000 tala. [@4] Police did not
confirm that the February consignment was linked to the December seizure, though
it was packed the same way. [@4]
