Police officers raided the uncompleted single room at Kasoa Red Top where Ama
Forson, a food vendor, lived with her son, and found what they described as
marijuana joints, one in an empty bottle by a tree and another in a lady's
handbag. [@2] Forson denies owning the handbag and the cannabis, and the search
of her room and cooking pots found nothing. [@2] Officers handcuffed her and
held her at a police station in Weija before moving her to the Kasoa Tollbooth
police station, where she spent eight months in a cell. [@2] She paid GHS6,000
to three lawyers, each charging GHS2,000, and all three advised her to plead
guilty. [@2]

In 2016 the Circuit Court in Accra convicted her of possessing narcotics and
sentenced her to 11 years in prison, a term she did not understand because the
proceedings were in English. [@3] She was transferred to the Nsawam
medium-security women's prison after sentencing. [@1] At trial the two police
prosecution witnesses contradicted themselves under cross-examination about
where the cannabis was found. [@2] With the POS Foundation, an NGO running an
in-prison paralegal programme, she filed an appeal in 2020, and the Nsawam High
Court acquitted and discharged her. [@2]

One of her lawyers attended the sentencing while the others stopped appearing in
court, she told Angel TV. [@4] AfrikMag reported that she served four years of
that sentence before an appeal cleared her. [@1] Her only son died while she was
in prison, and she says she lost everything she had. [@1] Forson, now 68 and
from Nyanyano, told Crime Check TV that after her release her possessions were
gone and she has contemplated taking poison. [@1]
