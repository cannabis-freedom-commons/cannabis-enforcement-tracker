U.S. Customs and Border Protection officers at Miami International Airport
seized bulk shipments of marijuana being exported from the United States through
outbound cargo in April 2020. [@1] The four shipments originated in the
southeastern United States and were destined for Antigua, Dominica and St.
Maarten. [@1] CBP Port Director at Miami International Airport Christopher
Maston said the Miami cargo environment is a gateway to the Caribbean and South
America used to traffic weapons, currency and narcotics, and that sweeps of
outbound cargo disrupt criminal networks. [@1]

During April, officers intercepted more than 70 pounds of marijuana in four
outbound cargo shipments, with a combined street value of about $60,000. [@1] On
April 5, officers seized cardboard boxes holding 13 packages of marijuana
weighing 14.5 pounds (6.59 kilograms) destined for St. Maarten. [@1] On April 6,
officers seized 22 blocks of marijuana weighing 43.5 pounds (19.7 kilograms)
concealed in the frame and body of a washer that officers had to dismantle, in a
shipment bound for Dominica. [@1]

On April 20, officers seized 12.5 pounds (5.65 kilograms) of marijuana destined
for Antigua. [@1] Also on April 20, officers seized a second shipment weighing
one pound (0.425 kilograms) destined for St. Maarten. [@1]
