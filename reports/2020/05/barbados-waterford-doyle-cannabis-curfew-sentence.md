Police on patrol saw a young man in the area of the National Botanical Gardens
in Waterford, St Michael. [@1] Twenty-five-year-old Ramon Akeem Doyle, a
labourer from Leinster Road, Waterford, spotted the officers and tossed a
package into a grassy area. [@1] Officers stopped Doyle, retrieved the package,
found it contained cannabis and charged him with possession. [@1] He was also
charged with contravening Paragraph 1 of the Emergency Management COVID-19
Curfew No. 4 Directive for being outdoors on May 8 around 5:15 p.m. without
reasonable explanation. [@1]

After police executed a search warrant at his residence and found five plants,
Doyle was charged with possession, possession with intent to supply and
possession of a trafficable quantity. [@1] Sergeant Theodore McClean said the
drug weighed 36.37 grammes, with an estimated value of $181. [@1] Doyle pleaded
guilty to all the charges before Chief Magistrate Ian Weekes. [@1] He first told
the District D Magistrates Court he did not want to waste its time, then said he
worked picking coconuts. [@1]

On the drug trafficking charge he was placed on a bond to keep the peace and be
of good behaviour for six months, breachable by a $1,000 fine or six months in
prison. [@1] He was reprimanded and discharged on all the other drug offences.
[@1] For breaking the curfew he was given 16 weeks to pay a $2,500 fine or spend
three months in prison. [@1]
