National Drug Law Enforcement Agency officers intercepted 23-year-old student
Sunday Solomon Odi in the departure hall of Nnamdi Azikiwe International
Airport, Abuja, as he tried to board a Turkish airline to Northern Cyprus via
Istanbul. [@2] The NDLEA said Odi, from Ezza South Local Government Area of
Ebonyi State, had been offered a place to study Tourism and Hotel Management at
Near East University in Cyprus. [@2]

A search of his luggage found dried weeds, at least part of them positive for
Cannabis sativa, weighing 13.55 kilograms, concealed among dried hibiscus
flowers and stuffed into empty water bottles. [@3] The cannabis was wrapped in
small parcels that were pushed into bottles cut open and resealed with a label
reading Wonder Bitter Root. [@1] The bottles were then submerged among food
condiments and provisions in the luggage. [@2]

Odi said a childhood friend living in Cyprus, Emmanuel Onyedika Ihebekwe, had
arranged his university admission and told him to bring foodstuff and malaria
drugs in two padlocked Ghana-must-go bags, a Nigerian term for large striped
woven nylon holdalls. [@2] He said the bags were handed to him in Nnewi and that
he believed they held only foodstuff and malaria drugs. [@2] The commander of
the NDLEA's airport command, Kabir Sani Tsakuwa, called the concealment method
intriguing and warned young people against being lured to study or work abroad
and used to carry drugs. [@1]
