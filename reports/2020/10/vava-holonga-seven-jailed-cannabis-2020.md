Seven men, four of them teenagers, were sentenced to prison by the Supreme Court
in Vava'u for charges that included jointly cultivating 2,665 cannabis plants.
[@1] The plants were found at a tax allotment in Holonga, Vava'u, and weighed
99.6 kilograms, according to Matangi Tonga. [@1]

Kaniva Tonga named the seven defendants as Tonga Wolfgramm, Afei Tau'akitangata
Tatafu, Sulitomu'a Muna, Cullen Pongi, Heuati Toke, Misinale Lavemai and 'Amini
Topui. [@2] The court heard that the cannabis was hidden inside a kava
plantation the defendants tended as a church group of growers, and that sales
brought in 600 pa'anga a day. [@2] Police went to the allotment in September
2018 on a tip and found Sulitomu'a Muna, who fled carrying a .22 rifle and
dropped it near a house. [@2]

All the defendants admitted the offending and pleaded guilty, and Lord Chief
Justice Whitten said the case fell within large-scale growing for commercial
purposes. [@2]

Tonga Wolfgramm was sentenced to four years and six months for growing cannabis,
with the final 18 months suspended for two years. [@2] Sulitomu'a Muna received
three years for cultivation plus one year for an unlicensed firearm, with the
final two years suspended. [@2] Cullen Pongi, Heuati Toke, Misinale Lavemai and
'Amini Topui were each sentenced to three years for growing cannabis, with the
final two years or 18 months of their terms suspended. [@2] Afei Tau'akitangata
Tatafu was sentenced to three years and six months for growing cannabis, with
the final 15 months suspended for two years. [@2]
