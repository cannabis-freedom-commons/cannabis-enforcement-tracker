A drone dropped hundreds of bags of cannabis over Rabin Square in Tel Aviv on
the afternoon of Thursday 3 September 2020. [@1] The bags came from the "green
drone" Telegram group, which campaigns for cannabis legalisation in Israel and
uses the slogan "free love". [@1] The Times of Israel reported that over 100
packets fell on the square, and many were snatched up by passers-by, some
stepping into traffic to grab them. [@2] The packets held free marijuana samples
and business cards promoting the group's delivery service, bearing contact
details and the phrase "Free love". [@2]

Many of the packets drifted onto Ibn Gabirol Street after the drone's operators
apparently misjudged the wind, and people went into oncoming traffic to collect
them. [@2] Police said packets that landed in a children's playground were
confiscated to prevent them endangering minors. [@2] Police arrested two men in
their 30s at the scene shortly afterwards, linked them to the bags found across
the area and opened an investigation. [@2] The Jewish Telegraphic Agency, citing
the daily Maariv and a police spokeswoman, reported that the two men were
accused of drug trafficking. [@3]

A police spokesperson said distributing a suspected narcotic substance is
considered trafficking a dangerous drug, and that the arrested suspects would be
investigated accordingly. [@2] Before the drop the group announced its plans on
Telegram, saying it was launching a weekly "rain of cannabis" delivery of 1
kilogram divided into 2-gram bags to different parts of the country. [@3] The
group said the drops were meant to find a new way of getting marijuana to
consumers during the coronavirus crisis. [@3] Israel had partially
decriminalised possession of small amounts of marijuana at the time, while
distribution remained a criminal offence. [@2]
