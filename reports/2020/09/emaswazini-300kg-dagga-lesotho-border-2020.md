South African National Defence Force members deployed along KwaZulu-Natal's
international border with Lesotho carried out cordon and search operations with
the Upper Tugela South African Police Service in the eMaswazini area in the
early hours of Wednesday 9 September 2020. [@2] The soldiers inspecting an
abandoned residence found 300 kg of dagga, which the SANDF valued at an
estimated street value of R39 million. [@2] A rusted rifle and four live rounds
were found with the dagga. [@2] No one was found at the house and no arrests
were made over the dagga. [@3] A police spokesperson said the operation led to
the recovery of a rifle and six bags of dagga at a home at eMaswazini Reserve.
[@3]

The force's Joint Operations Division said a suspect was arrested in a separate
incident that morning after he was found with two 9 mm pistols, a magazine and
31 rounds of ammunition. [@1] The SANDF statement issued the same day likewise
said a suspect was arrested in another incident after he was found in possession
of two 9mm pistols and a magazine with 31 live rounds. [@2]

DefenceWeb reported that the operation near the South Africa/Lesotho border was
believed to be part of regular anti-crime work under Operation Notlela, the
SANDF's tasking in support of the national state of disaster to combat the
spread of coronavirus. [@1] The DefenceWeb account lists other recent border and
police successes, including a Hawks heroin seizure, but those are separate
events. [@1]
