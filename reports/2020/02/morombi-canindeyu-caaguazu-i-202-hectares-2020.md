About 70 SENAD agents worked for eight days in Morombí Natural Reserve, between
the departments of Canindeyú and Caaguazú, and destroyed 202 hectares of
marijuana. [@2] The operation was called "Canindeyú - Caaguazú I" and also
involved the National Forestry Institute (Infona), the Ministry of Environment
and the Public Prosecutor's Office. [@2] Nobody was detained during the
operation, whose procedures were led in Morombí by prosecutor Osvaldo García of
the Caaguazú anti-drug unit. [@2]

In Morombí alone the agents found 50 active marijuana plots covering 202
hectares of illegal plantation and destroyed about 600 tonnes of marijuana. [@2]
Authorities estimated the loss to trafficking groups in the area at 18 million
dollars. [@2] The agents also dismantled 23 camps set up by drug traffickers and
70 kilns where the remains of felled trees are turned into charcoal. [@1] La
Nación and Mongabay Latam reported the operation on a February morning, with the
helicopter base set up on cleared land ready for grain planting. [@2]

Commander Aldo Pintos of SENAD said the average marijuana plot is four to five
hectares, and that some of about 10 hectares had been found. [@2] Pintos said
traffickers know the agents are coming and have already left by the time they
arrive. [@2]

Satellite data showed Morombí lost nearly 4,000 hectares of tree cover between
2004 and 2019, most of it after 2014, and clearing resumed in the northern part
of the reserve after the February operation. [@3] A WWF report cited by Mongabay
Latam said 9,107 hectares had been deforested in the four reserves and parks
since 2004, at least 2,350 of them for marijuana plantations. [@1]
