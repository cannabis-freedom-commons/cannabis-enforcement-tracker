Officers of the Central African anti-drug office, the Office central de lutte
anti drogue (OCLAD), backed by the anti-banditry squad OCRB, searched a home in
Bangui's Combattant quarter on Friday 6 November 2020 and arrested two suspects
from the same family. [@1] The search found 15 sacks of cannabis herb, which the
OCLAD put at a total weight of 125.5 kilograms. [@1] Police also seized more
than a hundred Masse 36 war cartridges at the house. [@1] The home belonged to a
former Anti-Balaka self-defence militia leader known as "Chocolat", who was
already held at Ngaragba prison, according to the police account. [@1]

The deputy director of the OCRB said one of the two arrested men was the
Anti-Balaka leader's accomplice and that war munitions were found on the other.
[@1] One suspect said the war munitions were his own stock, which he had kept to
hand over at disarmament, and that he was surprised to be arrested. [@1] The
OCLAD said it would write to the public prosecutor to obtain an order to
incinerate the cannabis. [@1] The article's headline and opening paragraph put
the haul at more than 127 kilograms, the figure the OCLAD first gave, while its
commissioner later stated 125.5 kilograms. [@1] The article links the raid to
robberies and thefts that it says have become common in the Combattant quarter
and the eighth arrondissement. [@1]
