Gendarmes on duty at Meyo-Kyè in Ntem department arrested a 32-year-old Gabonese
driver on 10 November 2020. [@1] The Meyo-Kyè gendarmerie brigade stopped him
for a routine check at the village of Oveng-Essandone. [@1] At first sight the
pickup appeared to carry only foodstuffs, but a closer search exposed the
concealment. [@1] Under cassava leaves, banana bunches and sacks of other fresh
produce lay eight large sacks holding cannabis ballots. [@1] Taken to the post,
the suspected dealer denied the facts, saying he did not know he was also
carrying illicit goods. [@1] He told gendarmes the load was for a trader in
Libreville whose identity he did not know. [@1] Presented to the prosecutor, he
was charged with transport and detention of narcotics and placed in Oyem central
prison to await trial. [@1]

GabonReview described the load as more than 1,000 ballots of Indian hemp, the
local term for cannabis, estimated at 20 million CFA francs. [@1] A later court
account named the owner of the stock as drug trafficker Abraham Mve-Ndong, aged
33. [@2] It recalled that Mve-Ndong had been arrested by the Meyo-Kyè gendarmes
at Bitam. [@2] It said he was caught on 10 November 2020 at the wheel of a
Mitsubishi L200 filled with cannabis sacks. [@2] That account said 27 cannabis
ballots worth 20 million were thrown into the fire. [@2] It described the load
as 27 cannabis ballots in eight flour sacks destroyed by fire under procedure.
[@2] It estimated the stock at more than 300 kilograms, as reported by
provincial Radio Gabon. [@2]

Presented to an investigating judge, he was charged with illegal detention and
transport of Indian hemp. [@2] The offence was described as provided for and
punished by article 208 of the Penal Code. [@2] On 10 March 2021 the Oyem appeal
court and general prosecutor’s office incinerated the stock in the presence of
court chiefs and anti-drug officers. [@2] The prosecutor said the public
destruction of the dried herb followed several court decisions. [@2]
