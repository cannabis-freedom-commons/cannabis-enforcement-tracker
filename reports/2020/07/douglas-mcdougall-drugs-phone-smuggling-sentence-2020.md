The Court of General Gaol Delivery in Douglas jailed a 34-year-old Liverpool
construction worker for 11 years and 11 months after he admitted smuggling drugs
to the Isle of Man. [@2] Christopher McDougall admitted bringing £30,000 worth
of cocaine and cannabis to the island hidden in a car in 2019 and smuggling
mobile phones into Isle of Man Prison in 2018. [@1] At a previous hearing he had
pleaded guilty to production of more than £25,000 worth of cocaine to the island
and possession of the drug with intent to supply, as well as producing and
possessing cannabis with intent to supply. [@2]

A search of the car found 250 grams of cocaine worth £25,000 and £5,710 worth of
cannabis hidden in a secret compartment, along with a tick list and a large
amount of cash. [@2] A search of his hotel room found a further £1,000 in cash
and £100 worth of cocaine. [@2]

On Friday he changed his plea and admitted being involved in that £177,000
importation, having realised the other man was up to something but not what it
was. [@1] He had earlier admitted sending two mobile phones into Isle of Man
Prison concealed in a pair of trainers in March 2018. [@1] Deemster Graeme Cook
imposed eight years and eight months for the 2019 drugs offences, four months
for the phone offence and two years and eleven months for his involvement in the
2018 offences, and excluded him from the island for five years after his
release. [@1] Deemster Cook said the island took drugs offences seriously
because of the pain to society they caused, and that the drugs in the car were a
considerable amount. [@2] The judge said he had suspicions McDougall was more
involved in the earlier importation than he conceded, but accepted his basis of
plea of idle chit chat in the text messages. [@2] The defence said McDougall had
no involvement in bringing the earlier man's drugs to the island, although his
messages may have influenced that man. [@2] Deemster Cook said mobile phones act
as currency in prisons, letting inmates contact their families but also carry on
their criminal activities. [@2]
