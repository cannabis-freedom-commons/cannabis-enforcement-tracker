Customs Anti-Narcotics Unit (CANU) officers intercepted a fertiliser-laden truck
bound for Berbice on Mandela Avenue in Georgetown. [@2] The truck was parked at
the John Fernandes container terminal on Back Road, Mandela Avenue, where a
quantity of narcotics had been delivered to its driver, CANU deputy head Leslie
Ramlall said. [@1] The interception took place on 16 January 2020. [@4] CANU
said four people were arrested and taken to its headquarters, where they were
assisting with the investigation. [@3] The Guyana Chronicle reported that a
driver and two porters were arrested at the terminal. [@1]

Officers found two white salt bags containing 40 parcels of cannabis weighing 22
kilograms and two parcels of cocaine. [@2] CANU described the cannabis as
compressed 'poppy weed', a local name for the drug. [@1] Ramlall said the truck
was carrying fertiliser to Berbice and the cannabis was to be taken on to
Suriname. [@1]

A porter and a driver were jointly charged in January 2020 over the drugs. [@6]
The charge against the driver alleged that he supplied the 22 kilograms of
cannabis to the porter. [@4] The charge against the porter alleged that he
trafficked the same cannabis. [@4] Both denied the charges at the Georgetown
Magistrates' Courts. [@4] The magistrate remanded them to prison. [@4] The
porter was convicted of trafficking the drugs. [@5] The driver was acquitted
after the court found that CANU had not properly identified him as the supplier.
[@5] The porter was sentenced to three years in prison and fined GY$19.7
million. [@5] The porter told CANU officers that he collected the salt bags and
loaded them onto the truck to be taken to the Berbice Bridge for collection.
[@6]
