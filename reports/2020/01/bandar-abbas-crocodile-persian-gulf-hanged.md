Iran hanged a drug trafficker nicknamed the "Crocodile of the Persian Gulf" and
one of his accomplices. [@2] Both men were executed at the central prison in
Bandar Abbas, the capital of Hormozgan Province. [@7] The hanged leader was
36-year-old Eisa Zamin Koofte, whom the broadcaster Radio Farda named after
officials released only the initials A.Z. [@1] Radio Farda identified the second
hanged man as Masih Hatami, the second suspect in the case, after rights
reporters named him. [@4] Hormozgan's chief prosecutor, Ali Salehi, called
Koofte the leader of "one of the biggest and most vicious drug-trafficking
networks in Iran and the region". [@3] The judiciary said 18 other members of
the network had been convicted in the case. [@3] Iranian officials said all 18
were sentenced to life in prison. [@1] The BBC reported that other members
received up to five years or heavy fines, and that the gang's property was
seized. [@2]

Salehi said the network had smuggled more than 400 tonnes of drugs into Iran.
[@3] Koofte was arrested in 2015 while moving more than 100 tonnes of narcotics
through international waters. [@2] The prosecutor said the intelligence work
that led to the arrest lasted four to five years. [@3] Salehi alleged that
Koofte laundered about 20 trillion rials, the equivalent of $147 million,
through Iranian real estate. [@1] Haft-e Sobh reported that the network brought
its loads in by speedboat and dhow through the southern waters rather than the
eastern land border. [@5] The same daily said the cargo was opium and put the
total at about 400 tonnes. [@5]

Iran's anti-narcotics law makes possession of more than five kilograms of opium
or hashish, a cannabis-based drug, punishable by death. [@1] The hangings were
announced on 25 January 2020, but officials did not say on which day they were
carried out. [@6] Haft-e Sobh placed the executions at dawn on 23 January, three
days earlier. [@5]
