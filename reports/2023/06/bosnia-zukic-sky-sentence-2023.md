The Court of Bosnia and Herzegovina sentenced 32-year-old Alem Zukic to one year
in prison after he pleaded guilty to participating in a drug-trafficking
enterprise. [@1] The court also fined him 4,000 Bosnian marks, equal to about
2,000 euros, and ordered the deprivation of 2,000 euros described as unlawfully
acquired property benefit. [@1] The court said Zukic had cooperated from the
very beginning and that his early testimony significantly contributed to the
successful and efficient conclusion of the investigation. [@1] Zukic had been
arrested in April 2022 in the operation codenamed Diablo 2. [@1]

In that case he belonged to a group led by Sako Bibic and others suspected of
smuggling more than 1,000 kilograms of marijuana from Bosnia to European Union
territory. [@1] The verdict was reached through plea bargaining and was the
court's first based on evidence obtained through the global crackdown on the Sky
ECC and Anom apps. [@1]

Sky ECC and Anom are encrypted messaging apps popular with criminal
organisations whose intercepted communications Bosnia's top court accepted as
evidence. [@1] Lawyers in neighbouring Montenegro and Serbia question the
admissibility of Sky evidence and say transcripts alone should not support
detention and indictment without material evidence. [@1] A group of lawyers
protested in Podgorica on May 10, accusing Montenegro's Higher Court and Special
State Prosecution of misusing Sky ECC transcripts to order detentions and file
indictments. [@1]
