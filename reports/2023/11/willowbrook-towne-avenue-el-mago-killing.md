Eduardo Escobedo, 39, a convicted marijuana trafficker who worked for Ivan
Archivaldo Guzman Salazar, a son of Joaquin "El Chapo" Guzman, was shot dead on
Thanksgiving morning in the 14200 block of Towne Avenue, in an industrial
stretch of Willowbrook in Los Angeles County. [@1] Escobedo and a second man,
Guillermo De Los Angeles Jr., 47, died at the scene, and a third man was taken
to a hospital with gunshot wounds that were not life-threatening. [@1] Sheriff's
deputies went to the warehouses, truck yard and pallet storage area around 8
a.m. after the shooting, and Lieutenant Omar Camacho said there appeared to have
been a gathering or party at the location from the night before into the early
morning. [@1] El Financiero, citing the Los Angeles Times, reported that
Escobedo was shot on the morning of Thursday 23 November in an industrial zone,
and that a third wounded man was taken to hospital. [@4] NBC Los Angeles
reported that Escobedo was shot after an all-night party at what area workers
described as a clandestine club, and that the sheriff's department had not
revealed a motive. [@3] ABC7 reported that no arrests had been announced in the
killings. [@2]

Prosecutors said at a 2014 detention hearing that Escobedo had become the main
distributor of marijuana in Los Angeles for Guzman Salazar, and he laundered the
proceeds in part by buying exotic cars and shipping them to Culiacan, Sinaloa.
[@1] He served four years and nine months in federal prison after pleading
guilty to conspiring to distribute more than 10,000 kilograms of marijuana and
laundering drug proceeds, and was released in 2018. [@1] Prosecutors also
described how, in October 2013, Escobedo was recorded discussing with Guzman
Salazar the smuggling of more than five tons of marijuana through a tunnel under
the U.S.-Mexico border, and authorities seized 2.7 tons of cannabis from one of
his couriers. [@1]

Escobedo was alleged to have ordered the 2008 killing of a rival trafficker,
Jose Luis Macias, who was shot in a car on the 101 Freeway, but he was never
charged, while his brother and another man were convicted and are serving life
sentences. [@1] After his release Escobedo opened a chain of hibachi restaurants
and food trucks and successfully had his federal probation ended early, a judge
calling him "an enormous success." [@1] The other man killed, De Los Angeles,
was a member of the 18th Street gang who had been released from federal prison
in December 2022 after serving 10 years for distributing methamphetamine. [@1]
