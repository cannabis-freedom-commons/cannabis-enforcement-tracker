Jarrad Spence-Robinson, 26, from Enfield was convicted in February of murdering
23-year-old Seun McMillan on 2 May 2017. [@1] Mr McMillan was attacked in Cowper
Gardens in Enfield as he walked home with friends. [@1] He suffered a single
stab wound to the chest from a large bladed weapon. [@1] He claimed he supplied
Mr McMillan with a nine-ounce bar of cannabis and had been paid for some but not
all of the drugs. [@1] The court heard that he sought repayment for the drugs.
[@1] He accepted he caused the injury that led to Mr McMillan's death but
claimed he acted in lawful self-defence. [@1]

At the Old Bailey Judge Mark Lucraft KC sentenced Spence-Robinson to life
imprisonment with a minimum of 32 years for murder as well as conspiracy to
supply Class A and B drugs and conspiracy to transfer criminal property. [@1]
The conspiracy charges were unrelated to the murder charge. [@1] Having pleaded
guilty to the conspiracy charges, Spence-Robinson was jailed for 16 years for
conspiracy to supply Class A drugs, five years for conspiracy to supply Class B
drugs and four years for conspiracy to transfer criminal property, to run
concurrent with each other and the life sentence. [@1] Between March and June
2020 Spence-Robinson used the Encrochat handle Resilientmind to buy and supply
cocaine and cannabis at a wholesale level. [@1] The judge said Spence-Robinson
would have received a total of 373,500 pounds from the drug dealing, based on
the figures in the Encrochat messages. [@1]

He fled the United Kingdom after the killing and travelled to Northern Cyprus
before returning in August that same year. [@1] He gave no-comment responses in
police interviews and in 2017 the Crown Prosecution Service discontinued
proceedings before the case was reopened. [@1] He was charged with murder after
arriving at Heathrow Airport on a flight from Canada on 15 February last year.
[@1] Victim impact statements from Mr McMillan's family spoke of their profound
loss and described his killer as someone with no remorse who lived a life of
luxury built on dealing drugs. [@1] The court was told Spence-Robinson wore an
expensive watch and designer clothing, travelled widely and lived in a flat with
high security. [@1]
