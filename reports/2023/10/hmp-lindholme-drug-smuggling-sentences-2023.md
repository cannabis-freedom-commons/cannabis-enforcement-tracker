Sixteen people were sentenced on Friday for their roles in the complex smuggling
operation. [@1] They included five inmates of HMP Lindholme in South Yorkshire,
along with family members and friends who helped smuggle drugs into the prison
and launder the profits. [@1] Amy Hatfield, a mental health nursing assistant at
HMP Lindholme, flooded the jail with drugs by passing packages to prisoners.
[@1] Her inmate lover Joseph Whittingham recruited her to the conspiracy, and
she was jailed for 10 years and two months. [@1] Whittingham, one of the leaders
of the smuggling network, was jailed for 11 years and four months. [@1] Judge
Kirstie Watson, sentencing at Sheffield Crown Court, said drug-dealing in prison
was "an instrument of exploitation, oppression and power". [@1]

The group smuggled heroin, MDMA, spice, ketamine and cannabis into the prison,
and contraband sold inside for up to 10 times its value. [@1] South Yorkshire
Police uncovered the network after Hatfield was arrested in October 2019
carrying MDMA, cannabis and Ribena bottles filled with spice, along with
tobacco, anabolic steroids, mobile phones and chargers. [@1] Police estimated
the drugs she was caught arriving with had a prison value of £1 million. [@1]
The judge said Whittingham used Hatfield's access to drugs to gain power in
prison and significant financial gain. [@1] The court heard toxicology tests
found the spice recovered from Hatfield matched a batch that killed inmate Kyle
Batsford, who had been bullied into testing the drug in September 2019. [@1]
Another inmate was left in a coma for 10 days and permanently lost the use of
his legs after being pressured by Jordan Needham into testing a new batch of
spice. [@1]

Needham, who also recruited his mother Audrey into the conspiracy, was jailed
for nine and a half years. [@1] His partner Courtney Ward was jailed for four
years and six months, the judge calling her instrumental in organising smuggling
from outside prison. [@1] Anthony Campbell, serving a life sentence for the
fatal shooting of a 16-year-old boy in Liverpool in 2004, received deliveries
from visitors arranged by his mother Deborah Stoddard. [@1] Campbell was given a
further 11 years to begin when he would have been eligible for parole, and
Stoddard was jailed for nine and a half years. [@1] Kieran Murphy was jailed for
seven years and nine months. [@1] Aneeze Williamson was jailed for five years
and five months and Kora Haley for three years and four months. [@1] Audrey
Needham was jailed for four years and three months, Leighton Kemp for five years
and Lee Holmes for two years and three months. [@1] Natalie Williamson was
jailed for 12 months. [@1] Three defendants received suspended sentences for
money laundering: Paul Whittingham 20 months, Lucy Whittingham two years and
Lydia Pinnington 14 months. [@1] A seventeenth defendant, Francis, admitted
possession with intent to supply drugs and conveying drugs into prison and will
be sentenced separately in December. [@1] Police described the network as the
largest and most complex prison conspiracy ever seen in the UK. [@1]
