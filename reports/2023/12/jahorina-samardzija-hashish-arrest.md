Acting on an international warrant, police in Bosnia and Herzegovina arrested a
man wanted in Slovenia for trafficking drugs throughout Europe. [@1] Slovenian
authorities described him as dangerous. [@1] Media identified the man as
33-year-old Mladen Samardžija, a Slovenian citizen who used dual Bosnian
citizenship to avoid courts in Slovenia. [@1] Police said he is suspected of
unauthorized production and trafficking of narcotics, organized crime, money
laundering and obstruction of justice. [@1] He was arrested on Monday on Mount
Jahorina near Sarajevo. [@3] Republika Srpska police said he was apprehended in
the police operation codenamed Tokio aimed at arresting a member of an organized
crime group dealing in drug trade. [@3]

During the operation, police seized one kilogram of cocaine and a small quantity
of marijuana, along with cash, four pistols, ammunition, a laptop, phones and
several vehicles. [@1] Officers of the Republika Srpska Interior Ministry's
organized-crime directorate, under the supervision of the Bosnian Prosecutor's
Office, searched locations used by Samardžija in East Sarajevo during the Tokio
operation. [@2] In those searches, police listed 12 grams of hashish among the
items temporarily seized, alongside money, phones, pistols, ammunition,
notebooks, watches and bank cards. [@2] Police also seized five cars, described
as a Golf 7, Polo, Audi RS3, Ford Focus and Škoda Octavia. [@2] Investigators
said he was involved in the transportation and sale of over 40 kilograms of
cocaine and half a kilo of heroin. [@1]

After Samardzija fled Slovenia, he continued to run an organized criminal group
operating in the European Union, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Serbia and Montenegro.
[@3] The police operation was the result of an investigation carried out under
the supervision of the Prosecutor's Office of Bosnia and Herzegovina. [@3] The
operation was carried out with Europol support. [@3] The suspect was to be
handed over to the State Prosecutor's Office for further processing. [@3]
Regional media speculated about his clan ties, reporting in Bosnia and
Herzegovina that he was a close friend of Jovan Vukotić, the head of the
Škaljari clan. [@1] Some Montenegrin media linked him instead to the rival Kavač
clan, and both clans originate from Kotor and split over a cocaine deal that
went bad in 2014 after partnering in smuggling from South America into Europe.
