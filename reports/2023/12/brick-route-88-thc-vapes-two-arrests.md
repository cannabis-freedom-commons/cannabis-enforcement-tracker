Brick Township police said a lengthy investigation by the department's Street
Crimes Unit found that Welsh Farms, at 1765 Route 88, and Pantry Farm, at 2085
Route 88, were selling and distributing illegal THC products. [@1] Search
warrants were executed at both stores on 14 December 2023. [@2] Both stores were
shut down after the searches. [@2]

Sarajbit Kaur, 46, of Brick, the owner and operator of Welsh Farms, was arrested
and charged with five counts of distributing THC products, intent to distribute
THC products, two counts of possession of prescription legend drugs and money
laundering. [@2] Mannu Prasad, 61, of Brick, the owner and operator of Pantry
Farm, was arrested and charged with three counts of conspiracy to distribute THC
products, intent to distribute THC products, intent to distribute psilocybin and
money laundering. [@2] Kaur and Prasad were processed and released on summonses.
[@1]

Patrol Officer Victoria Finelli, a police spokesperson, said more charges were
anticipated. [@2]

Welsh Farms was inspected by the Treasury and Consumer Affairs inspectors, who
were filing charges of selling expired medication, selling flavored vape
products, which are prohibited, and attempting to sell merchandise without a tag
or label. [@2] The Pantry Farm inspection was expected to result in charges of
selling expired medication, selling prohibited flavored vape products, having an
unregistered weighing and measuring device and failure to have a registration
certificate. [@2] The New Jersey Department of Health and the Division of State
Lottery were to follow up with their own inspections at both locations for any
other violations. [@1]

Cannabis is legal for recreational use and sale in New Jersey but must be sold
through a licensed store, and Brick has previously prohibited recreational
cannabis sales by ordinance. [@4]

Brick police cited the owners of both stores, and owners of licensed vape and
dispensary businesses told News 12 that black-market sales undercut them because
the products are untaxed and unregulated. [@3] Vincent Mattia, owner of NJ Vapor
Vault, said black-market sellers "are not paying their taxes, and they're not
getting observed and watched". [@3]
