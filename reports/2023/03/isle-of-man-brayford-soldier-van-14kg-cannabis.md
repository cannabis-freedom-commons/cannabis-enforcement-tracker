Jordan Ashley Brayford, 29, a soldier from Bentilee in Stoke-on-Trent, was
jailed for five years and one month at Douglas Courthouse on 17 March 2023 after
bringing cannabis to the Isle of Man in a van. [@2] Deemster Graeme Cook
sentenced him to five years and one month for the importation and four years to
run concurrently for possession with intent to supply. [@3] He pleaded guilty to
importing cannabis and possessing the drug with intent to supply. [@1] The court
also banned him from returning to the island for five years after his release.
[@1] He had already admitted importing cannabis worth £284,000 and was remanded
in custody ahead of the March hearing. [@4]

He arrived from Heysham on the ferry on 27 January in a Mercedes van. [@3] He
left the van in a lane at Greeba and went drinking, then could not remember
where he had parked it. [@1] The following day he went to Ramsey police station
and told officers he had lost the van, and checks showed he was absent without
leave, so he was arrested. [@2] Isle of Man Today reported that police arrested
him at the Ramsey Park Hotel on 30 January and found the van at Greeba the next
day. [@3] He had been absent without leave since 2 January from the 25 Training
Regiment Royal Logistics Corps at Normandy Barracks in Leconfield, Yorkshire.
[@3]

Police found the van damaged and abandoned with the keys in the ignition, and a
search uncovered the cannabis in 30 vacuum-sealed bags in two concealed
compartments in the floor. [@1] The compartments were opened with a remote
control found on Brayford. [@1] Manx Radio said the packages were cannabis bush
worth £284,232, and Deemster Cook put the weight at 14.2 kg. [@2] His advocate
said he had agreed to drive the van to help pay off a debt, but had not known
how much cannabis was inside. [@1]

Deemster Cook said the quantity meant Brayford must be close to those involved
in organised crime, and that he had become involved in a bizarre set of
circumstances. [@1] The deemster said Brayford had been fortunate the drugs in
the van were class B rather than class A. [@1] He had joined the army in
February 2022 and had been discharged after his arrest. [@2]
