Former Customs and Border Control officer Edree Fisher was jailed for nine
months after she was convicted of conspiring to import more than six pounds of
ganja into Cayman through Owen Roberts International Airport. [@1] Justice
Marlene Carter imposed 18 months but suspended half the term for personal
mitigating circumstances, including that Fisher is the primary caregiver of two
young children, one of whom has a serious undisclosed illness. [@1] Cayman News
Service, which uses the spelling Eddree Joann Fisher, says she was first
arrested in May 2018, was charged three and a half years later, and then pleaded
guilty to conspiracy to import ganja in June 2022, admitting she had helped a
friend and steered him through customs with drugs. [@2]

Fisher was arrested on 14 May 2018 and charged on 15 December 2021 on a single
charge of conspiracy to import a controlled drug, ganja. [@1] She first pleaded
not guilty, and her trial was called off in September 2022, after she changed
her plea to guilty. [@1] In sentencing her on Wednesday 18 January, Carter said
Fisher's role was a leading one and that she abused her position of trust and
power as a Customs and Border Control officer. [@1] The judge said her intimate
knowledge of the service's procedures ensured the seamless entry of the drugs
through Owen Roberts International Airport and undermined its effectiveness at
detecting them. [@1] Cayman News Service describes Fisher as an essential cog in
a conspiracy with Corey James Miller and Kelven Claret that ran between February
and April 2018, when Claret and Miller were arrested in Miami. [@2]

Carter noted that if the same quantity of 6.48 pounds was smuggled in during all
five attempts, Fisher would have helped to smuggle more than 30 pounds of ganja
into Cayman. [@1] Cayman News Service says Fisher was charged only over less
than three kilos of cannabis on the final trip, and that Miller passed through
the airport on five other occasions when he is believed to have been carrying
ganja in washing powder boxes. [@2] Fisher was arrested by CBC Assistant
Director Tina Campbell, and her seized phones showed she had signed Claret's
customs declaration and exchanged messages with Miller to move the men through
the airport undetected. [@2]

Carter started from a sentence of three years, having noted that the maximum for
conspiring to import ganja was four years, then cut 25% rather than the
customary 30% because of the late guilty plea. [@1] Cayman Compass reported that
the remaining two years and three months were cut by three months for personal
mitigation and by a further six months for the delay between her 2018 arrest and
2023 sentencing. [@1] Cayman News Service said the court found the delay of
almost five years from arrest to sentencing excessive and said she had had the
case hanging over her before she was charged. [@2] A social inquiry report found
Fisher extremely remorseful, and she said she acted because she had a "stupid
but kind heart" and was concerned for her friend. [@2]
