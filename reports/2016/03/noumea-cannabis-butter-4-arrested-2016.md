National police narcotics officers searched a home in Nouméa on 8 March 2016 and
detained four people. [@1] The four habitually prepared their own cannabis
product from butter and cannabis. [@1] The butter mixture was used to prepare
dishes and desserts. [@1] After an extended period in custody, the suspects were
released. [@1] They were due in the criminal court in early September on a
drug-possession charge, carrying up to 10 years in prison and fines of several
million francs. [@1]

Officers seized 400 grams of cannabis butter, described as a green greasy paste
with a nauseating smell. [@1] Officers also found 3.5 kilograms of cannabis
plants and powder in the flat, meant for the next batch. [@1]

The seized butter was described as a local product made by Nouméa residents and
as a form of use said to have existed in New Caledonia for many years. [@1] The
report said cannabis eaten rather than smoked has particular health
consequences, and that with more than 80 percent tetrahydrocannabinol the
effects are stronger. [@1]
