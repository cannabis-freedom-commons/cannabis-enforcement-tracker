Federal agents seized what the U.S. Attorney's Office called the longest
cross-border tunnel ever discovered on the California-Mexico border, an
800-yard passage running from a house in Tijuana to an outdoor fenced
commercial lot in an Otay Mesa industrial park about 500 yards north of the
border. [@1] The tunnel sat 46 feet underground, had a ventilation system and
lighting, and ended at a lot advertised as a wooden pallet business. [@2]

Agents found two tons of cocaine and seven tons of marijuana, worth nearly 22
million dollars combined, and officials called it the largest cocaine seizure
ever associated with a tunnel. [@1] The marijuana totalled more than 14,000
pounds, about 6,350 kg. [@2] Agents had been chasing alleged traffickers when
they saw a commercial truck deliver an industrial dumpster full of wood scraps
to the lot and found the tunnel entry. [@2]

Six people were arrested in San Diego and charged with various drug
trafficking charges. [@1] U.S.
Attorney Laura Duffy said traffickers were moving multi-ton quantities of
cocaine and marijuana through the tunnel in full view of the world around it.
[@1] The tunnel was the thirteenth sophisticated tunnel found on the
California-Mexico border since 2006, and it was not attributed to any specific
trafficking group. [@2]
