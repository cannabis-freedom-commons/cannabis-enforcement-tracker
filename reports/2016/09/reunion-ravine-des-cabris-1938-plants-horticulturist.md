Police detained a horticulturist at his home on Impasse des Basilics, in the
Ravine-des-Cabris at Saint-Pierre on Réunion, over zamal, the Creole word for
cannabis, grown under cover. [@1] Inside they found 1,938 zamal plants that had
grown in four months in a space fitted out for the crop, with air extractors,
lamps and a blower. [@1] Officers also seized 8 kilograms of dried cannabis
heads, without leaves or branches. [@1]

The grower, a 23-year-old horticulturist from Saint-Joseph who had just moved to
Saint-Pierre, was taken into custody. [@1] On 22 September 2016 he was indicted
for drug trafficking and remanded in custody. [@1] The public prosecutor of
Saint-Pierre said the grower had himself claimed during his police custody that
his was "a quality zamal". [@1] Despite his cooperation, investigators suspected
he had not told the whole truth and that he supplied a wide network of dealers.
[@1] He said he had only just started producing zamal, but officers judged that
the dried cannabis could not have been produced in four months. [@1]

The Saint-Pierre criminal court tried the 25-year-old on 6 September 2018 for
possession and trafficking of drugs and gave him 12 months in prison, eight of
them suspended, plus a €15,000 fine. [@2] The four months of firm prison had
already been served in pre-trial detention. [@2] He had no previous convictions
before the indictment, was a cannabis user himself and said he had first grown
the plant for his own consumption. [@2]
