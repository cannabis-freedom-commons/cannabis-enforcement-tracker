Chijioke Stephen Obioha, a 38-year-old Nigerian, was hanged on 18 November 2016 at Changi Prison
Complex. [@1,2] He had been sentenced to death on 30 December 2008 for having 2,604.56 grams of
cannabis for trafficking, more than five times the 500-gram capital threshold. [@1,2]

## Arrest in 2007

Anti-narcotics officers arrested him on 9 April 2007 in a taxi with a bag holding thousands of
grams of vegetable matter, then searched his rented room. [@1] The prosecution tried him first on
2,604.56 grams of cannabis from the room while larger totals of cannabis and cannabis mixture were
also recorded. [@1] His appeal was dismissed in August 2010. [@1,7]

## Trial and death sentence

The trial judgment records that officers found twelve blocks of vegetable matter at Block 465
Choa Chu Kang Avenue 4 and that analysis showed 2,604.56 grams of cannabis; the prosecution
proceeded on that one trafficking charge and stood three further charges down. [@3] The High
Court convicted and sentenced him after a 21-day trial. [@4,5] He had come to Singapore in 2005 to
try out for a football club, and narcotics officers arrested him following a surveillance
operation. [@4,5] He was not hired and stayed as a middleman for electronics dealers; his immigration pass expired in January 2007. [@4,5]

Contemporary accounts described a wider April operation involving more than 20 kilograms of cannabis, valued at about S$410,000. [@8] The 2007 account said a 21-year-old Zambian woman arrived from Johannesburg with fourteen one-kilogram packets in her luggage. [@8] A June report identified her as Daka Guinea, accused of handing the drugs to Obioha at a hotel; both appeared in court that June. [@9] Early accounts called Obioha a 20-year-old Ghanaian, conflicting with the later official identification. [@4,5,6]

## Re-sentencing and final appeals

After Singapore amended the death penalty regime in 2013 he first declined re-sentencing, then
sought it in 2015 before withdrawing in August 2016. [@1,7] On 16 November 2016 his counsel asked
to stay the execution and commute the sentence to life; the Court of Appeal dismissed the motion on
17 November. [@1,2] Clemency petitions from him and the High Commission of Nigeria failed before
the hanging. [@1] The same morning another man was executed for heroin trafficking; that case is a
separate event. [@2]
