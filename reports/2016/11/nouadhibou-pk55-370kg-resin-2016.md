Mauritanian gendarmes seized 370 kg of chanvre indien, cannabis resin known
locally as "pierres marocaines", from a truck coming from Morocco. [@1]
Gendarmes arrested two Moroccans who were in the truck at a checkpoint 55 km
north of Nouadhibou. [@1] The drug was hidden inside a metal box welded to the
vehicle's frame and covered by a load of about 28 tonnes of potatoes bound for
the Mauritanian market. [@1,3] The driver's behaviour made the gendarmes
suspicious and they deepened the search with sniffer dogs. [@1] The drugs were
found at the PK55 post, the first control point on Mauritanian territory after
the border post with Morocco. [@2,3]

Nouadhibou's prosecutor's office and the local gendarmerie gave the details on
the Tuesday after the interception. [@2] The publication said truckers carrying
fruit and vegetables to Mauritania, Senegal and Mali also try to move drugs
using contacts at the checkpoints. [@2] Mauritania is not seen as a large
hashish consuming market, but its porous borders offer transit opportunities to
traffickers supplying Mali and Senegal, specialists say. [@2] Mauritania is not
considered a consumer market for the drug, the concern being how easily it could
become a transit point given its borders of more than 5,000 km on land and 740
km at sea. [@1] The Agencia de Presse Africaine (APA), the African Press Agency,
covered the seizure for the news agency and reported it from a security source.
