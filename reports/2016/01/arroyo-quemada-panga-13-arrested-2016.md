Thirteen defendants made their initial appearance in federal court in Los
Angeles on Monday, 1 February 2016, to face drug smuggling charges after
officers intercepted a Mexican panga carrying nearly 3,000 pounds of marijuana
in southern Santa Barbara County over the weekend. [@1] A criminal complaint
filed the previous Sunday charged the 13 with possession of marijuana with the
intent to distribute, and included Hector Raul Bernal-Lara, 41; Ricardo
Sanchez-Marquez, 36; Daniel Aguilar, 25; Mark Garcia, 23; Susana Tobaldo, 42;
Kevin Tes, 22; Josh Rubio, 21; Alfonso Aguilar-Ballestros, 48; Jesus
Moreno-Sepulveda, 31; Santiago Galvan-Carrillo, 48; Paul Armenta-Bueno, 34;
Bryan Castro, 18; and Daniel Fernando-Huizar, 26. [@1]

The U.S. Coast Guard spotted the panga with three occupants early on Saturday,
30 January 2016, at Arroyo Quemada Beach, about 20 miles west of Santa Barbara.
[@2] According to the affidavit, authorities believed three people were piloting
the boat and saw about 15 people on shore helping to unload the bales. [@1] Two
vans and a pick-up truck drove separately to the beach, bales were loaded into
the truck and one van, and the vehicles left about the same time in different
directions. [@1] Officers stopped the marijuana van in Camarillo, the second van
when it returned to Arroyo Quemada Beach, and the truck carrying marijuana in
Carpinteria. [@1] The Los Angeles Times reported that 13 people were arrested on
suspicion of distributing marijuana. [@2]

Law enforcement seized 114 bales of marijuana weighing in excess of 2,800
pounds, about 1,270 kg, from the two vehicles. [@1] U.S. Immigration and Customs
Enforcement’s Homeland Security Investigations led the investigation with the
Coast Guard, Border Patrol and the sheriff’s departments of Santa Barbara,
Ventura and Los Angeles counties. [@1]

U.S. Attorney Eileen M. Decker said smuggling by panga boat endangers the lives
of both the smugglers and the authorities interdicting the boats and their
payloads, and that law enforcement would keep using all available means to
curtail it. [@1] The charges in the complaint carry a maximum penalty of life in
prison, and the Times reported that each defendant faces up to life if
convicted. [@2] A criminal complaint contains allegations that a defendant has
committed a crime, and every defendant is presumed innocent until and unless
proven guilty in court. [@1]
