The entire 11-person Montenegrin crew of the Panama-flagged survey vessel Remus
was arrested on international drug-trafficking charges. [@2] Montenegrin police
said the 11 suspects were taken into custody by Italian police. [@1] Italian
police said they seized the vessel in international waters on July 31 and
escorted it to the Sicilian port of Palermo. [@2] A later Montenegrin police
account identified the vessel as the Panama-flagged Remus stopped with 11
Montenegrin crew members arrested. [@3]

Italian police said they found 20 tons of hashish in the fuel tanks of the ship
after it was stopped and escorted to Sicily. [@2] The drugs were packed in 650
sacks and hidden in two tanks. [@2] Officers drained the fuel tanks in Palermo
and found the hash wrapped in sealed brown bags. [@2] Montenegro’s early
announcement had given a preliminary figure of 2,000 kilograms of suspected
hashish. [@1]

The ship had sailed from the Montenegrin port of Zelenika and was suspected of
planning to load hashish in Morocco for smuggling by sea to Libya. [@1]
Montenegrin drug officers said they had obtained operational information that an
organized criminal group intended to smuggle a large amount of hashish from
Morocco to Libya. [@1] Because the route did not pass through Montenegrin
territorial waters, the officers said they exchanged the information with the
DEA, which then planned the joint operation. [@1] The international action was
coordinated by the United States Drug Enforcement Agency. [@1] Police said
statements were being taken from other crew members and that further
criminal-investigation activities would follow. [@1]
