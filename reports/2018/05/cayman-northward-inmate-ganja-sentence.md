Randy James Parchment was an inmate at Northward Prison with five years left on
a firearms sentence when he was sentenced to 30 months more for possession of
ganja with intent to supply. [@1] Parchment pleaded guilty to the ganja offence.
[@1] He had been sentenced in 2016 to seven years for possessing a handgun and a
concurrent seven years for possessing unlicensed ammunition. [@1]

Parchment was transported to a medical appointment at Cayman Islands Hospital
and asked to use the restroom. [@1] After leaving the restroom, police noticed
he was walking strangely and found a package secured inside his pants. [@1] The
package held three phones, three phone chargers, cigarette paper and .38
kilograms of matter later proved to be ganja. [@1] The cigarette paper was
presented as evidence that Parchment intended to sell the ganja at Northward.
[@1]

Defence counsel asked Magistrate Valdis Foldats to credit the early guilty plea
and consider totality at sentencing. [@1] Magistrate Foldats said Northward
drug-smuggling cases had come before him, but this was the first where the
alleged smuggler was a prisoner. [@1] The magistrate set three years as the
starting point with no mitigating factors, added two years for aggravating
factors, then applied a one-third discount for the early guilty plea to reach 40
months. [@1] After adding a triggered six-month suspended sentence, the
magistrate reduced the total of 46 months to a final 30 months because Parchment
still had five years to serve on the firearms charge. [@1]
