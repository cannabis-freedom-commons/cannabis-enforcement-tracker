U.S. District Judge R. Brooke Jackson sentenced Margarito Yepez-Sanchez, 25,
to 36 months in federal prison followed by three years of supervised release
for conspiracy to manufacture and possess with intent to distribute 100 or
more marijuana plants. [@1] The sentencing hearing took place on 15 November
2018 in case 17-cr-00372-RBJ-2, prosecuted by Assistant U.S. Attorney Emily
May. [@1,2]

Federal agents had discovered the illegal grow in the San Isabel National
Forest in central Colorado in August 2017, and officers from several
agencies executed a federal search warrant on the site on 20 September
2017. [@1] The grow held about 13,800 marijuana plants and at least 120
pounds (about 54 kg) of processed marijuana across some 12 acres of federal
land. [@1] The camp had sleeping areas, kitchens, drying areas and a
man-made reservoir, and agents found insecticide, rodenticide and
fertilizer. [@1] Yepez-Sanchez fled uphill when officers announced
themselves and was caught after a foot chase in which officers deployed a
Taser. [@1] He had been charged by criminal complaint on 21 September 2017
and pleaded guilty on 10 August 2018. [@1] The U.S. Forest Service and
Homeland Security Investigations ran the case, assisted by immigration
officers, the Custer County sheriff and the Colorado National Guard. [@1]
