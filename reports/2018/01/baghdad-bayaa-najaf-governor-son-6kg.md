The Baghdad Operations Command said a security force arrested a three-member
drug-trafficking gang in the Bayaa district, south of Baghdad, on Sunday 21
January 2018. [@1] A security source told Kurdistan 24 that the group had three
members and was led by Jawad Luay al-Yasiri, the son of the governor of Najaf.
[@1] Najaf governor Luay al-Yasiri said his son had gone to Baghdad two days
earlier with two friends, one of whom was under security surveillance and
carrying a bag of drugs. [@2] The governor said the bag held an estimated 5.8
kilograms of drugs and 1,000 pills, and that his son denied any connection to it
because the bag belonged to a friend. [@2]

Activists posted pictures of six kilos of cannabis, 800 narcotic pills, forged
identity cards and weapons that Iraqi security had seized. [@1] The pictures
showed official access cards for the intelligence service, the Iraqi cabinet and
Najaf province, all in the name of Jawad Luay Jawad, and one designating him a
lieutenant. [@1] The governor called for the case to be handled with full
impartiality regardless of the accused's identity, and said he suspected a
political smear campaign ahead of the May elections. [@1] The governor said some
websites had doubled the amounts seized, and that the badges found with his son
included a vehicle-driving permit, a governorate pass for his job there and a
shrine pass. [@2]

Interior Minister Qasim al-Araji ordered the officers who arrested the three
suspects honoured, and the officials who leaked pictures of the accused and the
incident report to be detained. [@3] On 11 March 2018 the Karkh Criminal Court
in Baghdad sentenced Jawad Luay al-Yasiri to life in prison and a fine of 30
million Iraqi dinars on charges of drug dealing and transport. [@3] A judicial
source said the son of governor Luay al-Yasiri had been caught red-handed
smuggling six kilograms of drugs and 800 other narcotic pills into Baghdad, at
the Bayaa police station. [@3] The Karkh Criminal Court said in a statement that
it sentenced Jawad Luay al-Yasiri to life in prison after convicting him of
possessing narcotics in Baghdad, and fined him 30 million dinars. [@4]
