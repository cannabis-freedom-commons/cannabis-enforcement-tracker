Michael John Ramos, 37, previously pleaded guilty and the court ordered a social
inquiry report before sentencing. [@2] He was sentenced on Wednesday to six
months’ imprisonment, suspended for two years. [@2] Magistrate Grace Donalds
imposed the six-month term for cultivating ganja without being authorized to do
so. [@2] Defence attorney Jonathon Hughes said Ramos had wanted the ganja for
medicinal purposes; he had since seen a doctor and sought a legal remedy for his
condition. [@2] The social inquiry report described him as a dedicated, hard
worker and a responsible provider for his children. [@2]

The charge concerned 42 ganja trees at a premises in Prospect, Grand Cayman.
[@1] Ramos was charged with cultivating the trees on 27 April 2017. [@1] He also
faced charges of possession of ganja with intent to supply, simple possession
and consumption. [@1] At his first appearance, Magistrate Angelyn Hernandez read
the Crown’s summary of the allegations. [@1] Ramos did not say anything, and the
magistrate appointed Jonathon Hughes as duty counsel to assist him. [@1] The
magistrate set the matter for mention again on 8 August 2017, and bail
conditions included surrender of his passport, a $1,500 local surety and
reporting to George Town Police Station twice a week. [@1] The summary said the
defendant seemed to believe ganja was going to be legal soon and would need a
large amount to produce oil for his personal use. [@1]

He had a suspended sentence for a previous offence, and Hughes asked that it not
be activated; Donalds agreed because the previous offence was of a different
nature. [@2] For possession and consumption, she imposed concurrent sentences of
three and two months. [@2] At the Crown’s request, she ordered the ganja and
ganja trees forfeited and destroyed. [@2]
