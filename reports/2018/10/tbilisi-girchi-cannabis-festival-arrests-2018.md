Police took presidential candidate Zurab Japaridze from a cannabis-legalization
festival in a central Tbilisi park to a police station. [@1] Three Girchi
members had been taken to police stations before the event began. [@1] Japaridze
was released a few hours later after being searched and questioned in detail in
an opened criminal case. [@1] Police said Japaridze went with officers
voluntarily to take part in investigative actions. [@1] The investigation
concerns alleged illegal acquisition, storage and distribution of marijuana
following Girchi's announcement of the festival. [@1]

Officers led Japaridze away after he demonstratively handed participants what he
called joints, marijuana cigarettes, to applause. [@1] He said he had
distributed three cigarettes and called the act distribution for which people
are jailed in Georgia. [@1] He urged supporters not to resist police if he was
detained. [@1] He did not resist himself, although some people tried to hinder
officers. [@1] Police had cordoned off the park where the festival was to be
held. [@1] Many people and many police were present at the festival. [@1] A
small counter-protest opposed the event, carrying a poster reading Drug
addiction is death. [@1]

Organizers said the festival was to be a place where people could buy
hemp-related items and spend time pleasantly. [@1] Japaridze called the handover
an act of civil disobedience, saying Georgia needed a lawful way to acquire
cannabis. [@1] The Interior Ministry had twice warned that it viewed Girchi's
festival announcement as intent to organize a crime and incitement to offences.
[@1] Georgia's Constitutional Court had cancelled administrative penalties for
marijuana use in July after a claim by Japaridze and Vakhtang Megrelishvili.
[@1] That ruling covered only consumption, while storage and especially
distribution of marijuana remained punishable in Georgia. [@1]
