On 4 October 2018 Attorney General Xavier Becerra announced that California's Campaign Against Marijuana Planting (CAMP) had eradicated 614,267 illegally grown cannabis plants during the 2018 season, in the largest illegal-grow eradication program in the nation. [@1,2] Agents raided more than 254 illegal grow sites across 40 counties over twelve weeks, arresting 52 people and seizing 110 weapons. [@1,2]

## Where and how

Four teams covering northern, central and southern California targeted grows hidden mostly on protected public land such as the Trinity Alps Wilderness and the Palomar mountains, Becerra said at a San Diego press conference. [@1,2] One site diverted water from the San Joaquin River in Stanislaus County, and several others used federally banned toxic pesticides. [@2] The operation combined the state justice department with the U.S. Forest Service, the Drug Enforcement Administration, the National Guard and local agencies. [@1]

## A smaller haul

The 2018 take was less than half the 1.26 million plants eradicated in 2017 and the more than 1.5 million destroyed in 2016. [@2] Becerra's office partly blamed the drop on that summer's wildfires, which burned over 688,000 acres of northern California and destroyed legal and illegal crops alike, alongside the effect of recreational legalization. [@2]
