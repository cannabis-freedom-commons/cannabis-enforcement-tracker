Nine alleged members of a marijuana trafficking network were arrested on 1
August in Salto del Guairá, the capital of Canindeyú department near Paraguay's
eastern border with Brazil. [@1] Among those detained were alleged leader and
main financier Flaviano Giménez, his son and alleged second-in-command Flavio
Junior Giménez Spaini, and alleged transporter Jorge Daniel Zorrilla Vera. [@1]
Auxiliary anti-drugs prosecutor Wilson Gauto Campuzano was arrested for
allegedly taking kickbacks to give the criminal group intelligence. [@1]
Ex-soldier Jorge Daniel Zorrilla was arrested for allegedly managing storage of
the drugs. [@1] Twin brothers Wilson Rubén Cáceres González and Wilson Darío
Cáceres González, the second an ex-policeman, were accused of helping move the
drugs through Canindeyú. [@1] Both brothers remained fugitives, according to
Paraguayan news outlet ABC Color. [@1] Several police officers accused of
accepting bribes were dismissed. [@1]

A year-long investigation identified a well-structured protection system of
serving and retired police, army, customs and prosecution officials who accepted
traffickers' bribes to ignore drug shipments and warn the network about
investigations and raids. [@1] During the operation authorities seized written
records of bribe payments, more than a dozen vehicles, several boats,
semi-automatic weapons and almost 16 tonnes of marijuana valued at about US$8
million. [@1]

Investigators said the scheme moved tonnes of marijuana from a remote plantation
through several department cities on main highways because of bribe payments.
[@1] Once shipments reached the departmental capital they were stored in a
facility near the local customs office and then moved to clandestine ports on
the Paraná River. [@1] From there the marijuana was taken by river to Brazil
aboard speedboats. [@1]
