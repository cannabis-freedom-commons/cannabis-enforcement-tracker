Lucas Crosby, a 63-year-old Grenadian national married to a Vincentian woman,
was stopped at Argyle International Airport (AIA) with 64 grammes of cannabis on
7 August 2018. [@1] A security officer found plant-like material resembling
cannabis on him and in his carry-on luggage, and a plain-clothes officer was
called. [@2] Crosby was arrested, taken to the Criminal Investigations
Department and charged at the Serious Offences Court the same morning. [@1]

He faced charges of possession with intent to supply, drug trafficking and
attempting to export cannabis, and pleaded guilty to all three. [@1] A
transparent bag wrapped in a jersey was found in his carry-on hand luggage, the
court heard. [@1] iWitness News reported that he pleaded guilty to possession of
64 grammes (2.25 ounces) of cannabis for drug trafficking, possession with
intent to supply and attempted exportation. [@2] The court was told he had no
previous convictions. [@2]

Defence lawyer Grant Connell said hasty packing on departure left a relatively
small amount of marijuana on Crosby and in his carry-on. [@1] Connell put the
value of the drug at $75. [@1] Connell called the case a reason to amend the law
towards a ticketing system, said it affected tourism as well as young people,
and warned that a criminal record would complicate Crosby's return to see his
wife. [@1]

Chief Magistrate Rechanne Browne-Matthias fined him $300 for possessing the
drug, $500 for drug trafficking and $500 for attempting to export it, a total of
$1,300. [@1] iWitness News reported that he was ordered to pay EC$1,300 or spend
five months in prison, and that the total fine was EC$1,300. [@2] Crosby paid
the fine and left court shortly afterwards. [@1]
