Two men face felony drug importing charges after authorities intercepted a
package containing 1,099 gross grams of suspected marijuana, according to a
magistrate's complaint filed in the Superior Court of Guam. [@1] The package
originated in Anaheim, California, and was addressed to Karl Melencio. [@1]
Melencio and his friend Trevor Moss were charged in connection with the package.
[@1]

After finding the suspected marijuana, authorities removed the drugs, replaced
them with a tracking device, and conducted a controlled delivery on Monday. [@1]
An undercover agent delivered the package, and Melencio later arrived at the
house, retrieved the package and left. [@1] Melencio drove to a grocery store,
where Moss took the package from the passenger side of Melencio's car, then sped
off. [@1] Officers believed Moss was trying to evade surveillance because he was
speeding and swerving into and out of his lane. [@1] Authorities eventually
pulled Moss over and took the package. [@1]

Moss said he met his friend Karl in Maite and that Karl had asked him to return
a package, adding that he saw vehicles following him and kept driving. [@1]
Melencio said he was going to return the package to the post office when Moss
called and asked if Melencio could give it to Moss. [@1] Melencio said he did
not know what was in the package but gave it to Moss, and said Moss had
previously asked him to use his mailing address. [@1] Melencio and Moss were
each charged with importation of a Schedule I controlled substance as a
first-degree felony and conspiracy to import a Schedule I controlled substance
as a first-degree felony. [@1]

A later Pacific Daily News roundup of priority-mail drug seizures lists 1,099
grams of marijuana in a package sent to a Dededo street address. [@2]
