Five men were arrested after Guyana's Customs Anti-Narcotic Unit stopped a
vehicle on Parika Public Road, East Bank Essequibo, on 6 June 2018 and found
cannabis and cocaine hidden in two plastic gas bottles in the trunk. [@2] The
agency said the haul was one of its biggest since it was set up: 35 kilogrammes
of marijuana and 13.8 kilogrammes of cocaine. [@1] Stabroek News reported CANU's
figures as 77 pounds of marijuana and 30 pounds of cocaine. [@2] Prosecutors
said the drugs had been brought to Parika from Charity by speedboat before the
handover. [@3]

The five were charged at the Leonora Magistrate's Court on 12 June 2018 with
trafficking 13.8 kilogrammes of cocaine and 35 kilogrammes of cannabis at
Parika, and all were remanded to prison until 3 July. [@3] Four of them pleaded
not guilty; the boat captain admitted carrying the drums but said he did not
know what was inside them. [@3] A CANU prosecutor told the court that one of the
accused had previously been convicted of drug trafficking in the United States
and deported to Guyana. [@3] His lawyer said the drugs were not found on his
clients, who were sitting in a car a short distance from the search. [@3]

Magistrate Rochelle Liverpool convicted the mechanic and sentenced him on 31
January 2019 to four years in jail for trafficking 13.8 kilogrammes of cocaine
and 35 kilogrammes of cannabis at Parika. [@4] The magistrate also fined him
$21,048,000 for possession of the cannabis, on top of a $28,996,000 fine for the
cocaine. [@4] The other four heard that there was insufficient evidence against
them and were freed. [@4] The magistrate said the mechanic's account had failed
to show he had no knowledge of the drugs, which were hidden in gasoline bottles.
[@4] CANU said the bottles had been cut at the bottom, rewired and covered with
a brown paste to hide the packages of leaves, seeds and stems. [@4]
