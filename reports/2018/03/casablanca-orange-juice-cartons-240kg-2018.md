On 8 March 2018, customs officers at the port of Casablanca foiled an attempted
export of 240 kilograms of chira concealed inside orange-juice cartons. [@2] The
port customs directorate announced the seizure in a communiqué published on
Tuesday. [@2]

The export consignment comprised 720 cartons holding 12 boxes of orange juice
each, and inconsistencies in the documents attached to the customs declaration
raised suspicion. [@1] Officers checked the cargo and found 240 kilograms of
cannabis resin as pressed slabs wrapped in plastic and aluminium foil hidden
inside cartons of a local juice brand. [@1] The traffickers had made up the
weight difference with sand so that each carton would match the normal weight of
a juice carton. [@1]

The document check on the declaration for the 720 cartons of 12 orange-juice
boxes destined for export revealed inconsistencies that alerted customs
officers. [@3] The follow-up verification, carried out with the anti-drug
brigade, found 240 kilograms of hashish as slabs wrapped in plastic and
aluminium foil inside the juice cartons. [@3]

The United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime describes Morocco as the world's
leading producer and one of its main exporters of cannabis resin, most of it
sold in Europe. [@1] The International Narcotics Control Board said in 2015 that
close to 50,000 hectares of farmland were devoted to the crop, mainly in the
northern Rif region. [@1]
