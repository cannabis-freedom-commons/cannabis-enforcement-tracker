San Diego County sheriff's officials said on 12 July 2001 that authorities
had uprooted more than 46,000 marijuana plants in a remote canyon on Palomar
Mountain. [@1] The find was the largest in the 22-year history of the
county's marijuana eradication efforts. [@1]

Sheriff's deputies had first entered the growing area on 9 July, when several
people fled and officers were unable to pursue them over the rugged mountain
terrain. [@2] By Friday 20 July, officers searching further along the slopes
of Palomar Mountain had found considerably more marijuana growing at the same
remote site, bringing the total number of plants uprooted there to more than
100,000. [@2]

San Diego County Sheriff's Lt. Bill Baxter described the bust as by far the
largest in the 22-year history of the county's eradication efforts and the
largest marijuana farm found in California that year. [@2] No arrests had been
made in connection with the gardens, and the reports name no suspects and give
no charges or later court outcomes. [@2]
