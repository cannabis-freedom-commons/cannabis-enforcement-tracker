Ontario Provincial Police seized about 20,000 high-quality marijuana plants from a farm near Brechin,
Ontario, in what the National Post reported as the province’s largest pot bust. [@1] Officers loaded
the plants into 50 cube vans and initially told reporters the crop would go to an incinerator. [@1]

## Disposal at Orillia

Sergeant Rick Barnum of the OPP drug section said burning was impossible because of the volume, so
police dug a pit at the Orillia city dump and covered the plants with compost and garbage. [@1]
Onlookers who saw the convoy guessed cannabis was being buried; Utro.ru reported that on the first
night about 35 people came with bags, and that crowds grew on following nights before police
patrolled the site and arrested six people for theft and possession. [@1,2]

## Value and context

The Post said the seizure was worth at least $20 million on the street. [@1] The farm raid came
after a Crime Stoppers tip and an OPP helicopter spotting three men harvesting plants at a property
south of Orillia. [@1] The opened sources describe the Brechin farm seizure and the Orillia burial
as one enforcement episode rather than separate cases.
