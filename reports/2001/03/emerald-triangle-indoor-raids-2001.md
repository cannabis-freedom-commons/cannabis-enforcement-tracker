About 120 local, state and federal agents with federal warrants swept across the backwoods
of Mendocino and Humboldt counties at dawn on 6 March 2001, raiding 11 indoor marijuana
growing operations near Laytonville, Garberville and Redway, some of them commercial
buildings made to look like houses. [@2] The raids capped a two-year investigation called
Operation Emerald Triangle, with the FBI and the California National Guard joining a
Humboldt County Drug Task Force briefing before 11 search warrants were served. [@1] By
midday officers had uprooted more than 14,000 plants and seized 206,000 dollars in cash,
arresting three men: Redway brothers Shane and Terry Miller, found with 200,000 dollars,
and Zachary Stone, taken into custody at a separate residence with 6,000 dollars on
suspicion of possession of marijuana for sale. [@2]

Follow-up searches brought the operation to 18 locations in the two counties, with more
than 30,000 plants, hundreds of thousands of dollars, automatic weapons, generators,
vehicles and a boat seized and a fourth arrest made. [@3] Stone, a convicted felon, and
his wife Bria had been arrested on weapons and cultivation charges after about 1,700
plants were found at a Salmon Creek building they owned; the sheriff said the case would
go to the district attorney, with tax-evasion and conspiracy charges possible. [@1,3]
Officials said the sites ran year-round under lights with security systems, and that the
medical-marijuana law had nothing to do with them. [@1,2]
