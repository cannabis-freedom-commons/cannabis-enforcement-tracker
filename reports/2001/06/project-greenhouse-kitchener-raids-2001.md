On Friday 22 June 2001, some 70 officers from local police, the RCMP, the OPP
and Hamilton, Guelph and Peel forces raided 17 homes, ten of them in
Kitchener-Waterloo and the rest in Guelph and the Toronto area, seizing almost
2,000 marijuana plants. [@1,2] Police called the six-month sting Operation
Project Greenhouse and arrested 23 people they described as an organised group
growing in a similar, elaborate style. [@1] Two of those arrested were serving
conditional sentences in British Columbia on similar charges before starting
home operations in Ontario. [@1]

The grows sat mostly in the basements of suburban homes, with some growers
using bedrooms as drying rooms and bypassing their hydro meters to feed
high-wattage bulbs, umbrella-shaped reflectors and floor fans. [@1] Couples
lived with their children in some of the houses while others were used only for
growing. [@1] Officers also seized $102,000 in cash, US$1,500 and $120,000 in
growing equipment and jewellery, including rings, a pearl necklace and Rolex
watches. [@1]

## A family case

Nine members of the Tran family, headed by 56-year-old patriarch Ba Thuan Tran
with his wife, six children and two sons-in-law, faced drug and conspiracy
charges from the investigation and were still before a Kitchener court in
September 2002. [@2] Their lawyer sought to have the charges thrown out over
trial delays, after the case spent 15 months in the courts with a 60-day
preliminary hearing possibly still a year away. [@2] Separately, a 21-year-old
Kitchener man had already received an 18-month conditional sentence for an
indoor growing operation. [@1]
