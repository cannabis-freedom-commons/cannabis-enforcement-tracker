Andrew Kavasilas, Donald Fuggle and Allen Moore were arrested on 14 May 2001 when
35 police officers and two sniffer dogs swooped on three Nimbin cafes in a raid over
cannabis offences. [@1] More than 100 residents reacted angrily, following the police
to the local station and chanting outside. [@1] The MardiGrass organisers' own history
records that police raided all of the village's cannabis cafes in one day in 2001 and
put an end to the cafe experiment begun the year before. [@2]

Less than two weeks before the raid, thousands of people had attended the annual
MardiGrass festival in Nimbin, where police declined to act on the open trade in and
use of cannabis; the festival included a 5,000-strong rally demanding drug law
reform. [@1] Lisa Yeates of the Nimbin Hemp Embassy blamed the raid on pressure from
conservative forces after the festival's success, naming the local newspaper editor
and the state MP's calls for zero tolerance. [@1]

On 4 June, about 200 people gathered outside Lismore courthouse in solidarity with
the three arrested, with speakers denouncing cannabis laws and warning that sniffer
dogs were part of wider moves to increase police powers. [@1]
