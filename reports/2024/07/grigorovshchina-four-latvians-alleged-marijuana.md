Four Latvian citizens were detained in Belarus. [@1] Their parents contacted
Latvia's foreign ministry. [@1] Preliminary accounts said they had come to
Belarus for a festival and that marijuana had allegedly been found in their car.
[@1] LETA confirmed at the Latvian Ministry of Foreign Affairs on 29 July that
four young people from Latvia had been detained, and that official information
was still being clarified. [@2] A social-media video indicated they had
travelled to a festival, that marijuana had allegedly been found in their car,
and that all four were detained. [@2]

On 26 July four Latvian citizens, three young women and one young man, entered
through the Grigorovshchina checkpoint on the way to a music festival. [@3] A
border-control officer saw a packet while inspecting the car. [@3] An
examination found that the substance in the car was marijuana, classed as a
dangerous narcotic. [@3] Investigators opened a criminal case for illegally
moving a dangerous narcotic across the state border. [@3] They were under a
written undertaking not to leave and were staying in a hotel. [@3]

A Vitebsk forensic official told state television that the Latvian nationals
arrested on 26 July had not been found to possess banned substances. [@4] The
Latvians were released and were back in Latvia. [@4] After the arrest they were
held at a detention center and then at a hotel. [@4] A Belarusian investigation
found them innocent and allowed them to return home, but only after they gave
interviews to local television channels. [@5] They were detained and could have
faced up to several years in prison. [@5]
