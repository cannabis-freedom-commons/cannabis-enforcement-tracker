Mpumalanga police arrested Bhekithemba Simelane, 43, a national of Eswatini, for
dealing in dagga on Tuesday, 20 February 2024. [@2] Simelane was a passenger in
a Toyota Quantum minibus taxi stopped on the N2 Piet Retief-Pongola Road. [@2]
Officers from the Mahamba port of entry, a border crossing into Eswatini, were
carrying out routine stop-and-search duties when they pulled the taxi over. [@2]
During the search police found dagga weighing 3.922 kg wrapped around Simelane's
body. [@2] News24 described the dagga as wrapped around his waist with packing
tape and gave the weight as 4 kg in its opening line. [@1] Police said
preliminary investigation showed he was travelling to Vryheid in KwaZulu-Natal.
[@2]

He was arrested and charged with dealing in dagga. [@2] Simelane made his first
appearance in the Piet Retief Magistrate's Court on Wednesday, 21 February 2024.
[@2] He was remanded in custody, with his next appearance set for 6 March 2024
for a formal bail application. [@2] The Post, an IOL title, gave the same
account attributed to Mpumalanga police spokesperson Captain Magonseni Nkosi,
also putting the dagga at 3.9 kg. [@3] News24 attributed the account to
provincial police spokesperson Brigadier Selvy Mohlala, who said Simelane would
remain in custody until his next appearance on 6 March. [@1] Simelane appeared
in court on Wednesday, 21 February, and was remanded in custody until 6 March
2024. [@1]

Mpumalanga police commissioner Lieutenant General Semakaleng Daphney Manamela
welcomed the arrest and said Mpumalanga routes should never be mistaken for
illicit activity. [@2] Dagga is the South African name for cannabis; the South
African Police Service described the substance as dagga in its account of the
stop. [@4]
