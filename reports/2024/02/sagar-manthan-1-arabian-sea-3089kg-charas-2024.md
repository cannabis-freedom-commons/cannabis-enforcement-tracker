The Indian Navy and the Narcotics Control Bureau (NCB) intercepted a suspicious dhow heading for Indian waters after intelligence from naval maritime-surveillance aircraft corroborated by NCB inputs. [@1,2] A mission-deployed warship located, tracked and boarded the vessel at sea about 60 nautical miles off the coast along the International Maritime Boundary Line on the morning of 27 February 2024. [@1,2] The boarding party recovered almost 3,300 kg of contraband: 3,089 kg of charas (hashish), 158 kg of methamphetamine and 25 kg of morphine, in packets stamped "Ras Awad Goods Co, produce of Pakistan". [@1] News reports quoting the NCB put the split at 3,110 kg of charas with 158.3 kg of methamphetamine and 24.6 kg of suspected heroin. [@2,3]

Five foreign nationals aboard were arrested, and the warship towed the dhow to Porbandar, the nearest Indian port, where the crew and contraband were handed to law enforcement agencies. [@2,3] The NCB chief called it the highest-ever offshore seizure and said the consignment, worth an estimated Rs 1,300–2,000 crore on the international market, had sailed from an Iranian port. [@2] The Navy said the smuggling route emanates from the Makran coast toward Indian Ocean countries, and the mission showed the two agencies' joint resolve against sea-borne smuggling. [@1]
