Metropolitan Police officers in tactical gear forced their way into a top-floor
apartment in London at about 04:00 BST, in a dawn raid on a property where
cannabis was being supplied. [@1] The officers secured the occupants and
searched the flat, where they found cannabis, weapons and a substantial amount
of cash. [@1] One man was arrested on suspicion of possession of Class B drugs
with intent to supply. [@1]

The other five or six people in the apartment were treated as potential victims
of cuckooing rather than as suspects. [@1] Cuckooing is the practice of drug
dealers taking over the home of a vulnerable person and using it as a base for
their operations. [@1] Detective Superintendent Kirsty Mead said cuckooing is
when an organised criminal network takes over a vulnerable person and uses their
home to stash and traffic drugs across London or out into other areas. [@1]
Inspector Niall McParland said the officers' task was to get in, secure everyone
and then calm the situation down, removing any handcuffs as soon as possible.
[@1]

Freedom of Information figures from the Metropolitan Police showed cuckooing
cases recorded by the force rising from 42 in 2018-19 to 60 in 2022-23, with
almost half the victims in the most recent year having some form of disability.
[@1] The raid was reported alongside the BBC's investigation into cannabis farms
set up in the homes of unsuspecting London landlords, and Steve Rolles of the
Transform Drug Policy Foundation told the BBC that raiding individual properties
was not shutting down the illegal market. [@1]
