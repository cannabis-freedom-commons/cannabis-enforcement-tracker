Acting Inspector General Deshabandu Tennekoon, backed by Public Security Minister Tiran Alles, launched a nationwide anti-drug operation on 17 December that arrested at least a thousand people a day in its first weeks. By 9 January the reported total had passed 29,000, with about 1,500 held in administrative detention and 1,600 sent to compulsory rehabilitation; most arrests were under the non-bailable drug-trafficking section. Human Rights Watch and other groups said many arrests had no apparent evidence, that soldiers took part in raids, and that detainees faced abuse in military-run centres. [@1,2]

The Sri Lankan government deemed the four-month-long crackdown a success so far, with over 58,000 arrests and drug seizures worth over US$20 million. [@3] Of the 50,000 people arrested and questioned to date, around 5,000 had been jailed. [@3] The United Nations called on the Sri Lankan government to halt and review the operation and shift towards policies underpinned by health and human rights. [@3] Public Security Minister Tiran Alles dismissed the concerns, said law enforcement had followed due process and challenged the United Nations to identify specific instances of abuse. [@3] Opposition politicians claimed the operation was an attempt to gain popular support ahead of pivotal presidential elections slated for late 2024. [@3]
