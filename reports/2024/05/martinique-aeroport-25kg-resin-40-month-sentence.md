Customs officers and police stopped a passenger arriving from Paris at
Martinique's Aimé Césaire airport in Le Lamentin on Friday 31 May 2024. [@1] A
suitcase coming from Paris was intercepted on arrival, in an operation involving
investigators of the Office anti-stupéfiants (Ofast) and customs. [@1] The
baggage held 25 kilos of cannabis resin. [@1] Its owner was immediately arrested
by police and placed in garde à vue, French police custody. [@1] The resin was
packed in large thermosealed clear plastic parcels hidden in the false bottom of
the suitcase, according to RCI. [@2] RCI reported that the passenger had flown
in from Paris Orly. [@2] The suspect and the drug were handed to the Ofast
Caraïbe, the anti-drug office for the French Caribbean. [@2] At his hearings he
reportedly told investigators he had agreed to carry the suitcase because of
money debts. [@2]

After his hearing he was sent to immediate-appearance proceedings before the
Fort-de-France correctional court. [@2] The court sentenced him to 40 months in
prison. [@1] He was kept in detention after the ruling. [@2] He was also ordered
to pay a customs fine of more than 220,000 euros. [@2] The national police said
the suitcase held close to 25 kg of cannabis resin and that its owner was
convicted and given 40 months. [@3]

Police said the case illustrated resin being imported to the Antilles by various
methods, including concealment in checked baggage. [@1] RCI said cannabis resin
is generally exchanged for cocaine heading back to mainland France, one kilo for
one kilo. [@2]
