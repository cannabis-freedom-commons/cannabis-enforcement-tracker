Cayman's Director of Public Prosecutions will look again at the decision not to
prosecute officials involved in decisions that led to a controversial raid on a
medical clinic. [@1] David Perry KC said the Director of Public Prosecutions
would examine the circumstances surrounding the police and customs raid on the
Doctors Express urgent care centre, where legally-held cannabis products were
seized in 2019. [@1] Perry told a hearing in front of Justice Jalil Asif that
the director had said “enough”. [@1] Justice Asif ruled that the judicial-review
application was now academic because the DPP intended to remake the decision on
charging. [@1]

The case began after Doctors Express on Godfrey Nixon Road in George Town was
raided a few days after it had advertised it was prescribing and supplying
medical cannabis under licence. [@1] A judge in 2021 ruled that the raid was
unlawful and that the search warrant that authorised it should be quashed. [@1]
The court added that Dr John Lee, chief medical officer at the time, had acted
improperly when he issued a cease notice to ban cannabinoid products that could
be vaporised. [@1]

Cayman Islands Urgent Care, which traded as Doctors Express, as well as
manufacturers Kaiser Day Cannaceuticals and Kaiser Day Pharmaceuticals, joined
forces to ask the director to charge those behind the raid. [@1] But the DPP
refused and Doctors Express and the other litigants asked for a judicial review
to uncover why there had been no prosecutions. [@1] Doctors Express and the
pharmaceutical companies had sought judicial review against the head of customs,
a justice of the peace, the police commissioner and the former chief medical
officer, who were all believed to have been involved in the discussions that led
to the seizures from the clinic. [@1]
