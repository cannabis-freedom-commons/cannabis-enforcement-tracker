On the morning of 29 October 2024, the Bryan County Sheriff's Office, the Oklahoma Bureau of
Narcotics (OBN) and the federal Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) seized several dump
trucks' worth of marijuana plants from a warehouse just off Highway 70 in Mead.[@1,2]

## One search among many that day

Bryan County Undersheriff Joey Tucker said the Mead search was part of a bigger operation
against illegal grows, with deputies serving multiple search warrants at locations across the
county throughout the day.[@1,2] Each seized plant would be photographed, weighed and
prepared for disposal at a destruction site.[@2]

Tucker said deputies pulled numerous people out of the Mead grow, people he described as
having no reason to be growing marijuana in Oklahoma, but neither source confirms arrests or
charges.[@1,2]

## Oversight and the environment

Tucker contrasted the site with licensed grows, saying the county knew what went into legal
products and what waste came out of them, while unregulated sites left those questions
unanswered.[@1,2] He accused illegal grows of polluting waters and neighbourhoods and warned
that their marijuana reached buyers without the testing licensed growers faced.[@1,2] He said
Oklahoma-grown marijuana was turning up in Texas, New Mexico and across the country.[@1]
