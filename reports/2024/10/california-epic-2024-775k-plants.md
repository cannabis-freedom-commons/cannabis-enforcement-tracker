On 21 October 2024 Attorney General Rob Bonta said EPIC teams had eradicated 774,829 illegally
cultivated cannabis plants, seized 106,141 pounds (about 48.1 tonnes) of processed cannabis and
made 282 arrests during the 2024 season, valuing the seizures at about $353 million on the
underground market. [@1,2] EPIC is a multi-agency task force led by the Department of Justice
that targets unlicensed cultivation tied to environmental damage, lost tax revenue and labour
exploitation. [@1]

## Operations

EPIC teams in Northern, Central and Southern California conducted 665 operations, recovered 201
weapons and removed infrastructure including dams, water lines and containers of toxic pesticides
such as carbofuran, which the release notes is effectively banned in the United States yet seeps
into soil and water near grow sites. [@1] Riverside County led the year with 136,601 plants
eradicated at 79 sites, followed by Mendocino with 133,702 plants at 116 sites and Kern with
89,819 plants at 60 sites. [@1] Monterey, Napa, San Benito, San Luis Obispo, Santa Cruz and
Solano saw reconnaissance only without reported eradication totals. [@1]

## Partners

Bonta credited local, state and federal partners including the U.S. Forest Service, the Bureau
of Land Management, the National Park Service, California State Parks, Fish and Wildlife, the
DEA, the National Guard Counterdrug Task Force and local sheriffs. [@1] San Bernardino County
Sheriff Shannon Dicus said illicit cannabis keeps leaking into the legal market and called
partnerships like EPIC a major step forward. [@1]
