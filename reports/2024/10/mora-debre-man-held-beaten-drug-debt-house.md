Mossos d'Esquadra, Catalonia's regional police, arrested a 37-year-old man after
he tried to leave a house in Móra d'Ebre by its roof and escape over
neighbouring rooftops. [@2] The man was held after neighbours in Carrer de la
Ribera d'Ebre called the 112 emergency number around midday on Tuesday 1 October
because a man inside the house was shouting for help. [@3] Officers of the
Unidad de Seguridad Ciudadana (USC), the uniformed public-security unit, freed
the man, who had been beaten, and detained one of the men who had forced their
way in. [@1]

El Caso reported that one of the assailants was an Equatorial Guinean man with a
criminal record and that he was arrested on suspicion of robbery with violence,
breaking and entering, unlawful detention and threats. [@1] He was taken to the
Móra d'Ebre police station while the case file was completed and was to be
brought before the duty court. [@1] The man who lives in the house is a Spanish
national with a record for offences against public health, and investigators
suspected the assailants came to collect a drug debt or to steal drugs, a
possibility El Caso attributed to the investigation's first hypotheses. [@1] The
criminal investigation division in the Terres de l'Ebre (AIC in the El Caso
account, DIC in Diari de Tarragona's) led the inquiry, and police said they were
still looking for other suspects. [@3]

Diari Més, citing police, said that drug trafficking had not been ruled out and
that further arrests were possible. [@3] Officers kept watch on the house
overnight and into the next morning while they waited for court authorisation to
search it for drugs, a search El Caso reported had not yet happened. [@1] No
cannabis was reported seized or found in any of the accounts, which describe a
suspected drug-related debt dispute and a police rescue rather than a drugs
operation. [@1] Mossos commanders praised the USC officers' response, and El
Caso reported that the arrest opened an investigation into assaults between drug
traffickers, which it said often go unpunished for lack of arrests or of
victims' cooperation. [@1] The same outlet said hashish entering through the
Ebro delta had increased insecurity in the Terres de l'Ebre, without giving
figures. [@1]
