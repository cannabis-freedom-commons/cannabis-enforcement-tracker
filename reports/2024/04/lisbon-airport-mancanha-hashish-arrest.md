Guinea-Bissau prosecutor Eduardo Mancanha was detained at Lisbon Airport on
Sunday 21 April 2024 while preparing to board a flight to Bissau. [@1]
Guinea-Bissau's Public Prosecution Service said he had about 200 grams of
hashish, known in Portuguese as haxixe, in his luggage. [@1] The council
statement said he had spent more than three years in Portugal on service leave
to study when officers caught him in flagrante delicto with 200 grams of a
product presumed to be drugs. [@1] Portuguese police apprehended him at Lisbon
airport. [@2] He was in Portugal on leave to prepare a doctorate in law. [@2]

The Superior Council of the Public Prosecution Service preventively suspended
him, citing the gravity of the conduct under the prosecution magistrates'
statutes. [@1] The council also opened disciplinary proceedings and named deputy
prosecutor-general Quintino Inbequi to conduct the inquiry. [@1]

Guinea-Bissau's prosecution office said it had been informed through appropriate
channels on Sunday the 21st of a magistrate's detention for alleged drug
trafficking. [@1] The prosecution leadership then convened the deputy
prosecutors-general and informed them of the magistrate's imprisonment in
Lisbon. [@1]

The Lisbon Central Criminal Court acquitted Guinea-Bissau prosecutor Eduardo Mancanha of drug trafficking on 27 January 2026, the Observador reported. [@3] The court found it not proven that Mancanha and his friend Alfa Iaia Baldé knew the parcel held the cannabis, the ruling said. [@3] The prosecution service charged three people on 27 February 2025: Mancanha, Alfa Iaia Baldé and Bá Samba Darame, who supplied the tea box. [@3] Bá Samba Darame admitted placing the drugs in the tea sachets and was given a suspended prison term of one year and three months. [@3] Prosecutors asked for Mancanha's acquittal at trial and did not appeal the ruling, which became final on the last working day of February 2026. [@3] The trial ran for about three months, between late 2025 and early 2026, before a panel chaired by judge Ana Paula Rosa. [@3] The seized cannabis weighed 194.178 grams, about 200 grams, packed in two wrappers in a tea box and wrapped in a film printed with a triangle and the words “As crenças secretas dos Illuminati.” [@3] The two packages of cannabis were found in a tea box during the baggage check on 21 April 2024, as Mancanha prepared to fly to Bissau. [@4] Guinea-Bissau's magistracy expelled him in October 2024, six months after the suspension, and he said he was never formally notified. [@3] Mancanha says the disciplinary expulsion is under challenge and has asked to return to Guinea-Bissau's prosecution service. [@3]
