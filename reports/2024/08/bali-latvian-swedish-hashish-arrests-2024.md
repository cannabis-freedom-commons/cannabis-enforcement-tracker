On 21 August 2024 the Bali Province National Narcotics Board (BNNP) presented two detained
foreigners accused of trafficking hashish through different networks. [@1,2] BNNP eradication
chief Senior Commissioner I Made Sinar Subawa said the first case emerged on 22 July through
joint work with Ngurah Rai Customs: a Latvian identified as VS was caught at I Gusti Ngurah
Rai International Airport with 440.41 grams of hashish and 977.83 grams of marijuana concealed
in his bag. [@1,2]

The second case surfaced on 31 July at a villa in Kemenuh Village, Gianyar, after public tips
about drug activity in the area. [@1,2] A Swede identified as SU had received an international
postal parcel from Thailand containing four solid hashish items weighing 201.28 grams in total.
[@1,2]

Both men were held in temporary BNNP detention on charges under Indonesia's Narcotics Law
Number 35 of 2009, which provides long prison terms and heavy fines for trafficking or holding
large quantities. [@1] Subawa said hashish is frequently misused by foreigners in Bali, often
originates in the Middle East, Pakistan, North Africa and Afghanistan, and is a Class I narcotic
with very high THC content. [@1]
