On 12 November 2024 the São Paulo Court of Justice allowed an appeal and
acquitted a 31-year-old man from Limeira who had grown cannabis at home. [@1] At
first instance he had been sentenced to 6 years, 9 months and 20 days of
imprisonment in an initially closed regime. [@1] He obtained habeas corpus and
appealed to the Court of Justice in liberty. [@1] The 12th Criminal Chamber
granted the appeal to acquit him for atypical conduct, subject to further
appeal. [@1]

The case dates to 16 February 2024 in Parque Abílio Pedro, after a complaint led
military police to go to the house to check for drugs. [@1] The man received the
officers, allowed them to enter and said there was a marijuana plant at the back
of the yard. [@1] Military police found two cannabis plants in his residence,
which he said were grown for his own consumption. [@1] During the inspection the
officers found the plant and a pot with a small seedling. [@1] Behind the
washing machine they found drug-packaging materials: 136 empty vials, 2 packs of
packaging and a precision scale. [@1] He admitted the plants but denied any
connection to those materials. [@1]

The court found no illegality in the officers' entry into the property. [@1] The
defence had sought reclassification from cultivation of marijuana as raw
material for drug preparation to personal possession, the subject of recent
decriminalisation by the Supreme Court. [@1] The reporting judge reasoned that
the accused admitted the cultivation was for his own consumption and recreation
and that there had been no prior investigation. [@1] The decision held that the
two plants were not significant enough to indicate cultivation destined for
third parties and fell within the six-female-plant limit set by the Supreme
Court. [@1]
