Patrick Lagrou, considered Belgium's main cannabis cultivator, was released
after fifteen years of legal proceedings. [@1] The Brussels Court of Appeal
ruled that his sentence was annulled due to a manifest exceedance of the
statutory time limit. [@1] On Thursday 7 November 2024, the Brussels Court of
Appeal finally ruled in favour of Patrick Lagrou, who is now 65 years old. [@1]

In 2009, police discovered 40,000 cannabis plants on Lagrou's farm in
Zandvoorde, West Flanders. [@1] The prosecutor's office had also seized
approximately 2.4 million euros. [@1] The farmer had been sentenced to five
years in prison but had appealed every time. [@1] The European Court of Human
Rights referred the case to the Court of Cassation, and a new trial took place
in 2022, 13 years after the case began and five trials later. [@1] By that date,
the cannabis cultivator had already served the entirety of his prison sentence.
[@1]

Last Thursday the Brussels Court of Appeal decided that the sentence had lapsed
due to a manifest exceedance of the statutory time limit, meaning the cannabis
farmer goes free. [@2] His lawyer Samuel Debruyne said more than 15 years of
proceedings had taken place and the courts had failed to conclude the case
within an acceptable timeframe. [@2] The Belgian state should now take
responsibility and compensate his client for the harm he suffered, Debruyne
said, noting vehicles, money and confiscations were made during the proceedings.
[@2] Lagrou's wife had lived in Thailand for all those years, and he looks
forward to moving there as soon as possible. [@2]
