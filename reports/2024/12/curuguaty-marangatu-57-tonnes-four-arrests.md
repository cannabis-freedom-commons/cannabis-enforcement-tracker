Paraguay's SENAD anti-drug agency and the CODI internal-defence command carried
out Operativo Marangatu against an alleged drug-trafficking network. [@1,3]
Officers detained four men, named as Carlos Daniel Cocco Rodriguez, Ruben Carlos
Morel Ortiz, Carlos Alejandro Candia Alcaraz and Ariel Ramon Ibanez Eydans. [@1]
Ibanez was wounded in the shootout and was taken to the Regional Hospital of San
Pedro de Ycuamandiyu, where his condition was reported as stable and out of
danger. [@1] The operation involved an exchange of gunfire between the security
forces and the armed group guarding the load. [@2,3]

The forces seized 57 tonnes of marijuana, described as pressed marijuana ready
for shipment. [@1,3] The authorities valued the seized drug at more than 22
million US dollars, plus seized weapons and vehicles. [@1,3] They also seized
eight high-end pickup trucks, including four Toyota Hilux, two Chevrolet S10 and
a Renault with Brazilian plates, plus a red Kenton motorcycle. [@2] They also
seized a 12-gauge shotgun, a 9mm Glock pistol with a RONI kit and full magazine,
a 5.56mm rifle and an undetonated calibre-50 cartridge. [@2]

The load was said to be destined for the Brazilian criminal market. [@1,3] The
Joint Task Force, made up of National Police, Armed Forces and SENAD personnel,
deployed by land and air in Curuguaty district, about 250 kilometres from
Asuncion, after intelligence about a large export-bound load. [@1] The Joint
Task Force spokesman said the marijuana could belong to Felipe Acosta Riveros,
alias Macho, whom the Paraguayan authorities link to drug trafficking. [@1,3]

The seizure totalled 4,172 packages holding about 57 tonnes of pressed marijuana. [@3] President Santiago Pena called it the largest pressed-marijuana load seized in Paraguay's history. [@3]
