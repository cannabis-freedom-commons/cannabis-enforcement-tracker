Medical cannabis company InterCure, chaired by Ehud Barak and led by chief
executive Alexander Rabinovich, announced it had raised NIS 66 million to
rehabilitate its facility at Kibbutz Nir Oz. [@2] The announcement was made on
20 December 2024. [@3] The company said the funding could rise to NIS 107
million to expand the facility with the Tkumah administration after the war.
[@3] It also obtained a binding commitment for a NIS 30 million loan from a
leading Israeli bank, for up to 24 months. [@3] The capital came from Rabinovich
and two existing shareholders, Yaron Yakobi and Tzahi Hagag, who would each hold
more than 5% of the company. [@3] The private placement covered 7,349,896 shares
at NIS 4.83 each. [@3] Warrants could buy a further 7,349,896 shares at NIS 5.70
each. [@3] The company said full exercise of the warrants could raise the
proceeds to about NIS 77 million. [@3] The placement was conditional on
shareholder approval and other closing conditions. [@3] The investment put
InterCure's market value at NIS 235 million. [@2]

The facility at Kibbutz Nir Oz had been damaged in the October 7 attacks and was
to be rehabilitated. [@1] InterCure said the NIS 200 million production facility
at Nir Oz was damaged on 7 October. [@2] The facility produced half of the
cannabis strains the company sells in Israel, its main market. [@2] Under
Israeli law the site is entitled to full compensation for direct and indirect
war damage, including lost profits. [@2] The company said it had received
advance payments of tens of millions of shekels from the Israeli authorities.
[@2]

Revenue in the first half of 2024 was NIS 125 million, 40% lower than a year
earlier. [@2] It posted a loss of NIS 62 million for 2023 because of the damage.
[@2]
