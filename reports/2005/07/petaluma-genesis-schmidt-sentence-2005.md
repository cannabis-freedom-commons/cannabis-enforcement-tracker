Robert "Duke" Schmidt, who ran the Genesis 1:29 medical marijuana dispensary from
his home in Petaluma, was sentenced to 41 months in federal prison on 7 July
2005. [@1] United States District Judge Charles Breyer imposed the sentence in
his San Francisco courtroom; Schmidt, who had already served seven months, was
to report to a minimum-security prison on 1 September. [@1,2]

Schmidt had founded the non-profit dispensary in 1999, supplying members from
his home grow and other sites while seeking federal approval for cannabis
research, including telling state officials in advance what he would plant.
[@1] DEA agents raided his house in the early hours of 12 September 2002 and
seized 2,600 plants from a garden he leased in Sebastopol; Schmidt was arrested
and charged with assaulting an officer. [@1,3]

His case had been paused pending the Supreme Court's ruling in Gonzales v.
Raich, which held that state-legal patients and caregivers could still be
prosecuted under federal law. [@2] At sentencing the judge applied the Booker
decision to depart below the guidelines, cutting the term by ten months partly
because Schmidt was ineligible for a prison drug-treatment program. [@2]
Schmidt had pleaded guilty to charges of cultivation and possession of 265
kilograms of marijuana more than two years earlier. [@2]
