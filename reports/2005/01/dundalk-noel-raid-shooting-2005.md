At 4:30 a.m. on 21 January 2005, Baltimore County police executed a search warrant at the
Dundalk home of Cheryl and Charles Noel after finding evidence of personal drug use in the
family's trash cans. [@3] The trash had yielded marijuana seeds and cocaine residue, and the
raid was ordered as a no-knock entry because two residents owned registered handguns and
Charles Noel carried a 30-year-old second-degree murder conviction. [@2,3]

Officers broke down the front door with a battering ram, set off a flash-bang grenade and
stormed upstairs, where 44-year-old Cheryl Noel stood in her nightgown holding a legally owned
handgun she had kept near the bed since her daughter's murder years earlier. [@1,2,3] Officer
Carlos Artson, firing from behind a ballistics shield, shot her twice; as she lay bleeding on the
bedroom floor he fired a third shot from close range, and the coroner found that shot immediately
fatal. [@1,3] Police found only a misdemeanor amount of marijuana in the house, and later charged
three residents with drug possession. [@1,3]

Noel, described by friends as a devout woman who led Bible study groups, had no criminal
record. [@1] Her family filed a civil rights lawsuit in 2006, and weeks later the department gave
Artson its Silver Star for his actions during the raid. [@1] In March 2009 a federal jury returned
a verdict for the police, and a federal appeals court upheld it in 2011. [@1,2]
