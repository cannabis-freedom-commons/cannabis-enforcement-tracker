Federal, state and county officers seized about 29,000 marijuana plants
across Hawaii in a 10-day Operation Green Harvest sweep from 31 May to 9
June 2005. [@1,2] Maui County yielded 16,869 plants, the Big Island 10,418,
Oahu 1,503 and Kauai 253. [@1] About 10,000 plants stood on a single private
estate on Molokai, within a couple of miles of the island's airport. [@1,2]
Officers also recovered 36 pounds of processed marijuana and arrested six
people for drug promotion, most of them on the Big Island. [@1]

More than 125 officers took part, drawn from the DEA, the National Park
Service, the state land department, the Hawaii National Guard's counter-drug
unit and all four county police departments, with seven to 10 helicopters
overhead. [@1,2] Some plots lay in parks and forests, but most were on
private land. [@1]

The sweep came the week the US Supreme Court upheld federal prosecution of
medical-marijuana patients; the DEA's Tony Williams said the mission was
unchanged and aimed at cultivators, not the sick. [@1] Officials valued the
haul at about $29 million at $1,000 a plant and said cultivation had fallen
far below the 1.9 million plants taken in 1987. [@2]
