DEA and IRS agents raided three San Francisco medical marijuana dispensaries
on 22 June 2005, part of a two-year investigation dubbed Operation Urban
Harvest. [@1,2] The dispensaries were the Alternative Relief Co-Op and the
Herbal Relief Center on Ocean Avenue and the Sunset Medicinal Resource Center
on Judah Street; 23 more residences, businesses and growing sites across the
city were searched the same day. [@1,2]

Agents hauled away more than 9,000 marijuana plants in the day's searches;
over the whole investigation they seized 18,000 plants with an estimated
wholesale value of $17 million, which officials called the largest federal
case ever involving dispensaries. [@2] Fifteen people were arrested on the
day, and 21 people were ultimately charged with conspiring to grow and traffic
marijuana, distribute Ecstasy and launder money. [@1,2]

## A front, prosecutors said

In unsealed court documents, prosecutors accused 33-year-old San Francisco
man Vince Ming Wan of leading a multimillion-dollar trafficking operation
that used the three clubs as a front, funnelling proceeds through 40 bank
accounts at 12 institutions, with some cannabis grown in Canada and money
trails running to Asia. [@2] An arrest warrant issued for Wan on charges of
conspiring to distribute more than 1,000 plants remained unexecuted the next
day, with seven other suspects also still at large. [@2] Mayor Gavin Newsom's
office said the raids showed the need to protect legitimate medicinal use,
while patient advocates warned dispensary users could be driven to the black
market. [@3]
