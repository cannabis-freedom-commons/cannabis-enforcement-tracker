On 10 November 2005 Attorney General Bill Lockyer said California's Campaign Against Marijuana
Planting (CAMP) had eradicated 1,134,692 illegally cultivated cannabis plants during the 2005
season, a record exceeding the previous year by 513,377 plants. [@1] CAMP, a multi-agency task
force led by the Department of Justice with the National Guard, the DEA, federal land agencies
and local departments, valued the removed crop at more than $4.5 billion. [@1,2]

## Season totals

Lockyer said teams conducted 237 raids in 31 counties during the traditional outdoor season from
late July through early October. [@1] Officers made 42 arrests and seized 76 weapons. [@1] About
73 percent of the plants grew on public land including state and national parks and forests, and
the rest on private land including ranches, vineyards and corporate property. [@1] Shasta County
had the largest county total with 214,319 plants, followed by Lake County with 133,441 and Tulare
County with 133,038. [@1] One officer-involved shooting occurred during the season, leaving one
suspect dead in Santa Clara County. [@1] In January 2006 the federal drug-control office gave
CAMP a Distinguished Service Award for its 2005 performance, noting that more than 110 agencies
had taken part. [@2]
