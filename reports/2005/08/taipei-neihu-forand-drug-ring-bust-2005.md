The Coast Guard Administration's Keelung mobile-investigation team broke up an
international drug ring based in Neihu and arrested eleven people, including
its alleged kingpin, a Canadian who taught English in Taiwan, after three
months of surveillance.[@1] Officers spotted a Taiwanese-American man, Su
Sheng-hsi, leaving a building after he had allegedly bought drugs, arrested
him, and then sent him back upstairs as bait to catch Mathieu Forand James,
the alleged ringleader.[@1] Seven other foreign nationals and three Taiwanese
Americans were also arrested in connection with the ring, whose members
included English teachers from Canada, Australia and Hong Kong.[@1]

The ring smuggled drugs into the country inside textbooks and sold them to
foreign students and returning Taiwanese students from overseas in pubs and
night spots, as well as over the Internet.[@1] On site the coast guard found
600 grams of cocaine, some marijuana and hundreds of ecstasy pills.[@1]
Forand, 28, of Port Moody, British Columbia, was found with cocaine, ecstasy
and marijuana when officers raided a party at his Taipei home, and
prosecutors were seeking the death penalty or 25 years in prison while his
lawyer argued for 10 to 15 years.[@2]

James and Su were held in custody for violating drug regulations while the
other arrested people were released to serve as witnesses, and the Coast
Guard said it was investigating whether more people were involved.[@1]
