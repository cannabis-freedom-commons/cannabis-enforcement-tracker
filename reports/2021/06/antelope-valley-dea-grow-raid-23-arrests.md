The U.S. Drug Enforcement Administration joined California law enforcement to
shut down cartel-tied illegal marijuana grows in Southern California on Tuesday
8 June 2021. [@1] Federal involvement followed a request from Congressmen Jay
Obernolte and Mike Garcia to Attorney General Merrick Garland asking Washington
to prosecute illegal growers to the full extent of the law. [@1]

The Los Angeles County Sheriff's Department and the DEA carried out the
operation in unincorporated Lancaster, developed after public calls for service
and complaints from residents, the department said. [@3] Twenty-three people
were arrested in the Tuesday crackdown in the Antelope Valley, about 70 miles
(112.65 km) north of Los Angeles. [@2] Five firearms were seized and two water
trucks recovered during the operation, the sheriff's department said. [@3] More
than 400 sheriff's personnel, the National Guard, the California Department of
Fish and Wildlife and deputies from Kern, Riverside, San Bernardino and Ventura
counties took part, the department said. [@3] Obernolte's office said over 500
local, state and federal personnel were involved in the bust in the Antelope
Valley with over 200 warrants served. [@1] Officials planned to bulldoze 500
illegal grows in the area over the following days. [@2] The largest grow found
that day had more than 70 greenhouses over 10 acres (4.05 hectares) and an
estimated marijuana crop worth $50 million, the sheriff said. [@2]

The department said a 75-greenhouse grow covering 10 acres was bulldozed during
the operation. [@3] Sheriff Alex Villanueva said the operation partly answered
residents' complaints that water was being stolen, and Lancaster Mayor R. Rex
Parris put the value of the infrastructure and marijuana found at $380 million.
[@2] Villanueva said he did not yet have a count of the marijuana seized, which
officers were measuring by the tons, and that charges for those arrested would
likely mix state misdemeanours and felonies, with possible federal cases. [@2]

Obernolte described cartel grows as taking water, damaging the environment and
intimidating communities, and said the San Bernardino County Sheriff's
Department had 850 potential grow sites under investigation. [@1] Seven
mixed-breed dogs, including four puppies, were taken from the grows and handed
to a non-profit rescue. [@3]
