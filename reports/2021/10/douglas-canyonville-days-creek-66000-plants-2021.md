Members of the Douglas Interagency Narcotics Team executed a search warrant on
5 October 2021 at a large-scale illicit marijuana growing operation in the
9000 block of Days Creek Cutoff Road, a few miles east of Canyonville. [@1,2]
The activity spread over two parcels of historic farmland along the South
Umpqua River with two residences, outbuildings, barns and recreational
vehicles. [@1,2] The site held more than 100 hoop-house greenhouses, most of
them full of illegal marijuana. [@1,2]

Officers interviewed numerous people at the scene, most of them immigrant
workers, and released them. [@1,2] They arrested 31-year-old Carlos Flores
Santacruz, who lived at the site, and lodged him in the Douglas County Jail
on charges of unlawful possession and unlawful manufacture of marijuana; he
was released the next day. [@1,2] In total, DINT seized more than 66,000 live
marijuana plants and over 11,000 pounds (about 4,990 kg) of processed
marijuana. [@1,2]

DINT said the operation was designed to look like a legal hemp business,
though the sites held no state hemp permits or medical-marijuana permits.
[@1,2] The team said such southern Oregon grows are largely run by foreign
drug cartels and cause environmental damage through fertilizers, garbage and
illegal water use during drought. [@1,2] Investigators described a seizure of
more than 56,000 plants at a nearby Tiller Trail Highway site two weeks
earlier as a related operation run by the same people, with further arrests
anticipated. [@1,2]
