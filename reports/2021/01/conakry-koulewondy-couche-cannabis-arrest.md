Guinea's special anti-drug services presented a young man accused of drug
trafficking to the press on 19 January 2021. [@1] The man was 33-year-old
Ibrahima Bah, arrested in Conakry with 80 grams of what police described as a
new type of cannabis in Guinea. [@1] Police said Bah was stopped on 13 January
2021 at about 2 p.m. in the Koulewondy quarter, holding eight sachets totalling
80 grams. [@1] Questioned by police, he admitted owning the parcels and named
American resident Jackson Williams as his main supplier. [@3] When questioned by
the press, Bah said someone else put the drug on his motorbike and insisted he
is only a currency changer who was framed. [@1]

The 80 grams were hidden in plastic under the seat of his TVS motorbike. [@1]
Police called the substance Couche, a kind of cannabis they said was previously
unknown in Guinea and imported from the United States and Europe. [@2] Police
said the drug is prepared in the United States, more specifically in California.
[@1] Police said it reaches Guinea by sea, most often hidden in used cars,
second-hand clothing and various objects. [@1] Police priced 10 grams of Couche
at 1 million Guinean francs, far more than the traditional cannabis known in
Guinea. [@1]

The special-services department said it opened an investigation to find possible
accomplices in the international traffic. [@1] The police spokesperson urged the
national police, gendarmerie, army and customs to pool efforts to confront the
unfamiliar drug and protect Guinean youth. [@1] A third account confirms Bah,
described as a man in his thirties, was presented to the press alongside
anti-drug communication officer Mohamed Mazo Mansaré. [@3]
