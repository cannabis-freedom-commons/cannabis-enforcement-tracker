Fresno County sheriff's deputies and California National Guard members spent
Friday 21 May 2021 tearing down a cannabis grow on a large former farm property
southwest of Mendota. [@1] The sheriff's office had estimated about 50,000
plants; three days later it put the total at 21,192 and said it had hauled away
25 tons of illegal marijuana. [@2] The plants were growing in 18 greenhouses in
what KFSN described as a former farm camp, where detectives believed 10 to 15
people had been living behind a roving patrol. [@1] The sheriff's office also
reported 20 greenhouses on the site and living quarters that could have housed
up to 15 people. [@2]

Detectives found one handgun and one shotgun in the living quarters. [@2] Nobody
was on the property when deputies arrived with a search warrant at about 6 a.m.,
and no arrests had been made. [@5] A narcotics sergeant said those responsible
would face only misdemeanour charges under Proposition 64, the state law that
legalised adult cannabis use, because cultivating more than six plants without a
licence is punished as a misdemeanour. [@5] The sheriff's office valued the haul
at $100 million on the day of the raid. [@5] A local news account later reported
an estimated street value of more than $31 million for the 25 tons. [@4]

The grow lay on land near S. San Diego and W. Lincoln Avenues, southwest of
Mendota, according to a sheriff's office statement published by Sierra Sun
Times. [@3] The sheriff's office said the operation was funded by a drug cartel
and that detectives considered it the most sophisticated grow they had found.
[@3] Detectives found the compound by air: deputies flying between two known
grows smelled it from about 1,100 feet and two miles away. [@1] A new owner had
taken over the property in October, and the sheriff's office said it would
contact that person but that landowners face no penalty unless they can be shown
to have been involved in cultivation. [@1]
