A three-judge Federal Court panel in Putrajaya on 1 November 2021 set aside
former cook Rozman Ibrahim's conviction and death sentence for trafficking
4,754 g of cannabis eight years earlier. [@1,2] The panel, led by Chief Judge
of Malaya Azahar Mohamed with judges Vernon Ong Lam Kiat and Zabariah Mohd
Yusof, instead convicted the 37-year-old of drug possession and sentenced him
to 18 years in prison and 10 strokes of the cane. [@1,2]

The court ordered the jail term to run from the date of his arrest on 12
November 2013. [@1,2] Rozman had been found guilty of distributing the drugs
in the Taman Medan Pejasa car park in Petaling at 7.30pm that day, under
Section 39B(1)(a) of the Dangerous Drugs Act 1952. [@1,2] The Shah Alam High
Court sentenced him to death by hanging on 31 October 2017, and the Court of
Appeal upheld that on 12 September 2019. [@1,2]

Azahar said the conviction and death sentence were unsafe because the trial
judge made confusing findings on possession and distribution and gave the
accused no direction on the burden on him, calling it a miscarriage of
justice. [@1,2] His lawyer Suzana Norlihan Alias argued he had not received a
fair trial, while Deputy Public Prosecutor Ng Siew Wee asked the court to
uphold the conviction and sentence. [@1,2]
