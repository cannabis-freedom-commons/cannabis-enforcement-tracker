The Court of Appeal in Putrajaya set aside the conviction and death sentence of Mohammad Habibul Hassan Khan, a 26-year-old Bangladeshi engineering student at a private university, for trafficking 3,875.66 g of cannabis. [@1,2] A three-member panel led by Justice Hanipah Farikullah, with Justices Che Mohd Ruzima Ghazali and Mariana Yahya, held there was merit in his appeal because the prosecution had failed to prove he knew about the drugs. [@1,2]

Habibul had said the bag containing the drugs, found in his hostel room, belonged to another student named Jawad, who lived outside the university and killed himself a day after Habibul was caught with the bag. [@1,2] The trial judge had dismissed that account as a bare denial and an afterthought, but the appeal judges held he committed an error of fact and law, including over whether Habibul had confessed to his hostel warden Shazereen Kamaruddin. [@1,2]

## The 2017 hostel case

Habibul was found guilty of trafficking the cannabis in his hostel room at the university in Semenyih at about 4 pm on 10 December 2017, and the Shah Alam High Court sentenced him to death on 10 April 2019. [@1,2] He was charged under section 39B(1)(a) of the Dangerous Drugs Act 1952, which then carried a mandatory death penalty. [@1] His counsel Hisyam Teh Poh Teik argued the warden's evidence about the confession was not corroborated by other prosecution witnesses, while the deputy public prosecutor maintained Habibul had custody, control and knowledge of drugs given to him by Jawad. [@1]
