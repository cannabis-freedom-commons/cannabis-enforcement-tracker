On 17 February 2021, Malaysia’s Federal Court set aside Muhammad Lukman Mohamad’s two remaining cannabis trafficking convictions and replaced them with possession convictions. [@5,6,9,10] The court imposed five years on each count, to run concurrently from his arrest on 7 December 2015. [@5,6,9,10] His lawyer argued that the prosecution had failed to establish the weight and volume alleged in the trafficking charges. [@9,10]

## Arrest and death sentence

Police arrested Lukman at his home in Bandar Mahkota, Banting, in December 2015, and also arrested his wife, who was pregnant. [@2,10] Bernama said the original charges described 3,010 millilitres of cannabis extract, 1,422 grams described as delta-9 THC, and 279.81 grams of cannabis. [@10] The Shah Alam High Court sentenced him to death on 30 August 2018. [@1,7,8,10]

Lukman said he made oil to help sick patients and gave it free to those who could not pay. [@1,2] Patients testified for him, and the prosecution argued that the health ministry did not recognise the product as medicine. [@1,2] A petition sought his release, and then prime minister Mahathir Mohamad called for the case to be reviewed. [@2,7,10]

## Appeals

The Court of Appeal upheld the death penalty on two counts and reduced a third to possession, imposing seven years in prison and ten strokes of the cane on that count. [@3,4,10] Coverage of the appeal said the panel found that there was no official evidence of medicinal benefit and that making the oil was covered by the drug law. [@3,4] After the Federal Court ruling, Lukman’s lawyer said he had already served the custodial portion of the earlier seven-year term with remission and expected his release after the whipping. [@10]
