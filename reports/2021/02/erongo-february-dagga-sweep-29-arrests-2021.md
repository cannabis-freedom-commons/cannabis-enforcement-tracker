Two foreign nationals were at the centre of a possible drug syndicate in the
Erongo region. [@1] A 22-year-old man claiming to be a Burundian national was
arrested at a warehouse in an industrial area of Swakopmund on suspicion of
selling powdered cocaine, crack cocaine, dagga and mandrax tablets. [@1] On the
same day, Erongo police apprehended a 27-year-old man at Mondesa, who said he
was from the Democratic Republic of Congo, for possession of 220 g of dagga
valued at N$11 000. [@1] Deputy commissioner Erastus Iikuyu, the Erongo crime
investigations coordinator, confirmed both arrests. [@1] Neither man had
identification documents, and verification of their refugee status was ongoing.
[@1]

The two men were linked with a 30-year-old Namibian man arrested at a house at
Tamariskia for dealing in five medium and seven large parcels of dagga with a
street value of N$425 000. [@1] They were also linked with a 31-year-old woman
arrested on 11 February at Narraville Service Station at Walvis Bay for
allegedly selling dagga with a street value of N$16 250. [@1] Since the
beginning of February, Erongo police had apprehended 29 people for dealing in or
possessing dagga, mandrax and cocaine with a combined street value of over N$1
million. [@1]

Police raided a dagga plantation at Omaruru, finding 22 plants weighing 14,945 g
with a street value of N$149,450. [@1] Two male suspects aged 45 and 65 were
arrested in connection with the Omaruru plantation. [@1] Most of the substance
was found at Swakopmund and Walvis Bay, with the plant also found at
Otjimbingwe, Henties Bay, Omaruru and Usakos. [@1] The use and cultivation of
dagga, the local term for cannabis, is prohibited in Namibia under Act 41 of
1971. [@1]
