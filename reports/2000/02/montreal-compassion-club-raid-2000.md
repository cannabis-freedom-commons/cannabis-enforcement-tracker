Montreal police raided the city's so-called Compassion Club, a storefront selling
marijuana for medical use, on Thursday 10 February 2000. [@1,2] Officers arrested two
employees and seized the names of 27 doctors who had recommended cannabis to their
ailing patients. [@1] Police said they had no choice because compassion clubs remained
illegal even though the federal government could permit medical cultivation and use;
commander Andre Lapointe said police felt pain for the suffering but had to apply the
law until legislators changed it. [@1]

Marc St-Maurice and Alexandre Neron appeared in court the next day charged with
possessing 66 grams of marijuana for the purpose of trafficking, enough for 150 to 300
cigarettes. [@1] Police confiscated evidence from physicians who had suggested marijuana
to 33 patients and forwarded it to Crown prosecutors, who were to decide whether doctors
could face charges of counselling a criminal offence. [@1] The patients included people
with AIDS and breast cancer as well as people with migraines and back pain, and only one
club member held a federal medical exemption. [@1] Police said they had built their case
under surveillance ever since the club opened, helped by its location across the street
from a police station. [@1]

Ten agents with a dog burst into the club at 950 rue Rachel Est, handcuffed the
volunteers and jailed them on possession and trafficking charges. [@2] They seized
medicine, demonstration vaporizers, water pipes, rolling papers, matches and patients'
files, and patients later received letters saying the club was illegal and that visiting
it risked prison. [@2] The club, which had opened in October 1999, closed for one month,
forcing patients onto the street supply. [@2]
