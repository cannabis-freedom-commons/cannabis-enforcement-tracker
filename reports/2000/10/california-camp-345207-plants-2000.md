On 23 October 2000 Attorney General Bill Lockyer announced in Madera that California's Campaign
Against Marijuana Planting had seized 345,207 marijuana plants during the 2000 season, 43 percent
more than the previous record of 241,164 set in 1999. [@1,2] His office valued the removed crop
at more than $1.3 billion. [@1,2]

## Season totals

Officers from more than 70 local, state and federal agencies, supervised by the Department of
Justice Bureau of Narcotic Enforcement, conducted 263 raids in 25 counties from late July through
mid-October. [@1] They made 16 arrests and seized 24 weapons. [@1] More than half the seizures
were in Central Valley and Sierra foothill counties from Calaveras to Kern, with over 174,000
plants taken in Calaveras, Kern, Fresno, Madera, Merced, San Joaquin, Tulare and Tuolumne
counties. [@1]

The largest single raid in the program's 18-year history took more than 59,000 plants worth over
$230 million in Kern County, in the Sequoia National Forest. [@1,2] No arrests were made in that
Kern County find. [@2] Lockyer said several raids uncovered operations of 8,000 to 15,000 plants
run as corporate-style enterprises directed by Mexican drug organizations, unlike the traditional
small gardens of a few hundred plants. [@1]

## Public-land danger

About 60 percent of the season's seizures were on state or national forest land or other public
ground, which Lockyer called dangerous for hikers, campers and hunters who could stumble on armed
garden tenders ordered to defend the crop. [@1] Investigators also found Mexican drug organizations
making methamphetamine and using the profits to finance the more lucrative marijuana
operations. [@1] The seized marijuana was destroyed on site. [@2]
