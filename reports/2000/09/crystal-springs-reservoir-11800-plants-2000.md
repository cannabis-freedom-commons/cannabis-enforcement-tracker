A Campaign Against Marijuana Planting (CAMP) helicopter team dropped officers on a 150-foot line into a steep hillside garden about two miles west of the Pulgas Water Temple on Monday 18 September 2000, finding 11,800 female cannabis plants growing under madrone, manzanita and cedar cover. [@1] The growers had fled into the woods, leaving breakfast tortillas cooking on a camp stove beside a two-storey tree house stocked with food, porn magazines and hundreds of empty beer cans. [@1]

A dozen agents from the San Mateo County Narcotics Task Force joined CAMP in the four-day operation, which ended on Thursday 21 September and was announced as the biggest cannabis seizure in county history. [@2] CAMP investigators put the crop's street value at about $45 million, saying the growers had likely spent under $20,000 on irrigation pipe, seed, fertiliser and labour. [@1]

A water district ranger had tipped off police in August after following black plastic irrigation pipe from a spring down to the edge of the field, and veteran CAMP officers estimated the garden had been producing for at least two or three years. [@1] The land belongs to San Francisco's watershed and is closed to the public, which spared the growers the hikers and hunters who expose grows in national forests. [@1]
