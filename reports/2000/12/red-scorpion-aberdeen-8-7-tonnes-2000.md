On 21 June 2000 officers aboard the Customs cutter Searcher spotted the pleasure yacht
Red Scorpion sailing just outside British territorial waters in the Fair Isle Gap and grew
suspicious of its story of a voyage from Bremerhaven in Germany, though it was
Maltese-registered and flying a German flag. [@1] With Malta's permission to board in
international waters, officers boarded on 22 June about 120 miles north-east of Aberdeen
despite failing light and severe weather, and found what proved to be 8,690.02 kg of
cannabis resin packed in 285 hessian bales. [@1]

The three Dutch crew were arrested aboard, brought into Aberdeen harbour, and charged by
Customs investigators with being knowingly concerned in carrying a controlled drug. [@1]
Albert Hulst, Arjeh Van Dijk and Christiaan Ekkebus, all resident in the Netherlands,
pleaded guilty at the High Court in Edinburgh on 11 December 2000. [@1]

Lord Hardie sentenced each man to 14 years on 12 December 2000, commending the Customs
officers whose vigilance removed cannabis with a street value of £43 million from the
market. [@1] Customs said the 8.7-tonne haul was a Scottish record and more than a fifth
of Britain's cannabis seizures that year, crediting the Scottish Drugs Enforcement Agency
and Grampian police as well as the cutter crew. [@1]
