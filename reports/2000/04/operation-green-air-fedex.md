Federal agents said they broke up a Jamaican-led network on 13 April 2000 that had bribed FedEx
drivers and warehouse staff to distribute Mexican marijuana across the United States, arresting 101
people including 22 FedEx employees. [@1] The Drug Enforcement Administration said Memphis-based
FedEx cooperated fully after an employee flagged a suspicious package that agents found contained
marijuana. [@1]

## Scale of the network

Acting administrator Donnie R. Marshall said smugglers had never before routed so much cannabis
through a single private courier, shipping more than 4,000 cartons sprinkled with detergent to
confuse drug dogs. [@1] CBS reported that investigators seized 121 tons of marijuana over the
18-month Operation Green Air, along with 18 firearms and more than $4 million in cash and assets.
[@1] The Washington Post gave the same arrest and seizure totals. [@2]

## Routes and suppliers

Marshall said the Arellano Felix organization in Tijuana supplied cannabis to Jamaican traffickers in
Southern California, who loaded FedEx aircraft through corrupt workers in Los Angeles, New York,
Atlanta and other hubs. [@1] U.S. Attorney Loretta Lynch said drivers were paid bribes of up to
$2,000 a week and that forged labels let the ring avoid more than $500,000 in shipping fees. [@1]
Arrests spanned California, New York, New Jersey, Connecticut, Massachusetts, Georgia and Florida.
