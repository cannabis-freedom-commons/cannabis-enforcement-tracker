On 29 March 2000 the U.S. Drug Enforcement Administration announced the results of
Operation Conquistador, a 17-day sweep that netted 2,331 arrests and 362.5 metric tons of
marijuana across the Caribbean and Latin America. [@1] Michael Vigil of the DEA said the
cooperation among the participating countries mattered as much as the drugs seized and the
networks disrupted. [@1]

## Command and scope

The operation was launched simultaneously in 26 countries on 10 March 2000 and concluded
on 26 March, commanded from the DEA Caribbean Field Division in San Juan with forward
posts in Trinidad and Tobago and the Dominican Republic. [@2] Besides Panama, Colombia,
Venezuela, Bolivia, Ecuador and Suriname, actions ran in 20 Caribbean states and
territories, coordinated through the UNICORN information-sharing network with U.S. Coast
Guard air and sea assets. [@2] The Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco and Firearms traced seized
weapons. [@2]

## A cannabis-focused result

Cannabis made up the bulk of the drugs taken by weight: 362.5 metric tons of marijuana,
alongside cocaine and heroin seizures reported in the same totals. [@1] Agents also
destroyed coca leaf and processing chemicals during the sweep. [@1]
