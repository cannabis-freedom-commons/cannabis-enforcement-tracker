Spain's Tax Agency announced on 9 October 2017 that it had dismantled a
hashish trafficking organisation operating between Spain and France in a joint
investigation with the French Gendarmerie, supported by Europol, Eurojust and
Interpol. [@1] The nine-month operation seized a total of 2,800 kilos of
hashish in four police operations and led to ten arrests, including the
ringleader. [@1]

The network was based in Girona and moved hashish by road in vans from the
south of Spain to premises in Sarrià de Ter and a warehouse in Bàscara, then
on to France in vehicles with forged French number plates, using shuttle cars
for counter-surveillance. [@1] Once in France, the drug was distributed to
buyers in the Marseille, Paris and Lyon areas. [@1]

In the final simultaneous operations on 1 October in France and Spain, the
French Gendarmerie and the Tax Agency's Customs Surveillance unit seized 346
kilograms of hashish from a Mercedes Sprinter van with a false bottom at the
Vienne toll in France and 30.7 kilograms from the organisation's warehouse in
Sarriá de Ter, arresting three people in France and three in Spain, including
the leader. [@1] Earlier seizures in the same investigation totalled 465
kilograms from an Iveco lorry in France in February, 1,276 kilograms from a
Spanish Scania lorry in France in March, 659 kilograms in Alicante in June and
24 kilograms at Melilla customs in August. [@1]

Eleven vehicles and 26,520 euros in cash, vacuum-packed and hidden in one of
the vehicles, were also seized. [@1] Those arrested face accusations of
smuggling, offences against public health, forgery and membership of a
criminal organisation. [@1]
