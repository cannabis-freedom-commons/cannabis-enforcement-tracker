A joint operation by authorities from Chile, Ecuador, Colombia and Peru seized
426 kilos of Colombian-grown “cripy” marijuana and broke up a Colombian gang
planning to sell it in Santiago. [@1] Three Colombians were captured during the
operation, including the alleged leader of the trafficking organization. [@1] On
the afternoon of 11 March the Colombian leader, nicknamed “Tío Chilo”, contacted
the driver to arrange delivery of the drug. [@3] At about 20:00 the suspects
arrived at the agreed handover point at Llano Subercaseaux and Carlos Valdovinos
in San Miguel, took the truck and the drug, and were arrested. [@3] Two of the
three detained Colombians had irregular immigration status in Chile. [@3] Those
detained were formally charged for violating the drug law. [@2] Those detained
were placed under pretrial detention. [@2]

Police found the load in a truck that had come from Colombia and entered Chile
through the northern city of Arica. [@1] Police in Ecuador and Peru monitored
the truck as it moved through those countries in a relay. [@3] The 426 kilos
were hidden in a false bottom of the truck and were destined for Santiago. [@3]
The load comprised 400 packages of “cripy” marijuana with five times the THC of
common marijuana. [@3] The seizure was valued at about US$9 million and
described as the largest seizure of this strain recorded in Chile. [@1]

The international operation “Pacífico Sur” brought together the OS-7 unit, the
police of Ecuador and Peru and the anti-narcotics prosecutor’s office. [@3]
Chile’s anti-drug prosecutor directed the operation while OS-7 agents followed
the recipients and tapped phones. [@3] Prosecutors said the drug’s strength and
price were comparable to high-purity cocaine. [@2] Prosecutors called it one of
the largest drug seizures in the southern prosecutor’s office’s experience. [@2]
