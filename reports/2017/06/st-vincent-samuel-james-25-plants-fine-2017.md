Samuel James pleaded guilty to cultivating 25 marijuana plants at Carib Mountain
on 5 June 2017. [@1] He also pleaded guilty to possessing 1,008 grammes (2.22
pounds) of cannabis with intent to supply it to another person. [@1] The court
heard that Regional Security System (RSS) members were patrolling Carib Mountain
on 5 June when they saw James in a marijuana field with a cutlass. [@1] The
officers watched him planting cannabis, approached him and uprooted 25 plants.
[@1]

James told police, “Officer, I am a musician and I plant weed to further my
career.” [@1] During mitigation, he said he combined legitimate farming with
marijuana cultivation at Carib Mountain and grew plants in the hills to sell and
make wine. [@1] He asked the court to impose a fine and give him time to pay.
[@1] During the trial, he asked Chief Magistrate Rechanne Browne-Matthias
whether she had tried marijuana wine, and she replied, “No thank you. I am not
into that at all.” [@1]

The magistrate noted James had no criminal record, credited his honesty and said
he would not receive a custodial sentence. [@1] She fined James EC$800 for
cultivating the plants and EC$1,500 (US$555) for possessing the cannabis. [@1]
The magistrate said the possession fine was a sizeable reduction from a likely
EC$2,600. [@1]
