Asylum seekers living at the Australian-run immigration camp on Nauru were
reportedly accused of using a teenage prostitute and growing marijuana. [@2]
Asylum seekers on Nauru were embroiled in a sex and drug racket. [@1] One
detainee was charged with indecent assault of a minor, while four others faced
drug charges. [@2] A sixth person, a Tuvaluan woman, was detained in May accused
of providing sexual services to male asylum seekers for money. [@2]

The detainees facing drug charges were accused of growing marijuana crops on the
island. [@2] The Department of Immigration and Border Protection said it was
aware of the allegations but said they were matters for Nauru Police rather than
the department. [@2] The Australian government pays Nauru and Papua New Guinea
to house asylum seekers in conditions condemned by human-rights groups. [@2]

Australia refuses to settle would-be refugees who arrive by boat, saying the
policy dissuades migrants from risking the dangerous journey. [@2] The previous
year, The Guardian had published more than 2,000 leaked incident reports from
Nauru including allegations of sexual abuse, assaults and self-harm among asylum
seekers. [@2]
