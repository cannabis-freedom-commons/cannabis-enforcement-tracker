A federal judge in the District Court of Guam sentenced former math teacher
Vincent John Quitugua to 57 months in prison on 6 July 2017. [@1] Quitugua, 47
and from Piti, was sentenced for possession of methamphetamine with intent to
distribute and for maintaining a drug-involved premises. [@2] He had taught math
at Jose Rios Middle School for eight years. [@1] The prosecutor told the court
he was living a double life, teaching by day and distributing marijuana by
night. [@1] The court ordered him to pay a $200 mandatory assessment. [@2]

Drug Enforcement Administration agents and Homeland Security Investigations
officers searched two homes on Edward Lane in Piti in March 2016. [@3] He was
indicted on March 16 on charges including manufacturing marijuana and
maintaining drug-involved premises. [@3] One three-bedroom residence held 121
marijuana plants in different phases of growth, which the prosecutor valued at
about $145,000. [@1] Investigators found 429 grams of processed marijuana,
$72,922.50 in cash, four firearms, four scales, glass pipes and paraphernalia.
[@2] The court ordered the seized currency, firearms and ammunition forfeited to
the U.S. government. [@2]

Quitugua told investigators he had been growing marijuana for about a year in
anticipation of being licensed as a legal grower of medicinal marijuana. [@1]
Chief Judge Frances Tydingco-Gatewood said it was alarming that a teacher was
running a sophisticated grow house, but found him remorseful and commended him
for staying clean. [@1] Charges of possession with intent to distribute
marijuana were dismissed. [@1] The U.S. Attorney's Office said the
methamphetamine sales and the marijuana grow ran close to a middle school. [@2]
