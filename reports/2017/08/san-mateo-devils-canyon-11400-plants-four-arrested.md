Authorities announced on 24 August 2017 the end of a two-month Campaign Against Marijuana Planting that netted 11,400 plants with a street value of $22.8 million in San Mateo County. [@1] Led by the California Department of Justice, the campaign targeted large-scale grows on public and private land that cause deforestation, damage wildlife habitat and endanger residents. [@1] The sites sat on San Francisco Water Department and Midpeninsula Regional Open Space District property near Devil's Canyon, west of Skyline Boulevard. [@1]

Four men were arrested over the sites: Joaquin Sanchez, 38, and Joel Ochoa Sanchez, 32, both of Redwood City, and Juan Farias Galeana, 28, and Jose Antonio Mendoza, 26, both of Michoacan, Mexico. [@1,2] The suspects were linked to 6,889 of the plants, had dammed a creek and diverted its water into a man-made pool, and used it to irrigate grows in the Skyline Ridge Open Space Preserve, according to the district attorney's office. [@1] Joel Ochoa Sanchez was also charged with unlawful cultivation of marijuana, resisting arrest and assaulting a police animal, and was due in court on 1 September. [@1] The plants ranged from two to ten feet tall and used sophisticated irrigation tapping local water sources. [@2]
