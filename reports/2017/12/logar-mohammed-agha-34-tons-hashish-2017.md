A combined operation by the Afghan Special Mission Wing and the National
Interdiction Unit hit a Taliban drug cache site in Mohammed Agha district, Logar
province, on 12 December 2017. [@1] The force seized 34 tons of raw hashish and
300 kg of processed hashish, a combined 34.3 tonnes. [@1] NATO said removing the
hashish took $5.6 million in potential revenue from the Taliban's trafficking
enterprise. [@1]

The hashish was destroyed at the site by the NIU and Afghan Air Force A-29
aircraft. [@1] NATO described the haul as one of the largest narcotics seizures
the Special Mission Wing, a special aviation wing supporting Afghan special
forces on counter-narcotics missions, had supported. [@1]

CounterVortex noted that NATO described the material as raw hashish, which it
took to mean herbaceous cannabis, alongside processed hashish. [@2]

CounterVortex reported that the Special Mission Wing provided air support to
Afghan counter-narcotics police in the raid on the Taliban cache. [@2]

General John Nicholson, then commander of NATO's Resolute Support mission, said
at a November 2017 Pentagon briefing that the Taliban had evolved into a
criminal or narco-insurgency fighting to defend its revenue streams. [@1] From 1
June to 30 November 2017 the NIU and Afghan army commandos seized and destroyed
narcotics, equipment and precursors worth more than $76 million, a campaign
total separate from this raid. [@1]
