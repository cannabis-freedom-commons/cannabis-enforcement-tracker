Mohamed Benabdelhak, a figure of French organised crime, was sentenced on Friday
by the Bordeaux court to 13 years of firm imprisonment for cannabis trafficking
between Morocco and France. [@1] He was prosecuted for complicity in the
transport and sale of narcotics in a state of repeat offending, and for criminal
association. [@1] His brother Rachid was sentenced to 11 years in prison, and
several other defendants also received firm prison terms. [@2] His lawyer Hugues
Vigier said they would appeal, arguing that the conviction did not reflect
Benabdelhak's actual involvement and that a reputation had been judged more than
the substance of the case. [@1]

Twenty defendants stood trial over about ten go-fast convoys used to move
cannabis in 2011 and 2012. [@3] Most of the other defendants came from Creil in
the Oise and were tried for organised drug trafficking between November 2011 and
November 2012 through ten go-fasts. [@1] Each defendant was pursued for one or
more of those trips. [@1] The convoys were used to supply drugs to the Paris
region and the Nantes region. [@3] The trial was held before the first chamber
of the Jirs, Bordeaux's interregional specialised court for organised crime.
[@1]

In this trial he was nevertheless described as the organiser who commissioned
the go-fasts from Morocco, with a very criminal and dangerous network. [@1] On
13 April 2014, another heavily armed commando opened fire while trying in vain
to enter Saint-Gilles prison near Brussels, where he was awaiting extradition to
France. [@2]
