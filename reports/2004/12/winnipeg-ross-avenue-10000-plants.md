Winnipeg police said an early-morning raid on Thursday 2 December 2004 uncovered more than 10,000
marijuana plants in a boarded-up brick warehouse at 468 Ross Avenue, five minutes’ walk from the
Public Safety Building. [@1] Constable Bob Johnson said officers were “somewhat astounded” by 5,000
square feet of hydroponic cultivation spread over two floors. [@1]

## Scale and logistics

The Free Press reported the seizure as the city’s largest grow operation to date, worth about $11
million on the street using police formulas. [@1] Johnson said the raid yielded more plants than
Winnipeg seizures in 2001 and 2002 combined. [@1] Police believed growers had invested about
$100,000 in lights and trays and that most plants were mature after roughly 60 to 90 days under
hydroponic conditions. [@1]

## Dismantling

Johnson said identification officers would fingerprint the site and drug-unit staff would catalogue
each plant for court before trucks hauled plants and equipment to an undisclosed destruction site.
[@1] Neighbours said they smelled marijuana from the building but had not called police. [@1] A
Winnipeg Police Museum history of the case describes the same Ross Avenue warehouse as a landmark
2004 indoor grow takedown, though it records a lower plant count than contemporaneous reporting.
