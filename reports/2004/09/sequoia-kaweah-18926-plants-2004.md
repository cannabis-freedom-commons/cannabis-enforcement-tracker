National Park Service rangers and officers from cooperating agencies removed 18,926 marijuana plants
from a plantation hidden in the East Fork drainage of the Kaweah River on 2 September 2004. [@1,2]
Thirteen park rangers, a special agent and ten state anti-marijuana (CAMP) officers took part, with
immigration-service helicopters overhead using infrared gear to check the site for growers before
teams moved in. [@1,2]

The farm was organized and well kept, with encampments and acres of cleared underbrush, and teams
seized firearms and hundreds of pounds of fertilizer and pesticides. [@1] The near-mature crop
would have been worth more than $75 million, the park service said. [@1,2] Investigators tied the
site to the 19 August arrest of four Mexican nationals and kept investigating the driver and
passenger of the car that collected them. [@1]
