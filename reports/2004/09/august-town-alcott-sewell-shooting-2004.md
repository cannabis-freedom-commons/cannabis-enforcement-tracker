On 19 September 2004, during a state of emergency declared as Hurricane Ivan approached, soldiers of the Jamaica Defence Force shot dead two people in August Town, St Andrew.[@1] Community witnesses said the soldiers approached 20-year-old footballer Gayon Alcott because he was smoking marijuana and shot him in the stomach, then shot him again as he tried to flee; Sandra Sewell, a community activist, was shot as she sought cover from the gunfire.[@1,2]

Amnesty International called for a full and impartial investigation, noting the army chief's 2003 assurance that abuse allegations would be thoroughly investigated.[@1] At the families' request, Dundee forensic expert Derrick Pounder observed the autopsies and visited the scene.[@2] He found Sewell killed instantly by a military-rifle shot consistent with crouching to avoid fire, and Alcott shot twice, findings he said supported the witnesses' accounts.[@3]
