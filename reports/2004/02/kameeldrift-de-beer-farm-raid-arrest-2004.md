Inspector Westmaas of the Assen police station obtained a search warrant and visited Russell de Beer's Kameeldrift farm near Rustenburg with fellow officers on 9 February 2004, after an informant claimed to have bought dagga there. [@2] De Beer could produce no permit, so Westmaas arrested him and took him to the Assen police station, where he was charged under drug-trafficking legislation and held overnight before release on bail the next day. [@2]

De Beer, who had lived in Switzerland, said he bought the farm to test whether cannabis could be cultivated in South Africa for industrial uses such as clothing fibre and soap, with the standing crop planted by the Agricultural Research Council in 2002. [@1] Officers photographed tubs of seeds, stems, flowers and leaves kept in his kitchen, which he said served as his laboratory, and he insisted he had never dealt in, sold or consumed cannabis. [@1] He spent a night in a police cell before being granted 8,000 rand bail at the Brits magistrate's court, and all criminal charges were eventually withdrawn. [@1]

The courts later backed the police action, with the Supreme Court of Appeal confirming the High Court's dismissal of his damages claim on the grounds that officers had reasonably believed an offence was being committed. [@2]
