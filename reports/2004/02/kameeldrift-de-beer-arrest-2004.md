On 9 February 2004, Inspector Westmaas of the Assen police station and fellow officers searched cannabis farmer Russel de Beer's farm in the Kameeldrift area on a magistrate's warrant, after an informant claimed De Beer was growing dagga on the farm and selling it. [@2] De Beer could produce no permit, so Westmaas arrested him and took him to the Assen police station, where he was charged under the Drugs and Drug Trafficking Act. [@2] He spent a night in a cell and was granted R8,000 bail the next day at the Brits magistrate's court. [@1]

De Beer said he had come from Switzerland to research industrial hemp with the Agricultural Research Council, that the council had planted the crop in 2002, and that he held a permit covering 1.2 tons of seed; he denied ever dealing in, selling or using cannabis. [@1] The criminal case dragged on for nearly a year before all charges were withdrawn. [@1] He then sued the police for R620,000 for malicious prosecution; the Pretoria High Court dismissed the claim, finding reasonable grounds for the arrest, and the Supreme Court of Appeal confirmed the dismissal on 3 September 2010. [@1,2]
