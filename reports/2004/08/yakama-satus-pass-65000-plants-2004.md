On 26 and 27 August 2004, agents from the DEA's Yakima and Spokane offices
with state, county and tribal officers eradicated a field of 29,000 mature
cannabis plants on Yakama Nation tribal lands in Klickitat County. [@1] DEA
air support then spotted two more fields, and agents eradicated about 40,000
further plants on 30 August, bringing the total above 65,000 plants worth an
estimated $35 million. [@1] The Yakima Herald-Republic described three huge
fields near Satus Pass, with plants up to seven feet tall uprooted over six
days and flown out of the woods by helicopter in cargo nets. [@2]

Three men apprehended at the grow site were named as Ricardo Rodriguez, 27,
Mario Rodriguez, 33, and Francisco Davila, 27, all of Jalisco, Mexico. [@1]
They were charged with manufacturing and conspiracy plus an immigration
violation of entry without inspection, and were held in Yakima County Jail
pending a first appearance before a federal magistrate on 1 September.
[@1] The Herald reported the men were paid $100 a day as gardeners, were
cooperating, and were expected to appear before a federal magistrate in
Yakima, with the resident agent anticipating more arrests. [@2]

The fields had elaborate irrigation with a drip line for each plant, four
camp sites for the hired tenders, and thinned trees for sunlight, all within
a few miles of Highway 97 at Satus Pass. [@1,2] A later DEA bulletin called
the grows sophisticated, each with its own camp for the tenders. [@3] The
plants were maturing fast but not fully grown, and would normally have been
harvested around the first freeze in October. [@2]
