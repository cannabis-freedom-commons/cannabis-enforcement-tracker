A 500 kg consignment of herbal cannabis that arrived at Tilbury Docks on 9 June
2004 aboard a ship from South Africa became the starting point for the Ealing
Operation Bandage investigation. [@1] Borough officers placed business premises
in Ealing, where the consignment had been destined, together with individuals
connected to it, under covert surveillance with help from the local
community. [@1] Detectives later said the seizure of the cannabis had led them
to the wider network. [@2]

The intelligence-led operation began on 9 June 2004 with the Ealing Police
Crime Squad and Intelligence Unit. [@1] A 33-year-old woman from Islington was
arrested at her home in June after using a series of false identities, and
further inquiries pointed the Operation Bandage team to a storage unit in
Aylesbury, Buckinghamshire, under her control. [@1] A search there recovered
180 suspected drug packages, of which 168 were believed to be 1 kg amounts of
cocaine while the remaining packages were still to be tested. [@1,2]

Scotland Yard said the haul of 180 packages weighing 168 kg was its largest
ever cocaine seizure, found in a storage-unit crate in Aylesbury after the
Tilbury cannabis seizure. [@2] Police believed both the cannabis and the
cocaine had arrived in Britain by ship from South Africa and were bound for
London and the Home Counties, with the cocaine intended for the crack
market. [@2] Officers put the combined street value of the cannabis and cocaine
at about 10 million pounds. [@1,2]

The woman and a 41-year-old man from Stoke Newington arrested during July were
both charged with conspiracy to supply cocaine and cannabis, the man on 26 July
2004. [@1,2] The reports give no full names for either defendant and no later
court outcome for the charges. [@1,2]
