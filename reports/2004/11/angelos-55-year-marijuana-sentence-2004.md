Weldon Angelos, the 25-year-old founder of Salt Lake City rap label Extravagant Records and a father of two, was sentenced to 55 years in federal prison on 16 November 2004 for selling small quantities of marijuana to a government informant. [@1] U.S. District Judge Paul G. Cassell, who imposed the sentence, called it unjust, cruel and even irrational but said mandatory minimums for possessing a gun during drug trafficking left him no choice. [@1,2]

A jury had convicted Angelos on three counts of carrying a pistol, twice in an ankle holster and once in a briefcase, while selling marijuana, though he never drew the weapon or used it. [@1] Each of the three sales involved eight one-ounce bags for 350 dollars, about 1,050 dollars in total, yet drew five years for the first gun count and 25 years for each of the next two, served consecutively. [@2,3] Cassell imposed a single day for the underlying drug counts and urged the defence to appeal and seek presidential commutation. [@2]

Prosecutors defended the term, saying officers found 26 empty duffel bags with marijuana residue and describing Angelos as a significant dealer who had turned down a 16-year plea offer. [@2] The same day Cassell sentenced Angelos, he gave 22 years to a man who had beaten an elderly woman to death with a log. [@1,2]
