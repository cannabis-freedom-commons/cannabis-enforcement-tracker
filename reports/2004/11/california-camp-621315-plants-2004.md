On 5 November 2004 Attorney General Bill Lockyer announced that the Campaign Against
Marijuana Planting (CAMP) had seized a record 621,315 plants during the 2004 season,
155,000 more than the previous record, with an estimated street value of nearly
$2.5 billion. [@1,2] A later department release confirmed the 621,315-plant total and
the $2.5 billion valuation, and gave the season's enforcement totals as 757 gardens
raided, 41 arrests and 53 weapons seized. [@3,4]

Riverside County had the highest county total with 97,104 plants, much of it in avocado
groves where officials said Mexican drug cartels set up after enforcement pressure in
the northern growing region; Fresno followed with 69,364, Lake with 64,198 and
Mendocino with 63,556. [@1,2] Some 58 percent of all plants seized were found on
public land, and officials said 80 percent of the plants were grown by Mexican drug
organizations, with about 500,000 plants attributed to them. [@1]

## A bigger, more airborne campaign

CAMP added a fourth team in 2004 and worked four regions for months across state and
national parks, national forests, ranches, vineyards and timberlands. [@1] Almost all
raids outside Riverside used helicopters for "short-haul" insertions, with agents
dangling from lines to drop onto gardens; only about a fourth of raids had used
helicopters five years earlier. [@2] Lockyer said the average 2004 raid netted 3,400
plants, against 300 to 500 per garden in 1994. [@4] The season saw two officer-involved
shootings and no fatalities, down from six fatalities and four non-lethal shootings
in 2003. [@1]
