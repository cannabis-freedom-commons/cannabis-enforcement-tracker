Weldon Angelos, 24, a successful music executive and father of two young children with no criminal
record, stood for sentencing in Salt Lake City after a jury convicted him of dealing marijuana and
related firearms and money-laundering counts. [@1,2,3] A government informant, Ronnie Lazalde, made
three controlled buys from him; on 21 May 2002 Angelos sold eight ounces of marijuana for $350 with
his Glock pistol by the car's centre console, and each sale was eight one-ounce bags for $350. [@1,2]
Angelos carried a handgun during two of the $350 deals, though he was never accused of using or
showing a weapon, and police found several more guns when they searched his apartment. [@1,3]

## Sentence and protest

On 16 November 2004 Judge Paul G. Cassell imposed 55 years and one day, the minimum the law allowed:
five years for the first gun count and 25 years for each of the next two under 18 U.S.C. section
924(c). [@1,3] Cassell called the sentence unjust, cruel and even irrational, and wrote that he would
recommend executive clemency to correct it. [@1,3] Angelos got a longer term than people convicted of
kidnapping, rape or second-degree murder, and the case became a leading example in the campaign
against mandatory minimums. [@3]
